## 1. Scope and calibration

- The unit of review is **a particular element, from a particular source community and period, used in a particular product**; not whether an entire culture is available for borrowing.
- Separate **legal clearance**, **cultural authority/consent**, **factual accuracy**, and **representational consequences**. A positive answer on one does not settle the others; this lane identifies referrals, not rights rulings.
- Publicly viewable, commercially sold, museum-held, out of copyright, and culturally unrestricted are different statuses. A publisher's licence can address its photograph without settling authority over the ceremony photographed [J; C].
- Living communities are neither homogeneous permission offices nor frozen survivals. Individuals can innovate, disagree, collaborate, and sell work without thereby licensing everything associated with their ancestry [E, principles 8 and 11].
- Broad comparisons of grammar, material constraints, or institutional functions are not equivalent to lifting distinctive names, stories, designs, or performances. Evaluate the retained specificity and social relationship, not an imagined purity of influence [R].
- **Documented** below means supported by the named source, not endorsed by every affected person; **context-dependent** requires local evidence; **contested** identifies analytical disagreement; **unknown `(to-verify)`** is not permission.
- The decision matrices and transformation checklist are **lane-authored craft heuristics**, not a validated harm score, legal test, or community-issued protocol.

## 3. Borrowing versus copying — name the relationship first

Rogers's categories [R] concern power and cultural relationships, not the number of altered details. The summaries below are diagnostic prompts; mixtures and disagreements are possible.

| Relationship | Working distinction | Worldbuilding question |
|---|---|---|
| Cultural exchange | Reciprocal sharing under relatively balanced relations | Who sets terms, receives credit, can decline, and benefits? A friendly encounter alone does not establish parity. |
| Cultural dominance | A subordinated group adopts a dominant culture's elements under unequal pressure | Is forced schooling, naming, or dress being mistaken for freely chosen imitation? |
| Cultural exploitation | Taking from a subordinated culture without adequate reciprocity or permission | Is prestige/profit flowing outward while source practitioners remain excluded or stigmatized? |
| Transculturation | Cultural forms emerge through entangled, unequal histories rather than sealed origins | Are multiple contributors and historical pressures acknowledged, rather than a fantasy of untouched purity? |

- **Contested:** definitions of appropriation, ownership, offence, and artistic freedom differ; [R] explicitly identifies tensions between anti-essentialism and ownership-based critiques. Do not present this taxonomy as scholarly unanimity.
- **Operational distinction:** borrowing a design problem—how clothing communicates mourning—is different from copying its distinctive local solution. A derivative solution may still require approval even when its fictional label changes.
- **Not a laundering rule:** hybridity is a historical observation, not a warrant to combine protected designs, ritual names, and dress from unrelated communities into an anonymous “tribal” package.
- Reject “change three details,” “mix several cultures,” and “credit makes it fine” as clearance tests: none is a permission standard supplied by these sources.

## 4. Sensitivity calibration — a non-additive risk screen

Record each factor separately. A serious restriction is not cancelled by excellent attribution, a small audience, another low-risk factor, or the creator's good intentions.

| Factor | Lower-concern indication, if documented | Escalation indication |
|---|---|---|
| Access and authority | Material expressly offered for this kind of learning or adaptation | Restricted, leaked, contested, coercively recorded, or authority unknown |
| Cultural specificity | General construction principle or widely shared linguistic possibility | Distinctive local narrative, name-set, emblem, pattern, ritual sequence, or linked bundle |
| Sacred/identity function | Ordinary public use with no known special restrictions | Initiation, ancestral obligations, burial, lineage entitlement, ceremonial office, or regulated wearing |
| Power and history | Source participants can negotiate terms and benefit | Suppression, dispossession, racialization, or commercial displacement shapes the borrowing |
| Representational role | Internal variation, ordinary lives, and agency remain visible | One-to-one coding as monsters, mystical helpers, primitives, erotic spectacle, or vanished people |
| Distribution and monetization | Limited use within explicit permission terms | Merchandise, executable rituals, marketing emblems, mass circulation, or new media beyond the brief |
| Evidence quality | Current local accounts plus contextualized scholarship | Colonial collector alone, unsourced motif boards, machine output, or inconsistent records |
| Reversibility | Review is early; assets can be replaced without exposing restricted content | Launch already fixed, permanent tattoo-like assets, leaked drafts, or hard-to-recall merchandise |

- **Routine research route:** documented general mechanism; no restriction signal; no distinctive source bundle; record provenance and review output. “Lower concern” is not an ethical certificate.
- **Consult route:** identifiable local expression, uncertain authority, sensitive representation, or proposed adaptation beyond published guidance; pause that element and prepare a bounded consultation brief.
- **Stop route:** explicit refusal/prohibition, illicit disclosure, or unavailable non-delegable authority; exclude the element. Do not approach more permissive individuals to defeat the relevant restriction.
- **Unknown route:** mark the missing answer `(to-verify)` and hold the element. Lack of funding or a reply does not convert uncertainty into consent; a new general-mechanism design can be reviewed separately.
- Apply this screen to the **combined culture kit** and to individual assets: phonological cues + clan-like names + sacred animal + garment silhouette can identify a source even when each isolated feature looks commonplace.
- Do not call a population “high-risk.” The assessed object is the proposed use, its evidence, and its consequences; consultation should enable responsible relationships, not make living cultures untouchable.

## 5. Transformation checklist — rebuild causes, not surface spelling

Use only material that is appropriate to study. Transformation is a craft test for independence and coherence, **not a way to cleanse a prohibited source** or substitute for a licence or consent.

| Coupled system | Potentially reusable abstraction | What must be independently designed or expressly authorized | Residue test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Language + names | Phonotactic constraints, morphological types, contact-induced variation | Lexicon, name morphology, social distribution, meanings, and historical layers | Are these real sacred/personal names with letters changed? Is a real language reduced to an “exotic” sound filter? |
| Writing + symbol | Relationships between medium, sign complexity, literacy, and institutional access | Sign inventory, ductus, layout, ownership, and use-context | Is pseudo-writing a distorted sacred passage or recognizable real text used as decoration? |
| Myth + ritual | Social functions such as succession, seasonal coordination, mourning, or legitimation | Agents, narrative relationships, performance sequence, authority, and competing interpretations | Does the same unusual story survive beat-for-beat beneath renamed deities? |
| Symbol + costume | How scarcity, maintenance, bodily movement, and viewer knowledge enable signalling | Emblems, pattern placement, entitlement rules, materials, and sanctions | Does the wearer still reproduce a particular living group's lineage or ceremonial identifier? |
| Era + material | Effects of trade, production change, conquest, revival, or regulation | Period sequence, vocabulary strata, institutions, regional variation, and dissent | Is the source community frozen in one colonial photograph while others receive technological change? |

- [ ] **Inventory before abstraction:** identify each retained phrase, name, motif, story relationship, script form, garment feature, and audio/visual reference, including inspiration inherited through another fantasy work.
- [ ] **Source card:** record author/maker, community and locality where known, source date, represented period, edition/page or collection identifier, access conditions, and exactly what was observed. Keep uncertainty visible.
- [ ] **Purpose statement:** state the fictional design problem without naming the source people. If the only goal is “make it feel like them,” reconsider the brief rather than merely randomizing details.
- [ ] **Mechanism statement:** distinguish observation from interpretation; e.g., “a garment marks office” is a hypothesis to verify, not something a museum image alone proves.
- [ ] **Independent constraints:** derive the alternative from its own ecology, fibre supply, tools, economy, institutions, language history, and internal disagreements; document the causal choices, not a percentage changed.
- [ ] **Coupling pass:** trace names ↔ offices ↔ mythic authority ↔ emblems ↔ garment entitlement ↔ era changes. A copied network can remain a copied network after every noun is replaced.
- [ ] **Near-source comparison:** compare outline, silhouette, naming patterns, symbolic arrangement, and social role side-by-side; commission informed review where recognizability or harm is uncertain. Do not demand restricted details as comparison material.
- [ ] **Rights/authority referral:** identify what still requires its maker's licence, relevant custodial approval, or qualified legal advice; no checklist completion here grants any of them.
- [ ] **Context pass:** examine who gets modernity, humour, political differences, and ordinary work; villain roles alone are not a test, but consistently mapping a living group onto an inferior species is an escalation signal.
- [ ] **Provenance survives transformation:** keep the influence record and agreed credit; do not erase sources merely because the surface now looks different. Obtain consent before naming private consultants or communities in publicity.
- [ ] **Review the released form:** an approved prose description does not automatically cover a voiced invocation, character skin, tattoo sheet, merchandise emblem, sequel, or generative-model training corpus.

## 6. Sacred-material red lines — project stop rules, not a universal taboo list

The principle is to respect the relevant holders' access and use conditions [U, articles 11–13 and 31; E, principles 4–5; J; C]. Sacred does not always mean secret; public worship does not automatically authorize every commercial adaptation.

| Material or condition | Required boundary | Responsible alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Explicitly restricted initiation knowledge, names, designs, narratives, or performances | Do not reproduce, translate, reconstruct, publish, or adapt against the restriction; an online copy does not lift it | Use independently researched general institutional questions, not an encoded reconstruction |
| Known illicit disclosure or recording contrary to community conditions | Stop circulation and seek appropriate handling guidance; do not preserve the content in a public evidence bundle | Retain a non-sensitive decision record, not the forbidden detail |
| Private sacred sites, burial information, or images/names subject to locally specified mortuary restrictions | Do not expose or repurpose beyond authorized scope; restrictions differ by place, person, period, and community | Ask what can be shown, withheld, anonymized, or replaced without requesting protected content |
| Lineage, office, membership, or earned-status identifiers | Do not imply actual entitlement, endorsement, or initiation; changing species or colour does not settle the issue | Invent a different institution and its own sign system, then review remaining resemblance |
| Permission a person cannot delegate under the applicable protocol | Payment, friendship, nationality, or a general consultant's approval cannot substitute for that authority | Respect refusal; redesign without the controlled element |
| Restricted material in an AI prompt, asset service, transcription system, or shared production archive | Do not upload outside explicitly agreed access, retention, and processing terms | Keep it out of the workflow; a generated paraphrase does not solve disclosure |

- These are not instructions to reproduce a sacred rite accurately enough to pass review. Ask about **the permissible process and scope**, not the secret that explains the restriction.
- Nor is this a ban on depicting religion, criticizing institutions, or engaging with a publicly shared tradition. Such projects still require contextual, representational, and legal review appropriate to their actual materials.
- A warning label, donation, disclaimer, fictional species, or renamed deity does not override an explicit prohibition. Credit and compensation address different responsibilities; neither purchases unrestricted cultural authority.
- If restriction status is unknown, write `access/use conditions: (to-verify)` and withhold the element rather than asserting either “all sacred” or “free to use.”

## 7. Consulting-source guidance — authority is specific, not demographic

| Source or participant | What it can establish | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Relevant community-issued guidance or designated cultural institution | Its stated protocols, scope, contacts, and procedures | Permission from unrelated communities or for every privately held element |
| Maker or performer | Their work, contribution, intentions, and rights within their control | Collective authority or another holder's rights merely because of ancestry |
| Holder or representative recognized through the relevant community's procedures | Decisions within that mandate; applicable access and use conditions | Blanket approval beyond the mandate or perpetual coverage of new uses |
| Community scholar, language worker, cultural practitioner, or specialist historian | Context, variation, interpretation, gaps, and possible referrals | Automatic custodial authority or legal clearance outside their role |
| Contextualized archive, excavation report, chronicle, or museum catalogue | Evidence with a traceable collecting/translation history | Present-day permission; collector classification as the community's own classification |
| General sensitivity reader | Representation and likely reader effects within their expertise | Certification for an entire people, every religion, or all rights |
| Mood board, tourism copy, online thread, or generated summary | Leads for verification | Reliable provenance, current protocol, authenticity, or consent |

1. **Prepare a bounded brief:** exact proposed elements, reference images/text where permissible, purpose, audience, media, commercial uses, deadlines, distribution, sensitive themes, and unresolved questions. Consultation begins before the design is expensive to change.
2. **Find the relevant pathway:** use current community-published contacts and ask who has authority for this material; do not appoint an elder, diaspora organization, national ministry, or academic by assumption. Respect privacy and requests not to contact further.
3. **Separate roles:** agree whether the person advises on accuracy, reviews representation, creates an original asset, or can authorize a specific use. Record the mandate, not merely a demographic label.
4. **Offer fair terms:** pay for time and expertise whether the answer is yes, no, or redesign; negotiate credit, confidentiality, ownership, and benefits. Where collaboration creates new work, commission and license it rather than assuming a review fee buys it.
5. **Seek informed, unpressured agreement:** explain adaptations, villain/sexual/comedic contexts, marketing, derivatives, and AI/tool use; allow time and an accessible review format. [E] specifies free, prior, sustained and informed consent, not a one-time surprise at launch.
6. **Respect plural and bounded authority:** record disagreement and jurisdiction; do not treat a majority vote, a convenient outsider, or a second consultant as a way to override the relevant holder's refusal. Avoid exposing confidential dissent in the production bible.
7. **Record the agreement:** elements and versions covered, approved/prohibited uses, distribution, duration, credit wording, remuneration, review points, confidentiality, and correction procedures. Obtain qualified advice for enforceable agreements; this entry supplies no contract or rights verdict.
8. **Plan change and withdrawal:** agree revision/withdrawal windows, re-consultation triggers, takedown responsibilities, and practical limits before release; do not promise that already distributed copies can all be recalled.
9. **Close the loop:** return the agreed version for review, record unresolved issues, deliver credits/benefits, and maintain an accessible correction contact. A consultant's silence is not an affirmative approval.

## 8. Era staging and sparse evidence — do not freeze a living source

Use these as research horizons where locally applicable, **not a universal sequence of civilization stages**. Source date, represented date, and present-day practice must be separate fields.

| Research horizon | Evidence questions | Coupled design check |
|---|---|---|
| Earlier documented practice | Who recorded it, in which language, and how close to the event? What oral or material evidence contextualizes it? | Do not infer myth, name entitlement, or ritual meaning from garment shape alone |
| Contact, migration, and trade | Which terms, materials, scripts, and religious forms circulated, and under what power relations? | Show loans, replacements, parallel registers, and mixed wardrobes without declaring them less authentic |
| Coercion or colonial administration | Were names, dress, education, worship, or recording conditions imposed? Whose categories entered the archive? | Do not treat a collector's “tribe,” missionary spelling, or compulsory costume as timeless self-description |
| Revival, reform, and nation-making | Which forms were reconstructed, standardized, contested, or newly created, and by whom? | Recent creation does not make community meaning fake; distinguish historical reconstruction from current rights |
| Contemporary and diasporic practice | What differs by locality, generation, gender, class, affiliation, and occasion? Who is innovating? | Allow current speakers, new textiles, modern media, disagreement, and changing symbolic readings |

- **Thin-knowledge warning:** this entry does not establish the language competence, ritual expertise, or permission procedures of any particular tradition. Community-specific restrictions and historical details remain `(to-verify)` until supported locally.
- Chronicle scarcity is not absence of culture or proof of extinction; oral histories and community scholarship are evidence with their own context and access rules. Do not assume they are public merely because written chronicles are scarce.
- Where only colonial or missionary records survive, name the author, agenda, translation chain, elicitation conditions, and missing voices. Corroborate where possible; label uncorroborated observations rather than inventing certainty [C; J].
- Keep **attested observation / scholarly inference / fictional design choice** separate. If a garment survives but its use is unknown, do not fill the gap with a universal colour dictionary, archetype, or monomyth; see `myth-symbol/comparative-frameworks.md` for framework calibration.
- No civilization dossiers are offered here: this is a verification method, not a substitute for language-family/writing, myth, symbolic, era, dress/material, and naming research about a particular society.

## 9. Failure patterns and release-form stress tests

| Proposal or rationale | Diagnosis | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| “The museum scan is downloadable, so the sacred design is available” | Confuses access, image rights, collection history, and cultural authority [C; J] | Inspect conditions and provenance; hold the design if authority is unresolved |
| A recorded restricted prayer is translated into invented vocabulary | Lexical substitution retains controlled performance content | Exclude it; do not test how far the disguise must go |
| Borrow a broad insulation principle and derive a different closure from local material constraints | Potentially general-mechanism work; surface resemblance still needs examination | Record technical sources, compare the resulting silhouette/pattern, and escalate specific retained identifiers |
| A fantasy name-set changes one syllable in real lineage names while retaining emblems and dress | Near-copying is concentrated in the coupled bundle, not just the word forms | Replace the source-dependent institution and naming history, or seek appropriate authorization |
| “The archetype is universal, so this particular story belongs to everyone” | An analytical comparison cannot settle authority over a particular expression | Verify the actual source and scope; do not use a pop framework as permission |
| A prose adaptation approved for a novel becomes a spoken chant and merchandise logo | New modality and commercialization exceed what may have been reviewed | Reopen scope and approvals before producing those assets |
| “Our paid reader approved it, so the community endorsed the game” | Misrepresents a bounded review as collective endorsement | Correct the claim; use only agreed credits and distinguish advisory work from authorization |

- These are hypothetical screening cases, not approvals of any real culture-specific material. A failed case is not repaired merely by assigning the source group a sympathetic character.
- Final comparison should include the actual pronunciation, visible writing, costume front/back, narrative role, symbols in marketing, credits, and distribution—not just a source list attached to a draft.

## 10. What a worldbuilder can take — and what stays unresolved

- **Take design questions and causal relationships:** who may name whom, how offices acquire symbols, how materials constrain dress, how myths legitimate change, and how contact creates linguistic and visual layers. Build their answers independently or through appropriately scoped collaboration.
- **Take historical dynamism:** local variation, disagreement, borrowing, suppression, revival, and innovation; not a timeless costume-and-pantheon capsule. Preserve the distinction between a living culture and one archive's representation of it.
- **Take a transformation record:** the design problem, permissible references, retained mechanisms, regenerated features, comparison results, and limits of evidence. Do not describe that record as a clearance certificate.
- **Hand off to lore-shokunin:** proposed systems and dependencies, source IDs, represented eras, attested/inferred/invented tags, permission scope where actually obtained, unresolved `(to-verify)` items, and elements excluded from use. Lore owns acceptance and canon; this entry establishes neither.
- Keep confidential agreements, personal contacts, and restricted content out of shared lore/corpus materials; hand off only the minimum non-sensitive status and approved credit information needed to respect the agreement.
- **Provenance cautions:** these broad frameworks do not establish local authority, settle contested rights, or replace community guidance. Consent cannot be inferred from a book, a museum label, descent alone, or a consultant's fee; lack of evidence must remain visible.
- **Release hold:** unresolved restrictions, missing required approvals, or source-dependent details with uncertain authority stay out of a release candidate. Escalate legal questions to qualified counsel and general research gaps to the appropriate research specialist; do not invent a ruling to complete the world bible.
