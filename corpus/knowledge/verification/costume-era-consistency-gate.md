## 2. Freeze the claim before reviewing it

Use one packet per outfit × wearer category × place × occasion × date window. “Medieval royal clothing” is not a reviewable packet.

| Input | Required resolution |
|---|---|
| Claim and snapshot | Outfit ID; brief revision; exact statements being tested; distinguish narrator fact, institutional rule, character belief, and design proposal |
| Era and geography | Local date interval, calendar conversion uncertainty, polity/jurisdiction, settlement, season; do not substitute a continental era label |
| Wearer | Office, estate, wealth/access, age, gender category as locally defined, relevant affiliation or household relation; do not derive these from phenotype |
| Occasion and audience | Work, court, mourning, initiation, procession, theatre, burial; who sees which layer, and under whose authority |
| Garment construction | Layers, dimensions, panels/seams, closures, support structures, lining, trimming, visible and concealed surfaces |
| Materials | Fiber species or uncertainty; staple/filament; weave/loop/nonwoven structure; dye/process; metal, leather, feathers, beads, substitutes |
| Supply and quantity | Local production, imported fiber/yarn/cloth/finished garment, gifts, tribute, captured stock, reuse; wearer count and replacement interval |
| Technology | Tools, skilled operators, preparation, yarn making, fabric formation, dyeing, finishing, cutting/sewing, maintenance; alternatives allowed |
| Rules and meanings | Dated prohibition/obligation/permission, authority, exemptions, enforcement evidence; motif meaning by user and setting |
| Language and naming | Local term, gloss, source language/script, attested or proposed date, register; garment name versus title versus personal name |
| Evidence and gaps | Author/year plus locus or object accession/context; date of evidence versus date described; confidence and unresolved dependencies |

A missing essential input yields **HOLD**, not an invented default. A fictional premise may be stipulated by the caller, but label it DESIGN rather than historical evidence or approved canon.

## 3. Verdicts: do not collapse separate questions

| Dimension | PASS means | REJECT trigger | HOLD trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| M — material/access | A specified local, imported, reused, or alternative route supplies the claimed item | Every route is explicitly excluded by the frozen brief | Route, identity, or availability is unknown |
| T — technical/scale | At least one allowed production route meets construction and quantity | Required operation or throughput contradicts an explicit bound | Capacity, tool chain, or alternative mechanism remains unresolved |
| E — era | Production/acquisition/use sequence fits local windows and object biography | A required event occurs after the claimed use, with no allowed alternate history | Earliest evidence is being mistaken for an invention date |
| R — rank/rule | Permission is supported, or an accurately labeled violation/exemption is accounted for | The packet simultaneously claims legal compliance and an applicable unexempted ban | Jurisdiction, version, wearer category, or exemption is unresolved |
| S — semantic/cultural | Names, mythic references, and symbols fit the audience, register, and proposed stage | Two explicit brief statements assign incompatible readings in the same context | Meaning is borrowed from another culture/era without support |

- **READY**: every applicable dimension passes; declared violations and design departures remain visible. READY means internally defensible candidate, not historical certainty or canon approval.
- **REJECT**: any demonstrated contradiction with the frozen inputs; **HOLD**: no demonstrated contradiction but an essential unresolved dependency. A REJECT packet may also contain HOLD findings; report both.
- Mark an inapplicable subtest **N/A + reason**; never use N/A for missing evidence. Record the finding ID, quoted claim, bound/source, consequence, candidate repair, and recheck condition.
- Proposed imports, licenses, inventions, or magic are **repairs to evaluate**, not silent waivers. They only affect the verdict after the input snapshot explicitly includes their costs, limits, and consequences.

## 4. Material availability gate — trace the whole route

Use a **chaîne opératoire**: source → preparation → yarn/material → fabric → coloration/finish → assembly → distribution → maintenance. Treat this as an audit graph; textile terminology from [T], archaeological comparanda from [P]/[A].

| Finding ID | Test | What is not sufficient |
|---|---|---|
| MAT-01 | Identify the material separately from its market name; record blended or substitute fibers | “Silk-like,” “scarlet,” or a translated cloth name as laboratory identification |
| MAT-02 | Show cultivation/herding/collection **or** an external supplier and transport path | Local climate cannot grow cotton, therefore no resident can own cotton cloth |
| MAT-03 | For imports, name the imported stage and its bottleneck: fiber, yarn, cloth, or garment | Imported silk robes as proof of local silkworm rearing, reeling, or weaving |
| MAT-04 | Track dyestuff, auxiliaries, substrate, water/fuel, process knowledge, and desired fastness separately | A surviving hue or painted garment as proof of one dye molecule or recipe |
| MAT-05 | Test bulk, frequency, affordability, and recipient access separately from physical possibility | One diplomatic gift as evidence that an army receives annual silk uniforms |
| MAT-06 | Account for reuse, inherited cloth, patching, removable trim, and secondhand exchange | Assuming each visible outfit consumed newly made cloth that season |
| MAT-07 | Include closures, sewing thread, padding, stiffeners, embroidery grounds, and metallic components | Reviewing only the outer fabric while hiding unavailable materials inside |
| MAT-08 | Specify washing, repair, storage, weather exposure, and replacement | A ceremonial textile surviving one rite as proof of daily workwear durability |

- **Closed versus open inventory:** an unspecified route is HOLD; a route explicitly ruled out is unavailable. “No evidence found” is not itself a closed inventory.
- Quantify each production route in comparable finished-output units per period; its output cannot exceed its slowest necessary stage. Sum only independent route outputs, then add usable stock; do not double-count imported yarn as finished-cloth supply.
- Shared labor, seasonal work, fuel, transport, and losses can tighten that bound further. Experimental rates are local to tool, fiber, skill, and task; no universal “hours per tunic” is supplied here.
- If supply or demand inputs are estimates, give ranges. **Minimum required demand > maximum feasible supply** establishes a contradiction; overlapping ranges require investigation, not a falsely precise PASS or REJECT.

## 5. Textile-tech bounds — test operations, not a silhouette

[T] supplies construction distinctions; [P]/[A] supply bounded historical comparanda. Regional first appearances and exact equipment reconstructions remain **(to-verify)** unless a specific source is attached.

| Claimed feature | Required dependency to inspect | False-positive guard |
|---|---|---|
| Spun yarn | Fiber preparation, twist insertion, suitable staple, operator capacity | A spindle can make fine yarn; fineness does not prove a spinning wheel or factory |
| Reeled silk | Suitable intact cocoons, reeling and subsequent yarn preparation, or purchased reeled yarn | Silk can also be **spun** from discontinuous fibers/waste [M]/[B]; “silk is never spun” is not a valid rejection rule |
| Woven cloth | Warp/weft system, tension, shedding and weft insertion, or purchased cloth | Felt, barkcloth, looping, and knitting are not interchangeable names for weaving |
| Wide unseamed panel | Actual fabric width, panel dimensions/orientation, preserved edges, and finishing shrinkage/stretch | A wide-looking garment may contain seams; a narrow loom does not forbid a wide assembled garment |
| Complex woven motif | A realizable selection/interlacement procedure, skill, and time | Repetition does not uniquely prove a drawloom or Jacquard device; hand selection may substitute |
| Smooth curved tailoring | Cutting/seaming method, fit, allowance, cloth behavior, and labor | Curved cutting does not require cheap industrial cloth; rectangles are not a universal premodern restriction |
| Pile/velvet appearance | Distinguish woven pile, knotted pile, raised nap, embroidery, and later imitation | Similar surfaces do not certify the same loom or production chain |
| Stretch or close fit | Knit structure, bias, lacing, shaped panels, elastic fiber, or another stated mechanism | Stretch does not automatically imply modern elastomer; specify the mechanism before dating it |
| Bright or durable color | Plausible dye–fiber–process combination and exposure conditions | Natural dyes can be bright; hue alone cannot diagnose synthetic manufacture |
| Named synthetic dyestuff | Actual synthesis inputs, process, production and supply date | A mauve hue is not necessarily mauveine; [S]'s 1856 anchor applies to that discovery, not every purple |
| Metallic embellishment | Solid metal, strips, wire, wrapped core, gilding, or imitation; access to each necessary craft | “Gold thread” does not automatically mean solid drawn gold or modern metallized plastic |
| Dense uniform outfitting | Spinning/weaving/finishing/assembly/distribution capacities and replacement | Hand manufacture is not impossible at scale, but costs cannot be waived by calling it traditional |

Do not infer an exact loom, laborer's gender, or political system from a weave alone. Test structure, tools, context, and alternative processes together; technique constrains options without determining social organization.

## 6. Rank-rule conflicts — legal dress is a dated predicate

Model a rule as **authority × jurisdiction × effective interval × wearer category × garment/material/placement × occasion → obligation, permission, or prohibition**, followed by exemptions and sanctions. This is an audit notation, not a claim that every culture codified dress in writing.

1. Locate the actual rule and its version; distinguish law, court ordinance, ritual prescription, household instruction, commentator's ideal, and later summary.
2. Match the regulated property precisely: dye or hue, fiber or price, garment or lining, badge or whole robe. Do not replace a commodity restriction with a universal color taboo.
3. Match the wearer using that rule's categories, including office, household relation, income assessment, or licensed service where applicable; do not substitute a modern class pyramid.
4. Resolve exemptions before declaring a conflict: grant, livery, office, ritual role, wedding/festival, theatrical performance, foreign envoy, or other **documented** category.
5. Record enforcement separately: inspector, assay or visual proxy, venue, discretion, penalty, prosecutions or complaints. No enforcement evidence is not proof of perfect compliance or total failure.
6. Decide whether the claim is **lawful wear**, **actual wear despite prohibition**, **disguise/contested recognition**, or **misdescribed insignia**. Only the first is falsified merely by an applicable ban.

| Observation | Gate treatment |
|---|---|
| Prohibited cloth, explicitly called a risky breach | Material/technical review continues; R can PASS with violation, recognition, and likely response declared |
| Prohibited cloth, described as ordinary lawful entitlement | R REJECT if the dated rule applies and no exemption does |
| Royal/official grant, exemption scope confirmed | R PASS only within that scope; permission for a badge is not permission for every elite garment |
| High wealth, legal rank unknown | HOLD the permission claim; economic access and entitlement are different variables |
| Two authorities prescribe conflicting garments | Record an authority conflict; HOLD unqualified legality until jurisdiction or priority is specified |
| Wrong badge, audience notices or wearer is impersonating | Potentially coherent scenario; do not silently promote the wearer to the badge's office |
| Wrong badge, narrator says it correctly certifies an incompatible office | R/S REJECT against the stipulated code; examine reuse, transfer, or retrospective misidentification before repair |

[L] explicitly distinguishes fabric, garment placement, degree, income, service, and exemptions. It therefore falsifies “one color = one rank” as a sufficient reading of this particular rule, not as a theorem about all dress regulation.

## 7. Anachronism sweep — date manufacture, circulation, wear, and description

Keep separate clocks: **technical existence; local access; social diffusion; garment production/repair; wear/deposition; source composition; later transcription/translation**. An heirloom may predate its wearer; a later chronicler may rename it.

| Sweep | Question | Calibration |
|---|---|---|
| EAR-01 material | Was this fiber, species, dye, or imported substitute reachable here then? | Global existence ≠ local availability; trade can precede local production |
| EAR-02 process | Does the claim require a specific machine/process, or merely its visual result? | An alternate manual route may save the result but change labor and scale |
| EAR-03 ensemble | Do cut, underlayers, support, fastenings, and accessories belong to compatible local windows? | A single old brooch is not proof that every garment in the ensemble is equally old |
| EAR-04 institution | Did this office, rank insignia, title, or prohibition exist under this ruler/authority? | A later code cannot be back-projected merely because the polity name persists |
| EAR-05 vocabulary | Is the garment word contemporary, a modern analytical label, a loan, or a translator's convenience? | Modern English narration may use an explanatory label; diegetic speech needs separate staging |
| EAR-06 meaning | Is the motif's proposed reading attested for this audience and occasion? | A sacred sign can be reused, disputed, secularized, or misunderstood |
| EAR-07 evidence | Does the source describe its own time, an imagined antiquity, or an earlier object? | Copy date, depicted date, and object manufacture are not interchangeable |

- **First surviving example ≠ invention date.** Absence is especially weak for perishable textiles; [P]/[A] concern surviving samples, not exhaustive wardrobes.
- **Latest production ≠ latest wear.** Repair, inherited regalia, used imports, and revivals can extend use; require an object biography rather than an automatic expiration date.
- Historical anchors constrain historical analogies, not independently stipulated fictional timelines. An alternate invention needs its dependency chain and diffusion history, not a blanket “fantasy” exemption.

## 8. Era staging without a universal progress ladder

These are **supply states for a particular textile/process**, not compulsory stages for whole civilizations. States can coexist, regress, or be bypassed through trade.

| Supply state | Plausible distribution to test | Coupled change to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| External curiosity | Rare imported finished pieces, gifts, or old stock | Foreign names, prestigious misreadings, restricted access |
| Specialist enclave | Imported yarn/process and a small skilled workshop | Apprenticeship terms, technical loans, patronage, repair dependency |
| Regional network | Multiple workshops, distributed inputs and routes | Cloth grades, measures, guild or household vocabulary, wider imitation |
| High-volume provision | Capacity demonstrated for the stated recipients and replacement rate | Livery/uniform distinctions, quality control, cheaper substitutes, new policing |
| Heritage survival | Old process retained after alternatives become available | Ritual restriction, authenticity disputes, archaic names, curated regalia |

Do not infer metallurgy, weapons, religion, literacy, or a “modern mindset” from one textile innovation. Date the relevant network rather than assigning a single technology level to a population.

## 9. Couple language, myth, symbol, and costume explicitly

- **Lexical access:** a language may name imported, legendary, prohibited, or obsolete cloth. Domestic inability to manufacture it does not prohibit a word, a basic color category, or a metaphor.
- **Term identity:** store local spelling/script, transliteration, gloss, material identification, date, and register separately. A cloth name can outlive its original fiber/process; an etymological place-name is not proof of current provenance.
- **Mythic warrant:** name the narrative/ritual that licenses a garment and the group making that claim. “The temple calls this ancestor-cloth” is not analytical proof of its age or fiber.
- **Symbol reading:** record motif × placement × wearer × occasion × audience. Do not import a universal color dictionary, animal-rank ladder, or one-to-one pantheon–wardrobe mapping.
- **Naming:** separate personal name, inherited surname, occupational designation, office title, and garment epithet. Similar words need not denote the same role; borrowing and prestige renaming need their own proposed history.
- **Disagreement:** priest, inspector, dyer, trader, and wearer can identify the same textile differently. Preserve the disagreement when coherent; reject only incompatible assertions presented as the same unqualified fact.
- Exact name attestations, rare garment translations, and unconsulted ritual restrictions remain **(to-verify)**. The coupled seed files are research leads and method, not substitutes for historical evidence.

## 10. Bounded civilization calibration: England, apparel orders of June 1574

This packet is a **narrow legal-reference window**, not a reconstruction of all English dress or all inhabitants of the British Isles.

| System | Calibration packet | Provenance caution |
|---|---|---|
| Language family + writing | Early Modern English, West Germanic/Indo-European; Latin-alphabet written proclamation [E/L] | The written legal register is not everyone's spoken register; local spellings and exact word attestations need checking |
| Mythic/religious core | Christian creation, salvation, and afterlife narratives; Protestant establishment alongside continuing religious division [R] | Not a polytheistic pantheon; Haigh's interpretation is debated, and policy does not establish private belief |
| Symbolic system | Degree, office, service, material, and garment placement interact; the proclamation exempts “a servant … wearing any cognizance of his master” beside henchmen, heralds, pursuivants at arms, and apparel given by the Queen [L] | The exemption is not unlimited permission to wear every restricted garment, and the proclamation prescribes no badge imagery: it names the cognizance, not the device worn on it [L] |
| Era model | Greenwich, 15 June 1574; the text explicitly adapts earlier Henrician/Marian statutes [L] | Reuse is visible in the document; do not treat “Tudor” as one unchanging rule set |
| Costume/material signature | Wool, silks, cloth of gold/silver, fur, embroidery, linings, gowns, doublets, hose, and separate women's provisions [L] | A prohibition lists regulated possibilities, not frequency, affordability, exact cut, or successful enforcement |
| Naming practices | Given name plus surname is the ordinary formula for the settled English population in this window: hereditary surnames were general by the mid-15th century, unevenly by region and class [N] | Not a universal formula: surname type, orthography, and local transmission vary, and an occupational surname does not attest its bearer's trade [N]; per-document spelling and person identification still follow that document's own evidence [E] |

**What a worldbuilder can take:** test the compound permission rule, the separate supply route, and the gap between public regulation and lived practice. Do not copy the proclamation as a census, a universal rank-color chart, or an entire civilization's costume grammar.

## 12. Application, handoff, and provenance cautions

1. Freeze the packet; split a generic outfit into layers and claims. Label historical reference, fictional stipulation, institutional assertion, and interpretation distinctly.
2. Run M → T → E → R → S, retaining independent findings; resolve alternative routes before declaring impossibility. Record each source's actual scope and each unverified locus.
3. For each failure, offer the **smallest candidate repair**: change a material, add a costed route, reduce distribution, alter construction, narrow the date, or label a violation. Never silently rewrite the culture.
4. Re-run every affected dimension after a repair: an import may change names and prestige; a substitute fiber may change dye behavior; a granted office may change title and ritual access.
5. Handoff to lore-shokunin: packet/revision, per-dimension verdicts, finding IDs, source loci/access limits, declared violations/departures, repair alternatives, and unresolved questions. Lore owns reconciliation with the world bible and all canon decisions.

- **Question-relative evidence:** an analyzed fragment supports fiber/structure; an account supports procurement; a law supports prescription; a portrait supports representation. None alone establishes an entire population's daily outfit.
- **Preservation/selection bias:** elite, funerary, excavated, and museum-held textiles are not a random wardrobe sample. Faded color and conservation/restoration can complicate identification; require the object's technical report before precise claims.
- **Colonial-source bias:** separate observer vocabulary, coercive dress reform, local testimony, and translation history. A colonial chronicle may record practices while misunderstanding their rank or ritual meaning; triangulate rather than discard or trust it wholesale.
- **Thin coverage:** this entry's strongest comparative footing is technical terminology, excavated medieval London, and the cited English legal window. Other traditions require their own dated textile scholarship and, where appropriate, practitioner or community knowledge; no Asian, African, Oceanian, or American rank schedule is certified here.
- **Scarce chronicles:** missing writing does not imply missing technology, naming, law, or symbolism. Use archaeological context, textile analysis, images, and oral/practitioner evidence within their scopes; uncertain reconstruction stays **(to-verify)**.
- **Discredited shortcuts:** “primitive to advanced” dress ladders, timeless ethnic costumes, universal color meanings, and phenotype-to-costume inference are not acceptable gate criteria. Familiar tropes are not evidence.
- **Closeout:** preserve provenance and uncertainty even when the candidate is READY. The deliverable is method plus reference constraints and alternatives, never a new fictional canon entry.
