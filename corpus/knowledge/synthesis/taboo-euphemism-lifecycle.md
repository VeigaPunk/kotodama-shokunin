## 1. Scope, evidence grades, and the core distinction

- This is a construction and inference method, not a fictional history. All anonymous era labels, example expressions, and proposed sanctions below are design variables, not canon.
- **Attested practice:** a located word, prohibition, artifact, or reading instruction; **historical inference:** an explanation of that distribution; **design hypothesis:** an option for fiction. Keep these grades separate in handoffs.
- AB06 and B12 ground the pragmatic and lexical mechanisms; C13 grounds relative chronology; W22 and T12 ground source criticism. Their combination into the workflow below is a craft synthesis, not a named scholarly law.
- **Taboo attaches to an act-in-context:** saying, writing, displaying, hearing, quoting, or permitting access. A prohibited pronunciation need not imply a prohibited spelling; an exposed garment need not be an unspeakable garment-name.
- “Euphemism treadmill” names possible contamination of a replacement by the stigma of its referent. It supplies neither a constant replacement rate nor a compulsory cycle for every taboo (AB06; B12).
- **Verification boundary, 2026-10-17:** DOI metadata/abstracts, the legal text, Folger passage, Sefaria text/version data, dress bibliography, and Met API were checked. Publisher full-text access was limited; no unavailable page references or exact historical ban-onset dates are asserted here. Recheck before importing a precise historical chronology.

## 2. Mechanism typology: identify what actually changes

| Mechanism | Operation | Likely surviving trace | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Referential euphemism | Replace a dangerous referent’s label with a description, metaphor, title, or metonym | Parallel labels in funerals, petitions, medical records | That the referent itself became less feared |
| Minced form | Clip, distort, rhyme with, or substitute part of a charged expression | Oath families sharing a rhythm or initial segment | A regular sound law affecting the whole language |
| Name avoidance | Avoid a particular person’s name, name-character, or eligible homophone | Renamed persons/places; substitution or defective graphs | Wholesale replacement of every similar-sounding word |
| Reading substitution | Retain the written form but prescribe another spoken realization | Marginal instruction, vocalization, reader training | The original spelling’s disappearance |
| Register partition | Restrict a term to a speaker, audience, place, or rite | Ritual doublets, specialist vocabularies | A sequence of successive eras: the forms may coexist |
| Lexicalization/fossilization | A former workaround becomes the conventional label; motivation may become opaque | An ordinary word, surname, garment-name, or fixed title | That speakers still recognize or obey the initiating taboo |
| Stigmatization/renewal | A replacement acquires unwanted associations; another is proposed | Competing official, intimate, ironic, and hostile labels | That each new label is merely dishonest or doomed |
| Reclamation/revival | Reuse an old form as solidarity, provocation, archaism, or quotation | Subcultural use; ritual restoration; antiquarian editions | Uninterrupted survival or general acceptability |

- Euphemism, neutral reference, and dysphemism are judgments of use, not permanent word-species (AB06). The same form can protect one participant and insult another.
- Political concealment, protective circumlocution, respectful address, and a community’s preferred self-designation need separate explanations. Do not classify every reform as a euphemism by default.
- First locate the **hazard bearer**: referent, divine/personal name, sound sequence, graph, image, body exposure, or unauthorized speaker. Then determine which channels are covered.

## 3. A branching lifecycle, not a universal ladder

| Stage label | Social event to specify | Linguistic result to generate | Era evidence to leave |
|---|---|---|---|
| S0: inherited inventory | Old names circulate with an existing distribution | Bare term plus any specialist variants | An early witness with a known genre and audience |
| S1: changed hazard | Succession, bereavement, reform, conversion, epidemic, or altered status relations | A rule targets specified uses | A dated rule, testimony, or dispute; not just a later origin story |
| S2: competition | Institutions and speakers propose or borrow workarounds | Several replacements coexist | Rival formularies, corrections, jokes, local variation |
| S3: conventionalization | A school, ritual office, guild, or household transmits a norm | One form becomes expected in a bounded setting | Copybooks, teaching scenes, labels, recitation instructions |
| S4a: renewal | Stigma or directness attaches to the replacement | A further workaround appears | A complaint about the “old polite word” plus actual newer use |
| S4b: stable fossil | The label remains useful after its motivation weakens | An ordinary lexical item or opaque formula persists | Etymological disagreement; use outside its former ritual setting |
| S4c: compartmentalization | Different institutions preserve different norms | Old and new forms split by register | A sacred/public/domestic distribution, not simple before/after |
| S5: reanalysis | Later speakers reinterpret, reclaim, or revive an old form | Folk etymology, archaizing oath, factional badge | Explicit antiquarian or political commentary |

- S3 can persist without S4a; S4b and S4c can coexist. Loss without a recoverable replacement is possible: taboo does not guarantee a readable fossil record.
- Give each domain its own schedule. A divine-name rule, death euphemism, and dress-modesty term need not change together or at equal speeds.
- Record actual transmission channels: memorized liturgy, legal copying, print editions, migrants, theatre, school discipline, or a guild’s labels. Their reach determines uneven uptake.
- Separate **rule declared**, **rule enforced**, **form first observed**, **form locally dominant**, and **later recollection**. These are different dates and often different centuries.

## 4. Minced forms: preserve the dangerous shape selectively

- Anchor example: **zounds**, conventionally derived from “God’s wounds,” retains Passion imagery in contracted form (Z; compare Ot1.1). Contraction is not proof that every contemporary listener found the result mild.
- Folger’s *Othello* 1.1 presents both “’Sblood” near the opening and “Zounds” at the lines beginning “sir, you’re robbed” and “sir, you are one of those.” This is evidence for a modern edited representation of early witnesses, not an unmediated transcript of English conversation.
- Build a family by choosing what must remain recognizable: stressed syllable, rhythm, onset, rhyme, or one semantic component. Preserve that cue while changing the forbidden portion.
- Distinguish a **deliberate lexical deformation** from subsequent ordinary sound change. Put the former on the taboo ledger and the latter on the language’s ordered sound-law list (B12; C13).
- Decide whether the rule targets intention, exact pronunciation, visible spelling, or recognizable allusion. A minced form can still violate an intention-based prohibition.
- Possible endpoints: socially milder oath; comic old-fashioned interjection; opaque interjection; newly offensive euphemism; specialized stage language. None follows automatically from phonetic distance.
- Do not derive every familiar exclamation from a god’s name. An attractive rhyme is not an etymology; uncertain historical expansions remain `(to-verify)` until a lexicographic argument or early witness is located.

## 5. Fossil diagnostics: persistence is not continuous belief

- **Design-only miniature:** feared creature-name D → protective descriptor “ash-feeder” → ordinary species label A after specified sound changes and lexicalization. Let A become opaque to townspeople while a hunters’ register independently replaces it with B. This is a proposed mechanism, not an attested etymology or canon.
- **Coupled strata:** an authorized old chant may preserve D, everyday speakers use fossil A, hunters use live substitute B, and the creature’s motif remains on cloaks. Define the motif’s permission separately; none of these survivals requires a universal renewal interval.

| Candidate fossil | Positive evidence to seek | Competing explanation to exclude |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary word with a descriptive etymology | Earlier restricted use plus later ordinary distribution | A descriptive coinage with no taboo origin |
| Divine epithet treated as the usual name | Multiple dated contexts and explicit naming commentary | A title that always coexisted with another name |
| Garment-name outliving its ritual meaning | Inventories linking the same term to dated forms/functions | A new object receiving an old prestigious name |
| Clan name or place-name with a replacement element | Genealogy/toponym witnesses and applicable name-avoidance rule | Administrative renaming, borrowing, unrelated homonymy |
| Opaque oath | Early expanded form, intermediate variants, later register evidence | Folk expansion devised after the interjection existed |
| Missing or altered character | Repeated targeted substitutions in a defined manuscript/edition | Damage, scribal abbreviation, ordinary allograph, typographic limitation |
| Blank emblem or renamed badge | Object history plus a rule applying to that wearer and occasion | Wear, repairs, thrift, generic decoration, modern restoration |

- A fossil can be opaque to everyday speakers but transparent to ritual specialists. Record **whose knowledge** has vanished rather than declaring a culture-wide forgetting.
- Word, referent, and symbol may separate: the garment survives while its name changes; the name survives while the cut changes; an emblem is removed but its nickname remains.
- Survival bias matters: officials preserve sanctioned forms; court proceedings may preserve condemned words; collectors preserve conspicuous dress. An archive is not a random sample of speech or wear.

## 7. Reference capsule: Jewish divine-name reading and textual transmission

| Required system | Bounded reference context |
|---|---|
| Language family + writing | Hebrew and Aramaic are Semitic; distinguish Hebrew square-script witnesses, other script traditions, Greek translations, and later vocalization systems (T12; R07). |
| Mythic/religious core | The covenantal God of Israel; divine-name reverence occurs within monotheistic practice, not a simple “dangerous god renamed” pantheon model. |
| Symbolic system | Written Tetragrammaton, spoken substitutes, and ritual permissions can coexist. R07 argues for *adonai* in Masoretic reading and *kyrios* in the Greek Pentateuch; the early Greek sequence is debated. |
| Era model | Temple-era practice, rabbinic recollection compiled around 200 CE, and medieval Masoretic witnesses belong to different evidential strata; do not stage them as one simultaneous scene. |
| Costume/material signature | Ex28 prescribes fine linen, colored yarns, gold, named tribal stones, and an inscribed frontlet. These are textual vestment prescriptions, not proof of one surviving Temple-period ensemble. |
| Naming practices | Distinguish a divine name, a substituting title, and names borne by persons. A name-bearing inscription may be visible where unrestricted vocalization is not allowed; exact community permissions require separate verification. |

- Y6:2 represents the High Priest pronouncing the Explicit Name and the assembled people responding. It provides a **speaker/place/occasion exception**, not evidence for an unrestricted everyday pronunciation.
- **What a worldbuilder can take:** keep “written,” “read aloud,” “authorized speaker,” and “displayed on regalia” as independent fields; loss of a Temple can change an utterance’s opportunity without erasing its written record.
- **Provenance cautions:** the Mishnah is a later rabbinic source, not an eyewitness transcript of every Temple-era community. R07 states an argument in an ongoing debate; do not turn it into a universal ban-onset date. This entry’s coverage of pronunciation history and regional Jewish practice is thin; exact onset and diffusion remain `(to-verify)`.

## 9. Institutional renaming: an unusually sharp anchor with narrow reach

- RL10 changes specified United States federal-law references to “intellectual disability” and related forms. §§2(k) and 4 preserve the relevant meaning and disclaim intended changes to coverage, eligibility, rights, and responsibilities.
- §3 explicitly accommodates old terminology in regulations before amendment. The statute therefore documents a transition mechanism, not an overnight purge of all old words.
- Use this as a **legal-versioning comparison**, not as proof that disability self-designations are mere euphemisms, that all jurisdictions changed together, or that the replacement must later become derogatory.
- For fiction, pair a renaming decree with its implementation clauses: copied forms, old eligibility rules, archive search aliases, legacy labels, and communities with different preferences. These can leave simultaneous documentary strata.

## 10. Dating protocol: what a taboo stratum can constrain

1. Define the object being dated: composition, physical copy, correction, translation, publication, performed utterance, depicted event, or garment manufacture. Never use a single undifferentiated “date.”
2. Transcribe the relevant form diplomatically, including deletions and altered graphs; keep modernization and translation in separate fields. Record hand, medium, genre, speaker, audience, and location.
3. Establish the mechanism. Prefer a located prohibition, reading instruction, or contemporaneous explanation over an etymology inferred from a pleasant-sounding replacement.
4. Separate first attestation from first existence. A dictionary’s earliest located citation is not a coinage certificate; absence from a small or censored corpus is weak negative evidence.
5. Seek independent anchors: accession edict, dated teaching manual, securely dated copying hand, printing state, workshop technique, archaeological context. Sources copying the same chronicle are not independent confirmations.
6. Derive **terminus post quem** (earliest possible date) and **terminus ante quem** (latest possible date) only under stated assumptions. A mandatory form can constrain a compliant official copy, not every utterance in the language.
7. Test preservation and revival: quotation, sacred archaism, regional survival, copied exemplars, satire, deliberate restoration, and forgery can all place an old form in a new artifact.
8. Cross-check dress and symbol without circularity. If the garment was dated only from the same disputed chronicle, it cannot independently confirm the word’s date.
9. Report an interval plus confidence and alternatives. When anchors are missing, report relative sequence or “insufficient evidence,” not an invented century.

| Observed pattern | Safe inference | Unsafe inference |
|---|---|---|
| New form in a securely dated witness | The form existed by that witness’s production | Its invention occurred in that year |
| Consistent taboo graphs added by a later hand | That intervention follows the underlying writing | The entire composition belongs to the later regime |
| Old oath beside newer dress vocabulary | Mixed register, copying, or reuse needs investigation | One of the two must be an authorial mistake |
| Two rival euphemisms in one archive | Variation existed in the sampled institutions | A precise moment of society-wide transition |
| Obsolete garment-name on a later garment | Terminological continuity or prestige transfer is possible | The garment is as old as the name |

## 11. Era-staging exercise: anonymous, non-canonical variables

Assume a proposed culture has three securely anchored events; these are fabricated inputs for testing the method, not reference history.

| Input event | Proposed consequence | Surviving mismatch to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| E1, year 120: office mandates replacement R1 for term D in newly issued funeral rolls | Clerk training shifts; older rolls remain legible | Families and quoted wills may retain D; R1 may have existed before the decree |
| E2, year 165: a securely dated local workshop introduces badge technique K | Newly manufactured K-badges cannot locally predate introduction, assuming no earlier imports/prototypes | Old badges remain in use; old cloth can receive a new badge |
| E3, year 190: same office mandates R2 for newly issued rolls | R1 becomes an archival/search alias, not universally forbidden speech | Conservative rites and copied records may retain R1 |

- A **newly issued, unrevised, compliant roll** using R1 in that office plausibly belongs before E3; a later copy using R1 need not. Stated exceptions or noncompliance defeat the upper bound.
- A K-badge constrains its manufacture or attachment, not the text’s composition. Only a demonstrated association between roll and badge licenses combining their intervals; mere storage together is insufficient.
- If an original commission securely links both artifacts, the conditional overlap is years 165–190, with the endpoint interpreted against the reform’s exact effective date. Without that link, retain separate intervals.
- Add no “three replacement steps = three generations” calculation. This exercise is about bounded institutional events and object biographies, not word-count chronology.

## 12. Application packet for a fictional culture

1. Choose the charged domain, who benefits from restricting it, and whether harm is believed, legal, interpersonal, or occupational. Do not assume the supernatural belief is objectively true in-world.
2. Specify an exception matrix: permitted speaker × audience × location × medium × occasion. Include quotation, education, outsiders, childhood learning, and deliberate offence.
3. Generate competing forms with different mechanisms, then let institutions transmit different subsets. Give at least one replacement a stable endpoint instead of renewing every ladder forever.
4. Couple each change to myth and symbol explicitly: changed divine relationship, inherited ancestral obligation, removed emblem, altered oath gesture, or a myth explaining the new title. Treat the myth’s claimed origin separately from the earliest witness.
5. Couple dress by an evidenced carrier: embroidered name, badge caption, garment inventory, mourning prescription, ritual frontlet, or licensing mark. A word prohibition does not automatically ban a color, cut, or material.
6. Stage a conservative rite, a newly trained official, and an outsider reading an older label. Test whether their disagreements follow the exception matrix rather than an authorial convenience.
7. Hand off options, rejected alternatives, evidence grades, and confidence to lore-shokunin. Do not assign any option to the world bible or resolve canon here.

Minimal ledger fields: `domain; referent; hazardous act/channel; form; mechanism; speaker/audience; place/occasion; sanction; authority; first observed; possible-use interval; copy/composition distinction; material carrier; source; alternatives; confidence; canon status = proposal`.

## 13. Calibration and provenance cautions

- **Unsupported as clocks:** a fixed euphemism lifespan; one civilization-wide sacred → sexual → political taboo sequence; a neat toilet-word ladder shared by all English regions; descriptive animal-names as automatic proof of a hunting taboo. Require local evidence rather than importing these templates.
- **Contested:** exact beginnings and transmission paths of divine-name avoidance; reconstructions of prehistoric taboo motivations; whether a particular reform is euphemistic, emancipatory, coercive, or several at once. Record participants’ judgments and distinguish them from the analyst’s category.
- **Discredited as history-making shortcuts:** universal “primitive superstition → civilized rationality” evolution and speculative origin stories without recoverable witnesses. A useful fictional ideology is not evidence for real chronology.
- **Source bias:** official chronicles and regulations record institutions’ priorities; missionary, colonial, and outsider wordlists can misidentify restricted registers as ordinary vocabulary. Identify recorder, translator, interlocutor, access permissions, and collection circumstances before reuse.
- **Scarce chronicle evidence:** oral and restricted traditions may preserve sophisticated distinctions without dated written attestations. Do not translate “not documented in accessible archives” into “did not exist,” and do not fill gaps with a pan-Indigenous or universal taboo cycle. Such traditions are not reconstructed here; community-specific claims remain `(to-verify)`.
- **What a worldbuilder can take:** a branching replacement history, distinct spoken/written/display permissions, locally anchored era seams, and old forms surviving in different social niches. Take the mechanism and evidence discipline, not a detached sacred vocabulary or a supposedly universal cultural psychology.
- **Final provenance caution:** source editions, access limits, prescription versus practice, and copying versus composition must travel with every borrowed example. Lore-shokunin decides canon; this entry supplies methods and bounded reference knowledge only.
