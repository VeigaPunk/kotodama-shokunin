## 2. The culture brief: constraints do not dictate beliefs
- D1 occupies a cool, seasonally flooded river terrace. Flax plots, sheep on higher ground, clay, reeds, timber, red ochre, and charcoal are stipulated resources; no real archaeological site or traditional dye recipe is implied.
- The chosen priorities are **repair before replacement** and **obligations witnessed in public**. These are political choices, not inevitable consequences of rivers, bast fiber, or subject–object–verb word order.
- Grain porridge and river fish support bank-work meals; terrace houses have raised storage and shared drying yards. Cloth production competes with field labor. These assumptions explain work scheduling without claiming an entire economy has been simulated.
- Exchange brings tools and wool overwraps from upland markets; the one modeled lexical loan arrives with trading-house accounting (§8). No donor religion, costume, or second complete culture is imported.
- A “house” is a residence-and-work membership group, admitting adoptees and unrelated apprentices; affiliation is not automatically descent. Caregiving and birth relationships are distinguished socially, but their unbuilt vocabulary remains in English.
- Delegates rotate as witnesses; bank workers and cloth makers may be delegates. Everyday clothing does not sort people by gender. Public oath rites require the appointed witness to wear the shoulder strip over an ordinary wrap; other parties use oath-cloth, not office strips. Private bathing has no such requirement.
- Work chants coordinate pulling; their recurring English gloss is “hold, show, release.” No meter is claimed without conlang lines. Protective work equipment and winter layers remain practical, not universal priestly or warrior castes.

## 3. Language family, phonology, grammar, and writing
- **D family:** F1 is a stipulated fictional family: an E0 ancestor precedes the archival E1 stage; later River E2 changes /p/ to /f/, while an unsampled Upland sister retains /p/. This is an author's genealogy, not a comparative reconstruction inferred from one word list.
- **R comparator:** Japanese, a Japonic language, supplies a limited SOV/suffixing comparison (Shibatani 1990; Dryer 2013, example 2a). English supplies the contrasting SVO case. Neither comparator licenses importing its religion, dress, sound inventory, or naming hierarchy.

| Component | D1 specification | Operational consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Consonants | E1 /p t k m n s l r w j/; E2 replaces /p/ with /f/; romanization `y` = /j/, `r` = a tap [ɾ] | E2 everyday native words have `f`, not `p`; E1 recitation is separately tagged |
| Vowels and prosody | /a e i o u/; no contrastive length or tone; penultimate-syllable stress, or the only syllable | `na.li.fa.la` → /na.li.ˈfa.la/; spelling does not mark predictable stress |
| Syllables | (C)V; hiatus is separate vowel nuclei; no codas, consonant clusters, or geminates | concatenate whole roots without invented consonants; test every output |
| Nouns | `STEM(-ma)(-ni or -ta)`; `-ma` plural, `-ni` genitive/affiliation, `-ta` object; unmarked subject | modifiers and possessors precede the head; no gender agreement or noun classes |
| Verbs and conversion | `STEM(-ke)(=mu)`; `-ke` completed event, `=mu` reported source; `sena` is noun “witness” or verb “witness” | zero conversion is licensed, not a fresh unexplained homonym |
| Compounding and office | modifier + head; `-ye` derives a human custodian from a noun | `fala-ye` “cloth custodian”; productive grammar is separate from dictionary headwords |

- Clause executed: `Morisena nalifala-ta sena-ke.` / `Morisena oath.cloth-OBJ witness-PFV` → “Morisena witnessed the oath-cloth.” PFV = completed event; this is SOV, not an English-order substitution cipher.
- Belief report executed: `Nalisena nalifala-ta sena=mu.` / `Knot.Witness oath.cloth-OBJ witness=REPORT` → “It is said that Nalisena witnesses the oath-cloth.” The source marker reports speech, not proof a deity exists.
- Pragmatics: an officer is addressed by a current office designation in a hearing, by a given name at home. During a funeral the household substitutes an office or kin description for the deceased's given name until reaggregation; no wholesale sound ban is inferred.
- Writing: E0 has count tallies, not a script for speech. E1 introduces a local alphabet with sign IDs `P T K M N S L R W Y A E I O U`, left-to-right and word-divided; these Latin IDs are a working key, not drawings of historical letters.
- E2 keeps the old `P` sign but reads it /f/ in vernacular text: sign sequence `P A L A` represents E1 `pala`, E2 `fala`. E1 liturgical copies retain E1 readings. Store `{script-stage, spoken-stage, register}` rather than guessing a date from a glyph alone.
- Emblems in §6 are not alphabetic signs; a loop badge does not by itself encode the spoken word `nali`. Literacy, badge recognition, and ritual memorization are separate skills.

## 4. Era model: material, institutional, and language seams
All years Y are arbitrary fixture coordinates, not BCE/CE equivalents. E labels are the designer's periods; the quoted era names are translated participant labels, not additional conlang lexemes.

| Stage | Range | Language / evidence | Institution, myth, and material signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| E0, “Loose Banks” | Y0–79 | ancestral /p/ forms; tallies; no surviving chronicle stipulated | households repair bast wraps; local origin accounts circulate without a unified pantheon list |
| E1, “Tying” | Y80–159 | archival /p/ register; accounting loan `luma`; alphabet; vernacular /f/ variants appear from Y145 | assembly charter regularizes the three ritual offices; red-ochre beads on delegates' oath dress are legally reserved |
| E2, “Retied” | Y160–present; sample Y183 | /p/ > /f/ standard by Y170; old liturgical register persists; house registers gain public copies | charter revision opens red beads to everyone; only the duty-issued witness strip remains restricted; repair imagery is promoted as shared membership |

- Sound law L1: **/p/ > /f/ in all positions within the River branch**, including compounds and names; no other segment changes in this sketch. Spread across cohorts Y145–170 is not an instantaneous effect of the Y160 charter (historical-method comparator: Campbell 2013).
- Worked correspondences: `pala → fala`, `pano → fano`, `nali-pala → nalifala`, `Pala-sena → Falasena`. Upland `pala` is a retention, not evidence that Upland shares a River innovation.
- The administrative era changes at the next year opening after ratification; older contracts remain valid. Y80 = Tying 1; Y160 = Retied 1; therefore Y183 = Retied 24 = continuing Tying 104. The old count survives in household copies, with the counting convention recorded.
- Festival timing follows the observed opening of the lower work gate, not an asserted star alignment. The fixture has no solved lunar intercalation or astronomical orientation; those are `(to-verify)` before a calendar calculation or temple azimuth is requested.
- E0 chronology is deliberately thin: no chronicle verifies who first told a myth. E1 charters establish what an assembly prescribed, not that every household obeyed. E2 reform rhetoric cannot be projected backward as prehistoric consensus.

## 5. Pantheon, genres, and an enacted rite
This is one interconnected pantheon, not a renamed historical one. B describes what stipulated performers say; gender, family relations between gods, and their literal existence are not settled by the fixture.

| E2 name; early E1 form | B office and relation | Mythic action → daily institution | Friction / counter-reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Nalisena`; `Nalisena` | Knot-Witness binds the parties, not the river itself | holds two banks apart so a channel can remain → two parties visibly tie their ends of oath-cloth | delegates praise binding; debtors ask when a bond may end |
| `Talakera`; `Talakera` | River-Opener makes release possible | opens an outlet before the water breaks the banks → an oath includes an expiry or release hearing | a dissenter invokes release against perpetual house claims |
| `Falasena`; `Palasena` | Cloth-Witness records what was done, rather than deciding its justice | leaves the joining seam visible → witnesses inspect and preserve repairs | an accurate record can expose an unjust bargain |

- **Origin myth, B:** the river had passages but no resting bank; holding, opening, and visible joining together made inhabitable ground. No single slaying hero or universal chaos-monster is required.
- **Founding legend, contested within D:** a delegate supposedly opened the first assembly to all houses. E1 institutional copies praise this; no independent E0 chronicle supports the claim. **Folktale:** a bird unties an over-clever pledge; performers call it amusement, not doctrine. No ATU or motif number is assigned without lookup.
- Relational/structuralist design lens: holding ↔ opening is mediated by a repair that remains visible. This is a declared generator, not evidence of an unconscious universal binary; a variant praising invisible repair would defeat that particular reading.
- Passage rite executed with van Gennep's separation → transition → incorporation heuristic: release the ordinary affiliation tie; stand without an office strip while two parties explain the bond; put on the witnessed, retied cloth and return to the work meal. Not every local practice must fit this sequence.
- The rite generates vocabulary, public venue, work interruption, food obligation, naming permissions, and a garment change. Whether participants' words actually alter reality is an unanswered lore question, not a conlang property.

## 6. Symbol set: five registers, limited closure, and drift
The E1 assembly teaches a three-station instructional diagram: upper post, landing, lower gate. It is an engineered civic code, not a claim that all D1 thought is a closed matrix. E2 retains its domains while changing display access.

| Primary domain / deity | Graphic and color register | Animal register | Number register | Celestial register | Direction / material ground |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| binding / Nalisena | paired loops; `ranu` red ochre on a removable clay bead | coiled eel outline, chosen for graphic resemblance, not “universal serpent wisdom” | two parties tie two ends | crescent drawing, conventional association; no alignment claimed | upstream post; an actual tied cord indexes joining, but a drawn loop is only a conventional badge |
| attestation / Falasena | visible seam; `weli` unpigmented cloth | `mori`, a stipulated pale riverbird, drawn standing watch | one public record at the landing | sun disk; hearings held in daylight by rule, not solar determinism | landing; an actual repair scar indexes mending, not proof of honesty |
| release / Talakera | broken loop; soot-darkened detachable tab | moth outline, locally chosen as an emergence image | three stitches unpicked from the tab | **UNALIGNED**: no third sky body assigned | downstream gate; the missing stitches index the release action, not a wearer's moral character |

- Primary domains are context-specific defaults. Licensed satellites: witness-cloth → testimony by metonymy; broken loop → mourning by analogy with a suspended relation; obsolete red delegate bead → claimed seniority by an E2 event/history association. None means “red always rank” or “black always death.”
- Grounding audit: tying, a repair scar, and removed stitches have physical causal links; bead pigment, depicted animals, numeral associations, and celestial drawings remain conventions. Do not count every red object as an index of a delegate.
- Rendering rule: a closed paired-loop badge belongs on the oath tie; the released tab shows an opening. A bare seam on workwear is **UNMARKED**, not automatically sacred. Direction terms are river-relative, not an unbuilt north/east color cosmology.
- Era drift: E1 red-bead access = delegates; E2 red-bead access = anyone. Domain “binding” persists; rank inference is removed, not secretly retained in adjacent animal or direction cells. The old delegate reading survives only as a labeled fossil in older portraits and partisan speech.
- Productivity outputs: “bird + visible seam” = attestation; “broken loop + three missing stitches” = release; “red bead + work apron” = ordinary E2 membership display, **not** delegation. Apply era and carrier before interpreting hue.
- Collision log: separate a material seam from the civic seam badge; separate binding from attestation despite both theonyms ending in `sena`; leave the third celestial cell unaligned rather than inventing a planet.

## 7. Costume system: term, manufacture, wearer, reader, and sanction
R: Roach-Higgins & Eicher (1992) widen dress beyond a garment alone; Barber (1991) supplies a textile-technology comparator. D1's cuts and meanings are design choices, not reconstructed Japanese or Aegean costume.

| Term / carrier | Construction and material | Who wears it / when | What a trained viewer may infer | Restriction and era |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fala`, base wrap | plain-woven bast rectangle, hemmed; shoulder and waist ties; wool overwrap in cold weather | all genders; adjusted for work and weather | fiber, wear, and repair quality, not a fixed rank | no reserved color; E0 antecedent `pala`, E2 broader cloth sense |
| `fanofala`, bank apron | doubled work-facing panel; tucked ties; replaceable abrasion patch | bank workers over the wrap | current task and damage exposure, not hereditary caste | unrestricted; E1 `panopala` → E2 `fanofala` |
| `nalifala`, oath-cloth | portable selvedged band with two tied ends and a visible join | parties to a public obligation | an asserted bond, requiring a record to identify its terms | household supply; not a priest-only robe |
| `senafala`, witness strip | single unpigmented shoulder strip; duty seal stitched at the join | current appointed witness, only while on duty | claimed office; landing clerk checks the seal and docket | false office claim → two bank-service days under the fixture rule; E1–E2 |
| `kerafala`, released cloth | removable edge tab, three securing stitches deliberately unpicked; base wrap intact | mourners or parties awaiting a release hearing | a suspended relation; context distinguishes funeral from contract | rite display overrides office strip; no implied gender or lifetime widowhood |
| `ranu`, bead pigment | ochre on a removable clay bead, not an assumed wash-fast textile dye | E1 delegates; E2 anyone | E1 authorized binding role; E2 no office inference | E1 unauthorized display: surrender bead; E2 prohibition repealed |

- Production sheet: flax → retting → breaking/scutching → hackling → spinning → plain weave → hem/ties → detachable badge/tab → repeated patching. These are a bast-cloth process model; local labor hours, yields, skin comfort, and rub-fastness are `(to-verify)` with a physical prototype, not invented measurements.
- The technical choice is detachable signs rather than repeatedly destroying expensive base cloth. A cheaper alternative is an unmarked tie; households can choose it outside hearings. Material affordances constrain cost, not theology.
- Body/adornment channel: hair is tied for work and loosened during the rite's separation phase; that is an occasion marker, not a gender essence. Ceremonial care goes into evenly repaired edges, supplying a display channel even where expensive dress is discouraged.
- Enforcement pocket: landing hearings have docket checks; a market crowd does not. Witness dress is therefore a strong engineered office claim in one venue and an uncertain claim elsewhere. A clerk must not convict on color alone.
- Misreading executed: an E2 visitor sees a red bead on a repaired bank apron and addresses its wearer as a delegate; the wearer corrects the obsolete E1 reading. Conversely, a borrowed witness strip can pass visually until its docket fails.
- Object biography/deposition: a retired strip returns to the issuing house; its seal is removed and the cloth becomes repair stock. Broken clay beads enter ordinary refuse. Neither a bead deposit nor missing cloth independently proves a shrine, a caste, or an absence of ritual.

## 9. Naming pipeline: decisions and actual outputs
1. Select River E2, then an entity type and occasion; preserve E1 only with a register tag. Root pool = §8, not vaguely “exotic” syllables.
2. Use modifier–head compounds. Personal register slots are **affiliation-GEN + given + optional office**, not automatically inherited surnames. Members choose a given name at incorporation; childhood address uses a household nickname glossed in English here.
3. Generate forms; apply L1 to every early /p/; then run the (C)V and stress filters. Add case/office morphology only after the lexical stem is formed.
4. Screen the full divine names against ordinary given names; whole-name reuse is disallowed in a hearing, not a ban on every syllable appearing in a theonym. Apply the temporary funeral address avoidance separately.
5. Output spelling, segmentation, meaning, stage, and use. Check local exact collisions now; real-world homophones and unintended offensive associations remain `(to-verify)` before publication.

## 12. What a worldbuilder can take — and provenance cautions
- Take the **dependency chain**, not a ready-made canon polity: material choice → term → sound-law history → name/register → mythic claim → public sign → costume permission → dated misreading. Change an upstream choice and re-run every dependent artifact.
- Keep the irreducible lumps: an unaligned sky cell, competing readings of an obligation, an older recitation register, and an institution whose rule differs from street practice. Coherence does not require everyone to agree.
- Expertise limits: Japanese here is a narrow grammatical comparator; Japanese ritual, sacred naming, and dress comparisons are `(to-verify)` and not supplied. No living tradition's restricted ceremonies, deity identities, or sacred emblems have been transplanted as a design kit.
- Evidence limits: no real chronicle was consulted for this fixture. Its E0 chronicle scarcity is stipulated; E1 elite regulations cannot recover every wearer's practice. For real societies, require dated regional objects, texts, and specialist/local scholarship rather than filling silence with an invented “ancient custom.”
- Colonial-source caution: comparative and archaeological works are mediated selections, not a people's unfiltered voice. Do not universalize an Aegean textile account, a European analytic rite model, or institutional dress prescriptions; add community scholarship and provenance before making any direct tradition-specific comparison.
- Source metadata verification is not full-text verification or cultural permission. All physical estimates, astronomical calculations, unresolved historical comparisons, and external name collisions stay `(to-verify)` until actually checked; canon adoption remains lore-shokunin's decision.
