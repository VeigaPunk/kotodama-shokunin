## 1. The causal contract: myth does not mechanically generate phonology

- **Kotodama here is a craft shorthand**, not a claim that all word-power traditions instantiate one religion, that a sound has inherent supernatural force, or that one timeless Japanese doctrine explains them.
- Work through the chain **mythic proposition → valued distinction → recurring practice → authorized speakers/media → lexical selection/formation → conventionalized register → historical residue**.
- A creation-by-speaking myth may motivate ritual recitation; it does not entail tone, an agglutinative grammar, open syllables, or a particular consonant inventory.
- Distinguish **participants’ explanation** (“the name contains the deity”) from **designer mechanism** (“a licensed epithet replaces a restricted appellation”). Whether the explanation is literally true is a separate decision for lore-shokunin.
- Reverse causation is available: an inherited pun, opaque name, or script ambiguity may inspire a later myth. Label that direction rather than inventing a primordial derivation.
- Mark each proposal **EVIDENCE**, **INTERPRETATION**, **DESIGN**, or **UNRESOLVED**. The procedure and fixture below are DESIGN; the Egyptian card is bounded EVIDENCE plus explicit inference limits.

## 2. Keep the mechanisms distinct

| Mechanism / framework | Diagnostic | Fictional application | Calibration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arbitrariness, iconicity, systematicity — Dingemanse et al. (2015) | Conventional association; resemblance; statistical predictability, respectively | Let all three coexist within one sacred lexicon | Iconicity is not evidence of mythic ancestry; systematicity need not involve resemblance |
| Phonestheme — Bergen (2004) | Recurrent form–meaning association not straightforwardly assembled from productive morphemes | A local initial sequence becomes associated with binding, radiance, or concealment | English `gl-` light/vision evidence does not export `gl-` to every language |
| Productive morpheme | A segment has a repeatable compositional contribution and licensed distribution | An agent suffix builds “keeper,” “releaser,” and cult offices | Do not count derivatives of one root as independent evidence for a phonestheme |
| Ideophone / sound imitation | Depictive lexical behavior or imitation of a perceived sound, requiring its own analysis | Chant recruits a conventional sound of splashing | An ideophone is not automatically a sacred word, phonestheme, or divine name |
| Ritual/register convention | The same expression changes appropriateness with speaker, occasion, audience, or medium | Specialist diction distinguishes a blessing from ordinary description | Sacred status is not a phonological feature |
| Name avoidance / substitution — compare Allan & Burridge (2006) | An expression is avoided under specified social conditions | Public epithet, address-title, written omission, or temporary mourning substitute | Protection, deference, censorship, and secrecy are different possible motives |

A near-universal phoneme-to-meaning cipher is **unsupported as historical-linguistic method**. Pop “every /s/ means serpent/sun” decoding, Jungian symbol dictionaries, and universal monomyth templates cannot establish a word’s etymology; use them only as explicitly fictional interpretations, not linguistic evidence.

## 3. Derivation worksheet: establish the missing links

1. **Bound the substrate:** culture/community, era, genre, speaker interests, source status; distinguish court hymn, household practice, literary myth, and later antiquarian reconstruction.
2. **Extract propositions, not god slots:** “a voice binds an oath” is usable; “a wisdom god exists” does not yet predict lexical behavior. Record rival accounts without merging them into a single original.
3. **Identify a recurrent act:** swearing, curing, weaving, burial, harvest naming, investiture. Name the practitioner, recipient, audience, object, and occasion.
4. **Choose the mediation rule:** naming reveals, summons, protects, transfers, remembers, legitimates, or endangers; specify which of these is believed, enforced, and potentially real.
5. **Freeze a language baseline:** inherited inventory, phonotactics, root stock, word-formation rules, ordinary register, and script situation. Retrofitting every root to the myth destroys the baseline.
6. **Choose the linguistic operation:** semantic extension, compounding, affixation, borrowing, analogical recruitment, euphemistic substitution, formulaic retention, or spelling convention. Every output needs one.
7. **Build contrasts:** ordinary/sacred senses, public/restricted names, authorized/unauthorized utterances, and inherited/reformed forms; include exceptions and unrelated look-alikes.
8. **Stage and audit:** give changes an order, affected population, medium, and surviving fossil; test a new word, a name, a taboo edge case, and an era mismatch before handoff.

Minimum record per proposed form: `form-ID | phonological form | spelling-ID | segmentation | literal gloss | contextual sense | myth-link | operation | register | speaker/audience | era | alternative | evidence/status`.
A phonestheme annotation is not a morphological segmentation: keep the whole lexical root intact unless the grammar independently licenses its parts.

## 4. Bounded historical calibration: a Ramesside Egyptian name narrative

This card concerns the **particular manuscript and catalogue account**, not a uniform account of Egyptian belief across millennia. Spellings *Re/Ra*, *Isis*, and *Horus* are conventional scholarly forms here, not recovered ritual recordings.

| System | Bounded reference | Consequence / limit |
|---|---|---|
| Language family + writing | Egyptian belongs to Afroasiatic; family/script background: Allen (2014), introductory passage check (to-verify). Cat. 1993 itself is catalogued as **hieratic** | Do not infer spoken vowels or a sound-symbolic inventory from translated names or pictorial signs |
| Mythic/pantheon core | Isis constructs a snake that poisons the sun-god Re; healing is conditional on disclosure of his secret name; Horus is the permitted further recipient (Loprieno, n.d.) | The significant relation is restricted transmission, not merely “knowing any god-name gives unlimited power” |
| Symbolic system | The catalogue identifies Isis by the throne sign on her head and describes falcon-headed figures and Double Crowns | Signs identify divine figures within a specific iconographic convention; glyph, pronunciation, and referent are not interchangeable |
| Era model | Catalogue: New Kingdom, Dynasty 20, **1190–1077 BCE**; provenance listed as Thebes/Deir el-Medina **(?)** | Date the surviving witness; this does not date the invention of the story. Preserve the catalogue’s findspot uncertainty |
| Costume/material signature | Papyrus manuscript; divine beard, crowns, and head-emblems in its vignette (catalogue drawing description) | These are divine/regal visual identifiers, not a survey of everyday clothing or proof all ritual practitioners dressed alike |
| Naming practice illustrated | Publicly identifiable Re possesses a separately restricted name; revelation is granted to Isis with a specific onward-disclosure condition | Keep identity, public appellation, hidden appellation, and authorized recipient distinct; this is not evidence that every Egyptian personal name was secret |

- **What a worldbuilder can take:** design an access-controlled name relationship, a healing recital that draws on a mythic precedent, and a visual marker that identifies a figure without disclosing the restricted appellation.
- **Provenance cautions:** the accessible account is a modern museum summary, not a fresh translation; Borghouts (1978), text 84, pp. 51–55 is its checkable translation pointer. The catalogue’s Drovetti acquisition in 1824 is not an archaeological find report.
- **Thin coverage / scarce evidence:** this entry does not establish household belief, population-wide observance, exact historical pronunciation, or everyday naming frequencies. Manuscript survival and elite written genres are selective; later classicizing, colonial-era collecting, and modern translations require separate evaluation. Never fill those gaps with invented ethnography.

## 5. Worked derivation A: disposable myth-and-language fixture

Everything from this section through §10 is invented **method demonstration**, not a proposal that any named setting contains these people, deities, words, materials, laws, or events. It is not reconstructed Egyptian or Japanese.

| Input-ID | Provisional mythic proposition | Distinction to operationalize | Candidate practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | The first voice gathered drifting human voices into knots | Binding can preserve memory or constrain a person | Oath-taking with a knotted cord |
| M2 | Dawn returned when that voice loosened one knot | Release can restore light but also destroy an obligation | Dawn rite with a controlled untying |
| M3 | The river carries away names left unclosed | A personal/divine appellation can be dangerously exposed | Mediated address and restricted name transmission |

- **Language baseline:** an invented language with no genealogical relatives specified; do not claim a real-world family. Consonants `/p t k m n s h l r w/`; vowels `/a e i o u/`; syllables `(C)V(C)`, codas `/n l r s/`; no tautosyllabic consonant clusters; penultimate-syllable stress.
- Spellings below map letters directly to these phonemes; hyphens show analysis, not pronunciation. Capitalization of names is editorial, not a native category or a divine morpheme.
- Ordinary lexicon predates the proposed cult reorganization. These selected roots are not all the words speakers know; mundane meanings and unrelated formal neighbors must remain possible.
- **Writing:** E0 below has no script; E1 introduces a conventional alphabet and its own access rules. This is one optional history, not an oral→literate ladder of cultural advancement.

## 6. Worked derivation B: candidate phonestheme sets, with controls

Choose locally salient sequences from the existing inventory, then hypothesize repeated liturgical collocation and **analogical recruitment**. Myth motivates the promoted associations; it does not explain why the original roots had those sounds.

| Candidate initial sequence | Promoted whole roots and glosses | Unrelated root with the same sequence | Myth/practice route |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/na-/` | `nasu` “knot; bind”; `nali` “oath”; `naro` “remembered pattern” | `nape` “tuber” | M1: knot-making, vow recitation, and remembrance become a teaching cluster |
| `/lu-/` | `luma` “dawn”; `lusi` “loosen”; `luri` “return” | `luko` “elbow” | M2: the dawn recital repeatedly juxtaposes returning light and loosening |
| `/sa-/` | `sami` “river mist”; `saro` “erode”; `salu` “fade from hearing” | `sapo` “wooden scoop” | M3: river dispersal becomes an image of lost speech |
| Other roots / controls | `kete` “seam”; `kelo` “guard”; `hanu` “person”; `peta` “vessel” | `kete` expresses joining without `/na-/` | Useful meanings need not belong to the favored sound cluster |

- No productive morphemes `na-`, `lu-`, or `sa-` are specified; the remainders are not assigned compositional glosses. “Any new `/na-/` word must mean binding” would be a different, stronger grammar.
- Toy check: define binding set **B = {nasu, nali, naro, kete}** before counting. In the complete root table, `P(B | na-initial) = 3/4`; `P(B | not na-initial) = 1/12`. Count root types, not repeated tokens or descendants of one root.
- These are **manufactured fixture proportions**, not field observations, statistical significance, or a replication of Bergen’s priming experiment. The table is a reproducible design check, not evidence that a natural language developed this way.
- For a larger fictional lexicon, compare the entire eligible root stock; exclude productive prefix families and shared etymological families as independent evidence. Preserve false positives and false negatives; no universal enrichment threshold is asserted here.
- Possible later emic doctrine: teachers insist `/na/` is the primordial binding syllable. Designer annotation: **folk analysis of the lexical cluster**, not historical proof. Later reanalysis into an actual productive prefix would require a separately dated grammar change.

## 7. Worked derivation C: sacred lexicon, morphology, and divine names

Formation rules: modifier precedes nominal head; object precedes verb in an object–verb compound. Productive suffixes: `-ri` forms an agent from a verb/compound verb; `-ma` forms a consecrated implement associated with a noun; `-ta` forms a personal name meaning “dedicated to X” from a licensed sacred appellation.

| Output / analysis | Literal gloss → proposed use | Trace and control |
|---|---|---|
| `nasu` | knot/bind → binding an oath in ritual context | M1 + semantic specialization; the ordinary knot sense remains |
| `nasu-ma` → `nasuma` | knot–consecrated.implement → ritual binding token | Productive morphology, not another independent `/na-/` root |
| `nali-ma` → `nalima` | oath–consecrated.implement → consecrated oath cord | M1 + material specialization; an ordinary cord is not consecrated merely by resembling it |
| `luma-nali` → `lumanali` | dawn–oath → dawn oath; by metonymy, the associated observance | M2 + compound + event naming; it does not literally contain a “festival” suffix |
| `nasu-kelo-ri` → **Nasukelori** | knot–guard–agent → “Knot-Guardian,” restricted cult appellation | M1 + office title promoted to divine name; lexical transparency does not preclude restricted use |
| `lusi-ri` → **Lusiri** | loosen–agent → “Releaser,” public devotional epithet of the same figure | M2 + productive agent noun; public and restricted names are not two separate gods |
| `Lusiri-ta` → **Lusirita** | Releaser–dedicated.person → a permitted theophoric personal name | Name pool licenses the public epithet; not every noun accepts `-ta` as a personal name |

- Name ledger for this fixture: `D1 = divine referent`; `D1-R = Nasukelori, restricted appellation`; `D1-P = Lusiri, public epithet`; `P1 = hypothetical person bearing Lusirita`. The relation between P1 and D1 is dedication, **not identity**.
- The fixture’s theology may call D1-R a “source-name”; its demonstrable grammar still yields an intelligible compound. An opaque primordial name would instead require inheritance, borrowing, or an explicitly unanalyzed residue.
- Scansion: `na.su.ke.LO.ri` and `lu.SI.ri`; for the personal name, `lu.si.RI.ta`. Stress follows the same rule; do not invent “divine stress” after choosing the names.
- Rejected shortcut: deriving every god-name from `/na-/` because the culture values knots. An epithet can select the release cluster; a minor local cult may use an unrelated inherited root.

## 8. Worked derivation D: name taboo as a permission system

**Fixture rule at E1:** the restricted appellation and recognizable quoted/derived uses are controlled, not the syllable `/na/` or all reference to D1. Spoken authorization requires a licensed officiant, the dawn rite, a closed admitted audience, and the consecrated shoulder cord; clothing is necessary but not sufficient. Sealed manuscript access has a separate custodial license. Default public replacement: **Lusiri**.

| Parameter to specify | Fixture choice | Consequence to check |
|---|---|---|
| Object of avoidance | D1-R and its recognized derivatives/reflexes, not every same-onset word | `nasu` “knot” and `nape` “tuber” remain legal ordinary words |
| Address vs reference | Spoken invocation, third-person mention, and quotation of D1-R all require spoken authorization | “I only quoted it” is not a loophole; public reference uses D1-P |
| Spoken vs written medium | Admitted custodians may silently read/copy full D1-R in sealed ritual records; public texts use D1-P | Custodial access does not authorize reading aloud; public transcription is not permitted |
| Homophony vs interpretation | No general homophone ban is imposed; deliberate circumvention is adjudicated by the cult | Similarity alone does not replace the need to specify target, context, and intent |
| Human naming | Public personal names may use D1-P, not D1-R | `Lusirita` is licensed; `Nasukelorita` is well-formed but not publicly licensed |
| Duration and sanction | E1 violations trigger exclusion from rites; officeholders additionally lose office; no automatic magical death is specified | Belief in supernatural danger and observable enforcement remain separate fields |
| Inheritance / teaching | Teachers who are licensed officiants may transmit the name during admitted dawn sessions with the proper cord | A complete silence rule would otherwise make transmission impossible |

Derive effects only as licensed: **lexical** substitution and restricted name pools; **pragmatic** audience-sensitive reference; **graphic** closed/public textual variants; **social** custodianship. Do not posit a sound change merely because a word becomes taboo. Loss, preservation, or later recovery of a forbidden form depends on actual transmission; taboo does not guarantee archival survival.

## 9. Worked derivation E: era, symbol, and costume staging

These stages form one hypothetical sequence. They are not a general theory of religious or technological progress; elapsed durations are deliberately unspecified.

| Stage | Language/name state | Ritual, symbol, and material state | Fossil / audit target |
|---|---|---|---|
| E0: oral local practice | Ordinary roots and compositional titles; no centralized secrecy rule | Knotted bast-fiber cords accompany local oaths; an unbroken loop can symbolize retained speech | A later elite claim of E0 monopoly would be retrospective legitimation, not established history |
| E1: cult standardization | Name pool, permission matrix, alphabet, and sealed/public textual distinction are institutionalized | A consecrated bast-fiber shoulder cord marks an admitted officiant; `nalima` names its ritual role | Visible motif does not identify a speaker without admission records; no imported color meaning is assumed |
| E2: ordinary speech diverges | Regular intervocalic `/s/ > /h/` in ordinary speech: `nasu > nahu`, `lusi > luhi`, `Lusiri > Luhiri`, `Lusirita > Luhirita`, `Nasukelori > Nahukelori` | Taught liturgy retains E1 `/s/`; everyday imitation cords need not carry ritual authority | Both old and recognized new forms of D1-R remain restricted; a pronunciation change does not silently cancel a referent-aware taboo |
| E3: proposed institutional reform | A civil authority permits public citation while some ritual communities retain E1 restrictions | The loop motif can become heritage dress outside the cult; consecration remains community-specific | One era now contains competing rules; tag jurisdiction/community rather than declaring the taboo universally extinct |

- The E2 rule acts on eligible ordinary vocabulary whether sacred or mundane; `sami`, `saro`, `salu`, and `sapo` retain initial `/s/`, which is not intervocalic. No exceptional “holy immunity” is needed to explain their retention.
- Frozen liturgical `/s/` requires teaching and a distinct register; an old spelling alone cannot prove an old pronunciation survived. The E2 ordinary reflex is not a separate deity.
- Costume coupling is institutional, not anatomical: wearing the cord changes entitlement, not the speaker’s vocal tract. The named fiber and knotting are material specifications; dye symbolism, gold embroidery, and secret stitching are not automatic additions.
- For contact or conversion variants, keep donor form, adaptation, translator’s public substitute, and alleged equivalence as separate fields. A translated epithet need not share the recipient language’s phonestheme.

## 10. Exercise the proposed system before exporting it

| Trial | Expected result under the fixture | Defect exposed if the result differs |
|---|---|---|
| New consecrated implement `peta-ma` → `petama` | Well-formed “consecrated vessel,” with no `/na-/` requirement | Sacred vocabulary was confused with the binding sound cluster |
| E1 licensed officiant, proper cord, admitted dawn audience, D1-R invocation | Permitted under the complete permission rule | One of speaker/occasion/audience/object conditions was never specified |
| E1 unlicensed wearer of an imitation cord invokes D1-R | Not permitted; visual resemblance does not grant admission | Costume was mistaken for sufficient authority |
| E2 speaker says ordinary `nahu` “knot” at a market | Permitted; common-root meaning is not D1-R | Name taboo was silently expanded to the whole lexicon |
| E2 unauthorized speaker quotes **Nahukelori** | Not permitted under the E2 recognition rule | Sound change was used as an unmotivated taboo loophole |
| E2 public roll lists **Luhirita** | Permitted ordinary personal name; liturgical **Lusirita** needs a register explanation | Names were frozen or modernized without applying the declared chronology |
| E0 document uses the E1 alphabet and sealed-register policy | Anachronism unless later copy, interpolation, or revised staging is explicitly proposed | Institutional and script history were projected backward |

These are expected decisions from the specified design, not reported playtest results or evidence of actual supernatural effects. Extend with an outsider, a child learning the name, a contested license, a public recording, and a reform-era mixed audience; record whose rule adjudicates each scene.

## 11. What a worldbuilder can take

- A **derivation graph**, not a list of exotic sounds: each lexical item traces to a myth-link, recurrent practice, linguistic operation, register, and era.
- A small promoted phonestheme bank with unrelated neighbors and meaningful nonmembers; whole roots remain distinct from compositional affixes.
- A sacred lexicon that includes mundane/sacred double uses, implements, actions, observances, titles, and permitted personal names; not only deity labels.
- A divine-name ledger separating referent, restricted appellation, public epithet, translation, and theophoric derivative; a permission matrix makes secrecy usable in dialogue and documents.
- An era table connecting sound change, transmission, iconography, and ritual clothing without making any one of them automatically determine the others.
- **Handoff to lore-shokunin:** send these as unadopted options, with candidate IDs, derivations, incompatible alternatives, evidence status, unresolved checks, and the consequences of accepting each option. Do not write world-bible facts or resolve whether the deity/name-power actually exists.

## 12. Provenance cautions and stop conditions

- **Verified surface limits:** Bergen’s author-hosted opening/method discussion and the museum catalogue were inspected; Dingemanse et al.’s institutional abstract was inspected; Allan & Burridge’s publisher frontmatter verifies the edition and relevant chapter, not every example in the book. Borghouts’ passage pointer is catalogue-supplied; the translation was not independently inspected.
- **Open check:** Allen’s introductory classification/script passage remains (to-verify). Egyptian historical phonology and broader everyday naming practice are thin in this entry; do not expand the manuscript card into an ethnography or derive authentic Egyptian phonesthemes from it.
- A modern spelling, translated pun, shared motif, or repeated epithet cannot by itself prove common descent. Require linguistic correspondences, dated witnesses, and a plausible transmission history before making historical claims.
- Calibrate old comparative and colonial-source claims separately: who recorded the practice, in which language, under what power relation, and how long after the alleged event? Chronicle silence does not establish an absent belief or an unrestricted name.
- Do not promote a phonestheme into a universal sound essence, a design heuristic into an anthropological law, or a participant’s explanation into proven etymology. Preserve contested readings as alternatives; mark unsupported particulars `(to-verify)` rather than filling them in.
- Stop export when segmentation fails, ordinary roots contradict the permission rules, a historical claim lacks a checkable witness, an era change has no transmission path, or an example is being treated as canon. Repair the local method or hand off the uncertainty; canon remains outside this lane.
