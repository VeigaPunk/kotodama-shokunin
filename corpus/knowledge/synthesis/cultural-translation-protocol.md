# Cultural Translation Protocol: Terms in Context, Name-First Reading, and Translation Notes
- topic: Present a constructed culture through intelligible action rather than exposition dumps; stage unfamiliar terms, establish names before explaining their histories, and separate necessary comprehension from optional translation commentary.

## 1. Scope and calibration

- Output: a presentation policy, exposure ledger, and comprehension checks—not names, history, religion, or costume adopted as canon.
- Separate **world fact**, **a character’s belief**, **a translator’s rendering**, and **an audience inference**. A smooth English sentence does not collapse these into one truth.
- Constructed cultures lack an independently attested source-language original unless the project actually supplies one; applying translation scholarship here is an analogy, not scholarly validation of invented history.
- **Established terminology:** paratext/peritext/epitext; domestication/foreignization; thick translation. **Local craft heuristics:** the tiers below, name-first reading, exposure budgets, and deletion tests. None is an experimentally established law of reader cognition.
- Citations identify real works; linked records verify the stated bibliographic or summary-level claims, and the Appiah page-level check rests on independent scholarly quotations of p. 817 rather than on inspection of the paywalled full text. No direct scholarly quotation or uninspected page-specific attribution is supplied by this entry.

## 2. Frameworks: useful distinctions, not automatic prescriptions

| Framework | Operational use | Calibration and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Venuti (2018 [1995]): translator visibility; domestication/foreignization | Ask which institutions become deceptively familiar through English equivalents, and whose speech is marked as strange | Influential, contested historical/ethical argument; neither a binary scoring system nor “more invented words = more cultural respect” |
| Genette (1997 [1987]): paratext; peritext versus epitext | Treat attached glossaries, maps, notes, and prefaces as framing devices; distinguish them from surrounding interviews or publicity | Descriptive literary framework, not proof that readers consult every attachment; books, manuscripts, and interactive media have different affordances |
| Appiah (1993): thick translation | Make linguistic and cultural context available when a dictionary equivalent hides what an utterance does | Contextual, teaching-oriented argument (an academic rendering), not a demand to annotate every fantasy noun; identity, pagination, and the p. 817 definitional sentence checked through independent scholarly quotations, not a direct full-text collation |

- Practical synthesis: keep the scene’s operational meaning available in the scene; preserve additional context somewhere appropriate rather than deleting it merely to accelerate pacing.
- A heavily annotated fictional chronicle can be a deliberate form. “No exposition dumps” means avoiding inert, misplaced explanation—not banning explanation, scholarship, or translator presence.

## 3. Establish the translation contract before introducing terms

1. **Audience:** identify target-language readers and their expected knowledge; do not assume one universal outsider or that all readers need the same gloss.
2. **Viewpoint:** record what the focal character knows, mistakes, conceals, and would never bother explaining to another insider.
3. **Rendering convention:** distinguish translated ordinary speech, retained vocabulary, transliterated writing, and names. Romanization represents writing/pronunciation; it is not a semantic translation.
4. **Translator status:** choose ordinary authorial narration, an explicitly fictional translator, or a real localization layer; do not slide between their authorities unnoticed.
5. **Access route:** identify what must survive without maps, hover text, appendices, color discrimination, or pronunciation audio.
6. **Political vocabulary:** for words such as “lord,” “priest,” “tribe,” and “empire,” state which local function is approximated and which imported associations are not licensed.
7. **Stability:** retain one audience-facing handle per referent until a change has a scene-level reason; variation in address is not permission for untracked synonym churn.

- Translation choice and explanation depth are independent: a retained word may need only a category cue; a familiar English word may need substantial cultural correction.
- Keep these decisions in the proposal ledger. They describe presentation, not a right to rename a polity or decide which theology is true.

## 4. Terms-in-context tiers: choose by the next necessary inference

| Tier | Reader needs now | Presentation move | Candidate test |
|---|---|---|---|
| T0: ordinary rendering | Recognize an everyday action or object | Use the target-language word where its implications are acceptable | Does “coat” conceal a distinction necessary to this scene? If not, do not manufacture one |
| T1: category recognition | Know whether the unfamiliar item is a person, garment, rite, place, or substance | Supply a verb, physical interaction, or brief apposition | Can the reader identify the kind of thing without its etymology? |
| T2: operational meaning | Predict permission, danger, cost, obligation, or consequence | Show a choice, correction, denial, or penalty involving the term | Can the reader explain why the next action succeeds or fails? |
| T3: social differentiation | Distinguish rival registers, generations, sects, or local interpretations | Reuse the term across contrasting speakers or contexts | Can the reader attribute each interpretation rather than assume a universal definition? |
| T4: historical/metalinguistic depth | Understand etymology, disputed translation, archival form, or a suppressed reading | Use a motivated discussion, document, or optional apparatus | Does this additional history change an interpretation without being required to follow the present action? |

- These tiers describe disclosure, not a ladder every term must climb. Background texture may remain T1; a decisive legal term may require T2 immediately.
- Select the tier from the **consequence dependency**: if the audience cannot understand a vote, oath, escape, or insult without a distinction, supply it before that consequence matters.
- Do not confuse productive uncertainty (“which faction is lying?”) with missing orientation (“is this word a person or a building?”).
- An initial working budget is one unfamiliar function per action beat; this is a revisable editing heuristic, not a memory-capacity finding.
- Budget functions, not typography: familiar-looking English titles can introduce more conceptual load than several already-established conlang words.
- Recurrence should retrieve an earlier affordance, then add a distinction. Repetition of an unexplained spelling is not instruction.

## 5. The context bundle: category → affordance → consequence → contrast

- **Category:** “the [garment-term]’s hem” identifies clothing without stopping for a dictionary entry.
- **Affordance:** lifting its split hem clear of a fire gives the object physical reality; avoid a garment that exists only as a rank label.
- **Consequence:** someone bars its wearer from a room or grants a privilege; now the social code affects a decision.
- **Contrast:** another wearer, setting, or era alters the reading; record whether the difference is law, convention, error, or deception.
- **History:** explain origin only when characters have a reason to dispute, teach, investigate, or exploit it; a short narrator explanation may be more economical than contrived dialogue.

| Coupled system | Contextual evidence to stage | What it does not establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Language/register | Who uses a title, repairs a form of address, or switches vocabulary | That the language forces a worldview or that every speaker agrees |
| Myth/ritual | A named power invoked while an action is licensed, forbidden, or contested | That the power exists, or that one invocation summarizes a whole pantheon |
| Symbol | Someone recognizes, misreads, counterfeits, or refuses a sign | A timeless, universally decoded symbol dictionary |
| Era | Old and new words or insignia produce different reactions | A universal progress sequence or an automatic date conversion |
| Costume/material | Cut, fastening, wear, repair, fiber, access, and bodily movement | Rank from color alone, or actual practice from a sumptuary prescription alone |

- Reveal a coupling by making channels interact: the badge seems legitimate but its wearer uses the wrong address; the old garment name survives while the garment’s permitted use changes.
- Do not require every cultural detail to become a punishment. Affection, skilled handling, humor, comfort, and routine cooperation can also make terms intelligible.

## 6. Name-first reading: establish reference before etymology

- **Definition:** let a stable name become a usable handle for a referent before asking the reader to master its linguistic or mythic history. This is not “surname first,” nor an instruction to withhold basic identity.
- First contact should normally pair the handle with an action or relation: “[public-name] counted the witnesses” is easier to anchor than an isolated genealogical inventory.
- Add category or role only as needed: “[office-title] [public-name]” may establish a person and an office together, provided the scene shows the office’s relevant authority.
- Store **reference form** separately from **address form**: a private name, office, kin-term, praise-name, or avoidance form may identify the same person without being interchangeable in dialogue.
- Introduce an alias through an explicit bridge before using it independently; reveal historical name changes at the moment their political difference becomes useful.
- Explain a meaningful name when its meaning matters to a pun, oath, insult, claim, or identification—not automatically on first appearance.

| Name class | Audience-facing policy to propose | Safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| Personal or lineage name | Retain a stable form; gloss only relevant meaning | Preserve name-slot order and address rights from the naming design; do not add hereditary surnames by default |
| Toponym/endonym/exonym | Choose viewpoint- and era-appropriate form; bridge competing forms when needed | An outsider form is not automatically neutral, and multiple local languages may supply different endonyms |
| Office or divine epithet | Translate, retain, or pair with a narrow functional gloss | An office is not necessarily a personal name; an epithet does not establish equivalence between deities |
| Ritual/avoidance name | Make the restriction legible before a breach drives the plot | Do not expose restricted real-world names merely to demonstrate fictional secrecy |
| Written or archaic name | Keep script, transcription, reading, and proposed meaning distinct | A spelling can be known when pronunciation or etymology is not; mark uncertain readings (to-verify) |

- Avoid making unfamiliarity depend on reader-hostile typography: use consistent romanization and any necessary pronunciation help; do not scatter decorative apostrophes or selectively italicize only one group as inherently foreign.
- Orthographic similarity is a revision flag when readers confuse referents, not grounds to erase a language’s actual contrasts. Test the delivered spelling and audio forms separately.

## 7. Era staging: version the rendering, not only the historical fact

| Stage in the proposed chronology | Language/naming seam | Myth/symbol seam | Costume/material seam | Disclosure device |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Earlier attested state | Old title, spelling, or place-name | An earlier association is documented for a particular group | Earlier cut, fastening, fiber access, or badge use | A dated item or voice; do not infer its meaning solely from the present |
| Transition | Competing names and address conventions | Official reinterpretation meets rival readings | New regulation and old wardrobes coexist | A correction, inspection, argument, or dual-labeled document |
| Later ordinary use | A term becomes routine, opaque, or socially marked | A sign loses, gains, or splits associations | Old office wear becomes ceremonial, ordinary, scarce, or stigmatized | Show a surviving practice before explaining its genealogy |
| Retrospective edition | Editor standardizes or translates older forms | Editor imposes a later account of origins | Caption uses a later garment category | Mark the editorial intervention; distinguish it from contemporary testimony |

- These are possible states, not an evolutionary sequence. Different regions, households, and institutions may occupy different rows simultaneously.
- Ledger minimum: **referent | form | language/script | speaker/region | valid period | presentation tier | first-use cue | rendering policy | evidence status**.
- Record separate dates for an event, its first surviving attestation, and the edition that reports it. They need not coincide.
- An era-name may need an immediate relational gloss (“before the reform”) before a calendar conversion becomes useful; do not imply an exact conversion until one is supported.
- Version a garment’s referent and its social reading separately: the same word can name changed construction, and the same construction can receive a new name.

## 8. Translation footnote discipline: purpose, voice, necessity

- Distinguish **designer provenance** (real sources supporting the method) from **audience translation notes** (a rendering choice) and **fictional editorial apparatus** (a character’s claims). Give them different labels.
- Before adding a note, answer: what problem does it solve, whose voice speaks, what evidence supports it, and can a reader skip it without losing a necessary causal link?
- The skip test is an editorial default for action-oriented narrative, not a ban on scholarly editions, comic footnotes, or intentionally puzzle-like apparatus.

| Information proposed for a note | Preferred location | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Essential category, danger, permission, or joke required now | Main narrative, dialogue, image, or mandatory interface | Required comprehension should not depend on an optional detour |
| Recurring practical definition | Brief first-use context; optional glossary for retrieval | A glossary supports memory but should not be the only introduction |
| Translation trade-off, pun, untransferred register contrast | Concise translator note at a useful anchor | Explain what the rendering loses; do not claim an exact equivalent |
| Competing historical reading or uncertain pronunciation | Attributed note or appendix with evidence status | Preserve uncertainty without laundering speculation into narrator fact |
| Extensive morphology, chronology, or source comparison | Appendix or other suitable supplementary apparatus | Let an interested audience choose depth without interrupting each encounter |
| Fictional editor’s prejudice or censorship | Clearly voiced fictional apparatus, with the necessary reading path designed deliberately | A fallible editor is a narrative device, not real scholarly provenance |
| Explanation that merely repeats the sentence | Remove | It consumes attention without adding a distinction |

- Recommended note skeleton: **speaker/edition → term → rendering decision → relevant loss or uncertainty → source or internal evidence pointer**. This is a craft template, not Genette’s taxonomy.
- Notes should have one dominant job. Separate a pronunciation guide from a long institutional history when either can stand alone.
- Anchor a note to the relevant occurrence; avoid repeated footnotes on every recurrence. Add a fresh note only for a changed sense, era, or rendering problem.
- Do not reveal a mystery’s answer in an ostensibly harmless gloss unless the work deliberately makes apparatus part of the reveal.
- A real-world source needs author, year, identifiable work/edition, and a locatable passage when a specific claim depends on it; an invented chronicle must be labeled fictional, not cited as historical evidence.
- If a source, reading, etymology, or exact passage cannot be verified, mark it `(to-verify)` beside that claim rather than hiding the uncertainty in a general disclaimer.
- Prefer a short functional paraphrase over “untranslatable.” If there is residual mismatch, specify which relationship, speech act, register, or material distinction remains unrendered.

## 9. Delivery-mode adjustments

- **Print/ebook:** test first-use context without end matter; ensure notes and glossary references are reachable in the actual edition, not only the authoring draft.
- **Audio:** a spelling distinction or subscript note cannot carry essential identity alone; use a spoken relationship cue and decide whether optional notes are performed or omitted.
- **Screen/interactive:** a tooltip is optional apparatus unless the interface requires it; do not put the only explanation of a consequential choice behind an undiscoverable hover.
- **Visual costume cues:** support necessary color/rank information with another available channel—shape, placement, action, text, or sound—without pretending every channel communicates identical detail.
- **Localization:** flag puns, transparent names, honorific contrasts, and script-dependent rebuses as dependencies; offer the function to preserve, not an instruction to reproduce an English solution literally.

## 10. Coverage card when a civilization supplies reference material

Fill this card for each bounded reference case; a few translated nouns are not a civilization profile. Do not fill missing cells by analogy with a neighbor.

| Required field | What the designer records | What the audience may encounter first |
|---|---|---|
| Language family and writing | Specific language/variety, period, documented family affiliation or uncertainty, script, transcription convention | A label being read, translated, copied, or misread; script alone does not identify language |
| Mythic/pantheon core | Attested powers, relations, ritual setting, genre, and variants for the selected community | A relevant invocation or dispute; not an omnibus list of gods |
| Symbolic system | Sign, carrier, interpreter, context, competing readings | A consequential recognition, a routine use, or a mistaken reading |
| Era model | Local periodization, outsider analytic label, date/conversion confidence, regional overlap | A date formula, old title, or object whose age matters |
| Costume/material signature | Fiber, construction, trade/production access, wearer, occasion, prescription versus observed use | Movement, repair, fastening, material handling, and social response |
| Naming practices | Name slots, order, address/reference distinction, life changes, taboo status, endonym/exonym | A motivated introduction, correction, avoidance, or renaming |

- This protocol offers no complete real-civilization survey; tradition-specific expertise is thin wherever this card lacks checked specialist evidence. Such cells stay `(to-verify)` and do not become design facts.
- A term can connect several cells, but a connection requires evidence: shared spelling is not shared descent, shared deity-function is not shared theology, and similar dress is not proof of contact.

## 11. Scratch demonstration: disclosure order, not a culture proposal

All bracketed labels below are variables, not coined vocabulary. The institutional relationships are disposable teaching scaffolding, not an adopted world, a historical claim, or a conlang derivation.

> “[office-title] [public-name],” the usher announced. The clerk stood and offered the witness list.
>
> At the council door the visitor lifted her [garment-term]’s split hem clear of the brazier. The clerk pointed to its collar. “Old knot. No vote since the reform.”
>
> Her grandmother had called it the hearth-keeper’s knot; the clerk called it disloyal.

- First beat: a stable public name and title become attached to a person and a relevant responsibility; no genealogy or literal name meaning is needed yet.
- Second beat: the garment is physically legible at T1; the voting restriction supplies T2; “since the reform” gives relative era orientation without a chronology lecture.
- Third beat: the sign receives attributed, conflicting readings at T3. Neither speaker establishes its origin, the truth of a myth, or the views of the whole society.
- Possible optional note, explicitly a template: **Translator [edition]: retain [garment-term] because “robe” hides the office-bearing collar distinction; the historical origin remains [evidence status].**
- If collar construction itself becomes decisive, put the necessary distinction in the narrative before the decision; this note is not a substitute.
- Rejected approach: opening with a full lexicon entry, founder myth, clothing law, and obsolete spelling before anyone has used the term.

## 12. Apply, test, and hand off without claiming canon

1. Select an authorized scene premise or an explicitly noncanonical exercise; identify what choice or interaction the audience must follow.
2. Draw only needed dependencies from naming, pragmatics, ritual, symbol, era, and costume sheets; retain their uncertainty and viewpoint tags.
3. Assign a rendering policy and present-use tier to each load-bearing term; do not add vocabulary merely to display a designed lexicon.
4. Draft the category/affordance/consequence sequence; preserve a natural reason for any dialogue explanation.
5. Bridge aliases and old forms; check who is entitled to use each address form and which era’s costume reading applies.
6. Move optional etymology and translation history into purposeful apparatus; keep the scene’s necessary inference available on its intended reading path.
7. Ask an unfamiliar reader to identify referents, describe the relevant permission or risk, and attribute competing beliefs. Record their actual answers; unrun reader checks are not passed checks.
8. Remove the glossary and optional notes, then repeat the causal-summary check for the default narrative path; if comprehension breaks, restore context or explicitly redesign the intended reading path.
9. Try the alternate delivered mode: read aloud, inspect grayscale, or traverse the real note/tooltip path as appropriate; record unresolved dependencies rather than claiming cross-media equivalence.
10. Hand lore-shokunin the exposure ledger, rendering choices, source/evidence tags, competing options, and observed failures. Only lore can adopt world facts and validate bible consistency.

- Completion means the proposal’s required inference is available, aliases remain traceable, era/viewpoint distinctions survive, and apparatus has an identifiable purpose. No comprehension threshold or reader-test success is claimed by this reference file itself.

## 13. What a worldbuilder can take

- A two-layer presentation design: **action-level intelligibility** plus **optional contextual depth**, with explicit exceptions for works whose apparatus is part of the intended reading.
- A reusable exposure ledger that keeps names, mythic interpretations, symbols, era shifts, and dress practices coupled without introducing every system at once.
- A revision rule: add the smallest clarification that repairs the next necessary inference, while preserving disagreement and the possibility of later reinterpretation.

## 14. Provenance cautions

- Chronicle evidence for everyday address, pronunciation, informal worship, and ordinary dress may be scarce or elite-filtered. Record the gap; do not invent an ancient practice to make a scene feel complete.
- A chronicle’s composition date, narrated period, surviving copy, and modern translation are different evidence layers; prescriptions about names or garments do not establish universal compliance.
- If a term is known mainly through missionary, colonial, court, or antiquarian mediation, identify that mediation and its agenda. Do not treat a collector’s gloss as a community’s exhaustive self-description, or automatically dismiss every mediated record as worthless.
- Restricted sacred language and living naming practices require tradition-specific checking; fictionalizing the spelling does not itself settle permission, attribution, or harm. This lane does not adjudicate those rights.
- Do not replace a missing specialist account with universal “primitive mentality,” fixed color psychology, or an archetype dictionary. A character may believe a totalizing explanation; the reference entry must not endorse it as established scholarship.
- This entry’s strongest evidence is bibliographic and conceptual, not a tested prescription for every audience or a history of any named civilization.
