## 1. What this entry licenses

- A reference discipline for this lane, not a universal research manual, a civilization ranking, or permission to decide what is true in a fictional world.
- Default preference: **primary scholarship > specialist handbooks > popular mythography**. “Primary scholarship” means original analysis, field reports, and critical editions with inspectable evidence; “primary source” means a witness being studied. They are not synonyms.
- A royal hymn is primary evidence that a court circulated a claim, not independent proof of the king's divine ancestry. A medieval compiler is a primary witness to medieval reception, not automatically to an earlier oral performance.
- Source quality and borrowing permission are separate questions. Public availability does not make restricted ceremonies, sacred names, or community-held designs freely reusable; this lane does not issue rights or attribution rulings.

| Preference | Source class | Good for | Mandatory calibration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Critical text edition, excavated assemblage report, linguistic documentation, original specialist study | Establishing an attested form, dated association, or explicitly argued inference | Record object/text identifier, context, apparatus, method, and uncertainty; an original argument can still be wrong |
| 2 | Specialist synthesis or review that confronts competing interpretations | Assessing reception, alternatives, chronology, and changed readings | Distinguish the reviewer's synthesis from the underlying observations; newer is not automatically better |
| 3 | Scholarly handbook, grammar, dictionary, or museum catalogue | Orientation, terminology, comparative lookup, finding the original evidence | Trace load-bearing claims back to entries, references, and objects; a catalogue's interpretation is not the object itself |
| 4 | Classic theoretical work | What a named theorist proposed; designer vocabulary | Cite as theory or intellectual history, not as independent documentation of every tradition it mentions |
| 5 | Popular retelling, symbol dictionary, screenwriting manual, unsourced online summary | Reception history, plot prompts, search leads | Never sole support for antiquity, universality, ethnic identity, etymology, or ritual meaning |

- This is a preference order, not automatic truth by prestige: a corrected handbook can supersede an old excavation interpretation; a speculative original paper does not outrank converging contrary evidence.
- Oral testimony is not demoted for lacking writing. Record narrator/community, language, occasion, audience, collection date, transmission context, and access conditions; corroboration requirements apply to written testimony too.
- A local corpus entry is a navigation aid, not a new independent witness. Ten retellings dependent on one colonial collection remain one transmission line.
- Pair framework originals with critical reception: Csapo (2005) and Lincoln (1999) supply critiques, not an infallible replacement master theory; Wylie (2002) models accountable inference rather than certainty from objects.

## 2. Claim record: separate attestation, interpretation, and design

Use one record per consequential coupling, especially “a word proves a belief,” “a garment encodes rank,” and “a myth dates an era.” Confidence belongs to the claim at a specified scale, not to an author's entire reputation.

| Field | Required content | Reject this shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Proposition | Exact, bounded statement; community, place, period, and social register | “The Maya believed…” without city, corpus, or period |
| Witness | Tablet/manuscript/object/recording identifier; original language; condition and find/collection context | Image repost with no collection or excavation data |
| Chronology | Claimed event, composition, surviving copy, collection, and edition dates kept distinct | Dating an orally transmitted myth by the date of its modern translation |
| Edition and locator | Author/editor + year + edition; page, entry, text line, or catalogue number | Bibliography at the file head treated as a locator for every claim |
| Mediation | Translator, collector, commissioning institution, omissions, normalized names | Colonial spelling or missionary category presented as transparent native terminology |
| Inference | Observation → proposed relationship → alternatives → possible counterevidence | A symbol looks similar, therefore the peoples shared a cosmology |
| Independence | Which witnesses actually have separate transmission histories | Two handbooks citing the same passage counted as confirmation |
| Status and boundary | Registry status below; attested fact, scholarly inference, emic report, or designer proposal | Scholarly uncertainty silently converted to fictional world-fact |
| Recheck | Concrete trigger: new reading, revised dating, replication, or relevant specialist/community correction | Annual freshness badge with no issue to investigate |

- Normalize transliteration only for presentation; retain the source spelling and diacritics in the record. An English gloss is neither a reconstructed pronunciation nor a native name.
- Test a coupling in both directions: a dye may constrain display cost, but a color's prestige does not prove that dye; a theophoric name documents invocation, not every bearer’s personal theology.
- Evidential confidence is not canon authority. Even a securely attested real practice remains a reference option; lore-shokunin decides whether any fictional analogue exists.
- Do not promote `HARD/SOFT/DOCTRINE/DESIGN` from `material-culture-inference-method.md` into automatic canon permissions. A supported inference about an institution is not the same as that institution's own doctrine.

## 3. Contested-framework registry

Statuses are this corpus's operational handling rules, not claims of a unanimous scholarly vote: **S** supported within stated scope; **C** actively contested; **H** heuristic/generator; **R** rejected as stated or unsupported overclaim; **U** evidence does not presently decide. **V** = `(to-verify)` before factual reuse. Multiple statuses distinguish a useful tool from its excessive empirical claim.

| Framework or recurrent claim | Calibration status and reason | Allowed craft use / required evidence | Source anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frazer: magic → religion → science as humanity's necessary stages | **R** as a universal developmental ladder; evolutionist and colonial categories distort the comparison | Invent a faction that advocates the ladder, not a narrator who treats it as history; recheck each ethnographic anecdote separately | Frazer 1922; Csapo 2005; Trigger 2006 |
| Universal vegetation king / dying-and-rising god explanation | **R** as an all-purpose explanation; **C** for particular comparisons whose death, return, and ritual evidence differ | Specify the actual text, rite, season, and variant; similar narrative events do not establish one cult mechanism | Csapo 2005; Lincoln 1999 |
| Jung's inherited archetypes / collective unconscious | **H** for interpretive or psychological imagery; **R** as demonstrated proof of a shared cross-cultural myth mechanism | Build a dream vocabulary; do not explain a real tradition's symbolism by an untested psychic inheritance | Jung 1969; Csapo 2005 |
| Campbell's monomyth, including derivative heroine/hero templates | **H** as a selected narrative grammar; **R** as a compulsory or demonstrated universal life-cycle | Choose, omit, reverse, or combine stages; neither protagonist gender nor cultural ancestry mandates an arc | J. Campbell 1949; Csapo 2005 |
| Eliade: sacred/profane, eternal return, “archaic” ontology | **H/C**: influential comparative vocabulary; generalized timeless mentality is not an established description of premodern peoples | Generate institutions and ritual calendars, then document local histories and exceptions; no universal “primitive mind” | Eliade 1959; Csapo 2005; Lincoln 1999 |
| Lévi-Strauss: mythemes, binaries, transformations | **H/C**: influential analytic program; segmentation and selection of contrasts can impose the analyst's categories | Publish alternative segmentations and an awkward variant; a neat binary is not proof of what tellers consciously believed | Lévi-Strauss 1955; Csapo 2005 |
| Propp's function sequence and spheres of action | **S** as a historically situated analysis of a delimited Russian wondertale sample; **H** outside it | Generate a tale grammar; test its fit on the selected genre, not every myth, epic, or performance | Propp 1968; Csapo 2005 |
| Dumézil's Indo-European tripartite ideology | **C**: comparative historical hypothesis, not a demonstrated social constitution of all Indo-European speakers | Offer sovereign/warrior/producer contrasts as candidates; require philology, dated variants, and counterexamples; linguistic kinship does not prove three estates | Lincoln 1999 |
| Bascom's myth / legend / folktale distinctions | **H**, a comparative genre model with local limits; **R** if translated into automatic canon truth tiers | Ask what tellers treat as sacred, credible, entertaining, or disputed; sacred narrative need not be narrator-certified world-fact | Bascom 1965 |
| Thompson motifs and ATU tale types | **S** as documented indexing systems; **H** as comparative retrieval; **R** as proof of common origin | Record a candidate identifier only after checking the entry and variant; coverage and category formation are uneven, not a census of humanity | Thompson 1955–1958; Uther 2004 |
| Strong linguistic determinism: no word → no concept | **R**; language is not a prison that prevents paraphrase or learning | Make an ideology or taboo affect speaking practice without declaring non-speakers incapable of the thought | Lucy 1992 |
| Linguistic relativity as domain-specific habit or task effect | **C/S** only for particular findings and methods; no blanket magnitude follows | State the domain, task, training, and alternative causal direction; grammar alone does not establish a society's religion | Lucy 1992 |
| Berlin–Kay color-term regularities | **S/C**: influential empirical proposal with disputed sampling, categorization, and evolutionary interpretation | Distinguish lexical category, perceptual task, pigment, and symbolic use; no lexical sequence yields a ritual color dictionary | Berlin & Kay 1969; Saunders & van Brakel 1997 |
| Comparative reconstruction from recurrent correspondences | **S** as a disciplined linguistic method; reconstructions remain hypotheses at explicit levels of precision | Derive daughter forms and name strata; distinguish inherited forms, loans, and folk etymology; an asterisk marks reconstruction, not attestation | L. Campbell 2013 |
| Mass lexical resemblance / deep macrofamily certainty | **R** when resemblance alone is offered as genealogical proof; individual remote-relationship proposals need separate evaluation | Similar deity names are a lead, not a language family; contact and chance must be tested before myth inheritance | L. Campbell 2013 |
| Swadesh-style constant-rate glottochronology | **R** as a universal lexical clock; model-based dating is a separate, assumption-sensitive question | Keep relative ordering, calibration anchors, ranges, and model assumptions; never calculate a culture's age from a fixed retention rate | L. Campbell 2013 |
| Stone → Bronze → Iron as universal social progress | **S** for demonstrated regional archaeological sequences; **R** as one global ladder or rank of intelligence | Stage techniques independently by region and domain; institutions, myths, scripts, and garments need their own histories | Trigger 2006 |
| “Pots = people”; garment, script, language, and ancestry identify one bounded population | **R** as a one-to-one equation; specific historical correlations can be argued | Model trade, bilingualism, imitation, migration, reuse, and political labeling; triangulate rather than infer ethnicity from a silhouette | Wylie 2002; Trigger 2006; L. Campbell 2013 |
| Universal symbol dictionaries: serpent = evil, white = purity | **R** as culture-free semantics; **H** as a prompt inventory | Locate the sign in a named genre, medium, period, wearer, and audience; contradictory meanings can coexist | Black & Green 1992; Wylie 2002 |
| “Traditional costume” as unchanged national essence | **R** as default timelessness; **H** for analyzing deliberate codification and claims of antiquity | Date fiber, cut, trade, law, iconography, and heritage promotion separately; “invented tradition” does not mean fabricated from nothing | Barber 1991; Hobsbawm & Ranger 1983 |
| Figurines or cave images prove one mother-goddess religion or universal shamanism | **R** as inference from form alone; specific ritual interpretations remain **C/U** | Build several explanations with different material predictions; do not convert an evocative pose into recovered theology or political matriarchy | Wylie 2002; Trigger 2006 |
| Indus signs have a securely identified language, deity vocabulary, or personal-name list | **U**; proposed readings are not an accepted decipherment | Borrow production and exchange constraints; leave language family, divine names, and naming morphology unresolved | Kenoyer 1998; any claimed new decipherment **V** `(to-verify)` |

- “Contested,” “hard to test,” “not established,” and “discredited” are not synonyms. Reject the exact overclaim, not every observation made by its author or an entire interpretive school.
- Do not inherit blanket verdicts such as “three frameworks are discredited” from `comparative-frameworks.md`; use the claim-specific distinctions above. A generator's usefulness neither proves its theory nor immunizes it from criticism.
- Do not inherit causal recipes such as “delta state → combat cosmogony” or “pastoralism → one hero pattern” as empirical laws from `hero-journey-variants.md`; they are optional design associations, not ethnographic predictions.
- A political biography can expose selection risks, but is not by itself a refutation; evaluate the actual argument, missing evidence, and contrary cases. Lincoln's own political readings also require scrutiny.

## 4. Era staging: keep the clocks separate

| Clock | Evidence to seek | What it can date | What it cannot date alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linguistic | Stratified attestations, sound correspondences, loan adaptation | Relative sequence of language forms and contacts | The first performance of a myth using a later recorded name |
| Textual | Manuscript hands, copies, redactions, quotation, colophons | A surviving witness and sometimes a bounded composition history | An unchanged oral original centuries earlier |
| Ritual and mythic | Local calendars, named performers, recurring records, disputed variants | Documented performance and claims about primordial time | The literal age of the cosmos or continuous practice since an origin story |
| Material and costume | Fibers, weave, dyes, seams, wear, repairs, deposition, dated images | Production/use/deposition ranges with appropriate tests | The precise religion, language, or gender identity of every wearer |
| Political | Accession records, dated decrees, fiscal years, era names | Administrative dating and official period claims | A universal rise–peak–decline cycle |
| Modern reception | Collection, museum restoration, national codification, stage costume | The history of a reconstructed or promoted tradition | Proof that a modern ensemble existed intact in antiquity |

- Stage a fictional option with independent language, textual, ritual, political, and material phases. Couple them through a named event or institution, not a shared “Bronze Age” label.
- A conservative liturgical formula can coexist with an innovative garment; archaizing dress can be newly manufactured. Continuity requires a transmission account, not matching visual age.
- Date bounds depend on the object and test: a dated deposit is not automatically a garment's manufacture date, and inherited objects can predate a burial. A depiction is evidence of a depiction before it is evidence of everyday wear.
- Treat local era names, world-age doctrines, and modern archaeological periods as different types of classification (`era-periodization-models.md`); do not make a culture speak in the museum's period labels by default.

## 5. Civilization calibration cards: compare bounded corpora, not essences

These orientation cards exercise all six coupled fields. They are not complete reconstructions or interchangeably “ancient” kits; extraction of a specific name, garment, or emblem still needs the claim record and an item-level locator.

**Southern Mesopotamia: Ur III and Old Babylonian contexts, roughly 2100–1600 BCE**
- Language family + writing: Sumerian is a language isolate; Akkadian is East Semitic within Afroasiatic. Both use cuneiform; shared script and scribal education do not make them one language (Black et al. 2004; L. Campbell 2013).
- Mythic/pantheon core: city-linked deities including Inana, Enlil, Enki, and Nanna; Sumerian compositions have distinct transmission histories, not one recoverable original “Mesopotamian Bible” (Black et al. 2004).
- Symbolic system: divine attributes and inscribed/iconographic contexts help identify figures; a horned crown or star must be read with its object and period, not from a universal chart (Black & Green 1992).
- Era + costume/material signature: modern dynastic periods differ from indigenous year-name dating; wool and linen, robes and adornments belong to particular records and representations. ETCSL 1.4.1, lines 14–25, names Inana's regalia, not an average woman's wardrobe.
- Naming practices: theophoric and sentence-like names can invoke deities; keep Sumerian/Akkadian forms, divine determinatives in writing, and English glosses distinct. Exact segmentation of an imported name is **V** `(to-verify)` without a lexical/name reference.
- **What a worldbuilder can take:** the coupling of scribal register, city patronage, named regalia, and institutional memory. **Provenance cautions:** scribal copies need dating apart from narrated events; Sumerian Inana's descent is not interchangeable with every Akkadian Ištar version.

**Egypt: New Kingdom, roughly 1550–1070 BCE**
- Language family + writing: Egyptian is a branch of Afroasiatic; hieroglyphic and hieratic practices coexist. Middle Egyptian remains prestigious while Late Egyptian is increasingly attested; script/register is not a clean chronological replacement (Allen 2014; Lichtheim 1976).
- Mythic/pantheon core: Amun-Re, Osiris, Isis, and Horus occur in changing cultic and royal settings; Amarna-period changes warn against treating the whole period as one doctrinal snapshot (Lichtheim 1976; Robins 1997).
- Symbolic system: royal titulary, crowns, solar/funerary imagery, and signs of order are contextual visual rhetoric; an image's compositional role is not a literal dress instruction (Robins 1997).
- Era + costume/material signature: regnal dating is not the modern “New Kingdom” label; linen garments, pleating, wigs, and jewelry vary with status, occasion, and depiction. Elite tomb art overrepresents curated display (Robins 1997).
- Naming practices: distinguish personal names, deity-bearing name elements, epithets, and royal titulary; the royal multi-name system is not a naming rule for the entire population (Allen 2014).
- **What a worldbuilder can take:** register conservatism coupled to royal titles, ritual display, and political reform. **Provenance cautions:** funerary/elite preservation biases everyday dress and belief; no timeless “Egyptian color code” follows from one tomb.

**China: late Shang/Anyang, roughly 1250–1046 BCE**
- Language family + writing: early Chinese belongs to Sinitic within Sino-Tibetan; oracle-bone writing records language, not pure ideas. Proposed Old Chinese sound values are reconstructions, not recordings of Shang speech (Keightley 1978; Baxter & Sagart 2014).
- Mythic/pantheon core: royal ancestors, Di, and powers addressed in divination; the archive is especially strong on royal ritual, not a complete set of ordinary people's origin narratives (Keightley 1978).
- Symbolic system: pyromantic signs, inscribed bones, ritual bronzes, and animal-derived designs; calling a bronze motif *taotie* does not secure its original Shang name or theological meaning (Bagley in Loewe & Shaughnessy 1999).
- Era + costume/material signature: sexagenary day dating and royal ancestral sequences differ from modern dynastic chronology; silk traces, jade ornaments, and bronze production support material distinctions better than a complete reconstructed court outfit (Loewe & Shaughnessy 1999).
- Naming practices: royal ancestral day-names and kin titles are context-specific; do not project modern surname–given-name conventions or later emperor-name systems unchanged into this archive (Keightley 1978).
- **What a worldbuilder can take:** divination bureaucracy coupled to calendrical names, ancestors, and costly ritual materials. **Provenance cautions:** later transmitted chronicles are not contemporary transcripts; deity relationships, motif meanings, and exact pronunciations remain separately debated.

**Maya: Classic-period lowland courts, roughly 250–900 CE**
- Language family + writing: Mayan languages, with much Classic inscriptional language identified as Ch'olan-related; logosyllabic writing combines word signs and syllabic signs. “Maya” is not one language or one polity (Coe & Van Stone 2005).
- Mythic/pantheon core: maize, rain, ancestry, and dynastic divine relations appear in courtly texts and images; the later K'iche' *Popol Vuh* is a distinct witness, not a transcript of Classic lowland doctrine (Sharer & Traxler 2006; Tedlock 1996).
- Symbolic system: calendrical glyphs, divine/courtly imagery, and royal emblems work through local contexts; modern labels and revised sign readings must not be treated as stable ancient glosses (Coe & Van Stone 2005).
- Era + costume/material signature: modern “Classic” periodization differs from Long Count and interlocking calendrical counts; woven cotton, jade, shell, and feather regalia have unequal survival and disproportionately elite representation (Sharer & Traxler 2006).
- Naming practices: recorded royal names and titles can combine divine and other elements; separate a person's name, accession context, and political title. Check current readings before importing a familiar translated king-name (Coe & Van Stone 2005).
- **What a worldbuilder can take:** dated royal display coupling glyphic names, calendars, genealogies, and regalia. **Provenance cautions:** post-conquest K'iche' and Yucatec records cannot be flattened into one pan-Maya text; living Maya peoples are not remnants without histories of their own.

**Norse: Viking-Age evidence versus later Icelandic textual witnesses**
- Language family + writing: Old Norse is North Germanic within Indo-European; Viking-Age runic inscriptions and later Roman-alphabet manuscripts are different documentary settings, not evidence of a wholly unwritten culture (Jesch 1991; Larrington 2014).
- Mythic/pantheon core: Óðinn, Þórr, Freyr, and other powers appear across different poetic and material witnesses; the Eddic corpus is not a contemporary census of Viking-Age worship (Larrington 2014).
- Symbolic system: hammer pendants and other marked objects can support particular interpretations, but an uninscribed motif's meaning is not guaranteed by an Eddic passage from another region or century (Jesch 1991).
- Era + costume/material signature: modern “Viking Age” spans approximately the late eighth to eleventh centuries; wool, linen, metal fasteners, and grave assemblages vary regionally. Burial costume is selected by the living, not a universal daily uniform (Jesch 1991).
- Naming practices: compound names, divine-name elements, bynames, and patronymic relationships require period and regional checks; later saga usage is not sufficient evidence for every Viking-Age naming rule (Jesch 1991).
- **What a worldbuilder can take:** separate inscriptional names, praise registers, material display, and later literary memory. **Provenance cautions:** medieval Christian manuscript mediation matters; archaeological association is not a license to backdate every poetic narrative or costume detail.

**Indus: Mature Harappan archaeological contexts, roughly 2600–1900 BCE**
- Language family + writing: language affiliation is unresolved; seal and other sign sequences remain undeciphered. Their precise relation to language is debated; shared urban material culture does not settle a Dravidian or Indo-Aryan identification (Kenoyer 1998).
- Mythic/pantheon core: no securely translated indigenous narrative corpus supplies a recoverable pantheon. “Proto-Śiva,” “mother goddess,” and similar identifications are interpretations, not attested indigenous divine names (Kenoyer 1998).
- Symbolic system: animal motifs, seals, geometric signs, and patterned objects are observable; the meanings of the so-called unicorn and many other motifs remain **U**, not a blank to fill with later theology.
- Era + costume/material signature: Early/Mature/Late Harappan are archaeological classifications; cotton evidence, bead production, shell ornaments, and draped representations constrain some practices but do not establish a timeless modern national costume (Kenoyer 1998).
- Naming practices: personal names and naming morphology cannot currently be read securely from the signs; use no invented “authentic Harappan” name list. A proposed decipherment or named ruler is **V** `(to-verify)` against current specialist evaluation.
- **What a worldbuilder can take:** craft specialization, exchange, ornament production, and the productive uncertainty of a partial archive. **Provenance cautions:** this entry's linguistic/theological knowledge is deliberately thin; readable chronicle evidence is unavailable, so those fields must remain unknown rather than reconstructed by analogy alone.

## 6. Apply the discipline to a fictional culture

1. Specify a bounded reference question: a naming stratum, an accession garment, a festival word, or the social use of an emblem. Do not start with “combine civilization A's language and civilization B's gods.”
2. Fill the claim record for each borrowed relationship; select a primary scholarly anchor plus a critical synthesis where interpretation is contested. Keep a transmission tree so derivative sources do not inflate confidence.
3. Extract a mechanism, not an essence: who commissions the text, teaches the name, pays for the dye, licenses the robe, and disputes the festival's account? Material availability constrains options without dictating theology.
4. Choose language, mythic, symbolic, political, and dress phases separately. Make every intended cross-phase relationship explicit: inherited title, later translation, revived garment, borrowed emblem, or newly standardized festival.
5. Label each output **attested reference / scholarly interpretation / designer proposal / candidate emic belief**. A deity-bearing name does not establish that deity's actual existence in the fictional world.
6. Keep a contrary variant or alternative mechanism; ask which evidence would discriminate between them. If no evidence could, label the choice as a generative preference, not a historical finding.
7. Hand lore-shokunin the options, dependency map, source locators, statuses, and open questions. Leave acceptance, in-world truth, continuity rulings, and world-bible writes to that lane.

**Worked coupling audit: regalia and threshold speech, not a new fictional canon**
- Anchor: ETCSL 1.4.1, lines 14–25 lists regalia and named adornments; lines 129–163 narrate removal at gates and a repeated reply. The translation explicitly marks a manuscript variant at the first gate.
- Supported reference claim: this edited Sumerian composition couples garments/adornments, divine power, gate passage, and repeated speech. Cite those lines, not “universal initiation symbolism.”
- Textual caution: a composite translation combines witnesses and marks variants; it is not one intact ancient performance transcript. Translation glosses such as “garment of ladyship” do not supply a verified sewing pattern.
- Unsupported leap: the episode proves a historical seven-garment stripping ritual, a universal seven-stage initiation, or a Jungian meaning for each jewel. Each would need separate evidence; ritual-performance reconstruction is **V** `(to-verify)`.
- Designer proposal: test a system in which the removal of licensed insignia changes allowable address forms at thresholds; use an independently designed language and plausible materials. This is a proposal derived from a relation, not an attested Sumerian custom.
- Era and naming test: distinguish a garment's ordinary name, its ceremonial epithet, and a later lexicalized office-title; decide whether speech preserves archaic forms while textile production changes. These are design decisions, not deductions from the Sumerian lines.
- Deliverable: competing coupling diagrams with source/inference/design labels and an open historical-ritual question, never an approved rite, named polity, or canon event.

## 7. Provenance cautions, evidence gaps, and recheck triggers

- Colonial mediation: retain Indigenous names and narrator/translator contributions; inspect how “god,” “devil,” “idol,” “chief,” and “costume” translate local categories. Bias requires analysis, not automatic rejection or automatic trust in the opposite story.
- Chronicle scarcity: absence of a readable chronicle does not mean absence of sophisticated thought, long history, or internal disagreement. Do not substitute modern neighboring traditions as unchanged prehistoric testimony.
- Survival bias: stone and metal, courts and tombs, and collectible elite garments survive differently from workwear, speech, and perishable ritual apparatus. Negative claims must specify what could reasonably have survived or been recorded.
- Thin coverage: this entry is not grounded in community-specific review of Haudenosaunee, Aboriginal Australian, or Pacific ceremonial knowledge. Do not export the registry's labels into those traditions as substantive descriptions; acquire appropriate local scholarship and access guidance first `(to-verify)`.
- Living traditions: community scholarship and authorized testimony can expose archive errors; neither “Indigenous” nor “academic” is a single unanimous voice. Record named positions and disagreements rather than declaring one authentic version.
- Cross-corpus caution: Bascom's article is 78(307), not 78(308); source identifiers were checked against Crossref for [Bascom](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.2307/538099) and [Lévi-Strauss](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.2307/536768). Existing seeded notes are not silently corrected by this entry.
- Verification scope, 2026-10-17: article metadata, selected catalogue identities, the [Wylie publisher overview](https://www.ucpress.edu/books/thinking-from-things/paper), ETCSL passages above, and a [critical review of Lincoln](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2000/2000.07.29/) were checked. Catalogue records verify bibliographic identity, not every claim or edition detail; the listed standard books were not all consulted in full for this entry.
- Before a load-bearing extraction, complete its page/object locator and check the exact edition. Exact garment reconstructions, name etymologies, performance histories, and any asserted recent decipherment remain `(to-verify)` where no locator has been supplied.
- Recheck on a revised sign reading, newly dated witness, disputed translation, changed provenance, or relevant specialist/community correction. Revise the particular claim's status and explain the change; never upgrade an entire tradition from one new discovery.
- **What a worldbuilder can take:** accountable mechanisms linking words, institutions, myths, signs, time, and things; deliberate plurality and gaps are usable design resources. **Final provenance caution:** a compelling synthesis is still an authored hypothesis or proposal, and lore-shokunin alone owns its possible conversion into canon.
