## 2. Model the relation, not a costume dictionary

The arrows mean **compatibility checks**, not equivalence: one garment can have multiple terms and wearers; one office can require different ensembles by occasion. Never reverse “office-holders wear this” into “everyone wearing this holds office” (RE92; SA 38.2 supplies a useful exception).

| Required field | Record explicitly | Reject or split when |
|---|---|---|
| Garment | Body location, cut/wrapping, layer, attachment versus complete ensemble | A badge is silently treated as the whole robe |
| Material | Fiber; weave/knit/felt; dye or finish; metal/trim; access route | “Silk” and “satin” are treated as rival fibers; a depicted color proves its dye |
| Term | Language, script, citation form, sense, speaker/register, source date | A modern curatorial label becomes an attested historical native word |
| Who may wear it | Age/life-stage, office/status, community, occasion, authority, exceptions | Wealth, possession, or visual similarity is mistaken for entitlement |
| Rite/context | Participant role, donning/removal, transition, utterance if attested; or ordinary use | Every formal outfit is assigned an invented sacred ceremony |
| Era | Place and valid range; separately date object, term, rule, and interpretation | A dynasty label substitutes for a specific rule’s effective date |
| Myth/symbol | Observable feature; locally attributed interpretation; rival reading | Color or animal symbolism is imported from a universal dictionary |
| Evidence | Source ID, passage/accession, D/O/I/X, uncertainty, next check | A bibliography is present but no source supports the contested cell |

**Three frameworks, kept distinct:** RE92’s symbolic-interaction approach asks how wearers and observers negotiate identity; VG60 asks whether dressing marks a social transition; the permission predicate below is this entry’s designer tool, not an alleged native theory.

`eligible = era match AND ((ordinary wearer rule AND occasion match) OR an applicable documented exception)`

Record the result as **permitted / forbidden / required / customary / unknown**, with its basis. Historical evidence often only supports “customary” or “reported requirement,” not a judicially enforceable predicate.

## 3. Bounded historical context cards

These are comparative slices, not complete civilization surveys. Language family, script, pantheon, and garment cut do not predict one another; each needs its own evidence.

**Rome: late Republic and Augustan civic settings**
- Language + writing: Latin is Italic within Indo-European; the Latin alphabet writes these terms. Detailed period spelling and pronunciation are not reconstructed here; use DB96 and ../linguistics/writing-systems.md, not modern English readings.
- Myth/pantheon core: civic cults and household observances, not one canonical mythology; the relevant local link is Liber/Bacchus and the Liberalia in OF. Ovid offers alternative explanations rather than one established origin.
- Symbolic system: toga contrasts can index age and civic status; a purple border has different implications on a boy and a magistrate. A purple stripe on a tunic is not the toga’s border (PS75; SA).
- Era model: “late Republic/Augustan” is our historical grouping, not a native costume-era name. For in-world dating analogies distinguish consular-year practice from modern BCE/CE labels; see ../culture-material/era-periodization-models.md.
- Costume/material signature for this slice: draped woolen outer garment over a tunic; natural-white wool, border, and tunic stripe must be kept as separate features (PS75). Do not dress the entire imperial population in togas.
- Naming practices: distinguish personal-name slots from offices and garment epithets. Praenomen/nomen/cognomen is a useful citizen-male pattern, not a universal Roman naming law; women, enslaved people, freed people, and chronological change require their own records (S94; detailed distributions (to-verify)).

**Qing China: court/official insignia, late seventeenth–early eighteenth century**
- Language + writing: Chinese varieties are Sinitic within Sino-Tibetan; Literary Chinese uses morphosyllabic characters. Manchu is Tungusic and uses a vertical alphabet adapted through Mongolian; do not turn “Chinese empire” into “one language, one script” (N88; DB96; E02).
- Myth/pantheon core: court legitimacy and rites involve Heaven and ancestors alongside Manchu shamanism and Tibetan Buddhism; this is not a single unified pantheon or every subject’s religion (R98, chapters 6–8).
- Symbolic system: an official badge’s animal can classify rank; a crane image elsewhere need not classify its owner. The first-civil-rank identification in M1’s search excerpt is a curatorial interpretation; regulation text and exceptions remain (to-verify).
- Era model: separate dynasty, reign, and object attribution. M1 is catalogued to Kangxi, 1662–1722; M2 only to late seventeenth–early eighteenth century. Neither attribution dates a particular wearer’s appointment.
- Costume/material signature for this slice: applied textile insignia made by different techniques. M1 is silk/metallic-thread tapestry (kesi); M2 is silk/metallic-thread embroidery on silk satin. Neither object alone reconstructs the complete surcoat.
- Naming practices: distinguish an emperor’s personal, reign, temple, and posthumous names (R98, appendix 1); “Kangxi” here is a reign designation, not a personal forename. Han and Manchu personal-name conventions must not be flattened together; detailed non-imperial naming is thin here (to-verify: E02 and period personal records).

- **R1 ↔ R2:** identical term plus similar material does not produce a unique social identity; life-stage and office disambiguate.
- **R2 ↔ R4:** border and stripe are separate garment features. Translating both as “purple robe” destroys the social distinction.
- **R3:** mythic explanation and lexical play can accompany a rite without proving its historical origin; retain Ovid’s alternatives as alternatives.
- **R4:** a garment may signal an authorized relationship to an office rather than possession of that office; this is a built-in impostor-test counterexample.
- **Q1 ↔ Q2:** fiber, technique, and support cloth are independent variables. A stable visual category can be manufactured by different workshops/processes.
- **All rows:** ceremonial compatibility is not everyday prevalence. The absence of ordinary people from a court archive is not evidence of an absent wardrobe.

## 5. Decision table for fictional design

| When the design contains… | Ask this coupling question | Keep | Revise or hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| A reserved color | Is the privilege tied to hue, dyestuff, technique, price, or an issued object? | A specified local distinction and authority | “Purple = royalty everywhere”; no assay or entitlement evidence |
| Several dress names | Different referents, speakers, languages, or times? | Shop, court, ritual, and outsider terms with mapped senses | A synonym pile with no distribution |
| A sacred garment name | Is the lexical root material, action, office, place, or a later interpretation? | Attested derivation, or clearly labeled invented derivation | Back-solving ancient etymology from modern spelling |
| A new rite | Does dress change before, during, or after the participant’s status changes? | An explicit transition or a documented ordinary context | A vesting prayer fabricated to make the table look complete |
| A badge or emblem | Who grants, issues, copies, removes, and recognizes it? | A permission rule plus observed departures | “Anyone who can buy it is entitled to wear it” |
| A linguistic borrowing | Did garment, material, technique, word, and prestige travel together? | Separate adoption dates and donor/recipient registers | Automatic whole-culture diffusion from one loanword |
| An era change | Which relation actually changes, and which stays stable? | A dated delta with lag, reuse, and exceptions | Entire wardrobe/lexicon changing simultaneously without cause |
| A mythic reading | Who says the feature means this, and when? | Named institutional or popular interpretations | A designer’s symbolism presented as everyone’s ancient belief |

## 6. Filled exercise: one mechanism, not a canon culture

**X — disposable test fixture only.** Assume a temperate river settlement grows flax, maintains three named water channels, and imports blue wool trim. A proposed keeper institution uses a three-mark shell token; no real tradition is being described. Uppercase words below are semantic placeholders, not a finished conlang or sacred formula.

| ID / garment | Material ↔ mark | Term ↔ register | Who may wear it | Rite ↔ symbolic claim | Era |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| X1 / plain wrap | Coarse flax linen; no institutional token | WRAP+LEARNER in everyday speech | Enrolled adult novices of any gender; enrollment required, not wealth | Admission: receives work clothing but no keeper authority | E1 and E2, X |
| X2 / keeper wrap | Linen; imported blue wool edging; issued shell token with three marks | BLUE+EDGE+WRAP in trade; WRAP+OATH in ceremonial speech | Installed keepers of any gender; token issued by the council | After public oath and token transfer; marks refer to the three channels, not a universal sacred three | E1, X |
| X3 / keeper wrap after blue trim becomes cheap | Same cut; blue now affordable to non-keepers; issued token unchanged | Old ceremonial compound survives; shop name no longer proves office | Keepers retain token entitlement; non-keepers may buy blue trim but not claim the office | Oath/authority unchanged; scarcity meaning weakens, institutional meaning persists | E2 alternative for evaluation, X |

Execution trace: X1→X2 changes **status + token + register**, not merely cloth price; X2→X3 changes **material access + observer reliability**, not automatically the law. Removing the token should prevent an observer from confidently inferring office from blue trim alone.

Naming task before use: supply actual roots, compound order, inflection/classifier behavior, script spelling, pronunciation, and register distribution from the proposed language. If one ceremonial term is archaic, specify its transmission rather than inventing unexplained “ancient” sounds.
Myth task before use: decide whether the three channels support one account, competing keeper/grower interpretations, or no myth at all. The comparison does not require a pantheon or magical garment.
Handoff: these rows are alternative design constraints for lore-shokunin to accept, reject, or alter; their existence here does not establish a keeper institution in any world bible.

## 7. Era staging: version the relation, not just the silhouette

| Trigger | Garment/material delta | Language delta | Permission/rite delta to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supply expansion | Restricted trim becomes cheap; old cuts survive | Material word may broaden or remain an elite fossil | Does entitlement persist after scarcity disappears? X2→X3 tests this |
| Office reform | Token changes; garment reused | New office name may replace or coexist with old dress name | Who retains old regalia, and does a new appointment require redressing? |
| Contact/conquest | Imported technique may enter local clothing | Loan, calque, or no lexical change; donor marked separately | Borrowed appearance need not transfer religious authority |
| Religious reinterpretation | Existing garment retained | New ritual gloss can attach to an older name | Date the interpretation separately from the object and rite |
| Revival/display | New textile reconstructs an earlier appearance | Curatorial or revival label may be modern | Reenactment entitlement is not historical office-holding |

For each transition preserve old/new row IDs, locality, date precision, causal proposal, beneficiaries, and uncertainty. These are alternative mechanisms, not a universal sequence from “primitive” to “civilized.”

## What a worldbuilder can take

- **From the Roman slice:** use overlapping signs to create legible but non-unique identities; the child, magistrate, and authorized senator’s son defeat simplistic costume-to-rank decoding.
- **From the Qing slice:** separate portable insignia, complete ensemble, workshop technique, documentary authority, and multilingual terminology; neither one textile nor one dictionary entry supplies the whole institution.
- **From the exercise:** make one change propagate through multiple systems, but preserve unaffected relations. Cheap trim can alter class signaling without abolishing an office or changing a rite.
- **From the template:** a useful proposal predicts what a shopkeeper calls the object, who can wear it, what a witness may infer, and which era makes those answers valid. It need not prescribe a fictional culture’s canon.

## Provenance cautions and open verification

- **Thin coverage:** Qing Manchu-language garment terminology, women’s badge entitlements, local enforcement, and ordinary wardrobes are not established here. Locate period regulations, inventories, portraits with identified sitters, and relevant language expertise before adding those claims (to-verify).
- **Literary bias:** OF is an elite poetic account with multiple etiologies; SA is a later biographical account. Neither is a census of actual dress. PS75 aggregates texts from different centuries; its sweeping gender/status claims and reconstructed draping must be tested against SB94 and subsequent scholarship, not copied wholesale.
- **Object bias:** M1/M2 establish catalogued media and attributed periods, not complete entitlement rules. First-civil-rank identification, period Chinese terms, exact ceremonial use, and exceptions require full curatorial text plus regulation locators (to-verify); search excerpts are not a substitute for the underlying argument.
- **Collection/colonial caution:** museum accession is not excavated provenience, an identified original wearer, or a rights determination. Acquisition histories were not investigated here. Imperial, antiquarian, missionary, and colonial descriptions may privilege elite or outsider categories; translate those categories critically rather than treating them as timeless native self-description.
- **Scarce chronicle evidence:** worn, patched, reused, and non-elite cloth is unevenly preserved and recorded. Missing textile or diary evidence cannot establish that a group lacked a garment, term, rite, or permission; use “unknown,” not an invented reconstruction.
- **Calibration:** RE92 and VG60 are useful named analytical tools, not proof that every wearer agrees on a sign or every rite has identical stages. Fixed universal color meanings, costume-based racial essences, and script type as a ladder of civilization are invalid design premises; the adjacent symbolism and writing-system entries supply further cautions.
- **Stop rule:** unresolved exclusivity, sacred-language quotation, historical etymology, or precise rule-date claims stay marked (to-verify). Method and reference alternatives may be handed off now; only lore-shokunin can decide what becomes true of a fictional world.
