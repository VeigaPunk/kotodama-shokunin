## 1. Contract: interderive constraints, not cultural essences

- **Authorial method, not a discovered law:** this graph organizes design decisions; it does not predict a real population's beliefs from its clothing or grammar.
- Define `X[t, place, community, occasion] = {L, M, S, E, C}`: language varieties and scripts; mythic accounts; sign practices; era/reckoning models; dress and bodily presentation.
- Condition every state on `B` = ecology/material production, `I` = institutions/access, and `H` = inheritance/contact history. None is a substitute for people's choices.
- Separate elapsed time `t` from `E`: a new era proclamation can rename dates without changing phonology, while sound change can occur without a new era name.
- **No total-culture switch:** different communities can share clothing but not language, share a language but dispute gods, or reuse an emblem with opposing interpretations.
- Store each assertion as `form | user/interpreter | occasion | interval | place | mechanism | evidence/status`; “white = purity” without these fields is not a usable entry.
- Statuses here: **A** = externally attested with a source; **H** = argued historical inference; **D** = fictional design choice; **V** = unresolved `(to-verify)`. These are not levels of canon authorization.
- A fictional institution's claim is distinct from an authorial world-fact: “the temple says the seam binds the river” establishes neither actual magic nor universal belief.
- The worked example below is entirely **D**, including its apparent documents and reconstructions. It is a disposable method specimen, never a proposed world-bible entry.

## 2. Frameworks: what each licenses, and what it does not

| Framework | Usable operation | Calibration / limit |
|---|---|---|
| Keane's semiotic ideology and representational economy (2003) | Ask what users think words, objects, images, and agents can do; compare changes across media | Interpretations need social mediation; material qualities do not announce their own meanings |
| Icon / index / symbol, through Keane's Peircean account (2003) | Separate resemblance, material/causal connection, and learned convention in one garment | A single object can combine all three; these are relations, not exclusive object classes |
| Bell's ritualization (1992) | Design who differentiates an action as special, with what authority and bodily competence | Myth → rite is not a universal sequence; practice can reshape explanation |
| Assmann's cultural memory (2011) | Assign a carrier and renewal practice to remembered origins: recitation, monuments, archives, festivals | Writing is one carrier, not a prerequisite for memory; the framework is not a fixed lifespan clock |
| Comparative method and relative chronology (Campbell 2013) | Generate inherited forms by ordered sound changes; distinguish loans, retention, and innovation | Shared religion or similar-looking names does not establish a language family |
| Production history plus interactional dress (Barber 1991; Roach-Higgins & Eicher 1992) | Trace fiber → yarn → cloth → cut → fastening → use/repair; distinguish intended and received signals | Loom technique is not ethnicity; the wearer's body, work, and audience matter |
| Evidential cables / convergence (Wylie 2002) | Compare independently warranted observations before inferring an institution or meaning | Several modern books copying one chronicle are not independent corroboration |
| Invented tradition (Hobsbawm & Ranger 1983) | Separate age of a material practice from age of its official explanation or standardized uniform | Recent codification does not prove everything was recently invented or insincere |

## 3. Dependency graph: directed, typed, and time-indexed

Edge types: **F** = feasibility under stated resources/form rules; **I** = institutional or conventional permission; **T** = transmission through identifiable users/media; **R** = retrospective reinterpretation. Only **F** is a hard limit, and only within its explicit premises.

```text
(B, I, H)[t] ──F/I/T──> {L, M, S, E, C}[t]
L[t] ──form/wording──> {M, S, E, C}[t]
{M, S, E}[t] ──licensed practice / T──> {L, C}[t+Δ]
C[t] ──performance / reuse / T──> {L, M, S}[t+Δ]
{L, M, S, E, C}[t+Δ] ──R──> accounts OF earlier states
```

The last arrow changes an account of the past, not the past itself. Feedback is a sequence of dated actions; it is not evidence that two systems caused one another simultaneously.

| Coupling | Direction and concrete constraint | Required check / forbidden shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Base → costume | **F:** fiber, tools, labor, supply, and maintenance delimit feasible garments | Specify production or import; “silk-like” is not a supply chain |
| Language → myth; myth → language | **F/T:** theonyms and formulas use particular forms; **I/T:** cults select taboo terms, registers, epithets | Myth selects linguistic resources, not necessarily phonemes or obligatory grammar |
| Language ↔ symbol | **F/T:** a rebus needs an available reading; learned images can prompt new compounds | Date the homophony, language variety, and audience; a translation pun is not an in-world pun |
| Language ↔ era | **T/I:** inherited formulas constrain era coinage; administrations date, standardize, or rename texts | Administrative boundaries do not automatically create language stages |
| Language ↔ costume | **T:** terms preserve craft strata; **I:** recognized office dress can cue address choices | A garment cannot mechanically determine a speaker's register or the wearer's ancestry |
| Myth ↔ symbol | **T/I:** narratives authorize readings; images and performances also elicit new retellings | Record interpreter and setting; do not infer a whole myth from one motif |
| Myth ↔ era | **I:** origins authorize epochs; **R:** later regimes relocate or rewrite origins | Distinguish date of story, earliest attestation, and date of alleged event |
| Myth ↔ costume | **I/T:** rites prescribe materials or acts; repair, movement, and reuse can reshape explanations | “Priestly” needs an institution and occasion, not just an unusual silhouette |
| Symbol ↔ era | **I/T:** regimes change emblems; old signs persist on heirlooms; **R:** antiquarians redate meanings | Object manufacture, reuse, and interpretation have separate dates |
| Symbol ↔ costume | **F/I:** technique limits depiction; wearing context can turn an ordinary cord into an office sign | Show who recognizes it and how; no universal color-to-status lookup |
| Era ↔ costume | **I/T:** accession rules change regalia; surviving or revived dress advertises continuity | New law, actual adoption, and later revival are different events |

- **Not invertible:** a red cuff can result from fashion, office, mourning, reuse, or trade; it does not uniquely recover a myth, dynasty, language family, or personality.
- **Not complete:** leave an edge inactive when no transmission or sanction exists. Ordinary socks need not encode cosmology; minority practice need not fit a court's correlative grid.
- The closed alignment grids in `../myth-symbol/symbolism-synthesis-method.md` are useful optional design devices, especially for explicit institutional schemes; they are not a claim that every society has one closed symbolic grammar.

## 4. Applying the model: a constraint-propagation procedure

1. Fix a scene-scale brief: interval, location, speakers/wearers, audience, resources, and inherited facts supplied by lore; mark missing premises rather than supplying canon.
2. Inventory viable fibers, tools, media, contact routes, institutions, and already inherited forms. Record which are premises and which have reference support.
3. Choose a small number of mediators: a court, household, craft association, school, trading network, or festival. Name actual transmitters, not “the culture.”
4. Derive forms locally: pronunciation before puns; textile construction before insignia placement; office before official address; calendar authority before era names.
5. Run every active edge outward once; list required revisions, permitted alternatives, and unaffected systems. Revisit only when a changed premise breaks a previous output.
6. Stage lags explicitly: decree → local uptake; sound change → spelling reform; cheap cloth → status reclassification; revived rite → claims of antiquity. Their order is a design choice, not a fixed timetable.
7. Add dissent and partial knowledge: someone misreads a crest, retains an older date formula, rejects a temple etymology, or wears inherited regalia for practical reasons.
8. Test a mundane exchange, a ritual performance, and an era-boundary artifact. Resolve incompatible claims in the design packet, not by editing the world bible.

| Configuration to compare, not a civilizational ladder | Likely mediator to investigate | Counterweight to design |
|---|---|---|
| Central court with specialist workshops | Edict, school, regulated commission, official chronicle | Local lag, imitation, second-hand dress, counterfeits |
| Distributed household cults and craft learning | Kin interaction, apprenticeship, visits, recitation | Variant accounts, multiple calendars, nonexclusive signs |
| Port or frontier with multilingual circulation | Translation, marriage, military service, trade | Borrowed dress without conversion; shared rite without language shift |

## 5. Worked culture: the Savari estuary compact — invented throughout

**Scope:** an estuary league at a fictional late-preindustrial moment; the demonstration concerns one port's public institutions, with household and fisher alternatives. “Savari” is a political endonym, not a biological or uniform ethnic category.

**Premises:** flax grows on drained terraces; timber, spindle spinning, and narrow plain-weave looms are available; upland trade supplies wool. Seasonal gate repairs require coordinated labor. None of these premises forces the religion or the language chosen below.

| Required system | Concrete design output | Coupled consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Language family | Invented Sapic: Proto-Sapic → estuary Sava and inland Sapa; estuary branch innovates word-internal intervocalic `*p > v` | Related names have regular correspondences, not merely matching sea vocabulary |
| Writing | No phonographic writing posited for E1; E2 scribes adapt an alphabet through treaty contact, using inked wooden boards and incised sherds | Alphabetic spelling can preserve older forms while speech changes; tally marks alone are not assumed to be writing |
| Mythic/pantheon core | Temple account: Tapa, First Mender, joins the banks by agreement with Nara, the river interlocutor; household dead witness renewals | Cooperation is narrated as a covenant, not a universal flood/pantheon template |
| Symbolic system | A visible repaired joint signifies maintained obligation in gate ceremonies; dark wool on pale flax makes the joint legible | The sign depends on repair and occasion, not an animal/color/direction equivalence table |
| Era model | Compact years begin at the league's founding; E3 council introduces an additional “Red Opening” count, while fishers retain Compact dating | Rival date formulas reveal institutional allegiance without different astronomy |
| Costume/material signature | Narrow linen panels, replaceable ties, wool shoulder mantles in cold weather; office bands mark service only on duty | Panel seams become available for symbolism, but not every seam is a sacred emblem |
| Naming | Given name + `la` + residential house name; office title added separately, never inherited automatically | Dress and title identify present authorization, not a hereditary name caste |

### 5.1 Language, script, names: actually derive the forms

- Current Sava sketch: vowels `/a e i o u/`; consonants `/p t k m n s l r v w j/`; syllables `(C)V`; no consonant clusters/codas; penultimate stress, or the sole syllable in monosyllables. Roman `y` transcribes `/j/` if needed.
- E1 lacks `/v/`; the estuary innovation occurs during E2. Initial `/p/` survives. Learned ritual names can retain `/p/` through explicit rehearsal; this is a register exception, not an exception silently applied to ordinary inherited vocabulary.
- Productive pieces: `-ri` agent; `-ma` site; `-ni` object marker; `-ta` past. Predicates follow subject and object; the joining root also functions as a verb. `la` is a house-affiliation linker in names, not a claim about all possessive syntax.

| Designed earlier form | E3 ordinary form | Meaning / observable use |
|---|---|---|
| `*sapa` | `sava` | joining; join; `Savari` “joiners,” lexicalized political self-designation |
| `*tapa` | `tava` | seam; ordinary craft word, distinct from learned cult-name pronunciation `Tapa` |
| `*kapa` | `kava` | gate; `kavari` gate-worker/keeper; `Kavama` “gate-site” |
| `*mapa` | `mava` | fastening; `mavari` fastening-maker, an occupational byname |
| `*pana` | `pana` | basket; unchanged because `/p/` is initial, not between vowels |

- All stars denote invented antecedents, not reconstructions of any real language. The parallel outputs apply the same rule; no cultural meaning is inferred from the sound `/v/`.
- E2 official spellings retain the ancestral `p` sign in inherited words; the everyday forms above are normalized phonemic transcription, not facsimiles of the invented alphabet. A full glyph inventory is outside this specimen.
- E3 temple retelling: `Tapa kava-ni sava-ta` = `Tapa gate-OBJ join-PST`, “Tapa joined the gate.” This deliberately mixes a learned proper name with current language; it is not presented as an E1 text.
- A legal adult name is `Miri la Kavama`: personal name Miri, affiliation to the residential house Kavama. Miri/Nari are unglossed personal-name pool items; not every name needs an invented transparent etymology.
- `Miri la Kavama, Kavari` adds an appointed gate office. Address on duty is `Kavari`; a consenting friend can use `Miri` off duty. The rule belongs to this port's public convention, not an obligatory grammatical honorific system.
- A house move/adoption can change the affiliation slot; an old register can therefore name the same person differently. House membership is not inferred to be genetic descent.
- The temple reserves `Tapa` as a personal-name prohibition in its jurisdiction, but ordinary `tava` “seam” remains usable. An official trying to extend that ban to craft vocabulary creates a specific dispute, not a universal magical law.
- Nara is an opaque divine/river name in this sketch. No donor etymology is invented to make the pantheon perfectly regular.

### 5.2 Myth and symbol: institutions do the connecting

- Temple version: Tapa promises repeated mending rather than permanent conquest of the river. Renewal therefore requires showing an actual repaired joint, with witnesses naming the parties.
- Fisher version: Nara periodically lends passage; the river was never “bound.” Fishers can attend gate repairs without accepting the temple's account of cosmic jurisdiction.
- Household version emphasizes remembered repairers among the dead; it does not require a separate god for each craft. The disagreement is preserved, not flattened into one catechism.
- Keane-style analysis: the visible joint can be **iconic** of joining; wear and repair can **index** work; its recognition as a covenant sign is **conventional**. None alone proves the wearer is trustworthy.
- A dark repair cord on an everyday basket means repaired basket unless occasion and participants activate another reading. The same material does not carry compulsory sacred meaning everywhere.
- E2 council adds a red wool band to public gate-officers' ceremonial linen. Red denotes council commission there; it need not mean blood, fire, royalty, luck, or moral superiority elsewhere.
- Number, animal, and celestial registers are intentionally unaligned. No inference requires matching this covenant to a sacred beast or a cosmological triad.

### 5.3 Costume: production sequence, access, and performance

- **Production:** grow/ret flax → prepare and spin fiber → plain-weave narrow panels → sew joins → attach replaceable ties → inspect/repair. Upland wool is spun/woven separately for mantles and bands; this is a stipulated local workshop organization.
- The red band uses purchased madder-dyed wool in the scenario; madder/bedstraw reds on mordanted wool are externally documented rather than assumed here (see the dye references above), and no exact dyebath, mordant, yield, or fastness is claimed. A production-ready recipe remains `(to-verify)` by material testing and a textile specialist.
- **Work outfit:** short tied linen wrap and removable shoulder cover for gate work; cold-weather mantle is removed when it obstructs the task. Construction and mobility, not gender destiny, explain this choice.
- **Ceremonial outfit:** longer joined panels display the repaired seam; the officer wears the commissioned band for the gate hearing, not permanently as everyday caste dress.
- **Access:** E2 council pays for office bands; households repair ordinary linen; upland suppliers control some wool availability. Labor and procurement are part of rank display, not invisible background.
- **Body/adornment:** workers tie hair clear of tools; ceremonial witnesses add a removable fastening. Neither bodily form nor a mandatory tattoo is used as proof of birth identity.
- **Failure/reuse:** an inherited ceremonial panel can have older seams, newer ties, and a replaced band. Date each component; a visible “ancient” robe need not be one ancient object.
- Costume audit: woven panels explain seam placement; added bands do not require a figured loom; imported wool does not require local sheep husbandry; a new sign can be attached to old cloth without replacing the outfit.

### 5.4 Era staging: one change travels at different speeds

E1–E3 are designer-side stages. Their offsets below are invented convenience dates, not a proposed rate of cultural or language change; the inhabitants need not recognize those boundaries.

| Stage / elapsed Compact years | Language + record | Myth + symbol | Dress + era practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1, before year 0 | Sapic antecedents; orally transmitted names; sparse durable evidence in the designed archive | Household mending practices; no surviving contemporary text establishing a single origin story | Local panels/ties; household event counts, retrospectively converted with uncertainty |
| E2, years 0–179 | Alphabet adopted around year 20; intervocalic change stipulated within years 80–160; official spelling conservative | Council sponsors temple covenant narrative and commissioned red band; local accounts continue | Office bands supplied by council; Compact dating becomes administrative practice, not automatic household compliance |
| E3, year 180 onward | Ordinary Sava versus rehearsed Tapa; notices distinguish current authorization from inherited name/title | New trade access makes red bands easier to buy; council privileges an authenticated fastening; commentators claim this was always essential | Old red cloth remains wearable; office recognition changes; “Red Opening” dates appear beside Compact dates |

- **Trigger:** a reopened trade route increases access to red wool. It changes supply, not people's minds by itself; workshops imitate bands, and officers complain that spectators confuse purchased red with commissioned service.
- **C → S → I:** the council responds by requiring a registered fastening at hearings. A color once useful as a costly cue is insufficient; cloth need not be destroyed or recolored.
- **I → L:** notices reserve duty-address `Kavari` for current appointment; everyday occupational `kavari` remains broader. Same root, explicitly different pragmatic scopes.
- **S/I → M:** temple commentary emphasizes the witnessed joining rather than the red hue. This is an E3 reinterpretation, not evidence that E1 doctrine already prescribed authenticated fastenings.
- **I → E:** council celebrants date the reform as Red Opening year 1; fishers keep the older count. The date dispute is political memory, not a sound change or a change in year length.
- **Unaffected:** `/p > v/` is not undone, Nara needs no new etymology, workers still need repairable clothing, and inland Sapa need not adopt any of the reform.
- Counterfactual branch: if the council instead tolerates imitation, red becomes broader league affiliation and officials rely on verbal witnesses. Both branches fit the material premise; lore would choose, not this method entry.

## 6. Handoff and rejection checks

- Packet fields: `proposal-id | D/H/A/V | interval/place/group | source or design premise | active edge | derived forms/materials | alternatives | dissent | unresolved checks | canon-owner decision`.
- Hand lore the graph slice, sound-law/word table, era table, costume production/access sheet, and observable scene tests. Do not submit one flattened “culture description” with dissent erased.
- Reject a rebus if it depends on the wrong era's pronunciation; reject an office name if its affix is undeclared; reject a costume if its supply exists only after the scene.
- Reject a myth-origin claim supported only by a later chronicle; reject an assertion of universal compliance inferred from a law; reject identity inferred from one garment or sign.
- Keep production feasibility, social authorization, evidential confidence, and canon acceptance separate. Even an internally consistent **D** packet remains non-canonical until lore's owner acts.

## What a worldbuilder can take

- Take the **coupling mechanism**: repairable cloth → visible joint → witnessed obligation → official sign → address convention → later reform and competing memories.
- Take the **discipline of selective propagation**: a trade shock changes sign recognition and institutions without obliging a new pantheon, language family, or bodily ideal.
- Take the **era seams**: old spelling/new speech, old cloth/new fastening, new official dates/old household counts. These produce usable differences without one historical costume per century.
- Take the **comparison method**, not an alleged real-world ethnic package: specify actors and resources, develop alternatives, then let lore select or reject a candidate system.

## Provenance cautions and known gaps

- **Access calibration:** Keane's author-hosted text and Assmann's frontmatter support the cited conceptual claims directly; Wylie's publisher record/contents verify the named arguments. Other links verify bibliographic metadata, not a fresh complete reading. Bell's theory and the dress/production frameworks are used at synthesis level, not as page-specific quotations.
- Roach-Higgins & Eicher's bibliographic identity is confirmed by the Crossref record for the DOI printed above (title, both authors, *Clothing and Textiles Research Journal* 10(4):1–8, June 1992), and the University Digital Conservancy record is publicly readable, exposing the published abstract that matches the summary used here. No page-specific quotation or fine-grained attribution is supplied in this entry; a future verbatim quotation should cite the published article itself.
- **Contested, not universal:** causal priority of myth versus ritual; structural readings that make every object part of one closed code; claims that a recent codification exposes all tradition as false. Use Bell, Keane, Wylie, and Hobsbawm/Ranger as bounded analytic lenses, not consensus causal laws.
- **Rejected inference rules:** climate → grammar/personality; costume → ethnicity; color word → sacred meaning; shared motif → common ancestry; “primitive → advanced” as a mandatory era sequence. Pop correspondence dictionaries or universal plot/archetype templates cannot supply the missing evidence.
- **Colonial/archive bias:** a missionary's category “idol,” a conqueror's dress decree, or an elite chronicle records an interested description; it does not independently establish native categories, ordinary compliance, or a timeless pantheon. Seek local-language testimony, material context, and living specialists where applicable.
- **Thin tradition-specific competence:** this entry does not establish expertise in any particular living community's sacred names, restricted rites, dye recipes, or dress rights. A donor-specific borrowing or comparative claim stays `(to-verify)` until appropriate specialist/community sources support it; absence of a chronicle is not absence of practice.
- **Scarce chronicles in the specimen:** E1's fictional archive does not establish a unified cult or date the first telling of the temple story. Continuity claims remain `(to-verify)` within that invented evidential model; E3 commentary cannot fill the gap retroactively.
- The Savari, Sapic forms, dates, cults, documents, and clothing arrangements are authored examples, not historical discoveries. Citations warrant tools and cautions; they do not authenticate invented evidence or authorize canon.
