## 2. Six design decisions

Every row is a lever the worldbuilder sets, not a fact about any culture. The numbers are defaults chosen so a case can be played; change them and the institution changes. The third column names the decision's default and threshold; the attested material for each decision lives in §3, never in a row id.

| decision | options | default and threshold | failure if unset |
|---|---|---|---|
| docket: what may be charged at all | harm to persons or property / harm to crops and animals / harm by word / status offences | actionable only when the harm is nameable, dated within 12 months, and attached to a person; a charge with no date is filed as defamation, not as maleficium | a permanent docket: every quarrel can escalate, the court becomes a rumour mill, and the dead and the absent are charged by default |
| standing: who may accuse whom | the injured party / the household head / an office-holder / a paid informer / an accomplice | the injured party and the household head charge freely; a third party posts a bond of 12 units and pays the defendant's costs if the charge fails; an accomplice's testimony needs one independent corroborator | anonymous denunciation and an informer market; the docket is run by creditors and rivals, and the charge becomes a debt-collection instrument |
| evidence floor: what counts | two concurring witnesses / one witness plus a material trace / an expert reading / confession alone | two concurring witnesses, or one witness plus a trace (a found object, a mark, a purchase); confession alone never suffices; spectral sight is admitted only as a reputation fact, never as proof of the act | conviction on reputation; the charge becomes unfalsifiable, and every alibi is answered by a dream |
| proof ceiling: how far a case may escalate | oath-compurgation / ordeal / judicial combat / oracle | oath-compurgation for harms under 30 units; ordeal only for a capital charge with two witnesses or a trace; the ordeal is barred for the accused under 14 or over 60 | the ordeal becomes routine and loses its signal; the old, the poor and the friendless are convicted by default |
| confession rule: what a confession is worth | free confession / confession after the question / confession with corroboration / confession ratified after 24 hours | a confession taken under the question needs one corroborating trace and a public ratification after 24 hours; a retraction before ratification voids it; a confession that names accomplices is admissible only against the speaker | the confession becomes the whole case; the movement grows by names, and every prisoner becomes a witness against the next |
| brake: who may run a witch-finding commission, and for how long | a standing office / a per-case commission / a fixed-term commission / a fee-per-conviction finder | a commission runs 6 months or 20 cases, whichever comes first; its fee is a salary, not a share of confiscations; when the commission ends the finder may himself be charged | the finder becomes a permanent interest; the wave feeds itself, and the only exit is a scandal or a counter-charge |

The six levers are ordered because each constrains the next. A wide docket with no standing rule produces informers; a standing rule with no evidence floor produces reputation trials; an evidence floor with no proof ceiling produces routine ordeals; a proof ceiling with no confession rule produces confessions under torture; a confession rule with no brake produces a movement; a brake with no slander suit leaves the accused without an exit. Decide them in this order and write each decision into the world's law, not into a narrator's aside.

Two design consequences follow. First, the **proof ceiling is the institution's signature**: a culture that tortures and a culture that does not will run different dockets on the same charge, and the difference is visible in the numbers. Second, the **slander suit is not an epilogue**: it is the accused's only lawful exit, and an institution that does not provide one has decided that the accused's only exits are flight, death, or a counter-accusation.

### Labels and the vocabulary trap

- S914: the 1911 survey opens by narrowing its own term — 「a term often used of magical practices of all sorts, but here confined to the malevolent (“black”) magic of women」 (EB11 vol. 28, "Witchcraft", opening paragraph) — so a world's charge-word must be defined by the harm it names, not by the English word's breadth.
- S914: the same article calls the modern idea 「largely a learned product」 — 「the modern idea of witchcraft is largely a learned product—the result of scholastic and inquisitorial ingenuity」 (same article, "Witchcraft among Primitive Peoples") — the accused type can be younger than the institution that tries it, and the type is a product of the tribunal's own literature.
- S915: the 1865 survey notes that the charge ignores the body it lands on — 「children of tender years, have been committed to the rack and to the stake on the same accusation which condemned the old and the ugly」 (Part II, ch. I, "The Witch-Compact", the paragraph on the age and sex of the accused) — the charge's stereotype and its actual docket are two different things, and a world can make that gap a plot engine.
- S915: the same book records the earliest English case it knows, hedged as report (「The earliest judicial trial for the crime on record in England is said to have occurred in the reign of John」), as an accusation answered by proof — 「Agnes, the wife of Odo the merchant, accused Gideon of sorcery; and he was acquitted by the judgment of iron」 (Part II, ch. II, the paragraph on the earliest English trials) — an accusation is a claim, and the record preserves the acquittal as loudly as the conviction; write what the record owes the acquitted.
- S915: the survey's abstract of the criminal code opens the docket on talk alone — 「The arrest might take place on the simple rumour of a witch being found somewhere, without any previous denunciation.」 (Part III, ch. I, the Ennemoser abstract paragraph) — the first design decision is what a rumour must pass before it becomes a case.
- S403: the outsider label is the trap the specialist names outright — 「the perception of these objects as 'fetish' is an entirely spurious external view which classifies objects regardless of indigenous understanding」 (Mack, p. 54) — do not let the analyst's word become the culture's charge; the label is a second accusation, laid on the record after the first.
- S610: a practice's own teaching can deny the label its neighbours apply — 「the mezuzah sometimes has been accorded the status of amulet, a magical charm」 (My Jewish Learning, section "Is the Mezuzah an Amulet?") — write the denial into the vocabulary as a position, not as an error, and let the court hear both.
- S644: the dictionary sense of the remedy carries the harm inside it — 「Anything worn about the person as a charm preventative against evil, mischief, disease, witchcraft, etc.」 (Pitt Rivers leaflet, quoting the OED) — one English word covers the danger and the object that answers it, which is exactly the confusion a charge-word must not inherit.
- S645: the museum split turns on who supplies the power — 「The amulet is an object with natural magical properties, whereas a talisman must be charged with magical powers by a creator」 (AUB Museum, second paragraph) — the split is a claim about agency, so it belongs in the charge's vocabulary, not in a prop list; a culture that charges its talismans will have a profession to accuse.
- S280: legal personhood is itself a defined class with exclusions — 「a being having legal rights and obligations (including the state, etc.; not including slaves」 (Lewis & Short, persōna, II.B.2) — ask who the law counts as a possible accused before asking what the charge is; the excluded cannot be accused and cannot sue.

### The accusation grammar

- S916: the Bull defines the crime from the top and names both sexes in the same sentence — 「many persons of both sexes, unmindful of their own salvation and straying from the Catholic Faith, have abandoned themselves to devils, incubi and succubi」 (Bull of Innocent VIII, printed pp. xliii–xlv, crime paragraph) — the licence creates the class it prosecutes, and a gender-neutral charge-word does not make the docket gender-neutral.
- S916: the same Bull hands the inquisitors the whole discretion — 「be empowered to proceed to the just correction, imprisonment, and punishment of any persons, without let or hindrance」 (Bull, printed pp. xliii–xlv, empowerment clause) — when the commission's writ is open, the brake must be written somewhere else, in the commission's term, fee, or record.
- S915: the informer's motive is stated as ordinary — 「Cupidity or malice was the cause of this informer's accusation」 (Part II, ch. II, the paragraph on the De Spencers and John of Nottingham) — design the informer's bond, or the docket will be run by creditors; the accusation is a move in a dispute that predates it.
- S917: the Salem narrator shows the charge surviving a perfect alibi — 「it did not break the force of the accusation to bring hundreds of witnesses to prove that he was, at the very time, in another remote place」 (Upham, vol. i, p. 407) — spectral proof is the limiting case where no alibi can answer, and a world that admits it has repealed the evidence floor.
- S917: the same book lists what the tribunals admitted — 「If any thing strange or remarkable could be discovered in the persons, histories, or deportment of accused persons」 (vol. i, p. 408) — the evidence menu can include the accused's whole biography; write which facts are in and which are out, or the menu becomes a character assassination.
- S912: a legal boundary can be published as an enumerated table with a stated consequence — 「None of you shall approach to any that is near of kin to him, to uncover their nakedness: I am the LORD.」 (Leviticus 18:6, KJV) — the form to copy for a charge table is enumeration plus consequence, not a slogan; a docket written as a list can be argued with, a docket written as a mood cannot.

### The proof technology

- S901: the proof ladder's escalation is a sentence the pleader says — 「I swear alone, if thou darest, deny my oath and fight me」 (Lea, "The Wager of Battle") — the accusation's gravity is what selects the rung, so the docket must price the charge before the proof begins.
- S901: the ladder is not one system but a set of local charters — in the laws of Ghent (1178) there is no allusion to any species of ordeal, while the charter of Nieuport (1163) still names iron and compurgatorial oaths (Lea, charter evidence) — write the proof system per jurisdiction, not per kingdom, and let two towns on one river run different ceilings.
- S902: the proof has a birth statute and a procedure — 「The first legal establishment of these ordeals is to be found in the laws of Gundebald, King of the Burgundians, A. D. 501」 (Millingen, vol. 1) — date the proof by the statute that made it; when the statute decays, the duel is the afterlife, and the charge has moved from proof to status.
- S903: the element's rule is countable, which is what makes the verdict readable — 「in a Scandinavian law it is prescribed that the red-hot iron shall be carried nine steps」 (EB11 "Ordeal") — a proof with no number cannot fail, and a proof that cannot fail is not a proof; the number is the institution's falsifiability.
- S903: the rite includes the inspection, not only the carrying — the hands are examined for burn-traces and bound in leaves (same article) — write the reader, the moment of reading, and the record of the reading; an unrecorded inspection is a verdict without a warrant.
- S905: exclusion can be the sanction that makes a boundary real — 「To exclude any human being from their roof is thought impious」 (Tacitus, Germania XXI) — and the same chapter makes feud heritable, so exclusion and feud are one institution seen from two sides; the accusation's first punishment is often the door.
- S297: the omen procedure refers the sign to a licensed reader and answers it with a rite — 「ob cetera prodigia libros adire decemviri iussi」 (Livy 21.62.6) — divination enters the case as an institutional referral, not as a private hunch; name the reader, the book, and the rite that closes the question.
- S295: the anomaly is classified by what it portends — 「which were considered omens (zenchō 前兆), warnings, or punishment」 (Nichibunken glossary, kaii) — a culture that reads misfortune as punishment has already written the first line of its charge; the omen is the accusation's opening formula.
- S917: the failing body is read as the proof — 「their failure to come forth at the bidding of her defamers was regarded as a sure and irrefragable proof of her guilt」 (Upham, vol. i, p. 410) — a test that only the innocent would fail is a conviction machine; the same procedure searched the house for puppets after every arrest (vol. i, p. 408), so the trace was found because the procedure looked for it.
- S621: counter-witchcraft leaves a material record in the fabric — 「Witches’ marks were a common form of ritual protection in which symbols or patterns were carved into the stonework or woodwork of historic buildings」 (Provan Hall, "Witches’ Marks") — the defence is dated to the same fear as the charge, and a world can date a wave by its counter-marks.
- S622: the counter-object names its target — 「The bottle symbolised the witch's bladder, and so the sharp objects were believed to cause her much pain」 (Museum Wales) — a counter-accusation can be an object with a named victim; write whether the object is evidence of a crime or evidence of a fear.
- S605: protection can be worn or fixed, and its grammar is frontal — 「A frontal face was also used as a protective amulet to ward off the plague」 (Faraone, introduction) — write the surface the mark is put on, since that fixes who is protected and, by contrast, who is exposed.
- S914: England's proof menu is narrower because the courts do not torture — 「the absence of judicial torture made the cases proportionately less numerous than they were on the European continent」 (EB11 "Witchcraft", England paragraph) — the proof ceiling is a jurisdiction's signature, and a finder who imports a continental technique imports a wave with it.
- S914: the finder's technique is a body test — 「he pricked the body of the witch to find anaesthetic areas」 (same paragraph) — and the water test inverts the innocent body: 「those who did not sink were adjudged guilty」 (same article) — a proof that reads the body's failure as guilt needs a body that cannot pass.
- S914: the finder's own end is part of the life cycle — 「One of the most famous witch-finders was Matthew Hopkins, himself hanged for witchcraft after a career of some three years.」 (same paragraph) — the brake can arrive as a counter-charge, and the finder's method can be turned on him by the next court.

### Confession and its record

- S914: the manual and the bull together produce the confession — 「these admissions, wrung from their victims by rack or thumb-screw, were described as voluntary」 (EB11 "Witchcraft", third-period paragraph) — the voluntary fiction is the institution's central lie; a worldbuilder must decide whether the culture believes it, and what the record says when it does not.
- S916: the Bull's crime list is a public definition of the offence — acts, not persons: infants in the womb, cattle, crops, and men and women (Bull, printed pp. xliii–xlv, crime paragraph) — a confession that merely recites the Bull's list has added nothing the Bull did not already say, so the record's question is whether the confession contains any fact the list does not.
- S917: the record can preserve a retraction against the verdict — a confessing woman on her way to the stake declares 「I declare I am as free of witchcraft as any child; but, being delated by a malicious woman … I made up that confession on purpose to destroy my own life, being weary of it, and choosing rather to die than live.」 (Upham vol. i, p. 350, quoting Sinclair; the ellipsis marks the cut) — write the retraction's legal effect, or the record will read as consent and the execution as confession.

### Slander and social death

- S917: the acquitted man sues his accusers and wins — 「He instituted prosecutions of Procter and others for defamation, and recovered against them all.」 (vol. i, p. 190) — the counter-suit is the accused's lawful exit, and the record keeps it; a world without the suit has only flight, death, or a counter-charge.
- S917: the reputational sentence can precede the legal one — a woman told Mackenzie that 「being defamed for a witch, she knew she would starve」 (vol. i, p. 351, in Mackenzie's report) — the charge's first punishment is economic; write who may still buy, sell, hire, or marry the accused while the case is open.

### Movements and their life cycle

- S381: a movement's leader is tried by the existing authority and the sentence extends to the followers — 「Kimpa Vita foi presa, arguida pelo capuchinho Bernardo da Gallo e sentenciada à morte na fogueira como herege da Igreja de Roma.」 (Farias, p. 210) — the trial is the movement's terminal rite, and the court is the rival institution's court.
- S381: the aftermath is a dispersal, not an end — 「após a morte de Kimpa, seus seguidores foram castigados, condenados à morte ou escravizados」 (p. 210) — write what happens to the names on the list, because the list outlives the leader.
- S382: the movement's dress is an identity mark the court can read — 「Ces anges se distinguaient des autres parce qu’ils portaient sur la tête une couronne faite de l’écorce de l’arbre ensanda.」 (Laurent de Lucques, p. 226) — a costume that marks membership also marks targets; the record names the judge who pronounces sentences (p. 230) and the pyre stacked for the burning (p. 237).
- S854: an ailment can be reclassified as a spell — 「had been thought to be the effect of a spell cast by witch or a punishment from God」 (Sakalauskaite-Juodeikiene et al.) — the medical and the witchcraft aetiologies compete for the same body, and the winner is decided by who owns the diagnosis.

### Property, scale, and the boundary

- S909: a legal code can name and price the transfers a household makes — 「he shall give her the amount of her purchase money and the dowry which she brought from her father's house」 (Code of Hammurabi § 138) — if a charge can confiscate, the code must say what returns and to whom; the return rule is the difference between a fine and an expropriation.
- S914: the persecution's scale is itself a contested number — 「The total number of victims of the witch persecutions is variously estimated at from 100,000 to several millions.」 (EB11 "Witchcraft", closing paragraphs) — mark the estimate, never the census; a worldbuilder's number is a design choice, and a historian's number is a period claim with a date on it.
- S349: the outsider genre takes the story and misreads the people — 「The windigo is a personal spirit that still belongs to the Native American cultures from which it has been taken」 (Hunchman, p. 103) — the boundary anchor: no entry may convert a living community's tradition into a charge catalogue, and a fictional institution must be built from mechanism, not from a real people's names.
- S350: restricted motifs are not free material — 「They may not be suitable for mass communication or dissemination.」 (Australia Council, p. 44) — the borrowing rule for anything that looks like a real charge: permission from the custodians, or redesign the mechanism without the distinctive material.

## 4. The vocabulary set

Five person-slots plus the act-word, not one word. A language that has only a single "witch" word cannot run the institution, because the court cannot distinguish the charge, the practitioner, the rival, the reader and the complainant. The English set is a trap: "witch" carries a gender and a period, "sorcerer" carries a foreign origin, "healer" and "diviner" carry approval, and "accuser" carries the court's own grammar. Build the slots first, then the words.

| slot | what it must distinguish | test question | one invented solution (NON-CANON) |
|---|---|---|---|
| the act-word | the harm, not the person | can the language say "someone did this to me" without saying who the someone is? | *kavut* — "the unseen working"; the offence is named before any offender |
| the witch | the person under charge, gender-neutral | does the word also mean "poisoner", "rival", or "old woman"? | *kavun* — "one who works unseen"; neutral in form, pejorative in use, and the manuals later derive *kavun-sa* for the female accused |
| the sorcerer | the specialist whose practice is licensed versus the one whose practice is not | can the language rank two practitioners without calling either a criminal? | *sorun* — "lot-drawer", the licensed reader's rival; the translation "sorcerer" is a later legal borrowing whose root is divination, not harm (to-verify: the EB11 "Sorcery" stub carrying that etymology is a 71-word definition only and is filed rejected in the registry, so the clause needs a dictionary row before it is cited as fact) |
| the healer | the mender whose competence is social, not juridical | is the healer's failure a malpractice or a charge? | *melun* — "mender"; a failed cure is a debt, not a crime, unless the act-word is added |
| the diviner | the licensed reader whose reading is a court fact | can the reader be paid by a party without the reading becoming that party's evidence? | *ithun* — "reader"; the licence names who may pay, and a paid reading is admitted only as a reputation fact |
| the accuser | the party who sets a case before the court | can the language distinguish the injured party, the informer and the office-holder? | *tevun* — "one who sets (a case) before"; the bond-bearing informer is *tevun-ok*, a marked derivation |

Three translation traps follow from the table. First, **the remedy word and the harm word colexify in English**: a charm is defined against witchcraft, so the same sentence can describe the danger and the cure. Second, **the practitioner words carry approval**: "healer" and "diviner" are jobs, "witch" and "sorcerer" are charges, and a translation that maps one to the other has already convicted someone. Third, **the outsider word is always available**: "fetish", "witch doctor", "cult" are labels applied after the fact, and a world that lets the analyst's word into its law has imported a second tribunal.

The lexicon has a further consequence the designer should use: a culture's charge-word fixes **who can be named**. If *kavun* is gender-neutral, the docket can name men, and the movement's demography changes; if the female derivation exists, the court has a word for the accused it expects, and the manuals will find her. Neither is more historical than the other; both are choices, and the choice should be visible in the law's text.

## 5. The accusation grammar

An accusation is a speech act with four parts: a **harm** (what was done), a **victim** (who suffered it), an **agent** (who is said to have done it), and a **forum** (who may hear the claim). Most worldbuilding stops at the agent and produces a villain; the institution lives in the other three parts. Write them as a form the court fills in, and let the form's blanks do the work.

**Who may accuse.** Build a ladder, not a rule. At the bottom, the injured party and the household head may charge freely because the harm is theirs. Above them, a third party — a creditor, a rival, a neighbour, an office-holder — must post a bond, and the bond is the institution's answer to malice: if the charge fails, the accuser pays. At the top, the office-holder may charge without a bond but only within a written commission, and the commission's terms are the brake (§9). The informer sits outside the ladder: paid, protected, and never a witness to the act he reports. The accomplice sits inside it: his testimony is admissible, but it needs a corroborator, or the movement grows by names alone.

**What counts as evidence.** The menu is short and each item has a different failure mode. Two concurring witnesses can be coached; one witness plus a trace can be planted; an expert reading can be sold; a confession can be extracted; spectral sight cannot be cross-examined; reputation is not evidence at all but is the thing the trial actually decides. The design task is not to pick the strongest item but to write the **floor**: the minimum combination that lets a case proceed, and the rule that says what happens when the floor is not met. A charge below the floor should be dismissed with costs, and the dismissal should be recorded, because the record of failed charges is the only check on the next wave.

**When it is actionable.** Three gates, in order. The harm must be **nameable** (a fever, a dead cow, a failed birth, a lost batch of brine — not a mood). It must be **dated** (within the docket's window, 12 months in the default table; the window is what makes an old grievance a slander rather than a charge). And it must be **attached to a person who can answer** (the dead, the absent and the slave may be named in the record but cannot be tried; naming them is a separate offence, and the institution should say which). A charge that passes the three gates enters the docket with a grade: **suspected**, **defamed**, or **charged with the act**. The middle grade is the institution's most useful invention: it lets a court act on reputation without pretending to prove an act, and it gives the accused a status to appeal rather than a body to defend.

**The counter-accusation.** The accused has three moves. He may **deny and demand proof** (the trial), he may **sue the accuser for defamation** (the slander suit, §8), or he may **turn the charge back** — accuse the accuser of the same act, or of a worse one. The third move is the one that ends waves, because it converts a one-way procedure into a contest between two persons, and the court must then decide which accusation to hear first. Write the priority rule: the earlier charge, the graver charge, or the charge with the better bond. A world without a priority rule gets two simultaneous trials and a feud.

**The grades and their costs.** A suspected person may be watched, excluded from office, or required to find sureties; a defamed person may be shunned, but not imprisoned; a person charged with the act may be held, tried and, on conviction, punished. The grades should be visible in the law's vocabulary and in the record's columns, because the whole point of the institution is that most cases stop at the lower grades. A world where every charge is capital has no ladder, only a cliff.

## 6. The proof technology

Proof is the institution's engine, and every proof has the same three-part shape: a **question** put to something that can answer, a **rule** that makes the answer readable, and a **reader** who is licensed to read it. The menu below is a design palette; each entry names what the proof decides, what makes it falsifiable, and its characteristic failure.

| proof | what it decides | what makes it falsifiable | characteristic failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| oath-compurgation | whether the accused's denial is believed by his community | a fixed number of oath-helpers, named in advance; a helper who refuses is counted | the helpers are the accused's kin, so the proof measures standing, not truth |
| ordeal by iron | whether the element accepts the accused | a fixed carry (nine steps, a fixed weight), an inspection of the hands, a binding | the inspection is unrecorded, or the element is blessed by the party that laid the charge |
| ordeal by water | whether the element receives the accused | a fixed immersion and a fixed reading, witnessed | the reading is a convention that must be published: floating can mean the element rejects the guilty, or receives the innocent, and the two conventions produce opposite verdicts on the same body |
| oracle or omen referral | which of several readings of the harm is the official one | a closed sign space, a licensed reader, a second reading for a disputed case | the reader is also the accuser's client, and the second reading is bought |
| spectral sight | who appeared to the victim | nothing the accused can answer; admissible only as a reputation fact | it repeals the alibi and makes every dream a witness |
| possession | who speaks in the victim's voice | the possessed person's words are tested against the charge's formula | the formula is taught, so the voice recites the docket |
| confession | the act itself, and the accomplices | corroboration plus ratification; a retraction window | extraction replaces proof, and the names multiply |
| witness plus trace | the act, by human testimony and a material object | the trace is produced in court, and the witness is cross-examined on the finding | the trace is found because the searchers looked for it |
| judicial combat | the judgment of the stronger body | a formal challenge, a fixed procedure, a public field | victory is read as verdict, so the strong are innocent and the weak are guilty |

Two rules make the palette an institution. **The ceiling**: the docket's price decides which rungs are available, and a capital charge may reach the ordeal while a property charge may not. **The decay**: proofs are not eternal; a statute that creates one can be repealed, a charter that names one can lapse, and a technique that was decisive can become a show. The ordeal entry owns the ladder's history and its mountebank afterlife; this entry owns the accusation's use of the rungs — who is sent to which, and what the sending says about the charge.

The **witch-finder** is the proof system's professional. He arrives with a technique (a prick, a water test, a weighing, a shaving), a fee, and a commission. Design him as an office, not a monster: the commission's term, the fee's form, the record he must keep, and the liability he carries when the commission ends. The historical finder is a warning about the design, not a template for a character: a fee-per-conviction finder and a salary finder run different institutions on the same charge, and the difference is the wave.

## 7. The confession and its extraction

The confession is the proof the institution prefers, because it is the only proof that **names accomplices**. A witness can only say what he saw; a confession can populate the next trial. That is why the confession's rules are the place where the institution's honesty is decided.

Write the **taking** as a procedure with a record — its forms, its witnesses and its paper — not as a scene of cruelty and not as a technique. The question list, the sequence of questions, the promises made and withdrawn, the presence of the examiner and the scribe, the moment the accused is told what the charge is — these are the institution's forms, and they are what a worldbuilder needs in order to show how a confession is produced without turning the entry into a manual. The historical sources describe the sequence at the level of scholarship; the fictional institution should be described at the same level, and the actual words of an interrogation should stay off the page.

Then write the **worth** of the confession:

- **Corroboration.** A confession needs one fact the confessor could not have known from the charge, or one trace that matches the confession's account. Without it, the confession is a recitation of the docket.
- **Ratification.** A confession taken under the question is ratified in public after a fixed interval — 24 hours in the default table — and a retraction before ratification voids it. The interval is the institution's one concession to the possibility of false confession, and a world can make it long, short, or nominal.
- **Scope.** A confession is admissible against the speaker only, unless the accomplice's name is corroborated independently. This is the rule that decides whether a wave grows by names; the historical movements grew because the rule was absent or ignored.
- **Custody.** The record has a keeper, a place, and a rule for copying. A record that can be edited after the fact is not a record; a record that cannot be copied is a private weapon.
- **Effect on property.** A confession may attaint the confessor's goods, but the code must say what happens to the household, the heirs, and the dowry. The property question is where the confession stops being a religious procedure and becomes an inheritance mechanism.

The sources give the shape of the failure. A manual and a bull can produce a confession that the record calls voluntary; a court can preserve a retraction beside the verdict; a confessor can say, on the way to the stake, that she made the confession to end a life she could no longer live. The design lesson is not that the institution is hypocritical — every institution is — but that the **record's honesty** is a separate lever from the procedure's severity, and a worldbuilder should set it deliberately.

## 8. The slander side

The accused who sues is the institution's most interesting figure, because he uses the court's own grammar against it. The suit has four elements, and each is a design decision:

- **The false charge.** The plaintiff must show the accusation was false, not merely that it failed. An acquittal is evidence, not proof; a dismissed charge may still have been honestly brought.
- **Publication.** The charge must have been spoken or written to a third party. A private accusation to the magistrate is privileged; a rumour in the market is not. The line between them is the institution's free-speech settlement, and it should be written.
- **Malice.** The plaintiff must show the accuser knew or did not care that the charge was false. The historical record names malice as the informer's ordinary motive; the design can make malice presumed when the accuser is a creditor, a rival, or a paid informer, and rebuttable otherwise.
- **Damage.** The plaintiff must show a loss: custom, office, marriage, credit, or the price of the bond. The measure of damages is the institution's statement of what a reputation is worth, and it should be a number the court can compute.

The **symmetry problem** is the design's hard part. The same court hears the charge and the slander suit, so the accused's counter-suit may be heard by the same magistrate who committed him for trial. Write the separation: a different bench, a different town, or a different season. A world that does not separate the two proceedings has given the accused a remedy on paper and a second trial in practice.

The **chilling effect** is the other side. A slander suit that is too easy to bring will silence accusers, including honest ones, and a community that cannot name a harm is not safer, only quieter. The bond on the third-party accuser (§5) is the usual compromise: the injured party may speak freely, the busybody must stake something. Write the bond's size against the cost of the suit, and the institution will have a settled temperature.

One more figure belongs here: the **accused who cannot sue**. The slave, the stranger, the excommunicate, the dead — the law's person is a defined class, and the exclusions are the institution's real boundary. A world that gives the slander suit only to the full person has said, in its procedure, who counts.

## 9. Witch-finding movements: life cycle

A wave is not a mood; it is an institution with a budget, a staff, and a record. The cycle below is a design sequence, not a historical law; a world can stop at any stage, and the interesting worlds stop early for a reason.

| stage | what happens | design lever | what to write |
|---|---|---|---|
| anomaly | a run of harms, a failed harvest, a child's death, a strange illness | the diagnosis: who is allowed to say what the run means | the first diagnosis scene, and the reader who is believed |
| diagnosis | a reader names a cause and a class of persons | the reader's licence and fee | the reader's commission, and who pays for the reading |
| first charge | a single accusation, with a bond and a forum | the docket's gate and the standing ladder | the first case, tried by the ordinary court |
| expertise | a manual, a finder, or an office arrives | the commission's term and fee | the commission's text: 6 months or 20 cases, salary not spoils |
| expansion | accomplices are named, and the names cross households | the confession's scope rule | the corroboration rule, and the first name that does not check |
| peak | the elite split: some courts protect, some compete | the appeal and the jurisdiction | the two courts, and the case that goes to the higher one |
| brake | a scandal, a counter-charge, a cost, a skeptical judgment | the finder's liability and the record's openness | the trial of the finder, or the audit of the commission |
| aftermath | memory, compensation, denial, a new taboo on the word | the record's custody and the compensation rule | what the town does with the list of names |

Two levers decide most of the cycle. The **fee structure** decides whether the finder wants more cases or fewer: a salary finder can close the commission, a spoils finder cannot. The **confession's scope** decides whether the wave grows by one body or by every name a prisoner can be made to say. A world that wants a short, sharp wave should set a salary, a fixed term, a corroboration rule, and a slander suit; a world that wants a long wave should set the opposites, and then show the cost.

The historical material gives the cycle's terminal images, not its laws: a leader tried and burned, followers punished or enslaved; a finder hanged by the court that used him; a record that preserves a retraction beside a verdict; a town that later cannot agree on what happened. Use the images; do not import the numbers. The totals are estimates with dates on them, and the estimates disagree by orders of magnitude.

## 10. The social functions of an accusation

An accusation does work for the people who bring it. The functions below are analytical prompts, not a claim that every accusation has one; a case can carry several, and the same case can change function as it moves through the grades.

| function | who benefits | what the verdict redistributes | design signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| levelling | the envious, the indebted, the politically outflanked | status, office, custom, the accuser's bond | the accused is prosperous, odd, or newly risen; the accuser is a near-equal |
| scapegoating | the community that needs a cause | the blame for a run of harms | the harm is collective (harvest, plague, war) and the accused is a marginal person |
| boundary maintenance | the court and the orthodoxy | the line between the inside and the outside | the charge is about a practice at the boundary: a foreign rite, a forbidden word, a neighbouring healer |
| inheritance and property | the heirs, the creditors, the fisc | goods, land, dowry, office | the accused is old or dying, and the confession attaints the estate |
| political use | a faction, a court, a rival institution | the enemy's office, alliance, or life | the charge arrives with a political calendar, and the proof is a formulary |
| professional rivalry | the licensed practitioner against the unlicensed | the clientele and the licence | the accuser and the accused sell the same service, and the court is asked to rank them |

Two cautions. First, a **functional explanation is not a motive**: saying that an accusation maintained a boundary does not mean the accuser intended to maintain it, and a worldbuilder who writes the function as the character's plan produces a conspiracy, not an institution. Second, the **property function is the one most often left out** and the one that most changes the design: if a conviction attaints the estate, the accused's heirs, creditors, and neighbours all have a stake in the verdict, and the slander suit becomes a property suit in disguise. Write the return rule (§3, property anchor) and the institution will have an economy.

## 11. Contested frameworks

- **The wave as a single phenomenon.** The 1911 survey's three-period scheme (doubt, scholastic fusion, persecution) is a period synthesis, not a law; regional and chronological variation is the rule. Use the scheme as a design timeline, not as a historical claim.
- **Functionalist explanations.** "Social strain", "scapegoat", and "boundary maintenance" explain why a charge is *plausible*; they do not explain why it is *brought* in a particular case. Treat them as prompts, and never as a predictive law.
- **The learned/popular split.** The 1911 article's distinction between learned demonology and popular belief is its own framing; later scholarship complicates it. A fictional culture can run the split, but the entry should mark it as a model.
- **The numbers.** The totals vary from 100,000 to several millions in the period literature; the range is the finding. Never present a figure as a census.
- **Retro-diagnosis.** Psychiatric categories applied backwards to accused persons or to communities are discredited as explanations; the "psychosis" labels in the older literature are part of the history of the charge, not a diagnosis. Never diagnose a person or a community in a worldbuilding text.
- **The entry's own status.** This is a design synthesis for fiction, written from published scholarship and period sources; it is not a history of any region and not a claim about any living practice.

## 12. Build order

1. **Write the harm-word and the charge-word** (§4). Decide whether the charge-word is gender-neutral, and whether a female derivation exists. Write the colexification choices down; they will constrain every later scene.
2. **Write the docket** (§2, row 1): three harms that may be charged, one that may not, and the 12-month window. Put the window in the law's own words.
3. **Write the standing ladder** (§2, row 2): the injured party, the household head, the bond-bearing third party, the commissioned office-holder. Fix the bond's size against the cost of a trial.
4. **Write the evidence floor** (§2, row 3; §5): the minimum combination, the dismissal-with-costs rule, and the record of failed charges.
5. **Write the proof ladder and the ceiling** (§2, row 4; §6): which proof for which price, who reads it, what the reader is paid, and what the record keeps.
6. **Write the confession rule** (§2, row 5; §7): corroboration, ratification interval, scope, custody, and the property effect.
7. **Write the slander suit** (§8): the four elements, the damages measure, the separate bench, and the exclusions.
8. **Write the commission and its brake** (§2, row 6; §9): term, fee, liability, and the audit.
9. **Write the record** (§7, §9): who keeps it, who may copy it, what it must contain, and what it is allowed to leave out. The record is the institution's memory and the next wave's evidence.
10. **Play one case through** (§13): a single accusation from complaint to exit, using every lever, and write down which lever produced which turn.
11. **Test the failure modes**: a charge with no date; a third-party accuser who cannot pay the bond; a confession with no corroboration; a finder who reaches his twentieth case; an accused who sues and wins. If any of the five breaks the institution, the lever is set wrong.

## 13. Worked example (NON-CANON)

The town of Vered lives on salt. Its brine-boilers work the pans at the river mouth, its menders sell cures at the market, and its reader — the *ithun* — is licensed by the town council to read the flight of the estuary birds for the fishing calendar and, in a bad year, for the cause of a run of fevers.

In the spring of the ninth year, three children in the lower lane fall ill in a week. The reader is asked for a reading and names the cause: an unseen working, *kavut*, done against the lane. The council's docket admits the case because the harm is nameable (the fevers), dated (the week), and attached to a person (the reader's own rival, a mender named Sava, who had lately taken the reader's clients). It enters at the *defamed* grade — the grade that acts on reputation without proof of the act — and the council orders Sava to find two sureties.

Sava refuses the sureties and demands a hearing on the act, because a defamed man who accepts the grade carries the name. The act grade has an evidence floor, and the floor is not met: the reader has one witness (a neighbour who heard Sava say a harsh word about the reader's fees) and no trace. The council dismisses the act charge with costs, records the dismissal, and vacates the surety order; the reader's fee is returned to the lane. The dismissal is the first turn: the record of the failed charge is now the town's defence against the next reading.

Sava's brother, a boatman, sues the reader for defamation. The suit meets its four elements: the charge was false (the dismissal), it was published (the lane heard it), malice is presumed because the reader was Sava's professional rival, and the damage is priced (Sava's lost clients, the bond, the costs). The council, sitting without the magistrate who heard the charge, awards damages against the reader. The reader's licence is suspended for a season, and the council writes two rules into the town book: a third-party accuser must post a bond of twelve, and a charge may not pass from the defamed grade to the act grade without a trace or two concurring witnesses.

In the tenth year the fevers return, and a travelling finder arrives with a commission from the provincial court and the customary fee per conviction. The council's new rules meet him: the commission's term is six months, the fee is commuted to a salary, and the finder's names must be corroborated before they enter the record. The finder finds one case in the lane, is paid his salary, and leaves; the wave does not come. The town's record of the two years — the dismissal, the slander award, the two rules, the finder's one case — is the institution's real product, and the next reader who wants a charge must read it first.

Every turn in the example is a lever: the docket's date window, the standing bond, the evidence floor, the proof ceiling (no ordeal, because the charge was never capital), the slander suit, the separate bench, the commission's salary and term, and the record's custody. The example decides nothing about any world; it demonstrates the levers in the order §12 builds them.
