## 1 Calibration key

Every framework below carries a status and a reason, in the lane's vocabulary: **mainstream** (the field's working notation), **contested** (used but disputed as a universal claim), **discredited** (defeated as an empirical claim, usable only as a generator), plus three scope statuses this entry needs because most of its carriers are not research results: **taught method** (a syllabus records what a course teaches, not what the field has demonstrated), **proposal** (one author's model, stated as a proposal in the paper), and **tier-C vocabulary** (a practitioner's word, recorded as vocabulary and never as support for a claim). The statuses are this entry's calibration and not the sources' self-description; the reasons are drawn from the row's own tier and scope cells. A status is corrected by a superseding row, never by an edit in place.

Two consequences hold for every section below. A row that a syllabus or a practitioner page carries can license a record layout and a vocabulary, and cannot license a claim about how narratives work in general. A row that a primary article carries can license the article's own statement at the page it names, and nothing the article does not say there.

## 2 The premise, at the article's own scope

The article opens with the sentence a worldbuilder can actually use as a premise: «Innombrables sont les récits du monde.» What follows in the same opening paragraph is the reason the premise is not decorative: the narrative is distributed across a prodigious variety of genres, themselves spread over different substances (articulated language, oral or written; image, fixed or moving; gesture, and ordered mixtures of these), and it is present in myth, legend, fable, tale, novella, epic, history, tragedy, drama, comedy, pantomime, the painted picture, stained glass, cinema, comics, the news item and conversation (S885). That list is the article's, and it is the design licence for the first table below: a culture's narrative is not its literature.

The article then names the instrument. It proposes to distinguish **three levels of description** in the narrative work: the level of « fonctions », in the sense the word has in Propp and in Bremond; the level of « actions », in the sense it has in Greimas when he speaks of characters as actants; and the level of « narration », which is roughly Todorov's level of « discours » (S885). It adds that the three are joined by a mode of **progressive integration**: a function has meaning only insofar as it takes its place in the general action of an actant, and that action itself receives meaning only at the level of narration (S885).

The levels are hierarchical in a stronger sense than a filing order. The article states that each level has its own units and its own correlations and so demands an independent description, that **no level can produce meaning on its own**, and that a unit belonging to one level takes meaning only if it can be integrated into a superior level (S885). It then turns the hierarchy into a reading instruction: an exhaustive sweep over the horizontal set of narrative relations must also go « vertically », because, in the article's words, 'le sens n'est pas « au bout » du récit, il le traverse' (S885). The article marks its own inventory as provisional and almost exclusively didactic in advantage, which is the calibration this entry carries for the premise.

The functional level is where the drafting decisions bite. The article's first classes for its units are **noyaux et catalyses, indices et informants**, and it says expressly that the names do not matter (S885). It adds two remarks that a build order cannot do without: a unit can belong to two classes at once, so certain units are **mixed** (its example is a whisky drunk in an airport hall, which catalyses the cardinal notation of waiting and at the same time indexes an atmosphere); and the four classes submit to a second distribution, in which catalyses, indices and informants are all **expansions** relative to the noyaux, since the noyaux form finite sets of few terms, governed by a logic, necessary and sufficient, while the other units fill that armature by a proliferation that is in principle infinite: « le récit est infiniment catalysable » (S885). Indices require an activity of deciphering; informants bring knowledge ready-made, and their functionality is weak like the catalyses' but not null, since an informant (the precise age of a character is the article's example) authenticates the reality of the referent and roots the fiction in the real, which the article calls a **realist operator** (S885).

| Level the article names | What a world bible stores there | Failure the level catches |
|---|---|---|
| fonctions (Propp, Bremond) | the event chain, split into noyaux (the finite, necessary armature) and the expansions that fill it | a plot whose turning points are never separated from its atmosphere |
| actions (Greimas's actants) | who wants, who sends, who helps, who blocks, per goal | a tale whose engine is an event rather than a desire |
| narration (roughly Todorov's discours) | the telling: order, duration, frequency, voice, level | a story with no teller, where every voice reads alike |

| Drafting question | Answer the article gives | What it forbids |
|---|---|---|
| may I write the events first and the telling later? | yes, but the levels integrate upward, so the telling must be able to carry the events it holds | a narration that adds nothing to the event chain |
| may one unit serve two functions? | yes: units can be mixed | a taxonomy that forces one slot per unit |
| how much detail belongs in the event layer? | the noyaux are few, finite and necessary; the rest is infinite expansion | treating the whole detail list as structure |

## 3 Story and discourse, in the Chinese syllabus

The syllabus draws the distinction that decides what a world bible holds, and it draws it as a one-line thesis in its second lecture: 「故事是一种深层的抽象结构，必须通过话语呈现」 — the story is a deep abstract structure and must be presented through discourse (S888). The lecture's remaining headings supply the working vocabulary: the story's properties are 整体性、转换性、可调节性 (wholeness, transformability, adjustability); the story is composed of events, and event narration divides into dynamic and static narration; the lecture separates 核心事件与催化事件, core events and catalysing events; and it closes on the principles by which events combine, and on the story-structure analyses of Greimas, Propp and Bremond (S888). The core/catalysing pair is the syllabus's own wording for the distinction the article draws at its functional level, and reading the two as the same distinction is this entry's reading, not a claim either page makes.

The third lecture turns to 行动元与人物, the actant and the character, and schedules 格雷马斯的「行动元」 beside Propp's character types, then asks how a reader builds a character and how characters are classified (S888). What the syllabus does is schedule the notation, not argue it: the row's scope is a curriculum, so this entry takes the term and not a theory of the actant from it.

| Question | Layer that answers it | What the world bible stores |
|---|---|---|
| what happened, in what causal order | 故事, the deep structure | the event chain, written without a teller |
| who tells it, in what order, at what speed, how often | 话语, the surface | one record per telling, with its choices |
| which telling is the true one in the world | neither layer | canon, which belongs to lore-shokunin |

## 4 The actantial model, scoped to the goal

The Chinese syllabus schedules the actant notation under its third lecture, and this entry takes only that much from it (S888).

The description-format paper states the roles in full and states the constraint that makes the model usable in a build order: the actant theory's **six kinds** — 送り手, 対象, 受け手, 助け手, 主体, 敵 — are the well-known classification of a character's role in a story, but such role classifications change **per goal**, so they are best recorded at the quest unit rather than at the character (S890). That is the difference between a character sheet and a role sheet, and it is the paper's own reason for the choice.

| Slot (paper's order) | Question it asks of a cast | Design note |
|---|---|---|
| 主体 subject | whose wanting moves this tale | the engine is a desire, not an event |
| 対象 object | what is wanted | a person, a thing or a state |
| 送り手 sender | what makes the wanting binding | the institution behind the quest, not the quest-giver's mood |
| 受け手 receiver | who the outcome belongs to | separated from the subject, this is where a culture states its idea of benefit |
| 助け手 helper | who makes it possible | an alliance, and therefore a politics |
| 敵 opponent | who blocks it | an obstacle with a reason, never a moral grade |

Because the slots are quest-scoped (S890), the same character can hold different slots in two quests of one story, and a culture whose 敵 slot is always the same social group has stated its boundary map in that pattern. The reading of the pattern is this entry's; the per-goal scoping is the paper's.

## 5 Time on three dimensions, and the question set for voice

The syllabus gives narrative time three dimensions rather than one: 次序, 时长 and 频率 — order, duration and frequency — and it subdivides order into 回叙 and 预叙, retrospection and prospection (S888). It keeps story time and text time apart from the start, as 本文时间和故事时间的区分, so the three dimensions are differences between two clocks and not a single sequence (S888).

| Dimension | Question it answers | What changes when the culture sets it |
|---|---|---|
| 次序 | is the telling in event order, or displaced forward or back | suspense and disclosure become design choices rather than accidents |
| 时长 | how much telling does an event receive | pacing, and which events the culture treats as weighty |
| 频率 | how often is an event told | ritual repetition, variant tradition, an official telling against a folk one |

The same syllabus carries the voice side as a question set rather than a doctrine: it defines 聚焦, divides it into 外部聚焦 and 内部聚焦, and then treats 叙述层次与声音 under 查特曼的六种参与者, the six participants of Chatman, before the division of narrative levels and the classification of narrators (S888). This entry uses the questions and not a reconstructed list, because the syllabus names the six participants without enumerating them.

| Question | Failure it catches |
|---|---|
| who speaks | a tale with no teller, where every voice reads alike |
| who sees | a report of events no participant could have witnessed |
| who is addressed | a telling with no audience, which is a draft rather than a telling |
| at which level | a telling inside a telling whose frames silently merge |
| who may shift mid-tale | a narrator whose knowledge changes without the culture licensing it |

## 6 The three-layer format, and where 起承転結 actually sits

The description-format paper proposes a way of describing a story as a hierarchical structure of three major layers, story, quest and scene, and states that the same three-layer structure lets it describe not only the plot's development but also the characters and the events in a unified format (S890). The paper's own introduction places the proposal against the classical analyses it borrows from: Propp's thirty-one elements, Greimas's six character types, and Genette's analysis of the mismatch between the order of telling and the chronology of the story (S890).

The layer definitions are precise enough to build on. The **scene**, the lowest layer, is a unit roughly corresponding to Propp's narrative function and is treated in earlier narratology as the minimal unit of the plot's development; but because a 起承転結 can complete inside a single scene, the proposed scene keeps the conventional division as its base and splits even within one place whenever the narrative function changes (S890). The **quest** is the unit corresponding to the minimal unit of a 起承転結 structure centred on a particular goal, mishap or obstacle (S890). A short story can consist of a single quest, while most existing works are described as several quests combined, and the combination of two quests can be sorted, with exceptions, into three relations: sequence, nesting and parallel (S890).

The paper then says what attaches at each layer. At the story layer: the world setting and the characters' attributes and basic human relations, which hold across the whole story. At the quest layer: the quest's goal, the means applied toward it, and the final result, including interruption and the case where no ending is described. At the scene layer: place, time and the situation of that scene (S890). Characters take the same three layers in the paper's third section: immutable history and basic attributes at the story layer, role relative to the goal at the quest layer, emotional state and evaluative attitudes and speech-and-action at the scene layer (S890). Events take them in the fourth section: causal relations between events at the story layer, basic plot patterns at the quest layer, and action-level patterns within each function at the scene layer (S890).

| Layer | What attaches there | Drafting question it forces |
|---|---|---|
| ストーリー | world setting, character attributes, basic relations, event causality | what is this work, in one record a machine can read |
| クエスト | goal, means, result, the per-goal role of each character | where does this arc begin and close, and what counts as its completion |
| シーン | place, time, situation, speech and action, emotional state | what changes in this unit, and who is present |

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| place 起承転結 at the quest layer, where the paper places it | stretch 起承転結 over a whole work as a four-beat template |
| split a scene when its function changes, even in one place | split scenes by location alone |
| record a character's role per quest | file one role per character for the whole story |
| state the two-quest relation (sequence, nesting, parallel) | leave a multi-quest story as an unordered list |

The paper states its own limit: the individual parts of the framework have been checked against analyses of existing works, but the usefulness and validity of the structure as a whole are to be tested as empirical analyses accumulate (S890). That is why this entry carries the format as a **proposal**, not as a result, and why the format is offered here as a description schema rather than as a claim about how stories are.

## 7 The generation side, and the cultural-skew argument

The generation paper presents a research area it calls the narratology of narrative generation, covering the humanities (narratology), the information sciences including artificial intelligence and cognitive science, and the social sciences including economics, and it calls the same area post-narratology in the sense that it shows a vision of a novel narratology that uses narrative generation machines (S889). The author's own framework inside that programme is the multiple-narrative-structure model, and the paper also presents an information-narratology model that makes the sender–receiver communication structure explicit (S889). Its definition of the story is the one a schema needs: the story is the temporal series or chain of events, and an event is the unit of happening that changes some state (S889).

The paper's cultural argument is the part this entry most needs, because it is the reason a Chinese and a Japanese half sit beside the Western article rather than under it. The paper states that narrative is a universal human phenomenon and that narratology focused on that universality, but that, like many other disciplines, it carries a distortion or twist of Western origin; against that, the paper sets a culture-focused approach and finds theoretical narrative texts in Japan's own tradition, naming among others the reading of the Tale of Genji as history against narrative, Zeami's treatises on nō, and the theory of the jōruri structure (S889). The catalogue is the paper's, and it is the licence this entry takes for treating the Chinese and Japanese material as a second build order rather than as a translation.

The paper also supplies the check a schema designer needs on genre. It lists the four components commonly accepted in narratology — the story or the world to be narrated, the deep level of narrative discourse, the surface level of that discourse, and the narrator (sender) together with the narratee (receiver) — and it observes that a narrative's genre is in most cases defined more concretely at the level of narrative discourse than at the level of story: the same murder story becomes a detective novel or a psychological novel depending on its discoursing or plotting (S889). It credits Genette with the first systematic study of narrative discourse (S889).

| Side | Question it answers | What a worldbuilder does with it |
|---|---|---|
| reading side | what does this telling do to its audience | audits a finished tale |
| making side | by what procedure does this culture produce a tale | records the procedure as an institution, with its stages and its gatekeepers |
| genre | at which level is the genre fixed | files genre with the telling, not with the event chain |

## 8 世界观 as a rule system, at tier C

The practitioner section opens with the figure the term belongs to: the author likes to imagine writing a novel as inviting the reader into a fantasy house he has built, and the key that opens the house is 世界观 (S893). It then gives the working definition: in a web novel, 世界观 refers to the way the ten thousand things of the story's setting operate, the scene and the rules in which the story happens — rules that differ from those of the real world we live in, and it is precisely because of those rules that stories beyond imagining can happen (S893). It closes the argument on innovation: the innovation of a world view is in essence the innovation of the basic rules that constitute the novel's world, and once the rules are innovated the story's background and human environment change with them (S893).

That is a rule-system reading of the term rather than a props list, and it is exactly the reading a world bible can store. The status is **tier-C vocabulary**: an industry outlet, one author's account of his own practice, and never sole support for an admitted claim. This entry borrows the word and the rule-system sense, and borrows no warrant from it.

| Borrow | Do not borrow |
|---|---|
| 世界观 as the operating rules of a setting | any claim that a rule-set is good because it is novel |
| innovation located in the basic rules | innovation located in the props |
| background and human environment as consequences of the rules | background as an independent decoration layer |

## 9 Decision sequence for specifying one culture's narrative

1. Fix the event chain first, as 故事, and write it with no teller (S888).
2. Mark the noyaux: the few events that are necessary and sufficient, and the logic that governs them (S885).
3. Attach the expansions: catalyses, indices and informants, accepting that one unit may serve two classes (S885).
4. Choose the telling, and record it as 话语, one record per telling (S888).
5. Set the three dimensions of time for that telling, and mark the exceptions by genre (S888).
6. Answer the voice questions for that telling, level by level (S888).
7. Fill the actant slots per quest, not per character (S890).
8. Break the quests into scenes at function changes, and state each quest's relation to its neighbours (S890).
9. Decide whether the culture's own making procedure is part of the world; if it is, record it as an institution on the generation side (S889).
10. File the genre with the telling, and keep the event chain free of genre definitions (S889).

Steps 1 to 6 rest on the taught method of a syllabus and on one article's own levels; steps 7 and 8 rest on a proposal; step 9 rests on a research programme's self-description. None of the ten steps asserts anything about a real tradition.
