## 1. Campbell (1949) → Vogler (1992): the model and the compression

- Campbell's monomyth = 17 stages in 3 acts, assembled from Greek, Norse, Buddhist, Christian and psychoanalytic sources. The term "monomyth" is Campbell borrowing from Joyce's Finnegans Wake, not a scholarly coinage of his own.
- Vogler's 12 stages are a screenwriting compression (ordinary-world setup → ordeal midpoint → elixir payoff) with Campbell's mythology stripped off. Treat it as a modern literary genre convention, not comparative data.

| Act | Campbell stages (17) | Vogler stages (12) |
|---|---|---|
| Departure | call to adventure; refusal of the call; supernatural aid; crossing the first threshold; belly of the whale | 1 ordinary world; 2 call; 3 refusal; 4 mentor; 5 crossing the threshold |
| Initiation | road of trials; meeting with the goddess; woman as temptress; atonement with the father; apotheosis; ultimate boon | 6 tests, allies, enemies; 7 approach to the inmost cave; 8 ordeal; 9 reward |
| Return | refusal of the return; magic flight; rescue from without; crossing the return threshold; master of two worlds; freedom to live | 10 road back; 11 resurrection; 12 return with the elixir |

- Vogler maps roughly onto film acts: 1–5 act one, 6–9 act two, 10–12 act three. The load-bearing beats are the mentor's gift, threshold guardian, ordeal-as-symbolic-death, and the elixir (the hero must bring back value or the return has no dramatic point).
- Critique, stated plainly: (a) circular method — Campbell conflated already-similar tales, so the model "fits" by selection; most folklorists do not accept it as ethnographic description of any real tradition (Segal 1990/1999); (b) no single actual telling contains all 17 stages — the model is an average of selected tellings; (c) male-centric — Campbell himself said women don't need to make the journey because "she's the place that people are trying to get to" (Cousineau 1990), the anchor of the feminist critique; (d) his academic standing is low while his screenwriting influence is total — for craft purposes, cite the monomyth as a *story engine*, never as "what ancient cultures believed".
- Counter-pattern worth knowing: Lawrence & Jewett's "American monomyth" — community in peril, a selfless anti-democratic hero arises from outside, destroys evil, restores community, and departs without successor (superhero, western, action film). Useful for designing a *modern-feeling* culture's civic religion; structurally the inverse of Campbell's hero (who returns changed and brings the elixir).

## 2. Rank (1909) and Raglan (1936): the hero-birth pattern family

- Rank's pattern (the "exposure legend"): illustrious or divinely-begot child; conception attended by prophecy/dream/oracle; threatened or exposed at birth; set adrift in water in a basket or box; saved by animals or humble folk; suckled by animal or humble woman; raised by foster parents ignorant of origin; as a youth departs, discovers the true parents, punishes the father (or is recognized), and attains rank. Rank read it psychoanalytically (birth trauma, family romance) — that mechanism is dead; the *pattern* survives as a real tale-family.
- Rank's exemplars, condensed:

| Figure | Threat/exposure | Raised by | Return & recognition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sargon of Akkad | mother the high priestess sets him afloat in bitumen-sealed reeds | Akki, drawer of water | Ishtar makes him king |
| Moses | basket of bulrushes among the Nile reeds | pharaoh's daughter, then Midian | returns, confronts pharaoh |
| Oedipus (Rank's centerpiece) | ankles pinned, exposed on Mt. Cithaeron | Polybus & Merope of Corinth | oracle sends him *toward* his true parents — inversion: raised by the distinguished family, humiliated as "not really" their son |
| Perseus | locked bronze chamber, exposed at sea in a chest with Danaë | fisherman Dictys of Seriphos | accidental killing of grandfather Acrisius |
| Paris | mother Hecabe dreams of a burning torch; exposed on Ida | shepherd Agelaos | recognized, returns to Troy |
| Romulus & Remus | basket on the flooded Tiber | she-wolf, then shepherd Faustulus | kill the usurper Amulius, found Rome |
| Cyrus (Herodotus 1) | Astyages' dream doom; infant ordered exposed | herdsman Mithradates | returns, deposes grandfather |
| Karna (Mahabharata) | Kunti bears him by Surya, sets him adrift on the river | charioteer couple | recognized by Kunti/Krishna at the assembly, dies opposing his brothers |

- Raglan's 22 points compress to four clusters: royal birth (royal virgin mother, king father, unusual conception, reputedly god's son) → disposal and hiding (attempt to kill at birth, spirited away, fostered far away, childhood untold) → the kingdom (returns, defeats king/giant/dragon/monster, marries the princess, becomes king, legislates) → the fall (loses favor, driven out, mysterious death often on a hill, no successor, unburied body, holy sepulchers). Raglan's own scoring: Oedipus 22/22, Theseus 20, Moses 20, Arthur 19, Romulus 19, Heracles 17, Jason 15 — while his contemporaries score near the bottom.
- Raglan's strong claim — hero myths are garbled year-king rituals — belongs to the myth-and-ritual school and is largely rejected; the scoring itself is elastic (any long biography scores 6–8 points by accident; require ~15+ before claiming the pattern family). Dundes (1980) applied the combined Rank–Raglan pattern to the life of Jesus — the instructive result: it fits hagiography too, which is exactly the point for worldbuilding. **The exposure-legend is a legitimation device for humble-origin, high-destiny rulers: decide *which institution in your world manufactures that score*, and the pattern becomes politics instead of psychology.**

## 3. Dragon-slaying (Chaoskampf) corpus: functions and the fossil formula

- Structural readings to build with: Gunkel's Chaoskampf (primordial combat of order vs watery chaos); Eliade — the dragon-slaying victory *is* a cosmogony, ritually repeated; Lincoln — the combat myth legitimizes present political order (the slayer's heirs inherit the victory); Watkins — Indo-Europeanist reconstruction of an inherited poetic formula "he slew the serpent" (from *serpent* + *slay*; clearest reflex Vedic *ahann áhim*, RV 1.32), traceable from Hittite (Illuyankas) through Greek, Old Norse and Old English. For this lane that formula is the coupling hook: **give every culture's founding combat a fossilized linguistic trace — a royal epithet, a name-compound, a fixed line in the founding recitation — and you get free era-dating and dynastic texture.** Armenian Vahagn's title "dragon-slayer" (Movses Khorenatsi) and dynastic titles like "serpent-slayer" are the model.

| Myth | Slayer | Adversary | What the victory produces |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enuma Elish | Marduk | Tiamat | cosmos split from her body; humans from Kingu's blood; Marduk's kingship charter |
| Ninurta–Asag (Lugal-e) | Ninurta | Asag and the stone army | ordered landscape — mountains, dams, irrigation, agriculture |
| Rigveda 1.32 | Indra | Vṛtra | released waters; note the aftermath: Indra carries the sin of slaying |
| Book of Overthrowing Apophis | Ra (priests, ritually) | Apophis | nightly solar cycle maintained; execration ritual, calendrical not epic |
| Theogony 820–880 | Zeus | Typhoeus | secured cosmic sovereignty (variant: buried under Mt. Etna) |
| Homeric Hymn to Pythian Apollo | Apollo | Python/Delphyne | founds Delphi and the oracle |
| Hymiskviða / Völuspá | Thor | Jörmungandr | fishing episode; mutual death at Ragnarök — an *eschatological* dragon-slaying |
| Völsunga saga / Fáfnismál | Sigurd | Fáfnir | treasure + dragon-blood wisdom; Fáfnir is a *corrupted previous hero* — the dragon as moral failure of an earlier claimant |
| Beowulf | Beowulf | Grendel, his mother, the hoard-dragon | old-king death song; the hoard is cursed wealth |
| Kojiki | Susanoo | Yamata-no-Orochi (8 heads/tails) | sword Kusanagi from the tail → imperial regalia; bride Kushinada-hime |
| Golden Legend | St. George | the dragon of Silene | *taming* not slaying — dragon led by the princess' girdle; conversion of the city |
| Devimahatmya | Durga | Mahishasura the buffalo-demon | the gods' combined radiance; each god gifts her a weapon — canonical *female* dragon-slayer |
| Khorenatsi (Armenia) | Vahagn | dragon (višap) | ancestral royal epithet; fire-birth hymn |
| Russian byliny | Dobrynya Nikitich | Zmey Gorynych (3 heads) | rescue of captives; bogatyr-saint cycle |
| Fengshen Yanyi | Nezha | Ao Bing, dragon-king's son | rebellion vs water-power; self-sacrifice and rebirth from a lotus |

- Folklore layer: Thompson Motif-Index B11 (dragon; B11.1 fight with dragon); ATU 300 The Dragon-Slayer (youth slays dragon, rescues princess, **false hero claims the credit — the tongue-cut as hidden proof** is the type's signature courtroom motif); ATU 303 The Two Brothers (each brother slays a dragon, one is supplanted and killed, revived by the magic life-token; the twin-logic tale even for non-twin brothers).
- Function typology for assignment: cosmogonic charter (Marduk, Indra); calendrical/ritual maintenance (Apophis); temple/oracle foundation (Apollo); dynastic legitimation (Vahagn, Susanoo); coming-of-age quest (ATU 300); conversion legend (George); eschaton (Thor); hoard-greed cautionary tale (Fáfnir, Beowulf's dragon). One culture can run several in parallel with *different* institutions owning each.

## 4. Female-hero variants — and the forced-fitting critique in both directions

- The documented models: Murdock (1990), a Campbell student, proposes the heroine's journey — condensed to five functional moves: separation from the feminine; success in the masculine world (trials, allies, boons); spiritual aridity/meaninglessness; reunion with the feminine (meeting the mother); healing the wounded masculine and integrating both. Hudson's Virgin's Promise (2010) runs a different arc — creative/spiritual/erotic awakening against a dependent world, ending in showing the gift to the community rather than bringing back an elixir (paraphrase; her beat names to-verify). Frankel (2010) assembles world folklore heroines into a Campbell-adjacent arc.
- The calibration, run hard in both directions: (a) Applying Campbell to heroine tales erases what heroines actually do — mythic women's agency is usually relational, ritual or narrative (Isis reassembles Osiris and conceives Horus: Plutarch; Scheherazade survives by narrating; Inanna's descent is a power-grab stripped of her me and revived by Enki's creatures, not a self-rescuing quest: Wolkstein & Kramer 1983; Perera 1981 reads it Jungianly). These tales have no refusal/no mentor/no elixir beats at all. (b) The heroine-journey models mirror Campbell's universalism and import their own essentialism — Murdock's arc *presupposes* a patriarchal collision course; folklore heroines cluster instead in descent/service/bride tale-types (ATU 425C Beauty and the Beast, 480 The Kind and the Unkind Girls, 510A Cinderella/Donkeyskin) that fit neither model.
- Craft rule that follows: **choose the arc family by tale economics, not by protagonist gender.** Durga is a full dragon-slayer with weapon-gift iconography and praise-epithets; Atalanta is a hunt-and-race hero; Nüwa repairs the sky with five-colored stones; St. Martha tames the Tarasque (Golden Legend). A single culture can host a slayer arc, a reassembly arc and a service arc for women simultaneously — assigning one "female arc" to a whole culture repeats Campbell's category error in mirror image. For the world bible, record *who edited the heroine's tale and why* — recasting a warrior goddess as a virtuous bride (or the reverse) is exactly the kind of editorial history lore-shokunin can canonize.

## 5. Hero twins: Maya, Haudenosaunee, Indo-European, rival founders

- **Maya — Hunahpu and Xbalanque** (Popol Vuh, K'iche'; Tedlock 1985/1996; Christenson 2003): conceived when the skull of their dead father One Hunahpu, hanging in a calabash tree, spits into the hand of the maiden Xquic (a virgin-birth motif). They first out-trick their jealous half-brothers (monkey scribes, transformed), then annoy the lords of Xibalba into summoning them. Ordeal houses: survive the Dark House by substituting fireflies for glowing cigars; in the Bat House Hunahpu is decapitated (head swapped for a squash). Finally they *let themselves be killed* — bones ground and thrown in the river — and self-regenerate, then stage a traveling miracle show (burning a house and restoring it; sacrificing a dog and reviving it; cutting a man apart). The Xibalba lords demand the same treatment: the twins kill One Death and Seven Death and **do not revive them**. They rise as sun and moon (Tedlock's reading; others read sun and Venus — see Calibration). Function: the trickster-resurrection pair; death defeated by cooperation and staged performance; Classic-period iconography ties the pair to the Maize God's resurrection cycle (Miller & Taube 1993).
- **Haudenosaunee — Sapling (Good Mind) and Flint (Tawiskaron)** (Seneca tellings in Parker 1923 and Curtin & Hewitt 1918): Sky Woman (Ataensic) falls to the water world and earth is grown on Turtle's back; her daughter bears twins — Flint cuts his way out and kills their mother, Sapling is born normally; the grandmother favors Flint. The twins *create in opposition*: Sapling makes rivers that flow both ways, corn, berries, benign animals; Flint makes rapids, flint mountains, predators, obstacles. Sapling wins the final duel (deer-antler weapon in several tellings); Flint is banished through a hole in the ground or escapes west to keep causing harm — variant endings. Calibration caveat in §6: the crisp good-vs-evil framing may be post-contact editorial.
- **Indo-European divine twins** (Ward 1968): horsemen and saviors, one mortal one immortal who share immortality on alternate days (Dioscuri), dawn-brides (Vedic Aśvins and Sūryā), attested from the Alcis of Tacitus (Germania 43) to Hengist and Horsa. Function: rescue, fertility, maritime salvation — a *cooperative, soteriological* pair, the structural opposite of the rival founders.
- **Rival founders**: Romulus kills Remus over the city boundary — augury contest, then Remus transgresses the plowed sulcus (Livy 1.6–7; Plutarch, Romulus); Cain and Abel; Jacob and Esau. Function: the polis founded on fratricide; the lever is boundary-transgression.
- Typology table:

| Twin system | Relation | Cultural function | Dramatic lever |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maya Hero Twins | complementary tricksters | death defeated by performance | staged death / refusal to revive |
| Sapling & Flint | duality-creation, opposed | world needs both (vs moralized evil) | one twin demonized by editors |
| IE Divine Twins | cooperative saviors | rescue, fertility, alternating mortality | mortality-sharing pact |
| Rival founders | fratricidal rivals | political foundation | boundary-transgression crime |
| ATU 303 brothers | mirrored slayers | loyalty proven through substitution | false-credit proof motif |

- Craft note: twin systems give built-in symmetry — mirror the chosen axis in kinship terminology (paired kin terms), naming (alliterative or inversion pair-names), costume (twin masks, antler headdress motifs echoing the duel), and cosmology (twin constellations or sun/moon apotheosis).

## 6. Build order: applying this to a fictional culture

1. Assign the pattern family from political economy, not from the protagonist: canal/delta state → Chaoskampf founding (cosmos split from a slain body); sacred-kingship agrarian polity → Rank–Raglan exposure legend as dynastic legitimation; steppe/pastoral → inherited slaying-formula and cattle-raid quests; consensus confederacy → twin-duality creation with a contested moralization debate; oral performance culture → the pattern is the singer's beat-sheet, so variants coexist (couples orality-formulaic-language).
2. Fossilize the combat in language: a "serpent-slayer" royal title inherited by kings who never slew anything; a fixed formulaic line in the founding recitation; the title's oldest attestation becomes your internal dating evidence (couples historical-linguistics).
3. Score the founder on Raglan's 22, then decide *diegetic authorship* — which institution curates the score (temple chroniclers, court bards, a rival city's satire). Low-scoring hagiography of a real ruler is itself a story.
4. Plant a false-hero scene (ATU 300 tongue-proof) somewhere in the founding cycle — gives you a courtroom plot and a lesson that mythic credit is policed.
5. For heroines, pick arc families per tale and record their editorial history; give slayer-women weapon-gift iconography and praise-epithets (Durga pattern), and let at least one in-world scribe complain that the tale "doesn't fit the ancient pattern."
6. For twins, fix the axis (cooperation vs duality vs rivalry), then demonize or redeem one twin *deliberately* — and make the demonization traceable to an in-world institution (see §5, Haudenosaunee caveat).
7. Keep the emic/etic rule from comparative-frameworks.md §1: the culture never "has the monomyth" — it has a local beat-sheet; the monomyth, Rank and Murdock models are *your* tools, generation engines only.
8. Transcribe two or three in-world variants of the founding tale (court version, village version, rival city's version) — divergence at the edges with stability at the core is what real transmission looks like (Rubin's memory findings, couples orality file).

## 7. Calibration: what is contested or discredited

- **Campbell**: selection-circularity critique stands; the model is not accepted as description of any real tradition; academic reception low, screenwriting reception total. The Campbell controversy (Gill, NYRB 1989, on politics and alleged antisemitism) is documented — relevant if the world bible cites him as an in-world scholar.
- **Rank**: psychoanalytic mechanism (birth trauma, Oedipal reading) rejected; pattern survives as a tale-family observation. Note Rank's set includes historical figures (Sargon, Cyrus, Moses, Jesus) — for those, the "pattern" is hagiographic legitimation, which is usable material even though the psychology is dead.
- **Raglan**: the myth-and-ritual strong reading (hero myth = year-king rite) is largely rejected; scores are elastic and never descriptive of a life, only of a curated biography.
- **Heroine models**: Murdock is a mirror-model with its own essentialism; Hudson/Frankel are craft tools, not comparative findings; most folklore heroines live in tale-types (ATU 425C/480/510A) that fit neither arc.
- **Popol Vuh ending**: sun-and-moon (Tedlock) vs sun-and-Venus readings of the twins' apotheosis are contested; pick one per your world's astronomy and note the other as a scholastic dispute in-world.
- **Haudenosaunee twins**: all recorded versions are post-contact (17th c. onward); the sharp good/evil framing plausibly reflects Handsome Lake-era and Christian moralization (Wallace 1969; Fenton 1998). Earlier tellings may have treated Flint's harsh creations as necessary. Use "Flint as devil" only with that caveat attached.
- **Enuma Elish**: composition/redaction date contested (Old Babylonian vs Kassite/Neo-Assyrian phases); the combat-then-creation sequence is secure in the text regardless.
- **Faulkner 1933 (Bremner-Rhind), Richter & Lieberman 2004 (Rank translation), Schmidt 2001 chapter details**: flagged to-verify above; the underlying texts and claims are standard.
