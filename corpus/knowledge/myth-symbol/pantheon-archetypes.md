## 1. Craft premise: a pantheon is a functional map of a society

- Jacobsen (1976) reads the Sumerian pantheon as personified institutional powers — An as the authority of the assembly, Enlil as executive force, Inanna as the storehouse-and-eros power — with the divine assembly (Sumerian ukkin, Akkadian puhru) projecting real assembly politics. Generalize: gods cluster around the functions a society depends on but cannot control (rain, harvest, war outcomes, legitimacy, death).
- One-god-one-domain is a modern handbook artifact. Real gods are polymorphic and inconsistent: Versnel (1990/93/2011) and Vernant (1974) show gods carrying bundled, even contradictory domains. Design implication: build **epithet-gods**, not slot-gods.
- Keep two registers apart when designing: **emic** role labels (a god's own cult titles, festival, taboo) vs **etic** functional labels (the scholar's taxonomy). Fiction that talks only in etic labels ("she is the goddess of love") reads like a game wiki, not a religion.

## 2. Typology of recurrent roles (real exemplars, watch-outs)

| Role (etic label) | ANE exemplars | Indo-European exemplars | Other exemplars | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sky father | An (Sumer, remote); El "father" (Ugarit) | Dyauṣ Pitā (RV); Zeus patḗr; Iūpiter (< *Dyeu-pəter); Deipáturos (reported Illyrian, in Hesychius) | Rangi + Papa (Māori); Tian/Shangdi (China) | archetype worldwide ≠ PIE inheritance; office and name can split (see §5) |
| Earth mother | Ki / Ninhursag (Sumer); **inversion**: Geb male earth, Nut female sky (Egypt) | Gaia; Terra Mater; Nerthus (Tacitus, Germania 40); Jörð (Norse, marginal) | Pachamama (Andes); Papa (Māori) | "earth mother" often means the farmer's grain goddess (Demeter, Ceres), not earth-as-such; gender assignment is not fixed |
| Storm/weather | Baal Hadad (Ugarit); Adad (Akkad); Teshub (Hurrian); Tarḫunna/Tarḫunz (Hittite/Luwian) | Indra (RV); Perkūnas (Lith.); Perunъ (Slavic); Þórr (also the farmer's hall-god) | Susanoo (Japan — storm AND trickster); Tlaloc (Aztec rain/fertility, not war) | storm god is either king-maker (Baal, Teshub) or power behind the throne; ties to the dragon-slaying charter (§3) |
| Sun | Ra (fused with Atum/Amun); Shamash/Utu (sun + justice; Hammurabi stele) | Sūrya; Helios (minor in classical Greece; Sol Invictus state cult, Aurelian 274 CE) | **Amaterasu (female)**; **Sól (female, Norse)**; Tonatiuh (Aztec); Inti (Inca) | the sun's rank varies wildly: supreme in Egypt, negligible in early Rome and Norse; gender varies both ways |
| Moon | Sin/Nanna (Ur); Mēn (Phrygia) | Máni (Norse) | Tsukuyomi (male, Japan); Selene/Artemis (Greece, female) | in lunar-calendar cultures the moon god is a time-keeper and scribe-adjacent power, not a "romance" deity |
| Culture hero / trickster | Gilgamesh (king-hero); Adapa/Oannes (arts-giver) | Prometheus (fire-theft); Manu (first man/lawgiver); Okuninushi (Izumo land-builder); Loki (ambivalent) | Māui (Polynesia); Raven (Pacific NW); Coyote (N. America); Anansi (Akan) | Thompson A500–A599; the two commonest labors are fire-theft and flood-survival; see §6 on hero-pattern frameworks |
| Smith | Ptah (Memphis; creates by heart-and-tongue, Shabaka Stone); Kothar-wa-Hasis (Ugarit craft-god) | Hephaistos (lame); Völundr/Wayland (Völundarkviða); Ilmarinen (Kalevala) | Ogun (Yoruba: iron + war + path-clearing); Kanayago-kami (Japanese tatara iron folk cult) | smith = **liminal caste deity**: lame/blind/one-eyed, endogamous, feared-sacred (Eliade 1956; McNaughton 1988); fire+war+magic cluster |
| Death | Ereshkigal (queen of the dead, ETCSL 1.4.1); Nergal (plague/underworld); Osiris (judge; Book of the Dead ch. 125); Mictlantecuhtli (Aztec); Erlik (Turkic-Mongolic) | Yama — **first mortal to die**, then judge (RV 10.14); Hades (dead + wealth, not evil); Hel (half-corpse) | Izanami (Japan: dies bearing the fire god Kagutsuchi — death enters via childbirth) | NO reconstructed PIE death god; god of the dead ≠ god of death ≠ devil — a devil requires dualist soteriology (Zoroastrian → Christian import) |
| Love | Inanna/Ishtar (**love AND war**; Enheduanna's Nin-me-šara); Astarte; Hathor | Aphrodite (cult ties to the Levant via Cyprus commonly argued); Freyja (love AND war: Fólkvangr takes half the slain, Grímnismál) | Xochiquetzal (Aztec, flowers/love) | the Aphrodite-vs-Ares split is Greek-local; the cross-cultural constant is **eros–violence fusion**, often in ONE deity |
| War | Ninurta (royal campaigner, also agrarian); Huitzilopochtli (war + sun of migration) | Ares (despised: Iliad 5.890); Mars (**agrarian first**: Cato, De agri cultura 141; Matronalia 1 March); Indra; Óðinn (ecstasy-war, not battle-discipline) | Hachiman (deified Emperor Ōjin, samurai patron); Guan Yu (deified general); Kartikeya | war gods are politically coded: fear of berserk force (Ares) vs discipline of the citizen levy (Mars); "war god" usually means "god who legitimizes kingship by victory" |

Cluster notes:
- The love/war pairing has three real configurations: fused in one deity (Inanna, Freyja, Ogun), a married/sibling pair (Aphrodite–Ares, Odyssey 8 net-scene), or opposed moral poles (Mars civic vs Ares berserk). Pick per society, don't default to "love goddess + war god".
- The smith god is the best vector for caste material: endogamous smith castes with sacred dread, lameness/blindness as initiatory mark, fire as restricted power (Mande blacksmiths, McNaughton 1988).
- Culture heroes overlap with ancestors and kings; where historical founders get deified (Hachiman, Guan Yu, Romulus→Quirīnus), culture hero and war god collapse together — a later-era signature.

## 3. Indo-European comparanda

- **\*Dyēus ph₂tēr** "Sky Father": an inherited *formula*, not just a word — Greek Zeùs patḗr, Vedic Dyauṣ Pitā, Latin Iūpiter (< *Dyeu-pəter), plus Deipáturos reported for Illyrian (Hesychius) (West 2007; Mallory & Adams 2006). Note the formula "sky father / earth mother" as verse-pair recurs beyond IE (Vedic sky-father/earth-mother lines; Māori Rangi–Papa), so the archetype ≠ the reconstruction. Craft: a theonym that is a transparent compound ("Father Sky") reads as an archaic layer; opaque or taboo-substituted names read as later or reverent layers.
- **\*H₂éwsōs** dawn goddess: Vedic Uṣás, Greek Ēṓs, Latin Aurōra, Lithuanian Aušra/Aušrinė — a solid reconstruction. One step shakier: Germanic *Austrōn, which rests on Bede's single notice of Eostre (De temporum ratione, 725) — contested. Craft: dawn's slot is daily renewal plus dangerous eroticism (Ēōs' serial mortal lovers, Tithonus/Cephalus); low cult rank, high poetic rank.
- **\*Perkʷūnos** thunder god: Vedic Párjanya (RV 5.83), Lithuanian Perkūnas, Latvian Pērkons, Slavic Perunъ — but the Germanic reflex is problematic: Fjörgyn is FEMALE (mother of Þórr), Fjörgynn male (father of Frigg); the form and the oak-link (Latin quercus) are debated (West 2007; Mallory & Adams 2006). Craft: the thunder weapon varies — vajra/mace, hammer, axe, bolt — and is a perfect tech/era marker in costume.
- **Dragon-slaying formula** (Watkins 1995): an inherited poetic formula "hero slew the serpent" (*gʷʰen- "strike/slay" + *h₂n̥gʷʰis "serpent"): Indra–Vṛtra (RV 1.32), Zeus–Typhon (Theogony 820ff), Þórr–Jörmungandr; and the West-Asiatic Chaoskampf: Marduk–Tiamat (Enūma eliš), Baal–Yam/Lotan (KTU 1.1–1.6), Teshub–Illuyanka (CTH 321), Ninurta–Asag. Craft: this is the **charter of kingship** — the slayer is enthroned (Marduk's assembly grants kingship; Enūma eliš recited at the Akitu new year). Whether the IE lines inherit or borrowed from the ANE stream is contested; treat both, cite West (1997) for the borrowing channels.
- **Succession myth**: Uranus–Cronus–Zeus (Hesiod) mirrors Anu–Kumarbi–Teshub (Hurrian-Hittite) — widely read as borrowing, not common inheritance (West 1997). Craft: divine dynasties encode real dynastic politics; write them as court history with gods.

## 4. Dumézil's three functions — the framework, then the calibration

| Function | Vedic | Rome | Norse (Dumézil's mapping) |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 sovereignty, split into juridical + magico-terrible poles | Mitra (contract) + Varuṇa (magical bond) | Iūpiter; flamen Dialis; Dius Fidius as contractual pole | Óðinn (ecstatic-magical) + Týr (law/pledge) |
| F2 physical force / war | Indra (+ Maruts) | Mars (+ flamen Martialis) | Þórr |
| F3 fertility, production, sensuality | Nāsatyas/Aśvins | Quirīnus (+ flamen Quirinalis; "Sabine" third-function folk) | Vanir: Njörðr, Freyr, Freyja |

- Signature themes worth mining: the two-faced sovereignty (Mitra-Varuna, 1940); the "sin of the warrior" cycle — Indra's slaying of Vṛtra as brahmin-murder echoing into Starkaðr's triple crime (Dumézil 1968); Rome as a first-function project absorbing third-function Sabines.
- **Calibration (use the grid, not the doctrine):**
  - Method audit: Belier (1991) documents selective alignment, uneven source quality across the three corpora, and regroupings that cannot be falsified. Mid-century reception (Littleton 1966–82) treated it as near-proven; nobody serious does now.
  - Roman evidence is literary reconstruction, not cult documentation — Momigliano's critique (1984).
  - The Anatolian (Hittite) branch fits the scheme poorly — a real problem for a *PIE*-religion claim.
  - Lincoln (1999) historicizes the theory inside its interwar European context while keeping Dumézil's philology usable; his own Myth, Cosmos, and Society (1986) models the more defensible reconstruction style.
  - The 1980s political controversy (fascism allegations in the New York Review of Books exchange, 1985–86; Eribon 1992) is history of scholarship — track it, don't litigate it in canon.
  - Lane usage rule: the trifunctional grid is a strong **generator and stress-test** for fictional pantheons and a good lens on function-marked names (Óðinn/Týr as split sovereignty, Indra vs Varuṇa); it is NOT evidence that any real pantheon descends from three offices. Never hand lore-shokunin a "trifunctional = PIE religion" claim as fact.

## 5. How functional roles actually distribute (empirics to imitate)

- **Slots go unfilled or merged.** Greek has no thunder god distinct from Zeus; early Rome has no serious sun cult until Aurelian's Sol Invictus (274 CE); the Norse "earth mother" is a footnote (Jörð); no PIE death god reconstructs. A pantheon that fills every archetype checklist row reads as fake. Leave slots empty, or fuse two roles into one god.
- **The "creator" slot is unstable.** Zeus creates nothing; Norse creation is Óðinn and brothers killing Ymir; Egypt shifts creation among Atum, Ptah, Amun by theology; Japan's Kojiki (712) opens with abstract cosmogonic deities (Ame-no-Minakanushi) who then do nothing. Ptah's heart-and-tongue creation (Shabaka Stone) is the great word-creation charter — couple to the language files for kotodama-style systems.
- **Gods have careers.** Demyotion is normal: Dyaus fades to a poetic figure in India while the office of king passes to Indra/Varuṇa, yet the *name* keeps the kingship in Greece (Zeus); Tīwaz → Týr shrinks as Óðinn rises; Marduk receives the fifty names and assumes Enlil's offices (Enūma eliš VI–VII). Rises too: Amun's ascent; deified humans (Hachiman = Emperor Ōjin; Guan Yu; Romulus → Quirīnus). A pantheon with no mobility is a board game, not a history.
- **Names are titles.** Baal = "the lord", with many local Baalim; Zeus Polieus vs Zeus Agoraios; Amun-Ra fusion; epithet-theology is the real god-data. This is the deepest couple with orality-formulaic-language: singers keep the epithets, theologians keep the domains.
- **Contact works by interpretatio, not translation.** Caesar (BG 6.17) maps Gaulish gods onto Mercury/Mars/Jupiter/Apollo/Minerva; Tacitus does the same for Germans. Craft: when two of your cultures meet, map gods by ritual function, let the mapping distort, and let the distortions accumulate as historical evidence of contact.
- **Gender is a parameter, not a default.** Female sun (Amaterasu, Sól), male moon (Tsukuyomi, Sin), male earth (Geb); female war-love (Inanna, Freyja); one god bundling war + poetry + magic + the hanged self-sacrifice (Óðinn, Hávamál 138–139). Assign gender from your cosmogony's logic, then check the whole table for accidental sameness.
- **War theology tracks army type.** Ares = fear of unaccountable force; Mars = citizen levy and agrarian lustration (Cato 141); Ninurta = royal campaigner; Huitzilopochtli = migration war; Hachiman = professional warrior estate. Choose the war god by how your society fights, not by a slot.
- **Ritual address test:** every god a culture actually worships has ≥3 epithets, a festival, a taboo, an offering, and a priestly address-formula. If a god has only a myth and no ritual address, it is a logo, not a god — either give it cult or demote it to story.

## 6. Discredited or shaky frameworks (calibration bank — say no plainly)

- **Solar mythology** (Müller 1856: every myth a sun allegory, "disease of language"): dead by 1900; Lang (1884) did the demolition. Lesson: single-cause decoding of all myth fails; avoid fictional cultures whose priests do this unironically *unless* you want them to be wrong in-world.
- **Panbabylonism** (c. 1900 astral-everything): collapsed. Residue that survived: concrete borrowing studies à la West (1997).
- **Universal Great Goddess / matriarchal Old Europe** (Gimbutas 1991): rejected as prehistory by mainstream archaeology (Hutton 1991; Meskell 1995). Keep separate from Gimbutas' steppe/Kurgan homeland model, which survives in modified form and now has aDNA support (Haak 2015) — the genetics say migration, not religion.
- **Dying-and-rising god** (Frazer): the category was largely dissolved by Smith (1982) — Osiris dies and *becomes* king of the dead, he does not seasonally rise. Mettinger (2001) salvages a narrow ANE set. Craft: describe each deity's own death-return mechanics; never use the category label in canon.
- **Jungian archetypes** as history: unfalsifiable; fine as design vocabulary for role-bundling, never as an in-world or meta causal explanation.
- **Hero monomyth** (Campbell 1949, building on Rank 1909 and Raglan 1936): a useful plot grammar, overgeneralized from a narrow corpus; folklorists prefer Thompson's Motif-Index for actual distribution. Use the monomyth as scaffold, cite motifs as evidence.
- **Witzel (2012) Laurasian mythology**: sweeping reconstruction of a global mythological ur-stratum; interesting, contested — do not treat as settled for anything.

## 7. Build protocol: a pantheon for a fictional culture

1. List the society's irreducible dependencies (rain, sea, iron, legitimacy, trade routes, death logistics). These become candidate **functions**, not gods yet.
2. Assign each function a god, fusing or leaving empty slots deliberately; keep 20–40% of the classic archetypes unfilled or merged for realism (§5).
3. Pick a configuration for love/war (fused deity, pair, or opposed poles) and a war theology that matches the army type (§2 notes, §5).
4. Split F1 if the society has real tension between law/contract and charismatic-magical authority; that split (Mitra-Varuna, Óðinn-Týr) is the cheapest deep structure available.
5. Give every worshipped god an **epithet system** (local, functional, poetic, taboo-substitute names) — derive morphology from the conlang's own roots, couple naming-systems + orality files.
6. Write one **charter myth** that legitimizes the current regime: dragon-slaying, succession, or conquest-of-the-old-gods; let its politics mirror real court history (§3).
7. Give the smith (or equivalent restricted craft) a liminal caste god: bodily mark, endogamy, sacred dread, fire/iron taboos.
8. Design the death architecture separately from morality: who keeps the dead, is there a judge (Yama, Osiris) or only a place (Hel, Yomi), and whether a devil exists at all — dualism is an imported soteriology, not a default.
9. Parameterize gender per god from cosmogony logic, then audit the table for accidental uniformity.
10. Give the pantheon a history: one demotion, one rise, one deified human, one syncretic fusion, each datable to an era event you can point to.
11. Script inter-cultural contact with interpretatio mappings, and keep the mappings wrong in characteristic ways.
12. Stress-test with the trifunctional grid and the ritual-address test (§4, §5); flag every remaining blank as a plot opportunity, not an omission.
