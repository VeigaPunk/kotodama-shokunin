# Comparative mythology frameworks: monomyth, eternal return, structuralism, formalism — four generators, four failure modes

- topic: The four canonical etic toolkits a worldbuilder uses to generate and stress-test a myth system — Campbell's monomyth (plot grammar), Eliade's sacred/profane ontology (hierophany, axis mundi, eternal return as ritual-ideology generator), Lévi-Straussian structuralism (mythemes, binary oppositions, canonical formula as myth-family generator), and Propp/Dundes formalism (function sequences and spheres of action as tale grammar) — each with its scholarly calibration, since three of the four are discredited as *empirical claims* while remaining excellent as *generation engines*

## 1. What these frameworks are — and the one rule that keeps them safe

- All four are **etic** tools: outside analytic lenses an observer applies to myth. None of them is what a myth-telling community knows itself to be doing. The standing rule for this lane: **generate with the framework, never ascribe the framework.** A fictional culture's priests may genuinely teach "all heroes die and return"; they may not be taught Propp.
- Frame-of-claim discipline: a statement about a fictional myth system is either **emic** (an in-world belief: "the Ossuari say every fire is the stolen fire") or **etic** (a designer note: "this myth was generated by the raw/cooked code"). Tag outputs with which they are, or lore-shokunin cannot use them (couples linguistic-anthropology).
- Bascom's (1965) taxonomy is the load-bearing partition, and it maps directly onto canon tiers:

| Form | Told as… | Setting | Protagonists | Canon tier for a world bible |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Myth | true and sacred | remote "that time" (illo tempore) | nonhuman — gods, ancestors-as-powers | tier 1: doctrinal canon |
| Legend | true and secular | the historical past | human | tier 2: contested historical record |
| Folktale | fiction ("once upon a time") | no fixed time | human or animal | tier 3: entertainment, unreliable lore |

- Bascom's four *functions* of folklore (1954) — entertainment, validation of culture, education, maintenance of conformity — are a cheap sanity check: every myth you generate should be answerable to "who repeats this, to whom, to make whom do what?"

## 2. Campbell's monomyth: the plot grammar everyone already knows

- Structure: 17 stages in three acts (Campbell 1949). Departure: call to adventure, refusal of the call, supernatural aid, crossing the first threshold, belly of the whale. Initiation: road of trials, meeting with the goddess, woman as temptress, atonement with the father, apotheosis, ultimate boon. Return: refusal of the return, magic flight, rescue from without, crossing the return threshold, master of two worlds, freedom to live.
- Campbell's own exemplars: Buddha (renunciation → awakening → teaching-return), Osiris (death, dismemberment, resurrection), Jesus (tomb as belly of the whale; ascension as apotheosis), Moses at Sinai, Jason's quest. The reading method is allegorical projection: every myth is re-told until it fits.
- Calibration — the universality claim is discredited: the monomyth is not a discovered empirical regularity but a synthesis of Frazer, Jung, and Joyce, built by selective reading (Segal 1999). Folklorists and anthropologists reject it as comparative method; Dundes treated it as a cautionary example. Its cross-cultural "fit" is retroactive: inconvenient stories are already excluded by what counts as a "hero".
- Known ideological artifacts, useful as *flavors* not laws: the **American monomyth** (Lawrence & Jewett 2002) — community Eden → threat → selfless masked redeemer → Eden restored, minus moral growth — is a specific pop-culture dialect, not a human constant; the **heroine's journey** (Murdock 1990) exposes the male-hero bias of the stage inventory.
- How to use as a generation tool: treat the 17 stages as a **dialect to be broken**, not a template to be filled. (1) Fix the culture's supreme value (honor, purity, reciprocity); (2) write the call-to-adventure as a temptation of that value; (3) run the stage table *and mark every stage you delete or reorder* — a culture whose heroes refuse the return, or whose boons are revoked, has just told you something structural about it. (4) Never generate more than one culture's hero-cycle from the unmodified table; two cultures sharing all 17 stages reads as design failure, not archetypal truth.
- Descendants worth tracking for tone-matching: Vogler (1992) for screen-plotted worlds; Harmon's story circle (8 beats) for episodic, cyclic storytelling cultures.

## 3. Eliade: hierophany and the eternal return — an ontology generator

- Core vocabulary (Eliade 1949, 1957, 1958): **hierophany** — an act of manifestation of the sacred in a profane object or moment (a tree, a stone, a threshold, a king's accession); **kratophany** — manifestation of power pure and simple, ambivalent and dangerous; **axis mundi** — the cosmic center-pole linking planes (Yggdrasil; the Delphic omphalos; a temple's foundation stone); **imago mundi** — the built structure as model of the cosmos (temple = world); **eternal return** — ritual reenactment of the primordial time, whereby each renewal repeats the cosmogony; **illud tempus** — "that time" of origins; **terror of history** — the doctrine that archaic humanity escapes profane time and its irreversibility by living in the repetition of origins.
- Eliade's running exemplars: the Babylonian New Year (akītu) festival reenacting Marduk's victory over Tiamat (Enūma Eliš) as a cosmogonic renewal; Australian Aboriginal sacred geography where every landmark is a dreaming ancestor's deed; foundation rites that re-create the world before building; the king as imago Dei renewing creation at accession.
- Calibration: Eliade is a historian of religions writing **phenomenology with theological commitments**, not testable social science; "archaic ontology" as a description of what premodern people believed is asserted more than demonstrated (Segal 1999; Strenski 1987). The Enūma Eliš–akītu performance link itself is contested in Assyriology (to-verify). His interwar Romanian far-right entanglements are documented and matter for anyone adopting his nostalgia-for-origins rhetoric wholesale (Strenski 1987; contested by Rennie 1996). Use his terms as a **design language**, never as a claim about real cultures.
- How to use as a generation tool — Eliade is the best of the four for producing *institutions* rather than plots:
  1. Choose the culture's hierophany inventory (3–6 objects/moments where the sacred shows: springs, mountain gates, eclipses, first-fruits).
  2. Derive the axis mundi and the settlement plan from it (the temple as imago mundi gives you city layouts; couples culture-material).
  3. Script the calendar from the eternal return: every major rite = a reenactment of a tier-1 myth beat; the new-year rite reenacts the cosmogony specifically.
  4. Generate prohibitions and liminality from the sacred/profane split: thresholds, tonsures, siege lines, entry rites — each is a boundary rule (couples costume: the ritual garment is the hierophany you can wear).
  5. Add the kratophany layer: dangerous power that demands harness — why smiths, kings, and midwives are both sacred and quarantined.

## 4. Lévi-Strauss: mythemes, binary oppositions, the canonical formula

- Mechanics (Lévi-Strauss 1955): myth is a kind of language; it must be told in sequence (diachrony) but is intelligible as structure (synchrony). The **mytheme** is the smallest constituent unit — not a word but a *relation*; mythemes gain meaning only in **bundles of relations** read column-wise across the narrative, like a musical score read as chords as well as melody.
- The worked exemplar: the Oedipus myth decomposed into a table whose columns are crossed codes — overrated blood relations (mother-marriage; Antigone's burial) vs underrated blood relations (father-killing; fratricide), against denial of autochthony (the Sphinx killed; feet wounded — "born of earth" humans shown as malformed) vs persistence of autochthony (earth-born monsters; swollen feet). The myth's meaning is the *unresolvable mediation* between two codes: are we born of one or two? The same essay then walks a Zuni and a Maidu myth through the same column logic to show the method traveling.
- The **canonical formula**: Fx(a) : Fy(b) ∷ Fx(b) : Fa−1(y) — a myth resolves an initial logical contradiction by introducing a third term that transforms it (from "The Structural Study of Myth"). The Mythologiques tetralogy runs one vast code-family — raw/cooked, honey/rotten, fresh/decayed — through 800+ South and North American myths.
- Calibration: the method is **unfalsifiable in practice** — any myth can be cut into columns that yield a mediation, and the choice of mytheme segmentation is effectively arbitrary (critique going back through Leach 1970 to Derrida 1967). Binaries can be analyst-imposed rather than culture-real; Dundes (1962) answered with the emic correction: derive the oppositions from the culture's own categories, don't import nature/culture. Treat "the myth resolves X" as a *proposal*, never a finding.
- How to use as a generation tool — this is the **myth-family generator**:
  1. Pick 2–3 binary pairs that are *real emic axes of your culture* (cooked/raw for a hearth-religion; wet/dry for a delta people; named/unnamed for a taboo-language culture — couples semantics-pragmatics, politeness-taboo).
  2. Build the Oedipus-style column table for one myth; the invariant row is the unresolved mediation.
  3. Generate variants by **term substitution** along one column while holding the mediation fixed — you get a genuinely related myth family, not reskinned copies (the structuralist payoff: same code, different terms).
  4. Where the code is exhausted, the canonical formula tells you what a *new* myth must do: introduce the third term (eunuch, twin, hearth-less stranger) — i.e., myth-innovation in-world has a logic your culture's reformers and heresiarchs can exploit as plot.
  5. Index every generated motif with a Thompson Motif-Index or ATU number for your own tracking — but those numbers are librarian's metadata, not in-world facts.

## 5. Propp and Dundes: the tale as syntax

- Propp (1928) analyzed 100 Afanasyev Russian wondertales and found: tales are built from a closed inventory of **31 functions** in near-fixed order, stable regardless of who performs them; and from **7 spheres of action** (character roles): villain, donor, helper, princess (and her father), dispatcher, hero, false hero. A **move** is one full run from villainy/lack to liquidation; tales chain moves (trebling, pairing).
- The function skeleton, compressed: Preparation (1 absentation, 2 interdiction, 3 violation, 4 reconnaissance, 5 delivery, 6 trickery, 7 complicity) → Complication (8 villainy/lack, 9 mediation, 10 counteraction, 11 departure) → Donor sequence (12 donor's test, 13 reaction, 14 magical agent) → Struggle (15 guidance, 16 struggle, 17 branding/mark, 18 victory, 19 liquidation of lack) → Return (20 return, 21 pursuit, 22 rescue) → Hard task & recognition (23 unrecognized arrival, 24 unfounded claims, 25 difficult task, 26 solution, 27 recognition, 28 exposure, 29 transfiguration) → Finale (30 punishment, 31 wedding).
- Dundes's corrections and extensions: translate Propp's etic sequence into **emic units** per tradition (Dundes 1962); the attempt to fit Native American tales into Propp's 31 fails and must be re-morphologized culture-by-culture (Dundes 1964). Dundes's synthesis (1997): Propp gives folklore its **syntax**, Lévi-Strauss its **semantics** — use both, claim neither as universal.
- Greimas (1966) generalizes Propp's roles into six **actants** — subject, object, sender, receiver, helper, opponent — the standard quick-notation for any quest-like narrative in any culture.
- Calibration: the 31 functions describe **Russian wondertales only** — Propp said so; the sequence is not universal narrative grammar. Functions carry **no cultural content** (function 14 "receipt of a magical agent" is empty until you say what an agent is in this world). The monomyth's pop misuse ("Propp = hero's journey") inverts a formal system into an ideology.
- How to use as a generation tool — this is the **tale-grammar engine**:
  1. Write the function skeleton for a tier-3 folktale, then *break it locally*: a culture where function 31 is a funeral, or function 30 never comes, has a distinctive tale-dialect (per §2's logic).
  2. Bind each sphere of action to an **institution**: the dispatcher is the ancestors' council, the donor is a blacksmith lineage, the false hero is always the eldest son — role-institution mapping is exactly the kind of system output this lane hands off.
  3. Make the donor's test (function 12) and the magical agent (14) carry the culture's values — the test is where a moral system becomes plot.
  4. Generate variants by **move-chaining** and by sphere-swapping (helper promoted to hero) — mechanically what oral tradition does (couples orality-formulaic-language).
  5. What the tale *cannot* do: produce ontology. A Propp-legal tale family implies nothing about cosmology; supply that from §3 and §4.

## 6. Choosing a framework: what each actually models

| Framework | Unit of analysis | What it generates | What it cannot do | Overclaim risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Campbell monomyth | whole hero-biography | hero-cycles, initiation plots | institutions, ritual, non-heroic genres | universality claim — fully discredited |
| Eliade | ontology/ritual | sacred geography, calendars, hierophany sets, prohibitions | plot mechanics, tale-level variety | "archaic mind" essentialism; theological method |
| Lévi-Strauss | mytheme/code | myth families, variants, heresy logic | surface narrative charm, performance texture | unfalsifiable analyses; imposed binaries |
| Propp/Dundes | function sequence | tale grammars, role-institutions, variants | cosmology, sacred meaning, emotion | false universalization beyond the wondertale |

- Combination order that works: Eliade builds the sacred world → Lévi-Strauss builds the myth families that encode it → Propp builds the tales people actually tell → Campbell (with maximum suspicion) styles the exceptional founder-legend. One culture, four tools, each confined to its shelf.
- Cross-framework micro-example, the fire-theft (a genuinely pan-global motif family: Prometheus punished with the eagle; Māui's theft from the ʻalae mudhen in Hawaiian tradition; Raven's fire-theft in the Pacific Northwest): Eliade → fire is a first hierophany; every kindling rite reenacts the theft. Lévi-Strauss → the raw/cooked code's origin myth: cooking is the nature→culture hinge (the theme of Mythologiques vol. 1). Propp → lack of fire (8a), departure (11), donor's extortion (12–14), struggle (16), liquidation (19), plus Prometheus's punishment (30). Campbell → the boon won and the return refused/punished. Four outputs, one myth, zero contradictions — because each was asked a different question.

## 7. Calibration checklist: overclaiming, and its fixes

- Never write "all cultures have X" or "the universal hero myth" — not in canon notes, not even as flavor. The monomyth is the case study in how such claims get made (selective sampling) and broken (Segal 1999).
- Keep etic frames out of emic mouths: no in-world scholar "discovers" mythemes or functions; their equivalent is numerology, genealogy, and riddle-exegesis (couples orality-formulaic-language §7).
- Comparative method done responsibly = matched detail, not matched vibes (Doniger 1998): two myths are "the same" only with correspondences specified at motif level and indexed (Thompson/Uther); otherwise they are merely similar.
- Myth vs legend vs folktale is an emic boundary too: what is sacred truth to culture A is a children's tale to culture B — a standing source of in-world conflict lore-shokunin can use; flag, do not resolve.
- Falsifiability habit: for every structuralist column table, record what evidence *in-world* (a variant, a cultic split, a banned tale) would overturn your proposed mediation; if nothing could, it is a design note, not a fact.
- "Definition of myth" is genuinely unsettled (Honko 1984): any world bible definition of myth is itself an emic choice; hand candidates, not rulings.
- Campbell-derived vocab (call, threshold, boon, return) leaks pop-Hollywood tone into worlds that predate it; prefer Propp's neutral function codes and Eliade's ritual terms for drafting, and save journey-language for cultures that have earned it.
- Eliade's nostalgia rhetoric ("terror of history", the archaic as privileged) is a 20th-century ideological artifact with a documented political pedigree (Strenski 1987); if a fictional ideology *quotes* it, that is excellent material — as an in-world position, not as the designer's cosmology.

## 8. Era and handoff notes

- Era flavor: monomyth/heroic cycles dominate oral-then-heroic-age material (couples orality-formulaic-language); Eliade's ritual-ontology material is strongest for temple-state and agrarian-calendar cultures; structuralist myth families suit dense, long-lived, variant-rich oral corpora; formalist tale grammar is the everyday entertainment layer of any era. A culture's "myth age" is partly defined by which of these layers its traditions still perform.
- Provenance metadata for every generated myth handed to lore-shokunin: {tier (myth/legend/folktale per Bascom), generating framework + seed parameters, emic claims it asserts, etic design notes, ATU/motif index if applicable, open to-verify items}. This lets the bible owner re-derive variants without re-reading the framework files.

## 9. Canon boundary

Reference knowledge and method only. Which myths are true in-world, which culture holds which variant sacred, what the fire-theft "means", who the donor institution is — canon decisions owned by lore-shokunin. Hand off framework-tagged myth-family generators, function skeletons, hierophany inventories, and variant tables as era-anchored options; flag every contested or to-verify item (the akītu/Enūma Eliš performance link, any universalist claim) before it approaches the bible.
