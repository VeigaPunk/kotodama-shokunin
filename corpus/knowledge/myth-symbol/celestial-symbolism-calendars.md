## 1. Why the sky is the most transferable symbolic asset — and its hard constraints

- Every pre-telescopic culture sees the same inputs: ~5 wandering planets, one moon with a 29.53-day face cycle, ~1,000–4,000 stars depending on eyesight and darkness, seasonal star groups, eclipses at long intervals. Convergence in sky myth is therefore *not evidence of contact by itself* — the shared sky produces shared problems (when to plant, when to sail, when to fast), and cultures diverge in the *lexification*, not the perception.
- Three constraints do most of the design work for you: (1) **latitude** — which stars are circumpolar, whether there is a usable pole star (bright in the north: Polaris; none bright in the south), which zodiac signs ride highest; (2) **subsistence** — farmers anchor on heliacal risings, sailors on star paths, pastoralists on milky/river figures; (3) **economy of omens** — rare events (eclipses, comets, conjunctions) are only useful to institutions that can afford specialists to watch for them.
- Generation levers (pick one per culture, not all): a horizon calendar (rising/setting points), a star-path navigation corpus, a dark-constellation inventory (§4), a two-calendar interlock (§8), or an eclipse archive (§7). A culture with *none* of these should be a deliberate choice — forest-canopy or cave-horizon peoples whose sky is barely visible — and is itself characterization.

## 2. Sun and moon deities: the gender ledger (the pattern is that there is no pattern)

- The folk assumption "sun god / moon goddess" fails worldwide. Gender assignment correlates with local cosmology and social structure, not with the bodies:

| Culture | Sun | Moon | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Egypt | Ra (m); Eye of Ra as Hathor/Sekhmet/Tefnut (f aspect) | Thoth, Khonsu (m) | both bodies male; the *female solar Eye* is an aspect-switch (see below) |
| Mesopotamia | Shamash (m, god of justice — law sees by daylight) | Sin (m) | moon is the *calendar father*, father of Ishtar |
| Greece/Rome | Helios/Sol (m) | Selene/Luna (f) | Apollo/Artemis as sun/moon is a **later conflation** (Hellenistic onwards), not Homeric religion |
| India | Surya (m) | Chandra/Soma (m) | both male; Soma is also the pressed ritual drink — body and substance fuse |
| China | Xihe, mother/charioteer of the ten suns (f) | Changxi, mother of twelve moons; Chang'e (f) | suns are *plural and birthed*; Yi the archer shoots nine (Shanhaijing; Huainanzi) |
| Japan | Amaterasu (f) | Tsukuyomi (m) | Kojiki 712; her Iwato cave retreat = scripted solar-eclipse-style crisis rite |
| Norse | Sól (f) | Máni (m) | pursued by wolves Sköll/Hati; devoured at Ragnarök (Gylfaginning) |
| Inuit (Iglulik) | Malina (f, sister) | Igaluk (m, brother) | incest-chase etiology of solar motion (Rasmussen 1929) |
| Baltic | Saulė (f) | Mėnulis (m) | dainas record Mėnulis punished for adultery with the dawn star Aušrinė (Barons 1894–1915) |
| Aztec | Tonatiuh (m) | Coyolxauhqui (f), dismembered at Coatepec | moon as defeated sibling-foe |
| Maya | Kinich Ahau (m) | Moon Goddess (f; Ixchel postclassic) | Milbrath (1999): Classic-era *male* moon god(s) shift to a female goddess later — gender drifts within one tradition |

- Count the table: sun-female and moon-male pairings are fully attested (Japan, Norse, Inuit, Baltic, archaic China), as are both-male (Egypt, Mesopotamia, India). **Calibration:** never let a fictional culture's sun/moon gender be justified by "that's how it always is"; and treat any *universalist* claim about a primordial sun-goddess as discredited (§9; Eller 2000).
- **Aspect-switching** is the real-world mechanism behind apparent contradiction: one body, gendered differently by persona or episode. Egypt's Eye of Ra is the sun's dangerous emanation rendered female (Sekhmet), while Ra stays male — the same sky body carries two liturgical identities. Use this to give a fictional culture internal variety without incoherence.
- Consequences you can harvest: temple office inheritance (a sun-king/moon-queen split licenses dual monarchy), royal titles ("daughter of the sun" reads differently by culture), and eclipse theology (§7) which depends entirely on who is being attacked — god, goddess, sibling pair, or leash-slipped beast.

## 3. Planets as gods: the interpretatio chain and the fossilized week

- The five wanderers get divine identities in every literate tradition, and literate traditions *map them onto each other* — interpretatio is the mechanism. Tacitus, Germania 9 (the Germans "worship Mercury above all" = Wodan) is the classic example of the process being visible in real time.

| Body | Sumerian/Babylonian | Greek | Roman | Chinese (五行) | Sanskrit graha | Maya |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mercury | Nabu (scribe) | Hermes | Mercurius | 辰星 "Hour Star" | Budha | — |
| Venus | Inanna/Ishtar (war + love; morning and evening personae) | Aphrodite | Venus | 太白 "Great White" (martial) | Śukra (asura preceptor) | Kukulkan/Quetzalcoatl-adjacent (Milbrath 1999) |
| Mars | Nergal (plague, underworld) | Ares | Mars | 荧惑 "Confuser" (worst martial omen; 荧惑守心 "Mars guards the Heart" = Antares) | Maṅgala | — |
| Jupiter | Marduk | Zeus | Iuppiter | 岁星 "Year Star" — its ~12-year circuit names the 12 branches | Bṛhaspati (deva preceptor) | — |
| Saturn | Ninurta | Kronos | Saturnus | 镇星 "Pacifier" | Śani (slow, fearsome) | — |

- **Calibration:** the claim that Greece borrowed planet-god assignments *from* Babylon is plausible and widely argued (transmission via Hellenistic astronomy; van der Waerden 1974; Rochberg 2004) but the exact route is debated — do not write it as settled fact, in-world or out.
- Venus is the masterclass in dual persona: Greeks long treated morning-star (Phosphoros) and evening-star (Hesperos) as *two different stars*; their identification was traditionally credited to the Pythagoreans. Ishtar's morning/war vs evening/love aspects, and the Dresden Codex Venus table splitting 584 days into 236 (morning star) + 90 (invisible, inferior conjunction) + 250 (evening star) + 8 (invisible, superior conjunction), show the same split encoded in numbers and in ritual. The Maya timed "star wars" raids to Venus's heliacal rising (Schele & Freidel 1990) — a calendrically scheduled ambush is a superb worldbuilding device.
- **The week is fossilized interpretatio.** Hellenistic planetary hours (Chaldean order Saturn-Jupiter-Mars-Sun-Venus-Mercury-Moon, skip-counted through 24 hours) yield the day sequence Saturday-Sunday-Monday-Tuesday-Wednesday-Thursday-Friday (Zerubavel 1985; attested at Pompeii). Rome named days for its gods; Germanic peoples calqued them (Tiw→Mars, Wodan→Mercury, Thunor→Jupiter, Frigg→Venus); the same planetary week reached East Asia as 七曜 (日月火水木金土) via Indian-Buddhist astrology (8th c. Xiuyao jing), institutionalized in Japan's 1876 calendar — its weekday names are still spoken daily. A fictional world should be able to *read out its own contact history from its weekday names*.
- Planet-god personality is the cheapest way to differentiate two related cultures sharing a sky: same five lights, opposite temperaments (Nergal the plague-bringer vs Ares the mere fighter; Śani the dreaded vs Kronos the dethroned).

## 4. Constellations and asterisms: every sky is re-lexified

- Keep the terms straight: **asterism** = a perceived star pattern (the Big Dipper); **constellation** = one culture's formal partition of the sky. The IAU's 88 constellations (boundaries by Delporte, 1930) are a modern diplomatic convention, not a discovery — there is no primordial constellation set, and the zodiac is a Babylonian administrative grid (12 signs of 30°, fixed by the late 5th c. BCE; Rochberg 2004).
- Same stars, different readings — use this table to give cultures sharing a sky distinct lexical sets:

| Stars | Greek/Roman | China | Other readings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Big Dipper | Bear (Callisto myth; cf. Old Europe/Bering "cosmic hunt" — diffusion vs convergence contested, Berezkin 2005) | 北斗 Northern Dipper — celestial regulator; Daoist liturgy recites each star's *secret name* for lifespan (kotodama hook, §8) | wagon/plough in northern Europe; "Drinking Gourd" in African-American tradition — but the claim that the song encoded real escape routes is **largely retrospective folklore**, the song being collected in the 20th c.; Micmac/Iroquois bowl = bear pursued by three hunters |
| Orion | the hunter | 参 Shen, western (White Tiger) mansion; belt as 三星 "three stars" | Egypt: Sah = Osiris (Pyramid Texts); Maya: belt = the three hearthstones of creation (Freidel, Schele & Parker 1993); "Three Kings/Sisters" in European folk |
| Pleiades | seven sisters (§5) | 昴 mao, one of the 28 mansions | see §5 |
| Milky Way | Hera's spilled milk (γαλαξίας κύκλος; Pseudo-Eratosthenes) | 天河/银汉 sky river (§6) | see §6 |

- **Dark constellations** — figures read in the Milky Way's dust lanes, not its stars — are a genuine worldwide minority strategy with high differentiation value: the Emu in the Sky (Australia, Norris & Norris 2009), the Andean Yacana llama, fox, toad and serpent in dark clouds (Urton 1981), the Maya black road Xibalba be (Freidel, Schele & Parker 1993), the North American Path of Souls (Lankford 2007). A fictional culture that reads the sky's *darknesses* is instantly alien to its star-reading neighbors.
- Institutional astronomy grows out of folk asterisms via paperwork: Egypt's 36 **decans** (star groups marking 10-day weeks, painted on coffin lids as diagonal star clocks — Neugebauer & Parker 1960–69) become hours; Babylon's mansion lists (MUL.APIN, a compilation dated variously c. 1370–1000 BCE) become the zodiac; China's 28 mansions (xiu) partition the equatorial sky for both omenology and navigation of the calendar.
- Navigation is the other export: the Polynesian **sidereal compass** — ~30 rising/setting points of memorized star pairs, held as "star paths" (Lewis 1972; revived in the Hōkūleʻa voyage of 1976) — is a fully realized celestial *language of direction*, and pairs naturally with this lane's language-focus.

## 5. The Pleiades: the calendar star

- Why this cluster anchors more calendars than any other object: compact and unmistakable, yet close to the ecliptic — it spends roughly 40 nights in conjunction with the sun and vanishes, so its heliacal rising/setting is a sharp, publicly verifiable seasonal boundary. Hesiod already runs it as farm software (Works and Days 383–385: rise → reap, set → plough).
- Worldwide readings and uses (non-exhaustive; add locally):

| Culture | Reading | Calendric use |
|---|---|---|
| Greek | Seven sisters, daughters of Atlas; one lost (Merope/Electra ashamed) | harvest/ploughing marker (Hesiod); sailing season (Aratus) |
| Japan | 昴 Subaru "the gathered"; older 六連星 Mutsuraboshi "six stars" | none major — but the name was adopted as a car brand whose six-star logo shows how asterisms become *emblems* |
| Māori | Matariki, the mother star of a star-cluster whānau | heliacal rising (June–July, southern winter) = new year; national public holiday since 2022 (Matamua 2017) |
| Hawaiʻi | Makaliʻi | rising ~Nov opens Makahiki, the Lono rainy-season festival cycle (Malo 1898) |
| Inca/Quechua | Qullqa "storehouse" | days of invisibility used to forecast the coming year's crops; return ritually watched (Urton 1981) |
| Australia | Seven Sisters Tjukurpa songline (pursuit narrative) | law and land mapping more than planting (Neale 2017; Norris & Norris 2009) |
| India | Kṛttikā, the wives of the sages | six of them nurse the six-headed Kārttikeya (Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva); nakshatra head |
| Baltic | Sietiņš "little sieve" (Latvian) | sowing marker in dainas (Barons 1894–1915) |

- The **"six or seven" motif** (lost sister; Mutsuraboshi; six of seven Kṛttikās) recurs because median eyes resolve six stars and keen eyes find more — a shared *perceptual* fact, not proof of shared story. It is one of the few details where convergence and diffusion genuinely compete: flag it, don't resolve it.
- **Hemisphere inversion is the highest-value trick here:** the same heliacal rising is midwinter new year in Aotearoa, late-autumn festival season in Hawaiʻi, and a harvest deadline in Greece. Latitude, not the star, decides the festival — so a fictional empire spanning latitudes will have *the same festival at opposite seasons*, which is exactly the kind of schism lore-shokunin can eat.
- Eye-test cults: because resolving seven Pleiads (or splitting Mizar/Alcor) is a documented eyesight feat across cultures, a fictional rite of passage can legitimately be "count the sisters" — cheap, verifiable, and attested-adjacent (Mizar/Alcor as an Arabic horse-eyesight test).

## 6. Milky Way typology: one band of light, one figure per economy

| Type | Culture | Figure | Worldbuilding signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| River | China (天河/银汉), Japan (天の川) | the river separating Weaver-Girl star (Vega) and Cowherd star (Altair); magpie bridge once a year — Qixi/Tanabata, 7th night of 7th month (star pair already in Shijing, "Dadong") | riverine-farming cultures with flood calendars; love festival with exact date |
| River (double) | Inca (Mayu) | celestial river mirroring the Vilcanota; sky and earth drain in opposite flow; animals live in its dark clouds (Urton 1981) | irrigation states; astronomy integrated with canal polity |
| Milk/path | Greek | Hera's spilt milk (Pseudo-Eratosthenes) | pastoral etiology; the body of a humiliated goddess |
| Road of the dead | Mississippian SE North America (Path of Souls, Lankford 2007); Maya black road | souls travel it; the dark rift is the road (or the underworld gate) | funerary architecture orients to it; death is *navigation* |
| Bird path | Finnish Linnunrata "bird's path"; bird-path readings reported for Sámi tradition (Berezkin 2010: 14, after Toivonen 1937; Erdődi 1968) | migratory birds fly along it; souls as birds | migration-calendar cultures; season of the dead = season of bird departure |
| Accident | Navajo | Coyote, impatient, scatters the carefully placed stars from a bag — the disorder is in the origin (Zolbrod 1984) | worlds where imperfection is primordial and *someone is to blame* |
| Body of sky | Egypt | Nut, the sky-goddess, arched; recent argument reads an undulating line on coffin images as the Milky Way's Great Rift (Graur 2024, JAHH 27; arXiv:2404.01458) | recent single-author claim — treat as strong hypothesis, not consensus |

- Assign by subsistence and let the figure generate ritual: river → a ferry festival and bridge tolls; bird path → spring/autumn bird-taboo seasons; road of the dead → grave orientation codes; milk → dairy purity rules; accident → a trickster-feast that ritually *re-scatters* something (couples myth-symbol/comparative-frameworks for the myth family generator).

## 7. Eclipses as omens, rituals, and archives

- A typology of eclipse beings — pick one per culture; each licenses different rites:

| Mechanism | Exemplars | Ritual response |
|---|---|---|
| Devoured | Rāhu/Ketu, the beheaded asura who swallows sun and moon (India; from the churning of the ocean); celestial dog 天狗 (China); wolves Sköll/Hati (Norse); serpent biting the moon goddess in the Dresden eclipse table (Bricker & Bricker 2011); jaguar (Mesoamerica) | noise: drums, pots, shouting — drive off or distract the eater; India adds fasting, charity, bathing to aid the wounded body |
| Portent of state | China: sun eclipsed = "the minister eclipses the ruler"; the Chunqiu logs 36 solar eclipses as state records; Shijing "Shiyue zhi jiao" ties eclipse to misgovernment; Zuo Zhuan (Wen 15) has the court drumming at the soil altar | bureaucratic crisis protocol; moral self-critique by the ruler; record-keeping |
| Demonic descent | Aztec: during eclipse the tzitzimime descend to devour people; pregnant women are the specific vulnerable class (Sahagún, Florentine Codex Bk VII) | seclusion of expectant mothers, protective amulets (red cloth tied over the womb — attested for Ch'orti' Maya (Hull 2020: 280), recorded for Los Tuxtlas and Otomí and not for pre-Hispanic times (DEMTM s.v. "eclipse"); a specifically Yucatec red-thread version is reported but not confirmed) |
| Broken order | Norse: the wolves catch the luminaries at the end of time — eclipse as *preview of Ragnarök* | eschatological anxiety; the eclipse reminds of a scheduled ending |

- **The substitute-king ritual** (Assyrian šar pūḫi; Rochberg 2004): a solar/lunar omen threatens the real king, so a condemned man is enthroned for days, "eats the omen", and is executed — the state launders danger through a disposable body. One of the best worldbuilding mechanisms in all of divination: it makes astronomy, law, and capital punishment one institution.
- Omens are also *engineered*: Herodotus 1.74 — an eclipse (585 BCE) halts the Lydian-Mede battle of the Halys; tradition credits Thales with predicting it, but whether he could actually predict solar eclipses is contested (season-level plausibility, not to-the-day); Herodotus 7.37 and 9.10 show eclipses stopping armies and arguments. Columbus (1504, Jamaica) allegedly used a predicted lunar eclipse to extort food — the accounts are early modern (Oviedo 1535; Ferdinand Columbus) and possibly self-serving, but the mechanism is real: whoever can compute becomes whoever can threaten.
- **Eclipse archives are chronology's backbone:** the solar eclipse of 15 June 763 BCE, recorded in the Assyrian eponymy of Bur-Sagale, anchors the entire Neo-Assyrian timeline by retrocalculation; the Babylonian eclipse reports embedded in the Enuma Anu Enlil tradition give absolute dates (Rochberg 2004). Give a fictional state 300 years of dated eclipse entries and you have given it real historiography — plus a priesthood that must *maintain the secret of the cycle* (the saros, 223 lunations ≈ 18 years 11 days) while performing divine terror.
- Japan's Iwato myth (Amaterasu seals herself in the cave; the world darkens; laughter and a mirror lure her out) is the polished case of eclipse-style crisis converted into festival theater (kagura) — darkness ritually reversed by *performance and light* rather than noise alone.

## 8. Celestial cycles → calendars: the machinery, plus who owns the leap

| Cycle | Length | What it anchors | Exemplars |
|---|---|---|---|
| Synodic month | 29.53 d | lunar months; moon-face festivals (full moon = assembly night: Purnima, Mid-Autumn/Chuseok — the autumn full moon rises near sunset several nights running, the "harvest moon" effect) | Islamic calendar stays pure lunar by doctrine, so festivals migrate through seasons — a strong cultural differentiator |
| Tropical year | 365.2422 d | solstice/equinox festivals; Newgrange's winter-solstice roof-box beam (c. 3200 BCE, O'Kelly 1982) | Egyptian civil year of 365 days drifting through the seasons against the heliacal rising of Sirius (Sothic cycle ~1460 yr); Julian (46 BCE; a 445-day "year of confusion") then Gregorian (1582) reforms |
| Lunisolar intercalation | 19 yr ≈ 235 lunations | keeps lunar months in solar seasons | Meton of Athens 432 BCE; Babylonian court decrees regularized to 7 months per 19 years by the 5th–4th c. BCE (Parker & Dubberstein 1956); Hebrew fixed cycle traditionally credited to Hillel II (359 CE) — the historicity of that attribution is debated (Stern 2001) |
| Ritual counts | 260 d (13×20) | day-name divination | Maya tzolkʼin + 365 haabʼ interlocked in a 52-year Calendar Round, closed by the Aztec New Fire ceremony that re-lights every hearth (Sahagún Bk VII); a 260-day reading as two zenith-passage dates at ~15°N is an attractive *hypothesis*, not settled (Aveni 2001) |
| Planet circuits | 584 d Venus; ~12 yr Jupiter | war timing; year-names | Dresden Venus table (584 = 236+90+250+8; Bricker & Bricker 2011); Chinese Jupiter-year branches |
| Week | 7 d | market/rest days, planetary days | planetary week (Zerubavel 1985); note: the 7-day week is *not* a celestial period — cultures with 9-, 8-, or 10-day weeks are historically real and worldbuilding-fresh |
| Saros | 223 lunations ≈ 18 yr 11 d 8 h | eclipse prediction archives | Babylonian-Assyrian omen offices; Antikythera mechanism dials its Metonic and saros cycles in gearwork (Freeth et al., Nature 2006/2021) |

- **Whoever declares the leap month owns the feast calendar, the tax year, and loan maturities.** Late-intercalating Roman pontiffs could stretch a friendly magistrate's term; the Babylonian king's intercalation decree was an act of sovereignty; the modern echo is a parliament changing the clock. In-world, make the astronomer-priest who *forgives time* a political office with a murder-motive built in.
- Monument anchors, defensibly attested: Newgrange winter solstice (O'Kelly 1982); Chankillo's 13 towers registering the solar journey between solstice rising points c. 4th c. BCE Peru (Ghezzi & Ruggles 2007); Cuzco's ceque lines and horizon sun-pillars tying the Inca capital's 41 ceremonial rays to solstice and zenith (Zuidema 1964; Aveni 2001). **Calibration:** Hawkins's Stonehenge-as-computer (1965) triggered a backlash; the surviving discipline demands statistical alignment tests (Heggie 1981). Rule of thumb for fiction: 1–3 sky events per monument is plausible; a stone circle encoding twenty alignments is a claim that needs an in-world Thales.
- **Kotodama hooks (this lane's specialty):** the Daoist Big Dipper liturgy where the adept recites the secret name of his natal star to extend life (Beidou benming jing, 太上玄靈北斗本命延生真經; DZ 622, Zhengtong Daozang 洞神部本文類); Chinese-Japanese star-name registers (28 mansions, 七曜) as fixed liturgical vocabularies; taboo or euphemistic names for eclipse bodies spoken only during the event; dynastic renaming of stars; oaths sworn "by the rising of the Pleiades" — star names as performative speech, the 言霊 (kotodama) premise applied to sky lexicon.

## 9. Calibration: discredited and contested frameworks in sky myth

- **Solar mythology** (Max Müller's 19th-c. school: all gods and heroes are refracted sun) — dead as method; survives only as a cautionary tale about reductive etymology.
- **Panbabylonism** (Winckler, Jeremias: all mythology derives from Babylonian astral religion) — discredited; do not route every fictional constellation through one source culture.
- **Frazer's dying-god solar year** (The Golden Bough, 1890) — methodologically superseded; still useful as a motif catalogue, not as an explanation.
- **A primordial sun-goddess / matriarchal sky religion** (Gimbutas 1970s) — contested to discredited as prehistory (Eller 2000). Sun/moon genders are plural and local (§2); that is the finding.
- **Hamlet's Mill** (de Santillana & von Dechend 1969: all myth encodes precession knowledge) — unfalsifiable pattern-hunting in the eyes of most reviewers; precession *myths* exist (e.g., the Hipparcos-era discovery), but a secret global precession cult is a novel premise, not scholarship.
- **The Maya 2012 apocalypse** — the 13th bʼakʼtun of the Long Count was a calendar rollover (like a 52-year Calendar Round), not a doomsday prophecy; the apocalyptic reading is modern pop invention. Good example of what happens when someone reads an odometer as a bomb.
- **"Follow the Drinking Gourd" as an operational escape map** — retrospective folklore (§4). And any claim that a distant-culture sky story is "the same myth" must clear the bar in comparative-frameworks.md: matched motif-level detail or a datable contact path — otherwise it is convergence (the lost Pleiad is the honest test case: convergence is at least as good an explanation as diffusion).

## 10. Applying this to a fictional culture — a build order

1. Fix **latitude and hemisphere**: which stars exist, is there a pole star, when do the Pleiades rise (this single choice propagates through every festival date).
2. Assign **sun/moon genders** against the social structure (§2), and give one body an aspect-switch (Eye-of-Ra style) so the tradition carries internal tension.
3. Build the **planet pantheon** by interpretatio from the culture's dominant neighbor or predecessor (§3) — then derive weekday names from that mapping and let linguists of your world read the contact history off them.
4. Choose **one Milky Way figure** consistent with subsistence (§6) and one **dark constellation** as the culture's signature (§4).
5. Decide the **eclipse theology** (§7) and its institution: devourer-noise cult, state-omen archive with substitute kings, or eschatological preview — and give the archive real dates.
6. Pick the **calendar interlock** (§8): which cycle is sacred, which is administrative, who declares intercalation (a political office with motives), and which rare cycle (Venus? saros?) the priesthood secretly commands.
7. Anchor **2–3 festivals** to sky events and derive their costume from the event's logic (eclipse seclusion garb for pregnant women; New Fire firegear; Matariki-style dawn vigil dress) — couple to costume and culture-material.
8. Write the **sky lexicon as kotodama**: natal star names whispered in liturgy, taboo eclipse names, oath formulas keyed to heliacal risings, and a naming taboo that means the true name of the sun is known to one person (couple to politeness-taboo-euphemism.md and naming-systems.md).
