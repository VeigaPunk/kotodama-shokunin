## 1. The load-bearing idea: a genre is a teller's truth-claim plus a performance context, not a content type

- Bascom (1965) defines four prose-narrative forms by five axes, not by subject matter. The same plot (flood, bride-theft, clever youngest son) can exist in all four forms in one culture; what makes it a myth is that it is told as sacred truth about primordial time, usually by ritually licensed tellers. Record the form, never infer it from the plot.
- The five axes: form (sacred vs secular), time setting, place setting, attitude toward truth, principal characters. Bascom's own cross-tabulation:

| Form | Sacred? | Time | Place | Told as… | Protagonists |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Myth | sacred | primordial "that time" | other world, or the earth when it was other | true | nonhuman (gods, animal-people, ancestor-powers) |
| Legend | sacred or secular | historical past | today's world | true | human heroes (sometimes divine-adjacent) |
| Saga | secular | recent past | today's world | true | known or knowable real local people (genealogical, local) |
| Folktale / Märchen | secular | no definite time ("once upon a time") | no definite place | fiction ("only a story") | human or nonhuman, ordinary |

- Two systems cross-cut this in practice, both from von Sydow (1948): the **memorate** (first-person experience narrative: "I saw the lake-woman") vs the **fabulate** (the same story told as a third-person "someone saw"); and the **oicotype** — a type's localized variant family, marked by local formula words, flora/fauna substitution, names, and costume detail. A world's "ghost stories" genre is typically memorate-anchored and is where in-world "evidence" lives; couple to lore-shokunin's evidence handling, don't absorb it here.
- Bascom (1954) gives the four **functions** — entertainment, validation of culture, education for the young, maintenance of conformity — and Bascom (1965) crosses forms × functions. Bascom's table is etic and idealized: real cultures' genre vocabularies are richer and don't always line up (Honko 1984: "definition of myth" is genuinely unsettled; Ben-Amos 1971 relocates genre in the telling situation). Record the emic genre word(s) alongside the etic slot, always.
- Emic genre vocabulary is a lexicon task: give every culture words for "true old sacred tale", "hero story of real men", "nursemaid's lie", "riddle", "mocking song-parable" — a culture's contempt or reverence for a form is characterizable culture data (couples semantics-pragmatics). Genre-marking formulas are the audible signal: English "Once upon a time", German "Es war einmal", French "Il était une fois", Arabic "kāna yā mā kāna" ("there was, O what there was"), Japanese "mukashi mukashi" — the formula *is* the form's boundary marker in performance (couples orality-formulaic-language).

## 2. Stith Thompson's two tools: motif letters (units) and tale types (wholes)

- **Motif-Index** (Thompson 1955–58): the smallest indexable narrative unit is a **motif** — a single character, object, incident, or belief, numbered `letter.number.subnumber`. The 23 letter classes (letters I and O unused): A Mythological; B Animal; C Tabu; D Magic; E The Dead; F Marvels; G Ogres; H Tests; J The Wise and the Foolish; K Deceptions; L Reversal of Fortune; M Ordaining the Future; N Chance and Fate; P Society; Q Rewards and Punishments; R Captives and Fugitives; S Unnatural Cruelty; T Sex; U The Nature of Life; V Religion; W Traits of Character; X Humor; Z Miscellaneous Groups. Worked ids: A420 "water god"; C41 "tabu: offending a water deity"; D110 "transformation: man to object".
- **Type** (Aarne 1910 → Thompson 1928/1961 → Uther 2004): a **type** is a whole self-sufficient tale built from motifs in a customary sequence — e.g. ATU 510A "Cinderella" is one type assembled from many motifs. Motif = brick; type = house; Propp's functions (see comparative-frameworks.md §5) = the masonry grammar of one zone of houses.
- Craft rules: index at **both** levels when handing off (type for the tale, motif letters for searchable contents); never treat a motif as meaningful in isolation — the index is deliberately atomistic and strips meaning; and the indexes are librarian's metadata for this lane's own tracking, **never in-world facts** (no in-world scholar cites "ATU 300"; that's the emic/etic rule in comparative-frameworks.md §1).

## 3. ATU usage for a fictional world

- Chapter ranges (Uther 2004), with anchored exemplars:

| Numbers | Chapter | Anchored exemplars |
|---|---|---|
| 1–299 | Animal tales | ATU 130 The Bremen Town Musicians; 123 The Wolf and the Kids; 333 Little Red Riding Hood (indexed here, not under magic tales) |
| 300–749 | Tales of magic (Märchen proper; Propp's corpus) | ATU 300 The Dragon-Slayer; 313 The Magic Flight; 325 The Sorcerer's Apprentice; 330 The Smith and the Devil; 410 Sleeping Beauty; 425C Beauty and the Beast; 440 The Frog King; 480 The Kind and the Unkind Girls; 500 The Name of the Supernatural Helper (Rumpelstiltskin); 510A Cinderella; 510B Donkeyskin / Cap o' Rushes; 545B The Cat as Helper (Puss in Boots); 709 Snow White; 720 The Juniper Tree |
| 750–849 | Religious tales | God/saint-in-disguise hospitality tests; miracle and judgment legends |
| 850–999 | Novellas (realistic tales) | ATU 870 The Princess in the Mound; 931 Oedipus |
| 1000–1199 | Tales of the stupid ogre (giant, devil) | devil/giant outwitted by cleverness chains |
| 1200–1999 | Anecdotes and jokes | ATU 1525 The Master Thief |
| 2000–2399 | Formula tales | ATU 2030 The House That Jack Built (cumulative chain) |
| 2400–2499 | Unclassified | — |

- The age of a type is evidence, not decoration: ATU 510A-family tales are attested centuries apart and far afield — Rhodopis' sandal carried off by an eagle (Strabo, Geography 17.1.33, 1st c. CE) and Ye Xian's fish-bones and lost golden shoe in Duan Chengshi's Youyang zazu (9th-c. Tang China). When you deploy a recognizable type in-world, placing one "attestation" in your world's old texts is exactly how real tradition works (couples era files).
- Antiquity claims are contested terrain: da Silva & Tehrani (2016) put the ATU 330 Smith-and-the-Devil lineage plausibly in the Bronze Age via phylogenetic methods, but the method assumes tree-like inheritance where horizontal borrowing dominates, and Bottigheimer (2009) argues many "old" fairy tales are early-modern print products. Flag any pre-history claim on a type as contested.
- ATU is Europe-anchored even after Uther's international expansion. Assigning ATU numbers to a non-European or invented tradition flattens its emic structure (Dundes' emic-correction point, comparative-frameworks.md §5). House convention: for cultures far from the "European analog" zone, mint **lane-internal type ids** (`KTD-T####`, one per in-world type with its own name, formula, and cast), and map to ATU only where the analogy genuinely holds, as a designer's cross-reference — not as the record's primary key.

## 4. Myth as charter (Malinowski), and the four-function audit

- Malinowski (1926): myth "is not merely a story told but a reality lived"; it "expresses, enhances, and codifies belief; safeguards and enforces morality; vouches for the efficiency of ritual". Function: myth is a **social charter** — a warrant that makes current institutions look founded, inevitable, and ancestral. His Trobriand material: origin myths charter clan rank and garden-magic rights; ancestral emergence-sites ground land claims; Kula mythology warrants overseas trade claims.
- Charter is a *use*, not an origin theory: it explains what myth does for an institution, not where the institution came from, and charter is not the same as etiology (a "how the leopard got its spots" story explains; a charter obligates). Keep the two distinct in records: `function: etiological` vs `function: charter`.
- Per-record charter audit (mandatory, cheap, powerful): *who repeats this story, to whom, on what occasions, to make whom do what?* A myth that fails the audit gets tagged `vestigial` or `entertainment` — and that tag is itself strong culture data (institutional drift; a religion whose charter myths no longer govern is a church in decay, hand that observation to lore-shokunin, don't resolve it).
- Calibration: functionalist charter doctrine over-generalizes — not every myth charters, and post-Malinowski fieldwork found myth communities using narratives for play, subversion, and history with no charter work at all. Design corollary: **do not make every myth of a culture a charter**; a healthy myth system has vestigial, entertaining, and subversive records in its file set.
- Bascom's four functions (§1) become four tags per record — a record may carry several (a charter myth told as bedtime entertainment is a culture in the act of demoting its canon: see §5 strata).

## 5. Genre strata: demotion, promotion, and what they tell you about a culture's age

- Genres drift, and the drift is datable. Standard real-world trajectories: sacred myth → courtly literature → nursery tale (Greek myth as Hellenistic-Roman entertainment; Norse Þórr-material sinking into trickster nursery tales after Christianization); legend → "mere" folktale; folktale → canonized sacred text (rare but real: the reverse elevation of vernacular narrative into scripture is how several canonizations read). A culture's genre strata are a map of its religious and political history — generatable on purpose.
- Record rule: each narrative record carries a `stratum` field — the form it *currently* holds and the form(s) it previously held, with the in-world event that moved it (mission, conquest, literacy, court fashion). Demotion is the single most era-diagnostic fact a narrative record carries (couples era files).
- literacy interacts with strata: writing freezes one variant as canon and demotes oral siblings to "corrupt" copies — the Grimm-editing dynamic at work (see §7). A culture that canonizes its tales will tell you whether it has a priesthood with text.

## 6. Euhemerism and its inverse: the two-way street between gods and men

- **Euhemerism proper** (Euhemerus of Messene, fl. c. 300 BCE, at Cassander's court): in the Sacred History's fictional travelogue to Panchaea, the gods turn out to be deified mortal kings — Uranus, Cronus, Zeus as successive benefactor-rulers; Zeus has a tomb on Crete. Fragments survive via Diodorus Siculus 5.41–46 & 6.1 and Eusebius, Praeparatio Evangelica 2.2 (Ennius translated it into Latin verse).
- Historical euhemerism as a *practice*: Christian apologists (Eusebius) used it to reduce paganism to exaggerated biography; Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda prologue makes the Æsir human chieftains from Asia/Troy, Óðinn a migrating king deified after death; Saxo Grammaticus' Gesta Danorum treats the gods as early Danish kings and warriors. Note the power move: the euhemerizer *controls* the older religion by reclassifying it — a ready-made in-world ideology (a rationalist school, an emperor's clerics, a missionary church) with a 2,300-year pedigree.
- **The inverse (this lane's term: mythologization; some literature says "inverse euhemerism")**: real people accrete myth. Mechanisms, all attested: posthumous hero/ruler cult (Hellenistic kings, Roman imperial cult); genealogical backfitting — dynasties descended from gods (Anglo-Saxon royal genealogies from Woden, Bede HE I.15; the imperial line from Amaterasu, Kojiki 712 / Nihon Shoki 720); literary historicization of a legendary figure (Geoffrey of Monmouth c. 1136 turning Arthur into a chronicle king); legend-cycles grown on real rulers (Charlemagne's romances). 
- Craft levers, both directions: (1) decide which direction the *facts* of your world run — are the gods genuinely deified founders (euhemerism true in-world), or do they precede mortals (euhemerists in-world are wrong, and their "tombs of Zeus" are misreadings — the ambiguity is plot fuel)? (2) run mythologization on purpose for dynasties: give a founding warlord a Woden-style genealogy inserted 400 years late, and leave detectable seams (anachronistic cult-names in the genealogy, a hero-cult site older than the dynasty it anchors) — in-world scholars can find them, and lore-shokunin gets a working historical-method kit. (3) euhemerization and mythologization are the same pipeline run at different epochs: use them as a pair to give one culture both a deified founder *and* a clerical school that denies him.
- Calibration: euhemerism **fails as a general theory of the gods** — modern scholarship derives most pantheons' names and functions from inheritance and borrowing, not from mortals promoted; it survives as a description of specific historical processes (ruler cults) and as an influential ancient and medieval *interpretation* of myth. Never write "the gods were real kings" as designer fact; write who claims it, and why.

## 7. Calibration: dead frameworks and famous errors in this file's territory

- **Solar mythology / nature-mythology school** (Max Müller, 1856ff): myths as diseased metaphors of the dawn — fully dead by 1900; killed by Andrew Lang's anthropological critique (Custom and Myth, 1884) and by philology. Do not revive even as in-world philology without making it wrong in-world.
- **Panbabylonianism** (c. 1900, the "all myth derives from a Babylonian astral system" school): dead; cited today only as a diffusionist cautionary tale.
- **India-monogenesis** (Benfey, 1859): all folktales from Indian story-collections — dead as a universal claim; useful residue: demonstrable Indian-range diffusion of specific types.
- **Grimm "pure voice of the folk"**: discredited — the brothers worked from educated literary-minded informants, and Wilhelm re-edited across seven editions (1812–1857), Christianizing and moralizing en route (Tatar 1987; Zipes 1988). Craft residue: a "collected folklore corpus" is an *edited artifact*; if your world has a Grimm-like collector, that collection is a character and an agenda, not a dataset.
- **Bascom's four forms** are an etic idealization: boundary cases abound (sacred wonder tales, secular cosmogonies, local saints' legends with mythic time), and the performance/context turn (Ben-Amos 1971) relocates genre in the telling. Keep the table as filing furniture, not as a law of narrative.
- **Psychoanalytic universals** (Bettelheim 1976, The Uses of Enchantment): unsubstantiated as claims about tales' origins or function; treat as reception history only.
- Cross-file citation erratum: comparative-frameworks.md sources list cites Bascom 1965 as "JAF 78(308): 3–20"; the correct record is **78(307): 3–20**, DOI 10.2307/538099 — hand to parent for fix; not edited from this lane's file-writing pass.

## 8. Genre rules for worldbuilding records (house checklist)

Every narrative record this lane hands off carries these fields; a record missing any field is a draft:

1. `form` — Bascom slot (myth / legend / saga / folktale / anecdote-joke / formula tale) + von Sydow slot (memorate / fabulate) where relevant, + the culture's **emic genre word(s)** for it.
2. `truth-claim` — the teller's claim (sacred truth / told as true / told as fiction) as asserted in performance; designer's actual-world truth kept separate (emic/etic rule).
3. `setting` — time and place axes per §1 table; a myth with "recent past" time is not a myth — either the form is drifting or the record is miscast; say which.
4. `cast` — nonhuman vs human protagonists; the nonhuman-cast + primordial-time pair is the myth hinge.
5. `context` — teller class, occasion, venue, forbidden audiences (who may not hear it), expected response formula of the audience.
6. `charter` — Malinowski audit output: what conduct/institution it validates, or `vestigial` / `entertainment` / `subversive`.
7. `functions` — Bascom-1954 tags: entertainment / validation / education / conformity (multi-tag allowed).
8. `index` — ATU number if a type genuinely matches, plus motif letter-tags for search; or a `KTD-T####` lane type id with emic definition; indexes are designer metadata only.
9. `oicotype` — named local variant family and its markers (formula words, species substitution, names, costume details — costume is a couple point: the tale's dress is datable).
10. `contact` — if the same type appears in >1 culture: the contact event (trade route, conquest, mission) or the common-inheritance claim; **never unattributed diffusion** — tale types track language families and contact networks (da Silva & Tehrani 2016, with the §3 caveat).
11. `stratum` — current form + prior form(s) + the in-world event(s) that moved it (§5).
12. `variants` — variant list; never merge records by plot similarity; each telling-situation is its own record.
13. `canon-status` — everything above is a proposal for lore-shokunin; open questions carry explicit `to-verify` marks.

Anti-patterns to refuse: calling every origin story "myth" (it is only a myth when told as sacred truth of primordial time); making all myths charters; letting one performance context carry all four forms; assigning ATU numbers as if the index existed in-world; citing motif ids as "meaning".

## 9. Canon boundary

This file carries classification knowledge and method only. Which version of a story is true in-world, which form a culture holds sacred, who is licensed to tell it, whether the gods were men, and which demotion happened — canon decisions are lore-shokunin's. This lane delivers typed, audited record sets and the §8 checklist; contested items (type-age claims, euhemerism's in-world truth value, genre-stratum events) are flagged, not resolved.
