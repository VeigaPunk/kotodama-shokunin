## 1. Core framework — where sacred numbers come from

- **Craft maxim: a number is sacred because it is load-bearing.** Sacred numbers are almost never invented as mysticism; they are the numbers a culture's *institutions already count with*, then promoted. Three engines, usable as a checklist for any fictional culture: (a) **calendrical arithmetic** — the products of real cycles (7 = week, 12 = months/Jupiter years, 260 = 13×20, 60 = 10×12, 108 = 27×4); (b) **institutional arithmetic** — bureaucracy counts in numbers (provinces, court ranks, banners, gates); (c) **linguistic arithmetic** — the counting base, homophones, and letter-numeral systems of the language itself.
- **Exact number is a cultural technology.** Humans share an approximate number system; exact cardinals require language and counting routines (Dehaene 1997; Frank et al. 2008). Symbolism therefore clings to *named, exact* numbers — and a culture whose lexicon has only "one, two, many" cannot support cosmic arithmetic at all (§10).
- **Numbers organize the map before the heavens**: direction systems, province counts, and court ranks are the cheap observable layer; cosmology is built on top of them (Crump 1990).
- Schimmel (1993) is the standard one-volume comparative survey; Menninger (1969) covers the number-word/notational side; Chrisomalis (2010) is the typology of written numerals and the era-marker file.

## 3. Three — the number of pattern, story, and lot

- **Indo-European**: three-way division of cosmos by lot (Iliad 15.187–193); Vedic three worlds and early triads of gods; the trimūrti (Brahma-Viṣṇu-Śiva) is a *late*, Purāṇic assembly — do not backdate it. Rome: archaic triad (Jupiter-Mars-Quirinus) vs. Capitoline triad (Jupiter-Juno-Minerva) — two triads, one culture, both functional (Dumézil 1966).
- **Literary technology**: threefold repetition is the workhorse of oral narrative — the failed/failed/success schema (Propp 1928); Welsh *Trioedd Ynys Prydein* package entire mythological lore as triads (Bromwich 1961). Design use: triadic packaging is a memory artifact of *oral* strata — a culture with heavy literacy may split its triads.
- **Legal/ceremonial**: threefold oath and three-time repetition as ratification; triple office (consuls, triumvirs) as institutional three.
- **China**: sancai 三才 heaven-earth-human as the Yijing's cosmological triple — a different three with different load.

## 4. Four — the number of the map

- Four is the direction count of most settled cosmologies, and it *oscillates with five* — the moment an axis/center is needed, four becomes 4+1 (quincunx). Pick one per culture and stay consistent.
- **China**: four directional symbols with 7 lunar mansions each (7×4 = 28) — a rare case where two sacred numbers interlock visibly in the sky. The Zeng Hou Yi tomb lacquer box (Warring States) already shows the four symbols around the mansions.
- **Maya**: color-quarters — east red, west black, north white, south yellow, center green (yax, the ceiba); four Bacabs uphold the sky at the corners (Landa c. 1566; Freidel, Schele & Parker 1993).
- **Central Mexico**: Codex Fejérváry-Mayer p. 1 lays the world as a quincunx with the same quarters assigned partly different colors (commonly given as east red, north black, west white, south blue) — neighboring cultures, same structure, different values; variance is itself the lesson.
- **Elsewhere**: four sons of Horus (canopic jars); four dwarves holding the skull-sky (Gylfaginning); evangelist four (man/lion/ox/eagle, from Ezekiel/Revelation); four elements (Empedocles) → four humors (Galen) — medicine inherits the map; Ireland's four provinces + center Mide (Rees & Rees 1961); Inca four suyu radiating ceque lines from Cusco (Zuidema 1964).
- **Design shortcut**: if your culture is imperial, make the four the *administrative* skeleton (provinces, gates, seasonal courts) — then the priests can discover the same four in the sky, and it reads as confirmation rather than coincidence.

## 5. Five — phases, quincunx, ritual pentads

- **Wuxing 五行** (wood fire earth metal water) is the flagship pentad because it is a full correspondence engine, not just a count — the canonical matrix (Needham 1956; Graham 1986; Hongfan chapter of the Shujing):

| Phase | Direction | Color | Season | Organ | Planet |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| wood | east | qing 青 | spring | liver | Jupiter |
| fire | south | red | summer | heart | Mars |
| earth | center | yellow | (transitions) | spleen | Saturn |
| metal | west | white | autumn | lungs | Venus |
| water | north | black | winter | kidneys | Mercury |

- Calibration: the wuxing set is a Warring States–Han systematization; earlier texts count four or other sets — a "five phases" culture implies a bureaucratic-astrological state of a particular era type. Japan imports it wholesale as goshiki for court dress, capitals, onmyōdō (couples: myth/color files).
- **Quincunx**: four directions + center — the Maya and Central Mexican map (§4); the Luoshu 3×3 magic square (every line sums 15) is China's quincunx arithmetic (Needham 1959).
- **Ritual pentads**: five precepts (Buddhism), five pillars (Islam), five Ks (Sikh kakār), Pentateuch, five wounds (devotional Christianity) — five as the count of a *complete rule of practice*.
- **Pythagorean pentad**: pentagram as recognition sign (hygieia); 5 = 2+3, first sum of an even and an odd — the Greek speculative route into five.

## 6. Seven — the week that conquered the world

- **Origin chain, in order**: Babylonian hemerologies mark 7th/14th/21st/28th (and 19th = 7×7) as ill-omened days; the Jewish sabbath week; the **planetary week** — days named by the planet ruling the first hour, in the Chaldean planet order — attested in 1st-c. CE Rome (Pompeii), spread empire-wide, civil rest edict of Constantine (321 CE) locks it in (Zerubavel 1985). Rome's own 8-day nundinal week died in the process — a documented case of one week *replacing* another.
- **Seven heavens**: Jewish b. Hagigah 12b names seven heavens; Qur'an 2:29, 17:44 seven heavens and the seven sleepers (18:9–26). Seven gates of the underworld (Inanna's descent) — Mesopotamian antecedent.
- **Canonical sevens**: wonders of the world (Antipater of Sidon), sages of Greece, liberal arts (trivium + quadrivium, Martianus Capella), deadly sins (Evagrius's eight logismoi pruned to seven by Gregory the Great), seven heavens, seven seas.
- **Sky seven**: Ursa Major as seven sages (Greek), Saptarishi (India), Beidou officials (Daoist cult); Edo-period Japan assembles seven lucky gods (shichifukujin) — seven as a count that *collects* a pantheon.
- **Craft rule**: cosmic sevens follow the calendar seven. A culture without a seven-day week should not have seven heavens by default — export the week (with its liturgy or its astrology) first, and let the seven-ness ride along with the loan.

## 7. Eight and nine — the squares and the imperial counts

- **Eight (bagua 八卦)**: eight trigrams (qian dui li zhen xun kan gen kun) doubling to 64 hexagrams; two canonical arrangements — Fuxi "Earlier Heaven" and King Wen "Later Heaven" — and the Mawangdui manuscript (2nd c. BCE) preserves a *third* order (Shaughnessy 1996). Lesson: trigram order is a historical stratum; give your culture competing orders by school. Buddhist eightfold path and ashtamangala; Egyptian Ogdoad of Hermopolis as four primordial pairs (8 = 2×4); Manchu Eight Banners — a number organizing army *and* society; Sleipnir's eight legs.
- **Nine, China**: nine as supreme yang — Nine Provinces (jiuzhou, Yugong chapter), Nine Tripods (jiuding, legendary regalia in the Shiji), nine-rank bureaucracy (220 CE), gates studded 9×9, ninefold heaven (jiuchongtian), dragon's nine sons (Ming-era canonization), and the homophone jiǔ 九 ≈ 久 "long-lasting" — number luck anchored in phonology.
- **Nine, Norse**: *Hávamál* 138, Odin's nine nights on Yggdrasil; nine mothers of Heimdall (Völuspá hin skamma); Ægir's nine wave-daughters; Snorri's nine worlds — but see §11: the Eddas never enumerate them.
- **Nine, Egypt/Greece/Christendom**: Heliopolitan Ennead (Atum's generations = 9); nine Muses (Theogony); Pseudo-Dionysius's nine angel choirs in three triads — tripartition folding into nine; Dante's nine circles and nine heavens.
- **Design contrast**: nine in China is *vertical* (imperial, yang, rank-count); nine in the North is *initiatory* (self-sacrifice, ordeal, initiation intervals). Same number, opposite institutional load — steal the technique.

## 8. Twelve and thirteen — the calendar pair

- **Twelve's engine**: 12 lunar months per solar year, then everything else derives: Babylonian zodiac (Neugebauer 1952); 12 Olympians mirrored by 12 Titans; 12 tribes → 12 apostles; 12 Imams (Twelver Shi'ism); China's 12 earthly branches paired with 10 heavenly stems → the 60-year cycle, with the branches tracking Jupiter's ~12-year orbit (Needham 1959); Rome's Twelve Tables (c. 451–450 BCE) and twelve lictors; Herakles' twelve labors. Duodecimal residue in language: English *eleven, twelve* (Germanic *twalif "two-left" over base ten — standard hypothesis, contested in detail), dozen and gross, 12 inches, 24 hours.
- **Design note**: twelve is the number that survives de-nominalization — months stay twelve even when the calendar is reformed (intercalation absorbs the error via a 13th month, below).
- **Thirteen, the variance showcase**: (a) Western taboo — "13 at table" from Last Supper iconography; the fear is medieval-to-modern and was *provoked into strength* by the 1880s New York Thirteen Club dining deliberately on Fridays the 13th (Timpson 2015; Britannica, "Thirteen Club"); "triskaidekaphobia" is an early-20th-c. coinage (Coriat 1910; the variant *triakaidekaphobia* appears in 1890s medical dictionaries). (b) Italy flips the valence: 13 lucky, 17 unlucky (XVII rearranged = VIXI, "I have lived" = "I am dead"). (c) Maya: 13 is structural, not superstitious — the tzolk'in is 13×20 = 260, the sky is the realm of Oxlahuntiku (13 sky lords) against Bolontiku (9 lords of night), and the Long Count era rounds on 13 baktuns (Thompson 1950; Schele & Freidel 1990). (d) The 13th month recurs as the *intercalary* slot in luni-solar systems (Metonic cycle, 7 intercalations per 19 years; Hebrew Adar II; Chinese runyue) — 13 as "the month outside order," which is exactly the semantic hook a taboo or an honor can grow on.
- The Norse derivation "Loki was the 13th at Ægir's feast" is a modern head-count imposed on *Lokasenna*, not attested in the poem — do not cite it as a source of the taboo.

## 9. One hundred eight and forty — the derived and the durational

- **108**: a *derived* sacred number — 27 lunar mansions (nakshatra) × 4 quarters = 108; also in the 12×9 arithmetic family. Standard uses: 108-bead mala; 108 kleshas (defilements) — schemas for deriving 108 differ by school, which is itself the craft point: the count is fixed, the derivation negotiable; 108 bell tolls at Japanese New Year (joya no kane) for the 108 bonnō; the Muktikā Upanishad enumerates a canon of 108 Upanishads. Modern "108 = solar/lunar diameter ratio" claims are retrofits — do not use them.
- **Forty**: a *durational* idiom — "40" marks a complete trial, a generation, a purification period, not an exact tally: flood, Sinai, spies, wilderness, Elijah, Nineveh, temptation, resurrection appearances. It hosts real institutions: Lent (40 days), the Sufi chilla (40-day retreat) and the arbaʿīn "forty hadith" genre, the tradition that Muhammad was called at 40 (Ibn Ishaq), and Venice's quarantena — 40 days' maritime isolation, upgraded from Ragusa's 1377 trentino (30) (Tomić & Blažina 2015) — a sacred-adjacent number escaping into public health law.
- Mesopotamian god-numerology assigns each major god a number (Anu 60, Enlil 50, **Ea 40**, Sin 30, Shamash 20, Ishtar 15) written as plain numerals in texts (Livingstone 1986; Oppenheim 1964) — the cleanest ancient case of number as divine title; gives any pantheon a "registry" texture.

| Base | Where | Language-visible traces |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | worldwide default | PIE fully decimal and reconstructible to 10+ (Gvozdanović 1992; Fortson 2004); -teen/-ty layering |

| 12 (residue) | Germanic units, metrology | eleven/twelve as unanalyzable lexemes; dozen/gross; inches/pence |

| 5 (substrate) | quinary systems | usually folded into 10 or 20; "hand" as source of "five" is a widespread etymon (Heine 1997; Zaslavsky 1973) |
| body-part tally | Oksapmin (27 points, Saxe 2012); Torres Straits (Rivers 1901) | counting climbs the body; when the economy monetizes, the sequence gets stretched — arithmetic concepts track institutions |
| approximate only | Pirahã, Mundurukú | "one, two, many" lexicons; speakers estimate accurately but fail exact matching tasks without one-to-one correspondence (Frank et al. 2008; Pica et al. 2004) — contested scope (Everett 2005; Nevins et al. 2009) |

- **Homophony taboos and digit luck** (the phonology→symbolism channel): Sinosphere 4 (sì) ≈ 死 "death" and Japanese 9 (ku) ≈ 苦 "suffering" — floors, seats, plates skip them, euphemisms substitute (yon, kyū); 8 (fa 發) is purchased for luck in phone numbers and prices. This is the Asian structural counterpart to Western 13 — and both are *market-visible*: digit avoidance is traceable in architecture and commerce, a cheap realism marker.
- **Dual numeral systems**: Japanese/Korean/Vietnamese run native and Sino-Xenic numeral series side by side (Korean hana/dul/set vs. il/i/sam) — register and domain split the counting words; numerals are among the most borrowable word classes (Thomason 2001), so a culture's numbers are a map of its liturgies, trade, and administrations.
- **Letter-numerals breed textual numerology**: Greek isopsephy, Hebrew gematria, Arabic abjad — when the script *is* the numeral system, names have sums; the classic read is 666 = "Nero Caesar" in Hebrew letters, with the 616 variant attested in P115 (Metzger 1971; Aune 1998). Renaissance/Indo-Persian chronograms stash dates in verses.
- **Counting is grammar-visible in isolating languages**: Chinese/Japanese need classifiers per noun class (本/枚/匹…) — couples: morphological-typology. Grammatical number itself varies beyond plural: dual, trial, paucal (WALS 2013) — a language with a trial *counts witnesses* differently.
- **Base-arithmetic leak**: vigesimal languages show 20-based arithmetic inside their idioms (Yoruba ẹẹ́dọ́gbọ̀n 35 = "five less than forty" — subtraction-based vigesimal, Zaslavsky 1973); design consequence: let the base show up in prices, oath formulas, and poetry meters, not just in the grammar appendix.

## 11. Calibration flags (contested or discredited — say so in any lore doc)

- **Dumézil's tripartite ideology** (sovereignty/war/fertility as an IE inheritance) is contested: operational for generating consistent panthea, *not* evidence of Indo-European essence (Belier 1991; critiques also by Momigliano and Ginzburg in the 1980s). Use as a design device, label it as one.
- **Snorri's nine worlds**: the enumeration is 13th-c. systematization; no Eddic poem lists nine worlds. Treat as Snorri's catalog, not "the Norse cosmos" verbatim.
- **The "7 chakras" diagram** is one late standardization; tantric sources vary the count and layout widely (White 1996). Popular Western versions are 20th-c. blends.
- **Newton's seven rainbow colors** are an aesthetic choice matching the octave (see color-terminology file §7); a pre-modern culture with a seven-color rainbow is importing Newton.
- **108 = sun/moon diameter ratios**: modern esoteric retrofit, not tradition.
- **"Loki the 13th guest"** origin of the 13 taboo: modern reconstruction; **Friday the 13th** as a compound is 19th-c. (no attested conjunction of the two unluckinesses before the 19th c.; earliest documented English reference: Edwards 1869, *The Life of Rossini*, p. 340; cf. Clar 1957).
- **Trimūrti** is Purāṇic (late), and the wuxing pentad is a specific Warring States–Han systematization — neither is a timeless baseline.
- **Jungian archetypal number mysticism** (von Franz 1974, *Number and Time*) is evocative interpretation, not cross-cultural evidence; keep it out of claims about real cultures.
- **"Pirahã have no numbers"** headlines overstate: the finding is task-specific inexactness plus cultural refusal of counting routines (Frank et al. 2008's own framing; Nevins et al. 2009's critique). Write such cultures as *choosing* approximate practice, not as cognitively deficient.
- Biblical 40s are typological idiom, not arithmetic; do not total them.

## 12. Application protocol — building a fictional culture's number system

1. **Derive, don't decree**: every sacred number must equal a product the culture can actually count — calendar cycles, body-tally length, administrative divisions, market units. A "sacred 7" needs the 7-day week (or seven-anything load-bearing) behind it (§6).
2. **Build the counting language first**: pick the base, derive scale-word etymologies from real objects (tzontli "hair" = 400, xiquipilli "cacao bag" = 8000 — the Nahuatl move), and let those etymologies leak into idiom, meter, and oath.
3. **Institutionalize before mystifying**: provinces, court ranks, banners, gates, bead strings — numbers as bureaucracy first; the priests then "discover" the same numbers in the sky (§4's shortcut).
4. **Add the phonology layer**: generate 1–2 unlucky/lucky digit pairs from homophony, and show the consequences in floor numbering, name choice, and pricing (§10).
5. **Assign each number an institutional valence, not just a meaning**: vertical (imperial nine) vs. initiatory (Norse nine) vs. structural (Maya 13) vs. durational (Near Eastern 40) — same numeral, different load.
6. **Plan variance between neighbors**: contact cultures should share structure but flip values (Maya vs. Central Mexican direction colors; Italian 17 vs. Western 13) — variance is the realism.
7. **If the script doubles as numerals**, textual number-mysticism follows (isopsephy/gematria): saints' names, beast numbers, chronograms, forgeries detectable by sum.
8. **Order-systems as historical strata**: give competing schools different canonical orders (bagua arrangements; Mawangdui), and let a manuscript discovery threaten the orthodoxy — instant plot fuel.
9. **Failure modes**: a full "universal" lucky/unlucky set copied across all your cultures; sacred numbers with no counting institution behind them; cosmic sevens with no week; retrofit scientific constants; treating approximate-number cultures as broken instead of differently-tooled.
