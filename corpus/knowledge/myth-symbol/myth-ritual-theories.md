## 1. Scope, and the one rule that keeps this file safe

- comparative-frameworks.md covers the frameworks that *generate* narratives (monomyth, structuralism, formalism). This file covers the theories that answer a prior question: **where does myth come from and why does a culture have it** — ritual collapse, deified men, decomposed metaphors, social charters, mind structure, psyche, gender history. The two shelves interlock: an origin theory tells you what institutions exist to *keep* myths; a generation engine tells you what the myths then look like.
- Standing rule, per comparative-frameworks §1 and §7: **generate with the theory, never ascribe the theory** — with one principled exception noted in §10: euhemerism and allegory began as *in-world interpretive practices* (Euhemerus was a Hellenistic scholar, Cornutus a Stoic teacher), so those two have legitimate emic-native versions; the 19th–20th-century theories (myth-ritual proper, functionalism, structuralism, psychoanalysis, goddess theory) have only functional equivalents in-world.
- Kirk (1970) is the master calibration source: before extending any theory below, check whether he already demolished or bounded it.

## 2. Myth-ritual school: myth as the shadow of ritual (Frazer → Cambridge → Hooke → collapse → Burkert)

- Core claim: ritual comes first and is primary; myth is a secondary, often degraded byproduct — the words said over or about the rite, or the story invented to explain a rite whose meaning was lost ("ritual decay", Gaster 1950). Frazer's machinery: sympathetic magic on two laws — **similarity** (like produces like: effigies, rain-making) and **contact** (things once joined stay joined: relics, hair, nails) — and the figure of the **divine king** whose vitality is the land's (the rex Nemorensis of Nemi, slain by his successor). The **dying god** (Adonis, Attis, Osiris, Tammuz) is the fertility-year seen as a person.
- Cambridge Ritualists (Harrison 1912; Murray's ritual pattern in Greek tragedy; Cornford): Greek myth and drama "engendered" from initiation and year-rites. Hooke (1933) generalizes into the **pattern of ritual kingship**: (1) sacred marriage of king and goddess-priestess, (2) triumphal procession, (3) ritual humiliation/descent, (4) enthronement/renewal — read onto the Enthronement Psalms and the Babylonian new-year (akītu) rites; Raglan (1936) reduces the hero's life to a **22-point ritual pattern** (miraculous birth → no death, no known tomb), claiming heroes are dramatized ritual kings. Weston (1920) made the Grail a vegetation rite (and, via Eliot's notes on The Waste Land, a piece of modernist texture).
- Calibration — mostly collapsed, then partially rebuilt: Frazer's method (armchair compilation, quotation without context, selective sourcing) is dead; J.Z. Smith (1982) finds **no unambiguous ancient instance of a dying-and-rising deity** — the Tammuz/Adonis "resurrections" are inferences from lament texts that never say the god came back (Mettinger 2001 counters cautiously for a narrow set: Baal, Melqart, Eshmun; treat the whole dossier as contested). Harrison's and Hooke's specific reconstructions are not accepted as history. What survives: Burkert's revival (1972, 1979) is respectable — ritual as real, conservative, biologically grounded behavior, with myth as its explicable verbal complement ("myth as secondary elaboration of ritual" is his formula's direction), and the modest finding that *some* myths do reference rites. Also survives as craft: Smith's standing positions — the mythos-ritual relation lives in **performance**, not in the composition of the myth, and "the interpretation of ritual" is an oxymoron (ritual does not contain its own meaning; Imagining Religion, ch. "The Bare Facts of Ritual").
- Worldbuilding uses: (1) **rites without texts** — generate a culture's ritual core first (processions, calendar, regicide, mysteries), then let some rites have myths and others not; the mythless rite is a ready-made in-world mystery. (2) **Kingship engines**: divine-king vitality, ritual humiliation-and-renewal, regicide succession — pairs with costume (regalia as the myth you wear). (3) **Mystery-cult plots**: the initiation contains the truth; the myth told publicly is the cover story. (4) **Ritual-decay archaeology in-world**: a rite surviving its god, or a myth performed nowhere — either is a historical inference your in-world scholars can fight over. (5) Calibrate via Raglan: never let all heroes fit all 22 points; the deviations are the culture's fingerprint (couples hero-journey-variants.md).

## 3. Euhemerism: the gods were men once

- Core claim (Euhemerus, Sacred History, fragmentary): the pantheon is deified mortals — Uranus, Cronus, Zeus were ancient kings of Panchaea, promoted for benefaction. Transmission: Diodorus Siculus; Ennius' Latin translation; Lactantius (Divinae institutiones 1) preserves Euhemerus as a Christian anti-pagan weapon.
- Calibration — two things are true at once. As **universal history**, euhemerism is discredited: there is no evidence Zeus, Apollo, or the rest were historical men, and treating all gods this way is a category error. But as an **institution**, deification of mortals is real and datable: Hellenistic ruler cult (Alexander's theos decrees after 324 BC; Ptolemaic dynastic cults), the Roman imperial apotheosis (Divus Augustus; the funeral eagle), and non-classical parallels — Japan: Toyotomi Hideyoshi deified as Hokoku Daimyōjin, Tokugawa Ieyasu as Tōshō Daigongen at Nikkō; China: the general Guan Yu → Guandi, the 10th-century woman Lin Moniang → Mazu/Tianhou; Hawaiian deified chiefs; Malay keramat saints; the debated Khmer devarāja (whose very translation — "god who is king" vs "king of gods" — is contested; Mabbett 1969). Also the reverse current: legends accreting onto real founders (the Arthur historicity problem; culture heroes) — mythologization is as real as demythologization.
- Euhemerism as an **in-world genre** has a continuous real history: euhemerizing is what literate cultures do to dead pantheons — medieval Trojan-origin genealogies for national gods, and Snorri's Prose Edda Prologue, which makes the Æsir human chieftains out of Asia whose graves became cults. That is not 19th-century theory; that is an in-world Christian scholar already doing euhemerism on his own ancestors' religion. Hume (1757) is the modern naturalist descendant.
- Worldbuilding uses: (1) **Pantheon growth mechanics**: gods are added by ledger — benefaction, conquest, plague-ending, martyrdom as promotion criteria; a "who may be deified" law is instant political theology (living rulers: yes/no is the fault line). (2) **Historiographic schism**: temple orthodoxy (the god is eternal) vs royal chroniclers (the god was the dynasty's 9th king) — a native euhemerist is a believable heretic. (3) **State-shaking debunking plots**: whoever proves the god was a man has reorganized the constitution. (4) **Cult competition**: Mazu-style title escalation (a local ghost promoted through court petitions to national goddess) is a whole genre of bureaucratic religious politics. (5) Per §1, this is one of two theories a fictional culture can hold *natively* — an in-world royal genealogist is a euhemerist avant la lettre.

## 4. Allegory and the nature-myth school: two different things that both collapsed (one survived as a practice)

- **Ancient allegory** is exegesis, not origin theory: myths *already exist*; the interpreter finds other content in them. Physical allegory (Stoic): Zeus = aithēr, Hēra = air, Poseidon = water (Cornutus, extant); Theagenes of Rhegium c. 525 BC is the traditional first Homeric allegorist (known only via later testimony). Moral/spiritual allegory (Neoplatonic): the Odyssey as the soul's voyage (Porphyry's Cave of the Nymphs); the medieval Christian **fourfold sense** (literal, allegorical, tropological, anagogical) and Jewish PaRDeS are institutionalized descendants. Renaissance handbook: Conti's Mythologiae (1551) — the era's costume and emblem trade (Alciato's Emblemata) runs on it; Bacon's Wisdom of the Ancients is its late literary form. Status: not falsifiable (it is a reading practice), never collapsed as a practice, and still operating.
- **The nature-myth school** (Müller, 1856–75) was a genuine origin theory built on comparative philology: myths are "a disease of language" — decomposed solar metaphors. The engine: shared words yield shared gods (Dyaus Pitar = Zeûs patḗr = Iūpiter, the sky-father); then every hero becomes a dawn myth (Achilles' horses as morning/evening; the school overgenerated spectacularly).
- Collapse: Andrew Lang (1884, 1897) — the etymologies are mostly unsound, and the same tale-types appear among peoples whose languages could not have run the supposed process; the mechanism fails its own comparative test. By 1900 the school was a byword. **What survived**: the *Dyēus ph₂tēr* formula itself is sound and central to reconstructed PIE religion (Mallory & Adams 2006), and comparative Indo-European mythology is now done with formulae and narrative structures, not single etymologies (West 2007). Calibration lesson, one line: **an etymology can seed a myth; it cannot bear a theory.** Fictional mechanism note: folk etymology generating myth is real and generative — place-name legends, saint-name puns (couples historical-linguistics.md); use it as an in-world process, not as your analysis of real cultures.
- Worldbuilding uses: (1) **Exegete factions**: temple riddlers (allegorists) vs literalists is a native, endless, low-violence schism — excellent background texture that also licenses re-readings of any canon text on demand. (2) **The dead-language fossil plot**: a myth that is a decomposed metaphor of an extinct language — decoding it reveals a predecessor culture (the Müller mechanism, made safe by being fictional and *constrained*). (3) **Emblem and costume systems**: allegory is the theory that justifies iconographic costume — each virtue/sin/season has a legible dress code (couples costume, symbolism-synthesis-method.md). (4) Second emic-native theory per §1: an in-world allegorist is period-authentic (Renaissance-like eras breed them).

## 5. Functionalism: myth as the social charter (Malinowski)

- Core claim: myth is not bad science or degraded ritual but a hard-working **social warrant** — it justifies institutions by narrating their origin as precedent. Trobriand material: garden-magic origin stories license land tenure and chiefly rank; Kula myths license exchange partnerships; the charter names names, places, and precedence. Malinowski: "the myth is to the savage what, to a fully believing Christian, is the Biblical tale of Creation" (Myth in Primitive Psychology, 1926 — the register is his; quote with care). Radcliffe-Brown (1922) is the adjacent "myth expresses social structure" line; Kluckhohn (1944) runs it on witchcraft ideology. Bascom's four functions (validation, education, conformity, entertainment) are the tidy descendant (cross-ref comparative-frameworks §1).
- Calibration: charter-talk explains **persistence**, not origin (why *this* story? functionalism is silent); it presumes consensus and stability, ignores power and history — charters legitimate *somebody's* advantage, and functionalism underdescribes who loses; it is circular as explanation (institutions exist because myths warrant them; myths persist because institutions need them). Source calibration: Malinowski's posthumously published Diary (1967) wrecked his saintly-fieldworker image; the analysis survives, the persona does not.
- Worldbuilding uses — the **charter procedure**, the most mechanical of all these tools: (1) pick the institution (irrigation district, ferry guild, marriage class, boundary shrine); (2) draft its origin myth with proper nouns — founder, plot, precedence claims, first precedent adjudicated; (3) attach it to a performance context (recited at the institution's festival, in archaic register — couples orality-formulaic-language.md); (4) **deliberately generate the rival variant**: the upstream clan's charter names a different founder and different precedence — the legal quarrel is pre-loaded, and lore-shokunin gets a live conflict, not a resolved one. (5) Charter register rule: the more archaic the language, the more binding the claim — charters are where language change becomes law (couples politeness-taboo-euphemism.md for the sacred-danger vocabulary).

## 6. Structuralism: myth as the mind resolving contradictions (pointer file)

- Origin claim (Lévi-Strauss 1955; Mythologiques 1964–71): myths arise because the human mind operates in binary oppositions and generates myths as attempts to mediate contradictions it cannot resolve (raw/cooked, nature/culture, autochthony/kinship — the Oedipus analysis is the exemplar). Unlike the theories above, this one needs no ritual, no history, and no society — just cognition.
- Calibration: as origin theory it is unfalsifiable — segmentation into mythemes is arbitrary (Leach 1970; Derrida 1967), and the "resolves X" conclusion can be produced for any input; Dundes's emic correction (derive the binaries from the culture's own categories) is the usable refinement. Full mechanics, worked example, and generation procedure live in comparative-frameworks.md §4 — do not duplicate them here; use that file's engine and this file's origin-claim framing when you need to know *why* a culture might have myth at all.

## 7. Depth psychology: Freud and Jung — and the cognitive successors that replaced them

- Freud: Totem and Taboo (1913) derives totemism, taboo, and gods from a single fantasy event — the primal horde's parricide and inherited guilt. Calibration: built on two pillars that are both gone — Darwin's primal-horde hypothesis and Robertson Smith's totemism; the anthropological data (Australia) does not say what he needed; Moses and Monotheism (1939) is speculative to the point of fiction. His **universal Oedipus** drew the first real cross-check: Malinowski (1926) showed the Trobriands route the lethal rivalry uncle↔nephew and reserve the strongest taboo for sister-incest — the complex follows kinship structure, not biology; Spiro (1958) later found partial Oedipal patterns in kibbutz children, so treat the anti-universality as strongly evidenced, not settled.
- Jung: the **collective unconscious** and **archetypes** (CW 9i; with Kerényi 1941/1949 on Kore and the Divine Child) — myths recur because they express universal psychic structures reached by the **amplification** method (piling up parallels until a pattern appears). Calibration: the argument is circular (archetypes are inferred from myth parallels, which are then explained by archetypes); there is no mechanism by which specific memories could be genetically inherited — say this plainly; unfalsifiability means Jungian readings are design choices, not findings. Kerényi's later handbooks (Gods of the Greeks, 1951) are, ironically, reliable sober mythography by the same hand — use those for data, not the theory.
- What replaced them (and is directly generative): the **cognitive science of religion**. Boyer (2001): religious concepts are **minimally counterintuitive agents** — ordinary intuitions plus one or two violations (a person who is dead but answers questions; a thing that is invisible but eats). Whitehouse (2004): **imagistic vs doctrinal modes** — rare traumatic revelation rituals vs frequent repetitive orthodoxy — predicts two whole religious ecologies from transmission mechanics. Lawson & McCauley (1990): ritual form (who acts on whom with what) predicts perceived efficacy.
- Worldbuilding uses: (1) Freud/Jung survive as **craft vocabularies** for character interiority — shadow-figures, anima/animus, family romance — and for dream-logic sequences; fine in the margin of your notes, never in in-world mouths. (2) An in-world analog exists as *moralizing exegesis* — priests who read myths as allegories of vices — historically native (medieval moralized Ovids) without any anachronism. (3) Boyer's MCI template is the **deity design spec**: 1–2 violations of intuitive ontology per supernatural agent; more violations and the concept is unmemorable, fewer and it is not divine (couples pantheon-archetypes.md). (4) Whitehouse's two modes generate your rite system's split: a Mystery (imagistic: rare, brutal, revelatory, no written doctrine) vs a Church (doctrinal: daily liturgy, text, orthodoxy) — one culture can hold both in tension.

## 8. Feminist critique and goddess theory: Bachofen → Gimbutas, calibrated on two separate tracks

- The line begins with Bachofen, Das Mutterrecht (1861): a universal stage of mother-right and matriarchy before patriarchy; Engels (1884) canonized the sequence on Marxist terms ("world-historic defeat of the female sex"). As history this is discredited: no attested matriarchal *state*; matrilineality ≠ matriarchy (descent through mothers is common; rule by mothers is not attested).
- Gimbutas's Old Europe thesis (1974, 1989, 1991): Neolithic southeastern Europe as a peaceful, sedentary, **goddess-centered matristic civilization** (figurines = goddess cult; one Great Goddess in many guises), destroyed by patriarchal Kurgan (steppe) horsemen. Criticism: Ucko (1968) already showed figurines are heterogeneous (male, animal, abstract) and context-starved; Meskell (1995) called the apparatus "New Age archaeology"; Tringham & Conkey (in Goodison & Morris 1998) found no basis for a unified goddess cult — the sober conclusion is that *some* female figurines may be divine, that "the Goddess" as a single pan-Neolithic religion is projection, and that Gimbutas's symbol "grammar" reads decoration as scripture.
- The **two-track calibration** — the nuance a careful lane must state: on one track, Gimbutas the Indo-Europeanist's **Kurgan/steppe dispersal hypothesis is now mainstream**, strengthened beyond her evidence by ancient DNA (Anthony 2007; Haak et al. 2015: massive steppe ancestry in Corded Ware Europe, against Renfrew's 1987 Anatolian-farming alternative (Jonathan Cape), which still holds for some branches — contested at the margins). On the other track, the **matriarchal-goddess reading is not accepted**. Same scholar, same books: vindicated archaeolinguistics, rejected religious reconstruction. Citing "Gimbutas" without splitting the tracks is the classic amateur tell; this lane splits them every time.
- The **movement** dimension is real and useful as design material: goddess spirituality (Starhawk 1979; Eisler 1987) constructs, and says it constructs — a living religion inventing an ancient mother-religion on purpose. That is an *in-world event generator*: any fictional culture can have a Gimbutas-like scholar and a revivalist movement making a usable past (the mirror-image of euhemerism's debunking). Serious matrilineal exemplars to draw on instead of stereotype: Minangkabau (West Sumatra — the largest matrilineal society; Sanday 2002 argues for "matriarchy" in a carefully redefined sense), Mosuo (matrilineal households, visiting unions), Khasi.
- Worldbuilding uses: (1) **Rival in-world archaeologies**: two schools fighting over the same figurine corpus (goddess-cult vs household toys vs votive anatomy) — a researched quarrel you can lift whole. (2) **Gender-coded institutions**: what an actually matrilineal-and-matrilocal culture produces (house-as-continuity, men-out, uncle office) is stranger and better than a generic "matriarchy" — model on Minangkabau, not on inversion-swap. (3) **Recovery plots**: suppressed older cult reconstructed from fragments — including the fraud temptation (the "artifact" too good to be true). (4) Warning: a one-Goddess monolith reads as Gimbutas pastiche; distribute the divine feminine into many uncoordinated local figures instead.

## 9. Adjacent theory: Dumézil's trifunctionalism (one paragraph, because calibration demands it)

- Claim: Indo-European myth and society encode a three-function ideology — **sovereignty** (magico-legal, often split dark/light: Varuna/Mitra; Jupiter), **war** (Indra; Mars), **fertility/production** (Nāsatyas; Quirinus) — mirrored in social estates (priests/warriors/producers) and epic (Odysseus/Achilles/Menelaus as function-triads). Status: heavily contested — comparison without the phonological anchoring that legitimates linguistic reconstruction (Belier 1991: single matches presented as systems); the "Dumézil affair" (Momigliano & Ginzburg's politics charges; the scholar's wartime far-right ties) kept it radioactive; Bruce Lincoln (1986 → recanted in the epilogue of 1999) is the exemplary trajectory: a brilliant practitioner who dismantled his own method. Use: generating a pantheon's *social* geometry (which god licenses which estate) is a strong tool; claiming an ideology is inherited Indo-European blood-memory is the failure mode. Model in-world analog: state theologies that map gods onto estates — that mapping is historically real everywhere (medieval estates sermons), so generate it freely, attribute it to nobody's ancestors.

## 11. Theories as in-world positions — the periodization move

- Emic-native theories (per §1): euhemerism and allegory have real historical in-world careers; assign them to offices — **royal genealogist** (euhemerist), **temple riddler / emblemist** (allegorist). All others get functional equivalents only: **liturgist** who dates every myth to its festival (myth-ritualist), **clan advocate** reciting charters (functionalist), **numerologist** pairing opposites (structuralist), **moralizing homilist** reading myths as vices (Freudian), **cloistered visionary** amplifying symbols (Jungian), **heresiarch priestess** recovering the Mother (goddess-theory), **bureaucrat of cult promotion** petitioning for a saint's title (euhemerist-institution).
- Meta-calibration (Lincoln 1999): the real history of myth scholarship is itself ideological — Müller's philology was racial, Dumézil's executor-politics were contested, Eliade's nostalgia had a politics, and the goddess debate is gender politics conducted with potsherds. In-world, the same holds, and it is a deep-time generator: **which hermeneutic a fictional era's scholars favor tells you what that era's power wants.** A three-age culture can shift temple riddlers (allegorists) → royal genealogists (euhemerists) → charter lawyers (functionalists) and thereby narrate its own regime changes without a single explicit history. Flag for lore-shokunin: the *theory history* is as load-bearing as the myths.

## 12. Era, costume, and handoff notes

- Era flavor: myth-ritual material is strongest for temple-state and calendar cultures (agrarian kingship); euhemerism needs literacy and an archive (it is a theory with a civil service); allegory flourishes in eras with canonical but embarrassing texts (conquest, contradiction, obsolete gods); charters suit customary-law societies; structuralist families suit long-lived oral corpora; depth-psychology texture suits dream/vision episodes; goddess-vs-steppe is a bronze-age-collapse-and-after flavor (Kurgan shifts, island refuges).
- Costume hooks, per theory: regalia of renewal (myth-ritual: the king's humiliated dress then enthroned splendor); deified-ruler iconography and shrine architecture (euhemerism: Nikkō-style mausolea, apotheosis coins); allegorical emblem dress (each figure's legible attributes — the Conti/Alciato apparatus); charter tablets and recitation staves (functionalism); votive figurines as contested evidence (goddess theory); mystery-initiation dress vs doctrinal vestments (Whitehouse's two modes, worn).
