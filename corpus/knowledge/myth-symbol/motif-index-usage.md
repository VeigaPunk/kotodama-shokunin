## 1. Two layers: motifs are atoms, types are bundles

- **Motif** (Thompson's own formulation): "the smallest element in a tale having a power to persist in tradition" — an incident, object, character, or relationship that travels between tales (Thompson 1946; Motif-Index introduction). **Type**: a self-contained traditional tale with a nameable plot arc, told as a whole, with a documented variant record (Aarne & Thompson 1961; Uther 2004).
- Notation: a motif is letter+number (C31, D672) with decimal depth and a `+` extension sign for composite references (e.g. `C40.+`); a type is a bare number with optional subletter (ATU 425C). Both grids are **etic inventories of real tradition**: they describe what is attested; they derive nothing and license nothing.
- The relation is many-to-many, and that is the tool. A type carries a bundle of motifs — its load-bearing string; a motif recurs across many types. Real worked example: **ATU 551 "The Water of Life" carries the motif references H80, H81.1, H1210.1, H1242, H1321.1, H1381.2.1** (Uther 2004) — the test/quest block of the H-chapter. Craft consequence: for an invented tale, write the string first, then look up which real types share it. The string is the culture's fingerprint; the "cf. type" is only its nearest Earth neighbor.
- Coverage asymmetry (maps onto Bascom's genre tiers, comparative-frameworks.md §1): the type index covers the folktale proper; migratory legend has its own index (Christiansen's ML 3000 series); myth has no types at all, only motif entries in the A-chapter. Audit a culture's three narrative tiers with different grids — tier-1 myth gets motif strings only.

## 3. The ATU type grid: seven genre groups, edition drift, regional practice

| Numbers | Group | Real exemplars |
|---|---|---|
| 1–299 | Animal tales | ATU 2 The Tail-Fisher |
| 300–749 | Tales of Magic | ATU 300 The Dragon-Slayer; 311/312 the murderous-bridegroom block (Fitcher's Bird / Bluebeard); 313 The Magic Flight; 410 Sleeping Beauty; 425C Beauty and the Beast; 510A Cinderella / 510B Donkeyskin–Cap o' Rushes; 551 The Water of Life |
| 750–849 | Religious Tales | ATU 750A The Three Wishes |
| 850–999 | Realistic Tales (Novellen) | ATU 870 The Princess Confined in the Mound; 875 The Clever Farmgirl |
| 1000–1199 | Tales of the Stupid Ogre | ATU 1000 Contest not to Become Angry; 1119 The Ogre Kills His Mother (Wife) |
| 1200–1999 | Anecdotes and Jokes | ATU 1525 The Master Thief |
| 2000–2399 | Formula Tales | cumulative and catch tales (The House That Jack Built family) |
| 2400–2499 | Unclassified Tales | the index's own residual — not free space (see §7) |

- **Edition drift is real and citable**: assignments and titles shift across Aarne 1910 → AT 1928/1961 → ATU 2004. Confirmed instance: type 1119 is "The Ogre Kills His Mother (Wife)" in ATU 2004, formerly "The Ogre Kills His Own Children". Always cite the edition with the number; a bare "AT 480" in an old source may not be Uther's 480.
- **Regional and genre indexes exist and should be imitated in-world**: Irish types (FFC 188), Chinese oral plus classical-literature types (Ting, FFC 223 — the Ye Xian variant in Duan Chengshi's Youyang zazu, c. 860 CE, is the classic payoff of indexing classical literature: a Cinderella-family tale a millennium before Perrault), Indic oral tales (Thompson & Roberts 1960), migratory legends (ML 3000 series). A serious world bible makes its own culture's index in the same shape: type name, motif string, variant list.

## 4. Calibration: the "six motif classes" garble, and what is discredited

- **There is no six-class scheme in Thompson.** The Motif-Index has 23 lettered chapters bound as six volumes; the type index has seven genre groups (ATU 2004). Briefs asking for "Thompson's six motif classes" are garbling the volume count (or the type-groups). Use the real structure. If a six-bucket view is operationally useful, use the derived grouping below — and label it as ours, never as Thompson's.
- **Derived six-family working grouping (kotodama lane; covers all 23 chapters):**

| Family | Chapters | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Genesis stratum | A, U, V | where the world, death, and cult come from |
| 2 Nonhuman population | B, G | who else is out there |
| 3 Sealed wonders | C, D, F | power and its prohibitions |
| 4 The other shore | E, M, N | death, doom, chance |
| 5 The moral sieve | H, L, P, Q, R, S, T, W | worth, desert, society |
| 6 The low road | J, K, X, Z | wit, fraud, laughter, residue |

A culture's palette reads as six sliders instead of 23 counts (see §5).

- **Discredited or contested, stated plainly:** Max Müller's solar mythology (tales as sky/weather allegory) is dead; Saintyves-style naturist allegorism died with it. Finnish-school single-origin claims ("type X originated in…") are hypotheses, not results — Krohn's variant-clustering is durable method, origin-assertion from it mostly is not. Propp (1928) showed the motif is not the productive unit of the wondertale — the function sequence is (comparative-frameworks.md §5); use motifs for inventory and comparison, functions for morphology. Dundes (1962): both indexes are etic — indispensable for cross-cultural contrast, silent on meaning; meaning requires emic reading. Witzel's (2012) "Laurasian mythology" mega-reconstruction is contested at the level of method, not of data availability.
- The index is a finding aid, not a theory of tradition. The same motif carries opposite meanings across cultures; treat any reading that treats a motif number as bearing its own meaning as an error.

## 5. The coherence audit: five checks on a fictional tale inventory

Procedure: (0) list the culture's tales, tiered (myth / legend / folktale per Bascom); (1) tag each with tier, provisional `cf.`-type, and motif string; (2) build the palette — counts per chapter, or the six sliders; (3) run:

1. **Negative evidence.** Hot and cold chapters must match material life: a seafaring, raiding culture with cold R (captives) and N (fate) chapters is wrong — or is data: make the absence itself a belief ("the Sea-Folk refuse tales of captives"). Absences are as load-bearing as counts.
2. **Tabu symmetry.** Every C-class motif must trace to a real tabu in the language/costume/cult files — and conversely, each major real tabu system (naming, food, sight, silence) should surface in at least one tale. Mismatch means the culture does not hang together (couples politeness-taboo-euphemism, naming-systems).
3. **Reward economy.** Q-heavy inventories encode earned moral causality; D/F/N-heavy ones encode gift-magic and luck. A culture whose official religion (V) preaches desert while its tales run on luck has a hypocrisy seam — excellent material, but make it deliberate, not accidental.
4. **Era calibration.** Palette shifts are era fingerprints: heroic/migration-era stock skews A/B/E/H; mercantile-urban stock skews K/J/X plus the 850–999 novellen. A culture retelling its founders' myths as K-chapter farce has told you its era changed without a word of your history text (couples era files).
5. **Contact fingerprints.** Shared type with different motif load = recent neighbors. Shared motif string with no shared type = deep stratum or old borrowing (couples language-contact). Identical type and identical string across two of your cultures = design failure, not kinship.

- Standing rule: **distort exactly one axis per culture** from the Earth-default distribution and log the distortion in the world bible. A culture matching real ATU statistics everywhere reads as cut-paste Earth.

## 6. Orphan motifs as worldbuilding evidence

- Real-tradition logic: motifs occur in families. Variants cluster (Krohn 1926/1971), oral transmission self-corrects (Anderson 1923), and motifs have cousins across types — E323.2 serves the 510A-family tales; D672 serves the 313-family flights. A motif attested once and nowhere else is, to the historic-geographic method, a flag: invention, mistranscription, or survival from an unrecorded tradition.
- In-world readings for an orphan in your inventory — choose deliberately, it is evidence either way: (a) **contact-zone survivor**: a single foreign tale adopted, its siblings rejected (evidence of a trade route, mission, or war prize); (b) **gatekeeper-class motif**: told only by priests, bards, or lineage singers, invisible to the folk layer (couples orality-formulaic-language); (c) **broken tradition**: a suppression event killed the tale-type but one motif fossilized in jokes or lullabies — the motif is the scar; (d) **era fossil**: an A-chapter creation image surviving inside a K-chapter farce — the culture outgrew the myth, not the image.
- Craft rule: **seed families, not loners.** Before an invented motif counts as the culture's own, generate 2–5 tellings: the type-analog, a variant, a half-remembered fragment, optionally a hostile parody. Worked micro-example — orphan: "an ember hidden in a pot of wet rice." Seed: a 480-family tale (kind girl rewarded by hearth-spirits), an A1335-fragment (fire withheld when death entered the world), a K-analog (the ember stolen from the sky-keeper's hearth). The family now evidences a fire-history, a hearth cult, and a theft doctrine — three files updated from one motif, and the orphan is no longer suspicious because it has relatives.

## 7. Notation and handoff protocol

- Strings: `cf. ATU 425C [C31 + D-series + F-series]`. Never mint ATU numbers — the 2400–2499 block is the index's own "unclassified" range, not free space for inventions. Earth types take "ATU"; invented tales take "cf." + nearest type + own string + the culture's own tale name.
- Cite the edition (AT 1961 vs ATU 2004) whenever the number matters; keep ATU 1119's re-labeling in mind as the standing example of drift.
