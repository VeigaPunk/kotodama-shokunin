## 1. The calibration rule — read before porting anything

- **NEVER port a color code across cultures wholesale.** A color never carries a meaning by itself; a **code** does. A code is a five-place tuple: *term* (what the language calls it) × *carrier* (dye, cloth, ink, skin, paint, flag) × *practice* (wedding, funeral, coronation, sacrifice, war) × *authority* (church, court, guild, caste, state) × *enforcement* (sumptuary law, taboo, liturgy, fashion). Port "white = purity" into a China-like culture and you have dressed the mourners as brides; port "black = evil" into an Egypt-like or Qin-like culture and you have painted the fertile soil and the imperial banners with sin.
- **The flagship inversion pair**: Europe runs *white wedding / black funeral*; Han cultural sphere runs (traditionally) *red wedding / white funeral*; India runs *red bridal / white widow*. Same two hues, complementary assignments — every assignment is code-internal, none is "natural."
- **Codes flip within a culture across eras too**: Japan's funeral black (*mofuku*) is largely a Meiji-era Westernizing import layered over the older white corpse-clothes (*shinishōzoku*), which persist beneath the black ((to-verify) on the Meiji dating); Rome's bride went veiled in flame-orange (*flammeum*), not white, and Europe's white bridal norm dates to Victoria's 1840 wedding plus 19th-c. dress culture (Sebesta & Bonfante 1994; Pastoureau 2023).
- **One color, two rival codes in one civilization**: classical Confucian purple is an *intercalary usurper* (§9); Daoist-imperial purple is transcendence itself. Eras apart, same civilization, same hue.
- **The lexicon and the symbolism are separable systems.** A culture can name few colors and wield them with ritual precision, or name many and attach little weight. Set the term inventory first (color-terminology.md §1–8), then load meanings here.
- **The in-world test**: can a character be *wrong* about a color? Can a mourning envoy in white horrify a black-clad court, a purple robe start a succession quarrel, an heirloom crimson pass as counterfeit? If no scene can misread a color, you have written a logo, not a code.

## 2. Named frameworks — what each is good for, what to distrust

| Berlin & Kay (1969) and successors | Implicational *lexical* stages; foci more universal than boundaries | Naming inventories (consistency checklist) | Says nothing about meanings; see color-terminology.md §1–3 — do not cite it as a symbolism source |

| Gage (1993) | Color *ordering* is period-specific: hue-wheels, primaries, and "complementary pairs" are post-Newtonian habits | Keeps pre-modern cultures out of anachronistic color space (pre-modern orders run on light/dark and saturation, not hue circles) | Giving an Iron Age culture a color wheel; cross-ref color-terminology.md §7 on Newton's rainbow |

## 3. The five-color family — wǔsè and its descendants

- **The Chinese pentad is a correspondence engine, not a palette.** Canonical matrix (Major 1993; Allan 1991; cf. number-symbolism.md §5, which owns the pentad arithmetic):

| Phase | Color | Direction | Season | Creature | Institutional load |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| wood | 青 qīng (blue-green-black span) | east | spring | Azure Dragon | growth; the lexical span warning below |
| fire | 赤 chì (red) | south | summer | Vermilion Bird | joy, life, Han dynastic color |
| earth | 黄 huáng (yellow) | center | (transitions) | — (the sovereign) | the imperial color itself |
| metal | 白 bái (white) | west | autumn | White Tiger | mourning; autumn = execution season (Bodde & Morris 1967 on Qing autumn assizes), so white/west carries death-dealing, not "innocence" |
| water | 黑 hēi (black) | north | winter | Dark Warrior (xuánwǔ) | Qin dynastic color; the dark of heaven (§5) |

- **Dynastic colors are phase-politics**: Qin claimed the water phase (black); the Han cycled through claims before settling on fire/red (Wang 2000; Major 1993). A color's rank is *worn by the state* and renegotiated at each succession — instant plot fuel.
- **Correct vs intermediate colors**: the ritual vocabulary splits 正色 zhèngsè (correct colors: the five) from 间色 jiànsè (intermediate/intercalary colors), with dress rules encoding the hierarchy — upper garment in a correct color, lower in an intermediate (*Liji*, "Yuzao"). Confucius states the politics outright: "I hate that purple overthrows vermilion" (*Analects* 17.18).
- **Rank ladders in dress**: the Tang sumptuary ladder (from the mid-7th c.) assigns purple to 3rd rank and above, crimson 4–5, green 6–7, cyan 8–9, yellow increasingly reserved for the throne (Benn 2002; the yellow edict year is usually given as 674 — (to-verify)). The Song founding is staged as a yellow robe being thrown on the general — 黃袍加身 "the yellow robe added to the body" = "to be made emperor." Qing bright yellow is the terminal exclusivity.
- **Descendants with local variance**: Korea runs the pentad as 오방색 obangsaek (five-direction colors) through dancheong polychromy, hanbok, and striped children's sleeves (saekdongot; (to-verify) for a standard reference); Japan imports it as goshiki for court dress, capitals, and onmyōdō (see color-terminology.md §5). Neighbors share structure, drift values.
- **The lexical trap**: qīng 青 spans blue, green, and black (bronze patina, sky, grass, black horse coats) — the pentad's "five colors" are not five paint chips (color-terminology.md §5). Design consequence: your five-color culture's five terms should each have a messy substance-span, and ritualists should argue about which hues count.
- **Not all quincunxes agree**: the Maya assign east-red, west-black, north-white, south-yellow, center-green (yax); neighboring Central Mexican codices flip some assignments (see number-symbolism.md §4 for the exemplars). Same structure, different values — variance is the realism, and the direct precedent for "your three neighboring cultures share the pentad but quarrel over which color owns the west."

- **Craft reading: white is usually "undone" — undyed, unfed, unattached — hence liminal.** That single logic generates mourning (removed from the color economy), renunciation (same), atonement (same), and bridal transition (same) *without* any shared "purity" concept. Derive your culture's whites from what white *is* materially (undyed hemp, bleached linen, bone, ash, chalk) and the meanings follow the material.

## 5. Black and white — dualism's three structural modes

- **Mode A, complementary**: dark and bright are interdependent cosmic phases, both necessary, neither evil. China: heaven is xuán (dark) — "heaven is dark, earth is yellow" (天玄而地黄, *Yijing* Wenyan); the taijitu diagrams dark-in-light, light-in-dark, each bearing the other's seed (Pregadio 2008, s.v. taiji; Graham 1986 owns the correlative logic — the popular yin-yang "fish" form is a late stabilization, (to-verify) on dating). India: kṛṣṇa "the black one" is the supreme beloved god, not a demon.
- **Mode B, triadic with a mediator**: Turner's Ndembu white–red–black is *not* a dualism — red (blood, hunt, menstruation, mediumship) mediates between the poles (Turner 1967). If you want polarity with warmth rather than war, make it a triad.
- **Mode C, antagonistic moral dualism**: this is the *rare* one, with a genealogy — Zoroastrian cosmic light-vs-dark war (Boyce 1975), absorbed into Christian Europe, where black becomes devil/heretic/sin coloring and white celestial (Pastoureau 2009). Modern racialized white-good/black-evil readings are a colonial-era overlay *on top of* this tradition, and questionnaire studies of "white = good" across cultures (Williams & Best lineage, 1970s–90s) are method-contested ((to-verify)); older systems simply do not sort that way.
- **Black's prestige ledger** (all real, all incompatible with "black = evil"): Egypt names itself the black land (km.t, the fertile flood soil) against the deadly red desert, and black is rebirth-fertile, not sinister (Pinch 2002); Qin's imperial color is black; Puritan Europe wears black as austere piety; 15th-c. Burgundian courts and 16th-c. Habsburg-Spanish courts make black the costliest fashionable color (Pastoureau 2009); Japan's formal dress is crest-black.
- **Institutional black/white warfare is documentable comedy**: Benedictine "black monks" against Cistercian "white monks," feuding for centuries over which habit heaven preferred (Pastoureau 2009). A fictional church schism over chromaticity is not parody — it is medieval Europe.
- **Pattern is not hue**: medieval Europe codes *stripes* as the mark of the excluded — heretics, jesters, hangmen, prostitutes, later convicts' uniforms (*The Devil's Cloth*, Pastoureau 2001). A two-color worldbuilding pass that only tracks hues will miss the second, older channel: pattern symbolism.
- **Design consequence**: pick the dualism mode first, then the chromatics follow. Complementary → two colors interlocked in one emblem (each containing the other); triadic → a third mediator color with passage-rights; antagonistic → rival banners, conversion as re-dyeing, and heresy law written in color.

## 6. Red — luck, protection, status, and the mediator

- **China, the luck/protective complex**: weddings (red bridal standard of late imperial custom — note Zhou ritual wedding dress was dark *xuán* with pale-red *xùn* hems, *Yili*, "Shihun li"; the red wedding is a development, not a prehistoric constant), red envelopes (hóngbāo), red spring couplets, red string amulets, red underclothes in one's zodiac year, and vermillion imperial rescript ink (Qing zhūpī). Red is the life-side of the fire phase; the west/white side owns death (§3).
- **India, the auspicious-marriage complex**: bridal red, sindūr and kumkum in the hair-parting, red powders at rites — marriage and fertility load (Kane 1930–62).
- **Europe, the status complex**: medieval red is *the* prestigious color — dye-scarce, legible as wealth; the cardinals' red hat granted at Lyon in 1245 (Innocent IV) institutionalizes red as rank; England's "Wars of the Roses" is heraldic packaging — the name itself is a Walter Scott coinage (*Anne of Geierstein*, 1829), not contemporary usage (Pastoureau 2017). Rome, by contrast, loads red as working-class/low while putting *purple* borders on status (Sebesta & Bonfante 1994).
- **Egypt's double red**: the red desert (dshr.t) is chaos — Set's color, hostile magic colored red — yet the Red Crown of Lower Egypt is itself named "the red one" (dshret). One civilization, red as threat and red as throne (Pinch 2002; Wilkinson 1994).
- **Deep history**: ochre at Qafzeh burials (~90–100 ka) is the earliest strong archaeological case for color *as symbol* — red was first (Hovers et al. 2003); consistent with red being the one near-universal early chromatic *term* (color-terminology.md §1).
- **African ochre complexes** (Himba, Maasai red-ochre body cosmetics; Turner's Ndembu red as blood/hunt/transition) keep red material: a substance rubbed in, not a chromatic abstraction ((to-verify) for a single comparative source; Turner 1967 covers Ndembu).
- **Calibration**: "red = universal danger/luck" is pop-code. Red's *lexical* priority is real; its *valences* (wedding/danger/threat/throne/low class) never travel without their carriers and institutions. And the lab effect (red teams win slightly more, Hill & Barton 2005) is too small and contested to be a cultural law.

## 7. Yellow, saffron, gold — sacred center vs marked stigma

- **China, the center**: yellow is the earth phase, the center square of the quincunx, and the throne's color — the exclusive end of the Tang-to-Qing reservation ladder (§3; Benn 2002).
- **The robe rules of renunciation**: Buddhist Vinaya restricts robe dyes to plant sources — roots, stems, bark, leaves, flowers, fruits — and to "any color except blue, deep-yellow, crimson, magenta, black" for the tenfault rules on materials; the point is modest non-gaudiness, from which regional palettes grew (Mahāvagga VIII; Schopen 2004 on robe economies). The robe-word itself, Sanskrit kāṣāya, means "reddish-ochre" (Monier-Williams 1899) — the color names the garment, and "saffron robe" in journalism is shorthand for a rule-bound family of ochres. Living divergence: Thai/Lao/Khmer ochre-orange, Myanma maroon, Tibetan maroon (Gelug "yellow hats" vs Nyingma "red hats"), East Asian grey/black/blue work-robes, Zen darks.
- **Hindu and Jain chromatic solutions**: saffron/ochre as the renouncer's color — traditionally glossed as fire-colored, burning off the householder identity (traditional interpretation; the institution is documented in Olivelle 1993); Sikhs keep kesari for the Nishan Sahib and Nihangs wear blue; Jains split *within one religion* — Śvetāmbara "white-clad" monks vs Digambara "sky-clad" (unclothed) — opposite chromatic ideologies, same lineage.
- **Israelite tabernacle**: the canonical textile is blue, purple, and scarlet (Exodus 26:1), with the blue tassel (tekhelet) as commandment-token (Numbers 15:38) — an *institutionally prescribed* three-color code at the text's core (dye-species identity in color-terminology.md §5).
- **Europe's yellow stigma**: Judas dressed yellow in medieval art (Giotto's Arena Chapel kiss); the Fourth Lateran Council (1215, canon 68) requires Jews and Muslims to be dress-distinguishable, generating the yellow badge — the direct ancestor-chain to the Nazi star is a real continuity, not an analogy (Pastoureau 2019). Then English slang flips yellow to cowardice and "yellow journalism" (1890s press wars) — a color stigmatized by *one* tradition and enthroned by another.
- **Ireland's trichromatic martyrdoms**: red martyrdom (blood), white martyrdom (exile/asceticism), blue/green martyrdom (penance) — an early Irish classification with three liturgical colors of dying (Stancliffe 1982).
- **Gold is not yellow's synonym but its tech**: divine flesh in Egypt (the gods' flesh is gold, their hair lapis — Wilkinson 1994; Pinch 2002), icon backgrounds, relic-work — gold = unquenchable light, affordable only to gods and their treasuries; ultramarine contracts show the same logic in blue (color-terminology.md §6).
- **The institutional color-clock**: Roman-rite liturgical colors are codified — white/gold (Christmas, Easter), red (Passion, martyrs, Pentecost), green (Ordinary Time), purple (Advent, Lent), black or violet at funerals, rose twice a year (GIRM 2002 §346); Orthodox practice runs a different, richer set. A fictional church's calendar-in-color is a cheap, deep realism device — and calendar disputes become chromatic disputes.

## 8. Blue and green — prestige flips and lexical spans

- **Blue's rise is the flagship prestige-flip**: in Rome, blue is barbarian/Celtic and low; blue eyes mark the foreigner. The medieval reversal runs through Abbot Suger's Saint-Denis glass (1140s), the Capetians' blue banner, and the Virgin's mantle — blue ends the Middle Ages royal and Marian (Pastoureau 2001; cross-ref color-terminology.md §6 for the ultramarine-contract economics).
- **Green in Islam**: green attaches to the Prophet and to the green dome at Medina, and becomes pan-Islamic over centuries; but the early caliphal banner colors are white (Umayyad) and black (Abbasid revolution) — green's monopoly is a later layering (Kennedy 1986; Pastoureau 2014). Do not paint early Islamic polities green.
- **Ireland**: the old royal-associative color is blue ("St. Patrick's blue"); green nationalizes with the United Irishmen era (1790s) — a modern political re-code ((to-verify) for a standard treatment).
- **Green's poison decade**: arsenic greens (Scheele's green, Paris green) decorate and kill across the 19th c. — wallpaper, dresses, corpses; a documented color *substance* horror (Finlay 2002). Design lever: your culture's signature color can be literally toxic, and fashion can wear it anyway.
- **Egypt's green**: wadj means green and "to flourish"; Osiris is green-faced as regeneration; "doing green things" = prospering (Wilkinson 1994). A green deity is a *growth* claim, not an envy claim.
- **China's green trap**: green is spring/east/wood in the pentad, but a Yuan-dynasty rule forced male kin of brothel households into green headwear — the ancestor of the idiom 戴绿帽 "wearing a green hat" = being cuckolded ((to-verify) on the edict text). One green, three loads: cardinal direction, spring rite, and a portable sexual insult.
- **Lexical spans leak into ritual**: qīng 青 and Japanese ao cover blue-green-black spans, and Japan's green traffic light is officially "ao" (color-terminology.md §4–5); Vietnamese xanh splits by *xanh da trời* (sky) vs *xanh lá* (leaf) — substance-qualified, Hanunóo-style. Ritual colors should cite *substances* (malachite, indigo, murex, kermes), not wavelengths.

## 9. Purple — the two prestige codes

- **Mediterranean: purple = monopoly rank.** Murex purple is fabulously expensive (thousands of snails per gram of dye; yields debated — Cardon 2007), becomes Rome's status marker (toga praetexta's purple border for magistrates and freeborn children; broad purple stripe for senators — Sebesta & Bonfante 1994), then Byzantium's imperial monopoly with the phrase "born in the purple" (porphyrogénnētos) and law-enforced exclusivity (Cardon 2007).
- **Classical China: purple = intercalary usurper.** It is a jiànsè, and the *Analects* line (17.18) names purple's "overthrowing" of vermilion as the emblem of disorder — legitimate color is the *correct five* (§3).
- **Imperial-Daoist China: purple = transcendence.** 紫氣東來 "purple vapor comes from the east" (Laozi's pass); the Ziwei 紫微 celestial enclosure around the pole star; the Purple Forbidden City (紫禁城) — purple as the color *north of north*, the emperor's cosmic address.
- **The same civilization then reverses itself institutionally**: Tang dress law gives purple to the 3rd rank and above (Benn 2002) — the usurper color wins and is legalized. Heian Japan inherits purple as high rank (murasaki; Dalby 1993 on court color-ranking), with Edo sumptuary edicts policing it against commoners ((to-verify) on a specific edict).
- **Craft reading**: "purple = royalty" is not portable — it is one historical resolution (dye-scarcity + monopoly enforcement) of a contest that classical China *also* ran, and answered differently first. Your fictional purple should arrive with its snails (or its monopoly edict), its laws, and ideally a rival faction still quoting the "usurper" doctrine.

## 10. Why codes exist — scarcity, law, enforcement, and collapse

| Chromophore | Source | Scarcity driver | Institutional effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| murex purple | Levantine sea snails (Cardon 2007) | yield per snail; process stench | Byzantine imperial monopoly; "born in the purple" |
| kermes → cochineal | scale insects (Greenfield 2005) | insect weight; then New World shipment | scarlet rank in Europe; cochineal displaces kermes post-1520s without renaming "crimson" |
| indigo/woad | plant fermentation (Balfour-Paul 1998) | skill; trade routes | indigo economies, Bengal's 1859–60 indigo rebellion; 16th-c. European bans on "devil's dye" indigo protecting woad |
| saffron | crocus stigmas (Finlay 2002; Cardon 2007) | hand-picked flowers, weight | ritual/elite yellow across Buddhist and Hindu worlds |
| ultramarine | ground lapis lazuli (Ball 2001) | mine + distance | contractually reserved for the Virgin's robes |
| Egyptian blue | first synthetic pigment (Ball 2001) | kiln tech | Egypt's cheap divine blue; tech-gated hue |
| aniline mauve, 1856 | coal tar (color-terminology.md §6) | none — synthesis | the great code-collapse: all hues go cheap, old color ranks decay into brands, kits, sports colors |

- **Sumptuary law is the enforcement layer**: 1363 Statute of Apparel (37 Edw. III) legislates English dress by class; Tang rank colors (§3); Edo palettes (color-terminology.md §4); Byzantine purple monopoly (§9); Victorian mourning becomes a legislated-feeling industry — warehouses, jet jewelry, graded half-mourning (Taylor 1983). Where there is a color rank, look for the court case and the fine.
- **Codes migrate when scarcity dies**: heraldry formalizes hue into a rule system (metals vs colors; the rule of tincture, no color-on-color — Woodcock & Robinson 1988) just as cloth ranks weaken; modern national colors are *constructed* — pan-African green-yellow-red descends from Ethiopia, pan-Arab red-white-black-green from the 1916 Arab Revolt, Dutch orange from the House of Orange (Smith 1975). A fictional industrializing culture should watch its sacred colors turn into football kits.

## 11. Calibration flags — contested or discredited, in one place

- **Turner's triad as human universal** — overreach by later readers; it is Ndembu-specific analysis (Turner 1967, incl. his reply to Turnbull's critique). Use as *one* documented polarity system, not the template.
- **"White bridal purity is ancient and European"** — false; Rome veiled brides orange (flammeum), and white bridal is post-1840 fashion culture (Sebesta & Bonfante 1994; Pastoureau 2023).
- **"Purple = always royal"** — *Analects* 17.18 is the standing counterexample (§9).
- **"Black = evil is human nature"** — Egypt's black land, Qin's black dynasty, xuan-heaven (§5); the moral-dark tradition is Zoroastrian-Christian-European in genealogy (Boyce 1975; Pastoureau 2009), and modern racialized readings are a colonial overlay.
- **Lüscher color test** — no accepted validity in mainstream psychometrics; marketing folklore ((to-verify) for a review citation).
- **Pop color psychology** (red wins games, brand-color personality) — small contested lab effects (Hill & Barton 2005) and corporate folklore; not cultural law.
- **"Flag colors are ancient identities"** — most national chromatics are 18th–20th c. constructions (Smith 1975).
- **"White = good" cross-cultural questionnaire results** (Williams & Best lineage) — method-contested ((to-verify)); do not treat as a cognition universal.
- **Newton's seven-color rainbow** in pre-modern settings — anachronism (color-terminology.md §7); same for hue-wheels and "complementary colors" before the relevant optics (Gage 1993).
- **Pattern symbolism is a separate channel** — stripes, dots, borders carry exclusion/rank codes independent of hue (Pastoureau 2001); a hue-only audit of your world will silently drop half the system.

## 12. Application protocol — building a fictional culture's color code

1. **Inventory chromophores from biome and trade** (murex coasts? kermes oaks? indigo belt? saffron altitude? lapis distance?). Scarcity gradient = prestige axis. (Lexicon steps 1–2 live in color-terminology.md §8 — run those first.)
2. **Assign anchor practices**: birth, coming-of-age, marriage, mourning, war, coronation, sacrifice. For each, derive the color from the *material logic*, not a meaning table: undyed = removed from the color economy (mourning/renunciation); fire-colored = burning an identity off (renunciation); gold = light-tech for gods; scarcest hue = throne.
3. **Choose the dualism mode** (§5): complementary, triadic-with-mediator, or antagonistic — then write the chromatic grammar each implies (interlocked emblem / mediator color with passage-rights / rival banners and re-dye conversions).
4. **Build the five-color-or-not decision**: if the culture is a correlative-cosmology bureaucracy, give it a pentad engine (§3) — and remember a quincunx culture implies a state apparatus; fisher-tribes get sea-snail monopolies, not five-phase mandalas.
5. **Add one deliberate inversion against a neighbor** (white/black mourning flip; green-hat insult absent next door; purple usurper vs purple throne) — shared structure, flipped values (§3, §8).
6. **Write the enforcement artifacts**: one sumptuary law with real penalties, one taboo (e.g., names written only in certain inks — the East Asian red-ink name taboo is the model; (to-verify) for a folkloristics reference), one proverb, one heraldic tincture rule. Codes live in courts, not in color swatches.
7. **Keep a ledger per color**: term | carrier | practice | authority | penalty | era-of-change. If a row's "era-of-change" is empty, either the color is dead in-story or you have not found its plot yet.
8. **Plan era drift**: what happens to the code when the dye economy shifts — the murex beds collapse, cochineal arrives by trade, someone synthesizes mauve (§10). Word-and-code survival after referent death is the realistic path (color-terminology.md §6).
9. **Test scenes**: a white-clad envoy at a black-funeral court; a purple-vs-vermilion succession dispute quoting competing doctrines; a counterfeit scarlet detectable only by smell or mordant; a calendar reform changing the funeral color. If none of these can misfire, the code is decorative.
- **Failure modes**: every culture's red = luck; black villains and white heroes everywhere; "purple is royal" as a world constant; bridal white spread across all eras; symbolic colors with no dye behind them; liturgical color wheels in pre-optics cultures; and the silent port — copying a real-world pairing (red wedding, white funeral) into a culture whose material logic would generate the opposite.
