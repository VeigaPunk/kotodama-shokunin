## 1. Frame: cosmology vs cosmography, and the design axes

- **Cosmology** = the culture's tier-1 account of world structure and origin (what is true in-world). **Cosmography** = the projected, inhabited version: how the world is drawn (maps), named (toponyms), walked (processions, pilgrimage), and built (cities, temples). The worldbuilder's real material is the **interface**: ritual architecture and calendar are cosmography made habitable — the places a culture checks its cosmology against horizons and seasons.
- Standing lane rule (comparative-frameworks.md §1): generate with the etic typologies below, never ascribe them in-world. A priesthood may teach "the sky is a drum" (emic); the designer note "they run a gaitian-type model" (etic) stays in the handoff sheet. Tag outputs.
- Every strong cosmography answers five independent questions: where is the **center**; how many **layers**; what **code** orders the directions; what **shape** encloses the world; which **age** are we in. The five families below are axes, not menus — mix them per culture (e.g. floating-island world + radial directions + catastrophe-loop ages).

## 2. Family A — the centered world: axis mundi

| Vehicle | Mechanics | Real exemplars |
|---|---|---|
| tree | roots drink underworlds, crown touches heaven; climbed (shaman's ascent); a bird often nests at the crown | Yggdrasil (Völuspá; Gylfaginning); ceiba as Maya world tree; Bodhi tree; Irminsul, the Saxon pillar-tree felled by Charlemagne in 772 (Royal Frankish Annals) |
| mountain | sky meets earth; storm-god's or ancestor's seat; temples build replicas of it | Meru / Sumeru (Hindu-Jain-Buddhist); Olympus; Kunlun (Shanhaijing); Zion / Gerizim; Mesoamerican pyramids as mountain-wombs |
| pillar | the separated sky is held off the earth by a first-structure | Egyptian djed ("stability"); the Ame-no-mihashira pillar circled by Izanagi and Izanami at their marriage on Onogoro (Kojiki); Delphic omphalos; the Sufi qutb, "pole" of the saints |
| pole star | the unmoving celestial pivot around which all wheels turn | celestial-pivot kingship in early Chinese astral-political ideology (Pankenier 2013); shaman's smoke-hole as sky door (Siberia; Eliade 1951) |
| river | world-edge or heaven-road | Okeanos encircling the Greek world (Iliad 14); Gaṅgā's heaven-descent caught in Shiva's hair (Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa); the Milky Way as Tianhe "sky river" (China); the Bitter River ring of the Imago Mundi (§6) |

- Wheatley (1971): earliest cities are **ceremonial centers** — cosmographic diagrams in mudbrick where the axis and quarters are performed, not merely believed. As history, the single-cause ritual-first model is heavily qualified in current urban archaeology; as a generator it is excellent.
- J.Z. Smith (1987) caution: axis-mundi claims are cheap — a comparativist finds a center wherever he looks. Require (a) built expression (a pillar, a street, a seat) and (b) a ritual that uses it, before crediting a fictional culture with a real axis.
- Generation procedure: (1) name the center in the culture's own language (§9); (2) decide access — king only, fire-tenders only, or nobody (an empty omphalos is a strong choice); (3) run one procession line through it; (4) place secondary axes at quarter-points; (5) break symmetry once (a market or gallows one step off-axis) so the plan reads built, not drawn.

## 3. Family B — layered cosmos: stacks, gates, and counts

| Tradition | Structure | Gates & travel | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mesopotamia | sky / earth / netherworld over the Apsû (freshwater abyss) | 7 gates of the underworld (Ishtar's Descent); grave shafts; dream descents | Horowitz 1998 |
| Egypt | no fixed count: sky (Nut) over earth (Geb), Shu between; Duat beneath | the sun's barque crosses 12 hours / 12 gates of the Duat nightly (Amduat) | Allen 1988; Hornung 1999 |
| Second-Temple Jewish → Christian | 7 heavens (2 Enoch 20; b. Hagigah 12b); Paul's "third heaven" (2 Cor 12:2) | ascent-apocalypse itineraries | — |
| Islam | 7 heavens of the Miʿrāj (Q 17:1; Sahih Bukhari) | night journey | — |
| India (Puranic) | 7 upper lokas over 7 pātālas, Meru at center | descent/ascent by karma and yoga | Viṣṇu Purāṇa 2 (Wilson 1840) |
| Buddhist | Meru (on the order of 80,000 yojanas) + 4 continents; 31 realms (kāma 11, rūpa 16, ārūpa 4) | realms ranked by meditative attainment | Abhidharmakośa (La Vallée Poussin 1923–31) |
| Norse | "nine worlds" — a modern tally; sources name worlds but never enumerate nine together | Bifröst; the Hel-road; Óðinn's riding | Faulkes 1987 (§10) |
| Nahua / Maya | 13 heavens over 9 underworlds — tidy early-colonial synthesis; source details vary | 13 day-lords and 9 night-lords of the 260-day calendar carry the count | Sahagún; Miller & Taube 1993 (§10) |
| Diné (Navajo) | successive worlds below, exited upward through a reed | emergence narrative; the hogan as model-cosmos, door facing east | Witherspoon 1977 |
| Dante (engineered fiction) | 9 circles / 7 terraces / 9 spheres + Empyrean | the poem itself is the itinerary | — |

- Craft rule: a layer count means something only when **bound to a ritual count** — 7 gates, 12 hours, 13 day-lords, 81 temple-padas. A stack without a count-bearing ritual is set dressing. When sources disagree on the count (they nearly always do), keep the disagreement: competing layer-counts are ready-made schism material.

## 4. Family C — the horizontal code: directions

| System | Code | Built / ritual expression | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| China: wǔfāng–wǔxíng | E blue-green/wood, S red/fire, W white/metal, N black/water, C yellow/earth | Kaogongji capital: 9-li square, 3 gates per side, ancestral temple E, soil-and-grain altar W, court S, market N; Beijing's 7.8 km central axis (UNESCO 2024) with round Temple of Heaven and square Temple of Earth; axis deviates ~2°12′–2°49′ counterclockwise from true north (measured; He et al. 2023) | Needham 1959; Pankenier 2013 |
| Mesoamerica: quincunx | E red, W black, N white, S yellow, C blue-green | year-bearers rotate through the quarters in 13-year bundles; Codex Fejérváry-Mayer p. 1 is the canonical diagram | Sahagún; Miller & Taube 1993 |
| Cusco: ceque system | not quartered — 41 radial lines from the Coricancha through 328 huacas, owned by royal lineages | roads (Qhapaq Ñan) plug the provinces into the diagram; huacas serviced on a calendrical round | Cobo 1990; Zuidema 1964; Bauer 1998 |
| Gaelic: body-coded | facing sunrise: right = deas "south", left = tuath "north"; deiseil = sunwise circumambulation | luck runs with the sun; widdershins cursing | — |
| Lakota: hoop | four directions as grandfather-powers; colors vary by band | Black Elk's scheme reaches us mediated by Neihardt (DeMallie 1984); never generalize "medicine-wheel colors" | DeMallie 1984 |
| Medieval Christian: T-O | east at top, Jerusalem center, three continents split by the T of waters | churches face east; Hereford Mappa Mundi c. 1300; T-O diagrams are symbolic, not survey maps (§10) | Isidore, Etymologiae 14 |
| Islam: qibla | one direction, everywhere the same | mosque walls set to Mecca; al-Bīrūnī computes qibla from coordinates | al-Bīrūnī, Taḥdīd |

- A direction code wants a **weight** per direction (color, bird, regalia, kin-branch, season) and a **route** per code (procession, migration memory, wedding circuit), then one asymmetry at the center.
- Generation procedure: (1) pick geometry — quartered, quincunx, radial (ceque-type), or single-line (qibla-type); (2) assign tokens from the color system (couples color-terminology: a culture with four basic color terms cannot code five directions by color — use birds or winds instead); (3) attach kinship/legal consequences (which moiety marries which quarter, which court hears which compass-point case); (4) build one structure per direction and one axis through the center.

## 5. Family D — world-ages doctrine

| Tradition | Scheme | Motion of time | Calendar hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hesiod, Works and Days 106–201 | five races: gold, silver, bronze, heroes (the odd intercalation), iron | downhill; iron ends when babies are born grey-haired | genealogical era-marking, no arithmetic |
| Ovid, Met. 1 | four ages, heroes dropped | downhill | Roman moral rhetoric |
| India (Purāṇas) | four yugas at 4:3:2:1 — Kṛta 1,728,000 / Tretā 1,296,000 / Dvāpara 864,000 / Kali 432,000 = 4,320,000-year mahāyuga; 1,000 mahāyugas = one day of Brahmā | downhill, with exact arithmetic; Kali epoch 3102 BCE (traditional; Āryabhaṭa, 499 CE) | pañcāṅga almanacs print years-remaining; priesthood owns the epoch |
| Iran (Bundahišn) | 12,000 years in four 3,000-year ages; frašagird (renovation) closes the term | fixed-term, then a perfect ending | scribes date events within the term (Gignoux, Encyclopaedia Iranica) |
| Aztec Five Suns | four prior suns each destroyed (jaguars, wind, fire-rain, flood); Fifth Sun Nāhui Ōllin "4 Movement" (earthquakes), lit at Teotihuacan when Nanahuatzin leapt into the fire | serial catastrophes; the present sun runs on sacrifice | 52-year New Fire cycle (§8); details vary by source (Bierhorst 1992) |
| Maya Long Count | creation at 13.0.0.0.0 4 Ajaw 8 Kumkʼu (3114 BCE, GMT correlation) | neither up nor down — pure count; baktuns roll over | 21 Dec 2012 was a rollover; the "apocalypse" is modern pop invention (Aveni 2009) |
| Buddhism → Japan | kalpas; the teaching valid 5,000 years; mappō "final age" dated 1052 CE in the common Japanese reckoning | decline; liturgy adapts (Pure Land turn) | mappō dates a religious regime change, visible in ritual and dress (§9) |
| Norse | Ragnarök, then the earth rises green again (Völuspá 59) | catastrophe-loop | computable future: Líf and Lífþrasir survive (Vafþrúðnismál) |

- Design rule: choose the **motion** (downhill, loop, serial catastrophe, flat count) and the **arithmetic** (ratios, era lengths, epoch). Exact arithmetic is a power instrument — whoever controls the epoch controls legitimacy. Then let in-world historians *disagree about which age they are in*: that dispute is a free engine for era files, and it has real precedents (§9).

## 6. Family E — world-shape: disc, canopy, floating, egg, sphere

| Model | Mechanics | Exemplars |
|---|---|---|
| flat disc + encircling ring | world = island or plate ringed by ocean-river or world-serpent | Babylonian Imago Mundi (BM 92687): Babylon at center, Euphrates, salt "Bitter River" ring, distant nagû regions (Horowitz 1998); Miðgarðr ringed by Jörmungandr; Anaximander's drum-shaped earth; Thales' earth floating on water |
| canopy | sky as solid over earth, held apart | Nut arched over Geb with Shu between (Egypt; Allen 1988); Chinese gaitian "canopy heaven": round sky, square earth (Needham 1959) |
| floating world | earth or islands float on primeval waters, or ride animals | Rangi and Papa separated by Tāne, Māui fishing up islands (Māori; Buck 1949); the Kumulipo rising from coral (Hawaiʻi; Beckwith 1951); Onogoro congealed from spear-stirred brine (Kojiki); the five immortal isles carried by giant turtles (Liezi, "Tang Wen"); earth on Śeṣa / Kurma (India) |
| egg / embryo | world hatches, or is dismembered from a body | hiranyagarbha "golden germ" (RV 10.121); Puruṣa dismembered — head sky, eye sun, breath wind (RV 10.90); Pangu hatching in the cosmic egg (first attested Xu Zheng, 3rd c. CE); world from a broken duck's egg (Kalevala, runo 1) |
| sphere | ball-earth under rotating shells | Pythagoreans / Aristotle (eclipse shadow, star drift); Ptolemy; Chinese huntian armillary and xuanye "infinite empty space" as rivals (Needham 1959; Pankenier 2013) |

- Calibration: "everyone before Columbus believed in a flat earth" is a 19th-century fabrication (Russell 1991); sphericity was standard ancient and medieval learning, and T-O diagrams are symbolic devices, not cartographic claims. Disc cosmologies are emic models, not geometry failures — and the gap is usable: model a culture confessing a flat-disc world while its navigators quietly compute with sphere math.

- Minimal encoding kit — **an axis, a count, a procession**: every drafted culture gets (1) one axis someone can stand on, (2) one count-bearing ritual (bricks, gates, hours, day-lords), (3) one procession that enacts the diagram, (4) a buried or hidden origin-place under the tallest thing, (5) one calendrical dawn the axis exists to catch.

## 8. Cosmography → calendar

- Egypt: 36 decans → 10-day weeks → 360-day year + 5 epagomenal days; decan star-clocks divide the night into 12 hours — and the Duat has 12 gates. Calendar and underworld share arithmetic (Neugebauer & Parker 1960–69; Hornung 1999).
- Mesoamerica: the 260-day tonalpōhualli / tzolkʼin (13 × 20 — the day-lord and night-lord stacks of §3) interlocks with the 365-day year into the 52-year Calendar Round; year-bearers rotate through the four directions; at cycle's end the New Fire is drilled on a sacrificed captive's breast at Huixachtécatl (Cerro de la Estrella; last performed 1507; Codex Borbonicus) and every hearth is relit from it. Direction, number, and time are one machine (Sahagún; Milbrath 1999).
- India: yuga ratios make the end computable — pañcāṅga almanacs print years-remaining; epoch ownership is institutional power.
- Babylon: the akītu reenacts creation annually — new year = new cosmos (performance link contested, §7).
- Japan: the dated onset of mappō (1052) resets what ritual *expects* — decline-dating changes ceremony and dress without touching the calendar itself.
- Alignments are the calendar's honesty check: Karnak's solstice axis, E-group sunrise lines, Venus windows. When a fictional calendar must intercalate, stage it as theological crisis — real precedent: the Gregorian reform (1582) cost ten days and provoked genuine calendar riots and centuries of Protestant resistance.

## 9. Couplings: language, names, costume

- Direction lexemes come from bodies and suns: Latin oriens / occidens "rising / setting"; Arabic sharq / gharb from "rise / set"; Hebrew qedem "east = before, and ancient"; Chinese běi 北 "north" first meant "back" (two figures back-to-back; 背 "back" derives from it); Egyptian jbt means both "east" and "left" (facing south); Gaelic body-codes right/south. When conlanging (couples conlang-methodology), coin direction words from sunrise verbs, wind names, or kin-orientation — then etymologize the capital as "navel" (Delphic omphalos; Quechua qusqu, Cusco) or "support/pillar".
- Toponyms are cosmographic claims: Teotihuacan glossed as "place where one becomes a god"; Jambudvīpa "rose-apple continent". A fictional map is a set of etymological arguments — hand the derivation table to naming-systems.
- Names from the calendar: the Mixtec king 8 Deer "Jaguar Claw" (Codex Zouche-Nuttall) carries his 260-day birthday as a personal name, as in Nahua tonalli-names. Calendar-name systems give instantly legible noble genealogies.
- Costume: quarter colors migrate to regalia (Chinese court colors by office and season; direction banners); age doctrine migrates to dress (mappō austerity vs gold-thread confidence); the year-bearer's direction can dress the whole court for a year. couples costume: the direction code is the color system a tailor can execute.
- Era coupling: give rival in-world schools different epochs — real parallels: Jewish anno mundi 3761 BCE (Seder Olam) vs Byzantine anno mundi 5509 BCE; Kali 3102 BCE by Āryabhaṭa's reckoning. "What year is it?" answered three ways is a border dispute waiting to happen.

## 10. Calibration checklist

- Hamlet's Mill (de Santillana & von Dechend 1969): precession encrypted in every myth — rejected as method; tone inspiration only.
- Panbabylonism (Winckler, Jeremias, c. 1900): all world myth derived from Babylonian astral religion — a dead school; do not resurrect it even ironically in canon notes.
- Eliade's universal axis mundi: the pattern may be a comparativist artifact (J.Z. Smith 1987). Verify a center with architecture + ritual, never myth alone (consistent with comparative-frameworks.md §3).
- Norse "nine worlds": no Eddic passage enumerates nine; the canonical-looking list is modern harmonization (Faulkes 1987). Keep source-visible variance in handoffs.
- Mesoamerican 13/9 stacks: the tidy counts are early-colonial syntheses and sources differ — keep the variance, not the neatness (Miller & Taube 1993).
- Maya 2012: Long Count baktun rollover, not a doomsday prophecy; the apocalypse reading is modern pop culture (Aveni 2009).
- "Medieval flat earth": 19th-century fabrication (Russell 1991).
- Medicine-wheel direction colors: vary by nation; Black Elk's set arrives mediated through Neihardt (DeMallie 1984). Never pan-Indianize direction colors.
- Waters, Book of the Hopi (1963): unreliable synthesis; prefer Hopi-authored sources (Geertz 1994). Apply the same discipline to any "secret teachings" cosmography.
- akītu / Enūma Eliš annual performance: the recitation is attested only in a Seleucid-era ritual text and the annual-reenactment model is contested (Sommer 2000; Debourse 2022).
- Wheatley's ceremonial-center urbanism: generator, not settled history — single-cause models are heavily qualified in current urban archaeology.
- El Castillo's equinox light-serpent: the phenomenon is real; builder intent is debated (Aveni 2001).

## 11. Canon boundary

- Reference and method only. Which world model a fictional culture holds, where its true center is, which age it believes it is living in, and what that belief costs — canon decisions belong to lore-shokunin. Hand off cosmography design sheets {families chosen, center definition, layer counts with ritual bindings, direction-code table, age doctrine + arithmetic, architecture/calendar encodings, conlang derivations}, each flagging contested or to-verify items per §10, so the bible owner can mint variants without re-deriving the typology.
