## 1. Core framework — what counts as a trickster

- **Hynes's six features** (Hynes 1993, in Hynes & Doty): (1) fundamentally **ambiguous and anomalous** — definitionally betwixt-and-between; (2) **deceiver and trick-player** — lying and cons are his mode, not an incident; (3) **shape-shifter** — body, anatomy, gender, species all fluid (Loki mothers Sleipnir as a mare; Monkey's 72 transformations); (4) **situation-inverter** — the weak beat the strong by their own rules; (5) **messenger and imitator of the gods** — the heraldic slot (Èṣù, Legba, Hermes); (6) **sacred and lewd bricoleur** — works with whatever is at hand; dirt and liturgy coexist.
- Minimal craft definition: the trickster is **a position, not a character** — the licensed crossing of a boundary a culture otherwise polices, told by whoever needs the crossing. Three design questions in order: *which boundary*, *whose license*, *what institution holds the license*.
- Ricketts (1966) argued the trickster is **not** a decayed culture hero — the roles are distinct and the historical direction between them unresolved. Do not write "the trickster is just a failed god."
- Trickster myth vs trickster tale: a **cycle** links episodes into one figure's biography (Wakdjunkaga; Xiyouji); a **tale corpus** reuses the figure in standalone episodes (Anansi, Coyote, Br'er Rabbit). Both are normal; a culture can hold both.

## 3. Tellers, genres, framing (context decides meaning)

- **The trickster can name the genre**: Akan *anansesem* ("spider stories") is the ordinary word for folktales — the trickster owns storytelling itself (Beckwith 1929 documents the Jamaican continuation).
- **Season and formula gate the telling**: Navajo Coyote tales are told only in winter and close with a fixed formula marking them as received, not invented — regulated, pedagogical, bawdy but not licentious (Toelken 2003).
- **Genre slots**: Lokasenna belongs to the *senna* (institutionalized insult-freestyle); Xiyouji is a print-era 100-chapter novel; Uncle Remus is a dialect-literary frame; Anansi survives in Caribbean children's books and in proverbs.
- **Liturgical slotting**: Èṣù must be honored before any rite (Bascom 1969); Papa Legba is saluted first and asked to open the gate in Haitian service (Métraux 1958). The gate-fee is the trickster's cult.
- **Institutional trickster persons**: Lakota heyoka — thunder-dreamers living contrarily (backwards gear; Walker 1980); Pueblo sacred clowns (koshare, koyemsi) in calendrical rites (well-attested; dedicated survey (to-verify)); the court jester's licensed truth (Otto 2001).

## 4. Radin's trickster cycle — and what is contested in it

- Radin (1956) published a full Winnebago (Ho-Chunk) trickster cycle, **Wakdjunkaga**, recorded from named consultants in early-20th-c. Wisconsin — a single lineage of versions, later generalized into "the" trickster cycle; cite it as *one* text, not the myth.
- Arc: the cycle opens in pure body (hunger, sex, excretion — the famous detachable-penis episode), has the trickster bungle role after role, and ends with self-recognition and the institution of the trickster dance. Radin read it as a biography of role-achievement: appetite socialized into office.
- Radin's closing **positional typology**: (a) trickster as autonomous creator; (b) as culture hero/transformer; (c) as companion or subordinate of a culture hero; (d) as demoted buffoon. His tentative reading of the series as **historical evolution** is contested (§9) — keep the typology, drop the chronology.
- The volume's other two essays: Kerényi reads the trickster as primeval anarchic world-maker (his case is Hermes); Jung as the "collective shadow." Both are reception psychology, not ethnography (§9).

## 5. Hyde — trickster as boundary-crosser and artist

- Hyde's (1998) core move: define the trickster by **where he lives** — roads, doorways, crossroads, riverbanks, borders, markets — the figure a culture uses for whatever must move between sealed domains: news, fire, stories, shame, luck.
- Method: start from appetite (food, sex, dirt) and watch each boundary the trickster breaks; his chapters run appetite → sex → theft → speech. Trickster imagination is presented as the artist's imagination — invention out of stolen material.
- The moral structure Hyde finds: **appetite → invention → community benefit**; and a check — the trickster must meet consequences (Loki bound, Monkey filleted, Coyote frozen in the ice fishing with his tail) or the art is mere rapacity. Use his boundary inventory as a design checklist (§10); treat his psychology as literary interpretation, which he says it is.

## 6. Structural, liminal, institutional readings

- **Lévi-Strauss (1955)**: the trickster is a **mediator**, and the North American tricksters are carrion-eaters (Raven, Coyote) — animals living between predator and prey, hunt and leftover, life and death; the ambiguous eater carries the ambiguous god. Design rule: pick the trickster's species from edge-dwellers by diet and habitat (carrion birds, coyote/jackal/fox, spider, hare).
- **Turner (1969)**: trickster-like figures cluster at liminal stages of ritual; betwixt-and-between persons and periods carry special license and anti-structure.
- **Babcock-Abrahams (1975)**: the trickster is a **"tolerated margin of mess"** — every society keeps a sanctioned zone of disorder, and trickster tales are the management rules for that zone, not celebrations of chaos.
- **Myth–institution pairing** is the repeatable pattern: Legba ↔ gate rite; heyoka ↔ thunder-dreaming; Pueblo clowns ↔ ceremonial calendar; jester ↔ court license; Coyote ↔ winter tale season. Craft rule: never ship a trickster without his institutional slot.
- **Vizenor (1988, 1994)**: the trickster as a modern Native critical method — *survivance* (active presence, not mere survival); trickster discourse as anti-ethnographic irony. Use when your fictional trickster tradition belongs to the colonized party and is alive, not antiquarian.

## 7. The transformer spectrum and etiological tails

- Spectrum of function, all attested within single traditions: **bringer/creator** (Raven's light; Coyote's monster-slaying that founds the tribes) → **transformer** (repairs a botched world) → **companion-clown** (Loki beside Thor; Anansi before the sky-god) → **pure flyting clown** (Lokasenna) → **eschatological destroyer** (Loki unbound at Ragnarök).
- **Etiological tails**: most episodes end with an "as it is now" clause — why skunk smells, why rabbit runs zigzag, why the herms stand at corners; Coyote's loss to Death is why mortals die. Design rule: every episode you write must deposit a permanent feature of the world.
- **Failure upward**: his failures fix the world as much as his successes — the world is the way it is *because the trickster tried to improve it*. This clause is what keeps trickster myth from reading as mere joke.

## 8. How trickster tales encode social critique

- **Hidden transcript** (Scott 1990): under durable power asymmetry, the weak's offstage voice takes comic-animal form; trickster tales are collective rehearsals of outwitting the strong by wits. Levine (1977) documents Br'er Rabbit functioning exactly this way among enslaved people; Roberts (1989) traces the continuum from trickster to folk badman.
- **Pragmatics of indirection**: the trickster owns the violated speech acts — insult, pun, feigned ignorance, cross-language mistranslation, loud-talking, the dozens. Gates (1988) builds a theory of African-American vernacular discourse on the Èṣù → signifying-monkey lineage. (Couples to politeness-taboo-euphemism.md.)
- **Inversion rituals**: licensed reversals (clowns, carnival, feast of fools). Gluckman (1954) argued ritual rebellion can *display and thereby reinforce* the order it inverts; the critique-vs-catharsis question is unresolved and culture-specific — decide it per your fictional teller class, not per theoretical fashion.
- **Frame capture**: the same tales get re-framed by dominant readers — Harris's plantation-nostalgia Uncle Remus frame wraps tales of resistance; Disney's *Song of the South* (1946) completes the softening. Always model who records, frames, and profits: the teller class and the frame are part of the myth, and colonizers renaming your trickster is itself realistic material (§9).
- **Lootable tale types** (Uther 2004): ATU 1 (Theft of Food), ATU 2 (Tail Fisher); the animal-trickster block runs through the ATU animal tales.

## 9. Calibration flags — contested, dead, or commonly botched

- **Radin's evolution-as-history**: dead as chronology (Hynes 1993); the positional typology survives. Also: his cycle is one consultant lineage, not a pan-tribal datum.
- **Jung/Kerényi universal-archetype readings**: reception phenomena, not explanations — they erase teller, genre, and institution, which are the actual data (the critique is Hynes & Doty's own).
- **"One trickster archetype in every mythology"**: overreach; the figure class is regionally and genre-linked, and many mythologies manage without it.
- **Griaule's Dogon** (Ogo/Yurugu, the pale fox — Pelton's fourth case): van Beek (1991) could not replicate Griaule's system and judged much of it produced by Griaule himself. Treat Dogon trickster material as unreliable ethnography.
- **Sun Wukong's origin debate**: Hu Shi (1923) derived the Monkey from Hanuman; Lu Xun argued a native lineage (the water-ape Wuzhiqi and earlier monkey tales). Unresolved — treat the figure as composite; neither origin is a fact to cite.
- **Èṣù/Legba = the Devil**: a missionary-era conflation; the ethnography shows messenger/gatekeeper, not evil principle (Bascom 1969; Thompson 1983). If your colonizing culture makes this error, model it as *their* error.
- **Loki as historically worshipped trickster god**: cult attestation is thin to absent in the sources; neo-pagan certainty outruns the evidence (survey (to-verify)). Dumézil (1948) treats Loki as Odin's ambivalent companion, with Nart-Syrdon comparisons — a position, not a consensus.
- **Br'er Rabbit as plantation charm**: that is the Harris frame, not the tales — African-hare-derived resistance stock (Levine 1977; Roberts 1989). Cite the tale level, distrust the frame.
- **Coyote's bawdiness as "primitive license"**: the tales are seasonally restricted, formula-closed, and pedagogically framed (Toelken 2003) — regulated, not licentious.
- **Gender**: the flagship tricksters are overwhelmingly male-coded; female trickster slots exist (Southwest grandmother-spider figures; wise-woman/stepmother tricksters in tale corpora) but the dedicated scholarly survey is thin ((to-verify)). Don't "fix" a culture's trickster gender without first deciding its teller class.

## 10. Application protocol — building a fictional culture's trickster

1. **Place the boundary**: pick 2–3 boundaries the culture actually polices (sacred/profane, noble/base, language A/B, inside/outside, living/dead). The trickster is defined relative to them; no boundary, no trickster.
2. **Pick the species/body**: an edge-dweller by diet and habitat (§6); decide shape-shifting range and gender fluidity as *features of the boundary*, not decoration.
3. **Assign the pantheon slot**: use Radin's four positions (creator / transformer / companion / buffoon) and decide whether the figure's position drifts by genre (Loki does).
4. **Assign the speech register**: the trickster owns specified pragmatics violations — lying, insult, punning, mistranslation, market talk. Decide exactly which violations *only he* may commit (couples to politeness-taboo-euphemism.md).
5. **Build the institutional pair**: a liturgical slot (gate-fee, first offering, crossroads shrine, winter tale season, clown society, licensed fool) plus a tale genre with rules — who may tell, when, and the closing formula.
6. **Write 3–5 episodes, each depositing an etiology**, and at least one failure-upward episode (his loss makes mortality, thorns, or hunger permanent — §7).
7. **Decide the tellers**: whose stories are these (low-status, enslaved, border people)? What hidden transcript do they carry? The strong must lose by their own rules — that inversion is the genre's engine.
8. **Bind him**: give the tradition a constraint device (fillet, chains until doomsday, tail caught in the ice, gate-fee paid forever) and an end-state (Ragnarök, buddhahood, the briar patch). An unbound trickster reads as mascot, not myth.
9. **Run the reception check**: what do outsiders call him? (Eshu=Devil, Anansi Anglicized, Loki=Satan analogue in missionary and pop readings.) The reception layer is realistic, generative, and belongs in your era planning.
10. **Failure modes**: cute mascot; pure evil; universal-psychology sermonizing; one "archetype" across all cultures; episodes with no etiological tail; a trickster with no teller class; a trickster with no institution.
