## 1. The classification spine: Hornbostel–Sachs (1914)

- **Origin and shape**: Erich Moritz von Hornbostel and Curt Sachs (1914), "Systematik der Musikinstrumente", *Zeitschrift für Ethnologie* 46 — a Berlin-museum curators' decimal taxonomy; English translation Baines & Wachsmann (1961), *Galpin Society Journal* 14. Five top classes: **1** idiophone (the body itself sounds), **2** membranophone (a stretched skin sounds), **3** chordophone (a string sounds), **4** aerophone (an air column sounds), **5** electrophone (standardized by the MIMO consortium revision 2011, rev. 2017; Sachs had already added it in 1940, *History of Musical Instruments*).
- **The decimal address**: each instrument gets a number-path assigned by *how the sound is excited*, not by what it looks like or does. The famous placements worldbuilders get wrong:

| Instrument | Folk class | H-S class | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| piano | "percussion" | chordophone 314.122-4-8 — struck board zither with keyboard | the string sounds; hammers merely excite it |
| kazoo | "wind" | membranophone (mirliton) | a membrane is excited, by the voice |
| accordion, harmonica | "wind" | free aerophone 412.13 (free reed) | reeds vibrate free of any pipe |
| mbira / kalimba | "thumb piano" | plucked idiophone (lamellophone) | plucked tongues of the body itself |
| didgeridoo | "flute" | trumpet (lip-vibrated, 423-class) | the lips buzz like a brass player; no edge, no reed |

- **Design lever**: an H-S-style decimal system is a ready-made in-world scholarly lingua franca (museum catalogs, treatises, trade). A *deliberately different* classification is a stronger signature — see §2.

## 2. Alternatives and folk taxonomies: classification is itself culture

- **Schaeffner's two-class alternative** (vibrating solid vs vibrating air) and other organologies are surveyed in Kartomi (1990) — the standard critical companion. H-S measures sound-production physics and is silent on function, status, and ensemble role — which is what most cultures actually organize by.
- **Folk taxonomies** collected by Kartomi: by material (the bamboo-instrument category across Insular Southeast Asia), by ensemble membership (an instrument *is* "what belongs in ensemble X"), by gender and status, by loud-soft aesthetics — the Javanese *keras* (loud/coarse) vs *halus* (soft/refined) axis, which sorts ensembles and occasions as much as instruments (Kunst 1949; Becker 1993).
- **Instruments as inscribed state property**: the Marquis Yi of Zeng bell set (tomb dated 433 BCE) — 65 bronze bells, each with two strike points producing two pitches, the bells *inscribed with pitch names*: an instrument whose catalog is carved on itself and whose ownership marks rank (Falkenhausen 1993).
- **Design lever**: decide what the culture's taxonomy sorts by — material (bamboo-culture), function (war/festival/ritual), deity-patron, ensemble slot, loud/soft status axis. Then have a neighboring culture's scholars mis-apply their own H-S analog to it: instant ethnographic friction.

## 3. Scale systems and their affect doctrine — emic, calibrated

- **Greek harmoniai and ethos**: Plato, *Republic* III (c. 375 BCE) legislates modes by character (soft Lydian/Ionian vs manly Dorian/Phrygian; the banned list differs by passage) and Aristotle, *Politics* VIII (c. 335 BCE) argues melody "imitates" ethos — with a different mode ranking; the doctrine traces to the musician Damon (pseudo-Plutarch, *De musica* preserves the tradition). Treat the attributions as doctrinal and text-bound, not acoustic fact.
- **Mode names are people-names**: Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Ionian, Aeolian are Greek ethnonyms/regions — scales carry tribal labels. Design lever: name scales for peoples, founders, or cities; the name then implies a political stance.
- **The medieval misattachment**: church-mode names reach the West via Boethius (*De institutione musica*, c. 500–520) with the Greek names *transposed onto different structures*; Glarean's *Dodecachordon* (1547) rationalized the twelve-mode system including Aeolian/Ionian. "Dorian is manly" is a doctrine with a transmission error baked in — see §7.
- **Church modes and the octoechos**: Byzantine chant runs eight *echoi* on a weekly rotation (Sunday begins mode 1) — a mode-per-week liturgical calendar; Western Gregorian psalm tones mirror the eight-mode frame. Guido of Arezzo (*Micrologus*, c. 1026) teaches the system and his hymn-syllable method gives solfège (*ut-re-mi-fa-sol-la* from *Ut queant laxis*; *do* replaces *ut* later, commonly credited to G. B. Doni, 17th c.). Design lever: bind scale to calendar — "this week is in the fourth mode" gives ritual life a harmonic clock.
- **India — raga, rasa, thaat, melakarta**: a raga is melodic grammar (ascent/descent forms, signature phrases, ornaments) + a time window + an affect. The aesthetic frame is rasa theory from Bharata Muni's *Natyasastra* (c. 200 BCE–200 CE): eight rasas (love, mirth, pathos, fury, heroism, fear, disgust, wonder), a ninth (peace) added later. Systematization: Bhatkhande's ten *thaat* (Hindustani, 1930s) in the north; the 72-*melakarta* scheme codified by Venkatamakhin (*Chaturdandi Prakasika*, 1660) and standardized by Govindacharya (19th c.) in the south. Solfège is movable (*sa re ga ma pa dha ni*). Standard teaching table — emic convention, varying by gharana:

| Raga | Time window | Conventional affect |
|---|---|---|
| Bhairav | dawn | solemn, auspicious |
| Yaman | evening | serene, devotional |
| Bhairavi | any hour; the concert-finale raga | pathos, closure |
| Malkauns | late night | grave, meditative |
| Darbari Kanada | late night | regal, weighty |
| Desh | evening; monsoon association | romantic, tender |

- **Arabic maqam**: "quarter-tone" is a misnomer — 24-TET is a pedagogical approximation; intonation varies by regional school and by *ajnas* (tetrachord building-blocks). Systematists: al-Farabi (*Kitab al-Musiqi al-Kabir*, c. 950), Safi al-Din al-Urmawi (*Kitab al-Adwar*, 13th c.). Emic ethos conventions: Rast (natural, sturdy), Bayati (tender; common for the call to prayer), Hijaz (solemn-sacred; *named for the Hejaz region* — toponym as mode name), Saba (sorrow; conventionally shunned for joyous songs), Nakriz (martial). The performance-affect culture of *tarab* (musical ecstasy) is monograph-documented in Racy (2003).
- **Java — laras and patet**: two tuning systems, slendro and pelog, coexist in one orchestra; *patet* carries time-of-day and mood associations (slendro nem/sanga/manyura ≈ morning/afternoon/night conventions); kepatihan cipher notation stamps numerals 1–7 onto non-diatonic pitches — proof that number-names are labels, not scale-degrees (Kunst 1949; Becker 1993).
- **China — wuyin and the lü**: the pentatonic *gong shang jue zhi yu* maps onto the Five Phases and organs in Han cosmology (*Huainanzi*; *Huangdi Neijing*): gong–earth–center–spleen, shang–metal–west–lungs, jue–wood–east–liver, zhi–fire–south–heart, yu–water–north–kidneys. Twelve pitch-standards (*lü*) correspond to the months (myth in §6). The *Yueji* (Record of Music, in the *Liji*; Cook 1995) legislates music's political morality — "the sounds of a well-governed age are content and joyful" — and condemns *zhengsheng* "licentious sounds"; court *yayue* vs popular *suyue* is the court/folk register split (DeWoskin 1982).
- **Calibration here (important)**: cross-cultural affect universals are contested science — scale-"sadness" is not wired in (McDermott et al. 2016; Savage et al. 2015; Nettl 2000). Mode-ethos material is *emic doctrine*: use it as what the culture believes and enforces, never as world-physics. Lomax's cantometrics (1968) is largely abandoned as an explanatory framework.
- **Design lever**: borrow the *structure* — named scales × time × affect × class × calendar — and swap the content. Enforce one taboo (a funeral mode banned at weddings) and one jurisdiction case (which mode is lawful for prayer).

## 4. Instrument naming patterns — a typology of strategies

| Strategy | Pattern | Exemplars |
|---|---|---|
| material | what it is made of | ʿūd 'wood' → al-ʿūd → *lute* (OED); *chelys* 'tortoise(-shell)' = lyre |
| sound / onomatopoeia | named for its sound or strokes | *pipa* 琵琶: pi 'push hand' + pa 'draw hand' (Liu Xi, *Shiming*, c. 100 CE); *didgeridoo* — English onomatopoeic coinage (earliest OED citation 1919) over Yolŋu *yiḏaki* |
| technique / body part | how it is played | *sistrum* < Gk. *seistron* 'thing shaken'; *uilleann* pipes < Ir. 'elbow' (bellows-blown); *clarinet* < It. *clarino* 'trumpet register' + diminutive |
| measurement | named by its size | *shakuhachi* = '1 shaku 8 sun' (~54.5 cm standard); longer variants named by their own measure |
| number + string | numeral morphology | Persian *dotār* '2 strings', *setār* '3', *čahārtār* '4'; Chinese *sanxian* 三弦 '3 strings' → Okinawan *sanshin* → Japanese *shamisen* (borrowing chain, New Grove); *sitar* < sētār 'three strings' despite ~18–20 modern strings — a name-fossil |
| shape metonymy | what it looks like | *yueqin* 月琴 'moon lute'; the *serpent*, named by its coil |
| eponymy | a maker or hero names it | *ruan* < Ruan Xian (3rd c. CE, one of the Seven Sages of the Bamboo Grove); *saxophone* < Adolphe Sax (1840s); *sousaphone* < Sousa |
| register / place | who plays it, where | maqam *Hijaz* < the Hejaz; Greek modes < peoples (§3) |

- **Word-radiation**: one name breeds a family across millennia: Gk. *kithara* → Latin *cithara* → *citole*, *cittern*, → Andalusi Arabic *qīṭār* → Spanish *guitarra* → *guitar* (OED) — the word travels through three languages while the instrument changes shape.
- **Class-jumping drift**: Arabic *ṭunbūr* (a lute) → Provençal *tambor* → French *tambour* 'drum' → *tambourine* — the word drifts from the lute family to the drum family (OED; *tabla* < Arabic *ṭabl* 'drum' is the same sound-family). Instrument words, like garment words, change referent in transit.
- **The naming cascade mirrors the garment lexicon**: vielle → *viola* → *violone* → *violino* → *violoncello* ('little big-viol' — a diminutive stacked on a diminutive): children leave home, parent narrows; cf. garment-lexicon-crosslinguistic.md §5 (coat cascade). Register strata too: German *Geige* (vernacular) vs *Violine* (learned Italian) vs English *fiddle* (folk) — the shirt/blouse stratification in instrument words.
- **Design lever**: pick 2–3 dominant strategies per culture (material + measurement reads archaic-technical; eponymy reads courtly-industrial); plant exactly one fossil name; run one radiation and one class-jump through your language family; stratify the vocabulary by class and genre.

## 5. Ceremonial music registers

- **Borrowed register names**: Japanese *gagaku* 雅楽 imports the Chinese court-music name *yayue* 雅乐 wholesale — character, concept, and instrument set (the winds *shō*, *ryūteki*, *hichiriki*; genres *kangen* vs dance-music *bugaku*). A register name travels like a loanword (New Grove s.v. gagaku; cf. language-contact.md).
- **Job-titled instruments**: South Indian *nagasvaram*, honored as *mangala vadyam* 'auspicious instrument' (temple and wedding); North Indian *shehnai* < *shāh* + *nāy* 'king's reed' — the wedding instrument; the conch (*śaṅkha*) as liturgical opener. Instruments carry titles, not just names.
- **Military time**: the Ottoman *mehter* (Janissary band) lent Europe a percussion register — bass drum, cymbals, triangle — "Turkish music" / *alla turca*: Mozart K.331 finale; Beethoven 9 "alla marcia". A contact register = borrowed instruments + borrowed prestige + a name marking the source culture. Bugle calls (reveille, last post) are a fixed ceremonial lexicon binding time-of-day to action.
- **Instrument-name fossils inside another instrument**: organ stop names — trumpet, clarion, flute, oboe, vox humana — a keyboard's register vocabulary is a stratum of dead instrument names.
- **Mode-days**: the octoechos weekly rotation (§3) binds calendar to scale; seasonal raga windows do the same for Hindu festival time (cf. calendar-festival-systems.md).
- **Design lever**: every ceremony gets (1) a named register, (2) an instrument set with titles, (3) a taboo (no drums in mourning; a mode banned at funerals), (4) one instrument only the ceremony may sound. Then let a borrowed register arrive with instruments and prestige intact — and watch the vocabulary stratify.

- **The talking drum is this lane's centerpiece coupling**: a tone language can be *literally spoken* on a drum — phonological encoding on a musical instrument (cf. phonetics-phonology.md). Drum and whistle speech are language phenomena, not merely music phenomena.
- **Design lever**: the inventor-hero slot is filled in every canon — choose your filler (god, sage, thief, accidental herdsman). Name the heroes' instruments (Panchajanya pattern) and give the national instrument an origin myth with a named owner. The contest myth sets musical jurisdiction; the affect-triad gives ritual a three-part grammar; the rejected-instrument myth encodes gender norms about who may play what — Athena's puffed cheeks are a politics, not a joke.

## 7. Calibration (contested / discredited — state plainly in any lore doc)

- **Greek ≠ medieval**: church-mode names are Boethius-era transpositions of Greek harmoniai names onto different scale structures; every "Plato approved the Dorian mode for X" claim needs the caveat that Greek structures and later ones do not match. Glarean (1547) cleaned up the count, not the ethos.
- **Ethos universals**: no robust cross-cultural basis for scale-affect mapping as biology (McDermott et al. 2016; Savage et al. 2015; Nettl 2000); mode-ethos is doctrine. "Raga therapy" and "healing frequency" popular claims stay under-evidenced: the raga-EEG meta-analysis is explicitly inconclusive (five studies, 71 participants — Babel, Baral & Srivastava 2023), and the systematic review of singing-bowl sound healing found only four peer-reviewed studies and "we cannot recommend singing bowl therapies at this stage" (Stanhope & Weinstein 2020). Both are emic-belief material here, never our-world fact.
- **Mozart effect**: debunked as an artifact (Pietschnig, Voracek & Formann 2010). Cantometrics (Lomax 1968) abandoned. If your world has a music-science, model its errors too.
- **Maqam "quarter-tones"**: a pedagogical approximation; intonation is tradition-relative.
- **Deep history**: the Geissenklösterle/Hohle Fels flutes (c. 40–42 ka) are the accepted-oldest instruments (Conard, Malina & Münzel 2009); the Jiahu bone flutes are playable, c. 6600 BCE (Zhang et al. 1999); the Divje Babe "Neanderthal flute" is *disputed* (cave-bear punctures vs workmanship — d'Errico et al. 1998) — never use as settled fact.
- **Etymology checks**: sitar < sētār 'three strings' is the standard Persian etymology (OED), but the instrument's lineage is multi-source; a Greek *kithara* ← Iranian link is not a specialist hypothesis but a popular-source story (Klein; Online Etymology Dictionary); classical dictionaries treat κιθάρα as a loanword from an undetermined source — Beekes (2010) as Pre-Greek, possibly related to Proto-Hurro-Urartian *kinnar 'lyre, harp' — and the Persian account runs into chronology (the word is Homeric) and phonology (Iranian θr- would not yield Gk. k-), so cite it only as a rejected etymology; didgeridoo's 1919 date is the OED's earliest record, coinage context undocumented.
- **Myth variants differ** on who invented what (Hermes vs others for the lyre; Athena's aulos and the Gorgon-lament vs the mirror-rejection tellings) — cite the version you use; do not blend silently.

## 8. Applying to a fictional culture — craft checklist

1. Choose the organology and its sorting axis (physics-decimal, material, function, deity, ensemble-slot, loud/soft) — it encodes what the culture thinks sound *is*.
2. Assign each scale a name, a time window, an affect, a class, and a calendar slot; enforce one taboo pairing (mode × occasion).
3. Plant one name-fossil ("three-string" with nineteen strings; a flute named by a length no longer standard).
4. Run one word-radiation (kithara-style) and one class-jump (lute→drum) through the lexicon.
5. Stratify instrument vocabulary by class and genre (fiddle/violin/Geige pattern).
6. Give every ceremony a named register, a titled instrument set, and a taboo; borrow one foreign register wholesale (gagaku and alla-turca patterns).
7. Fill the inventor-hero slot with a named figure; name the heroes' instruments (Panchajanya pattern).
8. Install an affect-triad for ritual (three strains) and a contest myth to set jurisdiction.
9. If the language is tonal, let an instrument speak (dùndún pattern) — and route the phonology coupling through the linguistics files.
10. Handoff discipline: this file supplies method + comparative data only; which instruments, scales, and myths exist in-world is lore-shokunin's canon call (lane rules).
