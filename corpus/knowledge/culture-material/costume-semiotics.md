## 1. The calibration rule — dress is a code, not a language

- **The standing overreach: "clothes are a language."** They are not. No duality of patterning, no stable lexicon, no grammar; dress codes are paratactic, context-bound, and read by *class of item* (a crown-shaped thing), not by item (the exact crown). Eco (1976) analyzes clothing explicitly as a *weak code*. The stronger your fictional culture's dress code, the more it should look like an **engineered system** (uniform boards, heraldic law, sumptuary courts) rather than like grammar.
- **Deniability is the design feature, not a bug.** Speech is attributable; dress is disclaimable — "it's just a coat." Cultures therefore use dress to say what speech cannot safely say: loyalty under a purge court, piety under a secular regime, grief a funeral oration would have to state. Write scenes where the alibi matters: the signal is deniable precisely so it can be sent.
- **Barthes' three garments** (*The Fashion System*, 1967): the *real* garment (technological object), the *photographed* garment (image), the *written* garment (text). His corpus was 1960s fashion-magazine copy (Elle, Jardin des Modes), and his finding is about the third garment: fashion writing converts utility into "Fashion" itself — the signified is the institution, not the coat. **Calibration:** Barthes describes the *discourse layer*, not what wearers signal; do not cite him as evidence that "clothes say X." His craft value: every dress-code culture grows a second, parasitic discourse layer — catalogs, fashion plates, sumptuary preambles, tailor's jargon — and the written garment will *diverge* from the worn one.
- **The code-strength spectrum**: engineered denotative codes (military rank, heraldry, livery, academic hoods, buzi rank badges — trained readers, imposture laws) at one end; connotative weak codes (street fashion, where even insiders misread) at the other. A world needs both ends *and the interface*: the smuggler's false crest, the fashionista connoisseur reading a single lapel stitch.
- **The in-world test** (mirror of color-symbolism.md §1): can a character *misread* dress with consequences? A mourner in full white taken for a bride; a diplomat's fur read as an insult; a merchant's borrowed silk exposed at the gate. If nobody can misdress or misread, you have drawn character sheets, not a dress code.

## 2. Named frameworks — what each is good for, what to distrust

| Framework | Claim | Craft use | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barthes (1967) | three garments; fashion-writing semes; signified = Fashion | discourse layer design; catalog rhetoric (§1) | magazine text only, synchronic, no wearers — not a theory of what dress encodes |
| Eco (1976) | clothing as weak code; "given" elements, strong on connotation | misreading engines; deniability | forcing denotation onto street dress |
| Simmel (1904) | trickle-down: elite differentiation + mass imitation drive cycles | court-to-street emulation dynamics | unidirectional; trickle-up (subculture, Polhemus 1994) and trickle-across (King 1963, in Greyser, ed., *Toward Scientific Marketing*) are documented countercases; McCracken (1988) rehabilitates a two-way model |
| Veblen (1899) | conspicuous consumption/waste; dress displays the wearer's (often the husband's) purse | cost-display engineering, substitution channels | a caricature of "savage" display; ignores taste-work and meaning |
| Bourdieu (1979/84) | taste as class habitus; "natural" taste = inherited capital | the connoisseur gap (§6 L2) | the frame is specific to French class structure — port with care |
| Davis (1992) | fashion as negotiated solution to identity ambivalence; codes unstable *by design* | oscillation devices (understate/overstate, street/formal) | not a typology; refuses the tidy code |
| McCracken (1988) | reputation transfers through consumption; innovation also bubbles up | two-way fashion politics | pop uptake flattens the argument |
| Joseph (1986) | uniforms engineer denotation: separation, standardization, legibility | the L6 end of the dial (§6) | covers only the engineered end |
| Roach-Higgins & Eicher (1992) | dress = body modifications + supplements (hair, scent, skin) — not just cloth | widen "costume" beyond fabric | definitional yield, low comparative content |
| Flügel (1930) | the Great Male Renunciation: Western men abandoned ornament c. 1800 | the flagship gender-drift case (§7) | his psychoanalytic machinery is discredited — keep the description, drop the libido theory |
| Hunt (1996); Hughes (1983) | sumptuary law = governance of consumption: legibility, fiscal, moral, protectionist motives | enforcement economies (§4) | laws ≠ practice; enforcement was chronically weak and sporadic |
| Wilson (1985) | fashion as modernity's utopia and mask; anti-determinist critique | women's experience, modernity texture | literary; not comparative |
| Signaling theory (Zahavi 1975; Zahavi & Zahavi 1997; Bird & Smith 2005) | costly displays are credible *because* costly (handicap) | why furs and banned silks make honest-ish signals | over-explains: cost alone generates no meaning; use as the economics under a code, not the code |
| Lurie (1981); Polhemus (1994) | dress-as-language; street style | idea catalogs, prompt generators | pop: the linguistic metaphor and the "tribes" taxonomy both overextend — treat as rhetoric |

## 3. The channel grid — what dress encodes

| Channel | Encodes | Real exemplars |
|---|---|---|
| rank / political | office, court rank, faction at law | Tang rank-color ladder (Benn 2002, via color-symbolism.md §3); Ming–Qing *buzi* rank badges: civil officials birds (1st = crane), military beasts (1st = qilin), nine grades (Garrett 1994; Cammann 1952); Roman senatorial *latus clavus* stripe and magistrates' *toga praetexta* (Sebesta & Bonfante 1994); Ottoman turban forms by station (Faroqhi 2000); heraldic surcoats and chivalric collars (Woodcock & Robinson 1988, via color-symbolism.md) |
| rite / lifecycle | marital status, mourning phase, age-grade, initiation | the *wǔfú* five hemp mourning grades (*Liji*; color-symbolism.md §4); Victorian mourning gradations crape → mauve half-mourning (Taylor 1983); the *toga virilis* assumption; the *yajñopavīta* sacred thread as twice-born marker (Kane 1930–62); Heian *kasane* layer-color conventions keyed to season and occasion (Dalby 1993) |
| region / ethnicity | group boundary, origin, allegiance to place | dress as ethnic boundary marker (Barth 1969; Eicher 1995); the invented Scottish clan tartan (§7); Greek island vs. mainland embroidery grammars (Johnstone 1961; Taylor 1998) |
| gender | sex/gender role, and policing of its transgression | the pink/blue flip (Paoletti 2012); the Great Male Renunciation (Flügel 1930); late-Roman ban on "barbarian" trousers within Rome (Cod. Theod. 14.10.2, 397); the 1800 Paris police ordinance requiring permission for women to dress as men — long obsolete, formally abrogated 2013 (Bard 2010) |
| occupation | service, craft, guild, unit | livery (badged servant dress — wearing the master's badge as portable identity; English anti-livery statutes 1377 onward — 1 Ric. II c. 7, statutes of livery from 1390 (McKelvie 2020)); religious habits (Benedictine black vs Cistercian white, color-symbolism.md §5); legal and academic robes; military insignia engineering (Joseph 1986); modern engineered codes: surgical scrubs by role, site hard-hat colors (convention, not law) |
| faction / allegiance | retinue, sect, street tribe | medieval *mi-parti* parti-colored dress of courts and retinues; the zoot suit as minority-style assertion and flashpoint — Zoot Suit Riots, Los Angeles 1943 (Mazón 1984); modern sports kits as the decayed form |
| age / grade | childhood, majority, seniority, retirement | Euro-American boys in dresses until ~age 5, pre-20th c. (Paoletti 2012); mourning phases graded by kinship distance (Taylor 1983); academic hoods by degree as engineered seniority |

- **Channels cross-cut, and conflict is plot**: one garment is simultaneously a rank, region, gender, and rite read. A high-rank woman in low-region fabric; a servant's livery worn out of service = impersonation; a widow's white at a wedding (color-symbolism.md §4). Design the grid so at least two channels collide somewhere in any named culture.

## 4. Sumptuary display — the law of legibility

- **Four documented functions** (Hunt 1996; Hughes 1983): (1) rank-legibility — anti-emulation, keeping the ladder readable; (2) fiscal — luxury taxes; (3) moral-religious — anti-luxury sermons turned statute; (4) protectionist — domestic industry (e.g., the 1571 English caps statute, 13 Eliz. I c. 19).
- **On the books ≠ on the street.** Enforcement was chronically weak (Hunt 1996); the statutes record elite *anxiety* as much as practice. Design consequence: a sumptuary regime is only as strong as its enforcement economy — informers paid from fines, dress inspection at city gates, guild self-policing, or nothing. Dead-letter laws that everyone violates are themselves realistic texture, and every violation is a scene.
- **The defiant-display channel**: prohibition displaces display rather than ending it. Edo *chōnin* moved luxury to linings, under-collars, and undersleeves — visible only up close, by the connoisseur (Dalby 1993); Roman matrons' wardrobe stood behind the *Lex Oppia* repeal debate (Livy 34); counterfeit rank was policed as its own offence, not just a metaphor — unauthorized purple use drew fines in late-Roman law (Cod. Theod. 10.20.18) and enforcement is attested (Suetonius, *Nero* 32.3).
- **Display is a dial, not a switch** (Veblen 1899; Currid-Halkett 2017): visible (cloth, hue) → quasi-visible (linings, hems, interiors) → performative (feasts, funerals, gifts, funeral processions) → positional (what one *declines* to wear — the understatement code; the modern "stealth wealth" endpoint).

| Regime | Instrument | What it polices | Fate / drift |
|---|---|---|---|
| England 1363, 1574 | Statutes of Apparel (37 Edw. III; Elizabethan revision; Hooper 1915) | cloth, fur, and jewel by income/rank class | repeated, poorly enforced, obsolete by the 17th c. |
| Tang–Qing China | rank-color ladder; *buzi* badges (Benn 2002; Garrett 1994) | hue by court rank; badge fauna by grade | collapses with the examination order; hue rank survives ceremonially |
| Byzantium | purple monopoly (Cardon 2007, via color-symbolism.md §9) | the imperial dye itself | monopoly decays with the murex economy |
| Tokugawa Japan | bakufu edicts on chōnin silk and pattern | fiber, pattern, sleeve length | evasion channel: hidden undersides (Dalby 1993) |
| Rome | *Lex Oppia* (215–195 BC); toga required of citizens in the forum; late trousers bans (Livy 34; Cod. Theod. 14.10) | women's finery; citizen body-cover; "barbarian" dress | repealed by political contest; dress bans die with the dress order itself |
| Ottoman lands | kanunname dress regulations by reign (Faroqhi 2000) | turban/robe forms by confession and station | Tanzimat-era relaxation as equality policy — law *lowering* the dial |

- **Craft rule: write the fine schedule and the court case.** Who profits from enforcement; what the rich buy instead (food, servants, jewels — the substitution channels, Veblen 1899); which statute everyone ignores and which one ends a family.

## 5. Modesty codes — readable, deniable, viewer-relative

- **Calibration first**: "modesty" is not a human constant about the body. Nudity norms vary enormously and track sociality, not anatomy (Barcan 2004). "Clothing began for shame" is the Eden theory among several origin hypotheses (thermal, protection, decoration) — none has consensus. Body-lice molecular clocks date sewn clothing to the late Pleistocene — ~72,000 ± 42,000 ya (Kittler et al. 2003) or 83,000–170,000 ya (Toups et al. 2011); use as "late Pleistocene sewn garments," not a date.
- **A modesty code is legible only against a simultaneous display economy.** Every modesty regime coexists with necklines, hems, tightness, transparency, movement — and a temptation law. A culture whose women cover hair but compete in embroidered veils has *both* systems on one garment; that braid is the realistic one. Design step: give the modesty code its display sibling and its policing speech.
- **Honor–shame coupling** (Peristiany 1965; Pitt-Rivers 1977): in Mediterranean-type complexes, dress indexes *family* honor — the garment is the household's face in public. Consequence for craft: dress control is kin politics; a hemline can be read as a treaty violation, and policing is done by relatives before constables.
- **Piety as willed sign** (El Guindi 1999; Mahmood 2005): veiling maps privacy, modesty, and resistance at once, and pious women may adopt the veil as *cultivated practice* — the same garment reads as oppression to outside observers and as agency to the wearer. This is the flagship outsider-misreading device; it also means two veils in one street can signal rival theological politics.
- **Modesty codes are viewer-relative, not garment-relative.** Locate, per culture: the covered/uncovered boundary per body-part; per context (bath vs. street, field vs. sanctuary); per lifecycle stage (maiden, wife, widow, renouncer); per audience (same-sex vs. mixed). A garment is modest only relative to a viewer and a room — the same wrap that is neutral at home is scandalous at the gate.
- **Failure mode**: "people here are modest" as flavor. If there is no display channel, no viewer-relativity, and no enforcement economy, there is no modesty code — just censorship of the author.

## 6. The legibility dial — how readable dress is in a given society

- **Definition**: the dial measures how much of the social order a passerby can read from dress, and who is licensed to read it. It is a property of the society's *infrastructure* (law + production + media), not of any garment.

| Station | Society type / exemplar | What a stranger reads | Craft consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| L6 engineered | army at inspection, heraldic law, Qing bureaucracy, Tokugawa status order | near-denotative rank, occupation, unit | imposture is a specific crime; a forged badge is enough credential; scenes: checkpoints, inspections, untrained foreigners reading nothing |
| L5 sumptuary | late-medieval city, Byzantium | rank mostly readable, policed by law | fines and denunciations; borrowed cloth as fraud; scenes: gate inspections, informers |
| L4 rite-indexed | small-scale, lifecycle-coded societies | marital status, mourning, age-grade — to locals | outsiders illiterate; the misread widow scene; dress as in-group key |
| L3 boundary | frontier and contact zones | ethnicity/faction readable; class less so | passing, border re-dressing; crossing a river changes your name-level |
| L2 connoisseur | industrial city (Bourdieu 1979; Currid-Halkett 2017) | class readable via fabric, fit, shoes, repairs — by initiates only | the "who is really rich" game; counterfeiting via tailoring; single-stitch reads |
| L1 noise | full casualization + deliberate anti-fashion | almost nothing; ambiguity chosen | legibility outsources to other channels — accent, ID, speech registers (sociolinguistics-registers.md); misidentification as plot engine |

- **Dial-movers** (what raises/lowers a society's station): (1) law — sumptuary enforcement up, repeal down; (2) production tech — bespoke, livery, guild issue = up; the patented lockstitch machines of the 1840s–50s and ready-to-wear = down; (3) urban anonymity and migration = down; (4) media — the fashion-discourse layer (Barthes 1967) manufactures fast-moving weak codes = down in denotation, up in connoisseur noise; (5) deliberate subversion — counterfeit crests, stealth wealth, uniform-wearing elites; (6) state collapse — the legibility crash, the city that loots its own uniforms.
- **The imposture theorem**: the higher the dial, the easier the costume-only fraud. At L6 a forged badge is a forged identity; at L2–L1 dress alone cannot carry the imposture — manners, accent, and vocabulary betray (hand the forger to the sociolinguistics file). Write impostors whose *outfit* passes and whose *speech register* fails.
- **Pockets**: every society runs multiple stations at once — a warship is L6 inside an L2 port. Pockets are scene engines: the same coat that is silent ashore is a verdict aboard.

## 7. Cross-era signaling drift — signals die, get invented, reverse

| Signal | Era A | Era B | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| silk | Chinese imperial monopoly cloth, rank-grade stuff | Italian silk cities under sumptuary limit → industrial silk affordable; rank force gone, formal residue remains | scarcity collapse via tech diffusion (sericulture reaches Byzantium mid-6th c.: Procopius, *Wars* 8.17 reports monks smuggling silkworm eggs c. 552 — the year is disputed, 551–554) |
| trousers | Rome: "barbarian" dress, banned within the city (Cod. Theod. 14.10) | medieval hose → trousers universal; *sans-culottes* make the trouser a political name | conquest-inversion: the barbarian's legwear wins and is re-coded as citizen wear |
| clan tartan | regional weaves; dress Act ban 1746, repeal 1782 | Scott stages George IV's 1822 Edinburgh pageant in Highland dress; 19th-c. commerce codifies clan-by-clan tartans on the strength of the forged *Vestiarium Scoticum* (1842) (Trevor-Roper, in Hobsbawm & Ranger 1983; some regional (not clan) weaves predate 1746 — the Glen Affric tartan, C14-dated 1500–1600) | invented tradition: ban → pageant → commerce → "ancient" |
| male ornament | ruffed, embroidered courts | the Great Male Renunciation c. 1800 → the dark suit as default (Flügel 1930) | gender-code inversion at the top of the market, then mass adoption |
| pink / blue | trade-press advice pins pink to boys (e.g., Earnshaw's, 1918, as collected in Paoletti 2012) | reversal settled mid-20th c.; now read as timeless | retail standardization retro-writing itself as eternal |
| denim | riveted workwear (US Patent 139,121, 1873) | youth-rebellion flashpoint (1950s school bans — Sullivan 2006) → global casual default → distressed/heritage denim as luxury signal | trickle-up + scarcity inversion: engineered wear becomes engineered expense |
| qipao / cheongsam | 1920s Shanghai modern fashion (Finnane 2008) | today read as "timeless traditional" dress | invented-tradition compression inside a century |
| mourning dress | crape industry, graded phases, warehouses (Taylor 1983) | WWI economies and supply collapse → plain black; reads now as "formal," not "grieving" | ritual economy collapse; meaning decays into default |
| zoot suit | wartime cloth rationing makes the long-jacket suit an open defiance (Mazón 1984) | minority assertion → riot flashpoint → folklore | signal manufactured *by the law itself*: rationing converts a cut into a statement |

- **Drift leaves strata**: a culture's dress is readable archaeologically — the old rank cloth survives only as wedding wear; the once-scandalous is now default; the "ancient" costume has a founding scandal two generations back. Give every timeless costume in your world a dated founding fight.
- **Synchronization device**: eras misalign across regions. A provincial envoy arrives at the capital wearing the *previous* era's signal — courtly silk the season silk fell. Instant date-and-class misreading; pair with the loanword-strata method in linguistics/language-contact.md.

## 8. Calibration flags — contested or discredited, in one place

- **"Clothes are a language"** — metaphor, not model; no grammar, no duality (Eco 1976). Lurie (1981) and Polhemus (1994) are pop idea catalogs; use for prompts, not premises.
- **Barthes as a theory of what dress encodes** — no: he analyzed fashion *writing*, synchronically, 1960s France (§1).
- **Simmel trickle-down as the law of fashion** — contested; trickle-up and trickle-across are documented (McCracken 1988; King 1963, in Greyser, ed., *Toward Scientific Marketing*).
- **Flügel's psychoanalytic engine** — discredited; the Great Male Renunciation as *description* stands.
- **Veblen's display caricature** — real economics under it (Bird & Smith 2005), but his ethnography of "savage" display is period-flawed; do not build "primitive = conspicuous" cultures on it.
- **"Traditional costume is ancient"** — usually false; most European folk dress (Tracht, clan tartan, Welsh hat — current by the 1830s, but Lady Llanover's role as "inventor" is contested (Freeman 2007)) stabilized in the 18th–19th c. under romantic nationalism and tourism (Hobsbawm & Ranger 1983). The tartan story has its own revisionism wars — best-supported line: clan-specific assignment is 19th-c. commercial codification; some regional weaves predate the ban.
- **Modesty-origin theories** (shame, thermal, protection, decoration) — all contested, no consensus; the lice-clock dates are ranges with moving point estimates (§5).
- **"Uniforms are always honest"** — every engineered code generates its counterfeit economy; write it.
- **The 1800 Paris trousers decree** — real (ordonnance of 16 brumaire An IX / 7 November 1800), long fallen into desuetude, formally abrogated 2013 (Bard 2010); do not cite it as a functioning law.
- **Fabric-blind analysis** — weave, weight, fit, and repair state carry class signal even where hue does not (Bourdieu 1979; Currid-Halkett 2017); a hue-only audit of your world silently drops half the system (pattern-symbolism warning, color-symbolism.md §11).

## 9. Application protocol — building a fictional culture's dress code

1. **Inventory materials from biome and trade** (wool, flax, silk, cotton, fur, leather, barkcloth). The fiber economy sets the display budget: who can afford what, and in what colors (dye side: color-terminology.md §8; color-symbolism.md §10).
2. **Pick the legibility station per region and era** (§6), plus one deliberate pocket exception (the L6 ship inside the L2 port).
3. **Fill the channel grid** (§3): for rank, rite, region, gender, occupation, faction, age — one garment or attribute each. Keep a ledger per item: term | carrier | wearer | viewer | penalty | era-of-change (mirror of the color tuple, color-symbolism.md §1). Empty "viewer" or "penalty" cells = the item is decorative, not coded.
4. **Write the enforcement artifacts**: one sumptuary statute with a fine schedule and its profiting informers; one imposture law; one modesty rule *and its display-channel sibling*; one defiance practice (hidden-lining type, §4).
5. **Build the discourse layer** (Barthes §1): fashion plates, catalog rhetoric, sumptuary preambles, tailor jargon — the written garment that diverges from the worn one.
6. **Write the reading scenes**: stranger-reads-stranger at the gate; the misread widow; the counterfeit badge that passes and the accent that fails (§6 imposture theorem); the veil read two ways (§5).
7. **Plan the drift** (§7): date the founding scandal of each "ancient" costume; decide what collapses next when the dye or loom economy shifts; keep one region one era behind.
8. **Wire the language** (Garment-lexicon lane work in semantic-fields-lexicon-building.md): loanword strata as trade receipts — *chiffon* (French), *seersucker* (< Hindi sīrsakar < Persian *shīr-o-shakkar*, "milk and sugar"), rank-badge fauna terms; dress verbs and honorifics of donning/robing as registers (sociolinguistics-registers.md); taboo and euphemism for undress (politeness-taboo-euphemism.md).
9. **Test the system**: if no scene can produce a misreading, an imposture, a fine, or a defiance, the dress code is set dressing.
- **Failure modes**: denotative street fashion (the language fantasy); every culture policing gender identically; sumptuary laws with no enforcement economy; modest cultures with no display channel; timeless costumes without founding scandals; one legibility station for the whole world; forgetting that fabric, cut, and fit signal even when the palette is uniform.

- → linguistics/sociolinguistics-registers.md: the register system is dress's linguistic twin — low-dial (L1–L2) societies outsource legibility from cloth to speech; the impostor's failure point moves from the tailor to the accent.
- → linguistics/semantic-fields-lexicon-building.md: garment lexicon inventories, cut-and-fabric terminology, layered terms (the Japanese layering lexicon is the model, Dalby 1993).
- → linguistics/historical-linguistics.md and language-contact.md: garment loanwords as trade strata; terminology outliving the referent (the "silk" word after silk falls, §7).
- → linguistics/politeness-taboo-euphemism.md and semantics-pragmatics.md: dress taboos, dress-politeness formulae, undress euphemism, the pragmatics of "you can't wear that here."
- → linguistics/linguistic-anthropology.md: dress as communicative practice alongside greeting formulae and address systems.
- → myth-symbol/ritual-liminality.md: rite-of-passage dress — segregation garments, liminal undress, reaggregation robes; mourning phases as a liminal sequence (Taylor 1983).
- → myth-symbol/animal-symbolism.md: fur, feather, and leather symbolism; *buzi*-style fauna rank badges; heraldic beasts worn as identity.
- → myth-symbol/number-symbolism.md: badge counts, pleat counts, plait numbers as graded signals.
- → culture-material/ sibling costume files: this file owns the sign-system layer; construction and cut files own the objects; cite both in any costume canon handoff.
- → era files: sumptuary regimes, enforcement economies, and §7 drift breaks as datable strata — a dress-law repeal is an era marker the same way a sound shift is.
- → lore-shokunin: reference only. Which culture canonically wears what is theirs; this file supplies the derivation method, the comparative exemplars, the drift strata, and the standing instruction that any canon dress code must survive §1's misreading test, §6's dial placement, and §8's calibration flags.
