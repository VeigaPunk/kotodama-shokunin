## 1. The frame: body as text, and the three-column ledger

- **Foundation texts**: Mauss (1935) — even walking, swimming, and digging are "techniques of the body," culturally transmitted and symbolically loaded; Bourdieu — posture and gait are *hexis*, class and gender written as durable bodily style; Goffman (1963) — interaction runs on an "expressive order" of body idiom, including *civil inattention* (strangers agree not to read each other); Elias — conduct books (Erasmus 1530, Della Casa 1558, Castiglione 1528) show manners having a datable, class-driven history. Rubin (1988) and Schildkrout (2004) are the standard surveys of body marking as social inscription.
- **The three-column ledger** (this file's organizing instrument): WEALTH codes are alienable, assayable, and displayable — they can pay debts; RITE codes are inalienable — they cannot be sold, only renounced, mourned, or disfigured; PUNISHMENT codes are administered — someone with authority makes the mark. Real systems mix columns, and the mix is where plots live: a punitive face-mark (Song convict) that becomes a badge of belonging; a ritual mark (moko) that becomes collateral (mokomokai head trade); a wealth ornament (pearl) that becomes contraband (sumptuary).
- **Craft rule**: a body code is a *language* only if it has a reading public — an unmarked adult in Gell's Polynesia read as unfinished, an unscarred Nuer man read as a boy, an ungreyed queue read as unfulfilled obligation. If nobody in your world can read the mark at interaction distance, it is private art, not status.

## 2. Jewelry as portable wealth

- **Weighed-metal regimes**: the Viking-world *hacksilver* economy — arm-rings and coins hoarded, folded, cut, and paid by weight against hand-scales (Graham-Campbell & Kidd 1980; merchant graves at Birka carry scales and weights). The Irish "ring money" of the later Bronze Age and the Atlantic gold tradition (Snettisham, Norfolk: Iron Age torc hoards) show bullion ornaments as stored value. Myths encode the physics: Odin's ring Draupnir drips eight copies every ninth night; lords are kenned as *ring-givers*; saga oaths are sworn on reddened temple-rings (saga formulae; exact locus (to-verify)).
- **Counted-object regimes**: bead and shell economies — Zulu beadwork as message-bearing personal wealth (Krige 1936), Maasai bead composition encoding age-set, clan, and community (Spencer 1988), wampum belts as both wealth and mnemonic treaty record (colonial tender rates from the 1630s (to-verify)), Fijian *tabua* whale teeth presented at weddings, funerals, and apologies. Malinowski's kula: soulava necklaces and mwali armshells circulate eternally, named, unspendable — wealth as relationship, not accumulation.
- **Inalienable heirlooms**: Māori *taonga tuku iho* — named pounamu (greenstone) mere and hei-tiki pass down with their histories; Hawaiian featherwork ('ahu'ula capes, lei hulu) concentrated rank in bird-catch labor. These objects refuse the market: stealing one is not theft but ontological insult — excellent engine for fictional feud.
- **Coin-as-jewelry and jewelry-as-document**: Roman *bulla* (freeborn boy's amulet, surrendered at the toga virilis), coin-pectorals, ceremonial Jewish marriage rings with architectural bezels; signet rings and intaglio seals make ornament *notarially functional* — the ring is a pen (couples writing-systems.md). Late-antique finger-heart-vein belief ("vena amoris") is reported in Macrobius/Aulus Gellius (locus (to-verify)) — in-world folk anatomy, not fact; keep it as a culture's belief, not your world's truth.
- **Bridewealth and the woman's portion**: jewelry as gendered capital — South Asian *strīdhana* (smṛti-law category) and the tāli/mangalsutra as marriage token; Fulani women's great gold earrings as transferable family wealth (kwottenai; art-historical literature). Goody & Tambiah (1973) is the comparative baseline: who *holds* the gold is a legal question with plot consequences.
- **Craft rules**: (1) decide whether wealth is *weighed* (metal — anyone with scales is an assay office), *counted* (beads/shells — messengers matter), or *inalienable* (heirlooms — courts and curses matter); (2) visible-wearing is the point: an economy of ornament is an economy of display, so its pawn curve is a social seismograph — Renaissance *Monti di Pietà* (Franciscan pawn banks; Perugia 1462 commonly cited as first (to-verify)) exist because dowry gold was regularly in hock; (3) currency reform and conquest hit the jewelry box first — melting a temple hoard or debasing the coin is how a regime announces itself on the body.

## 3. Tattoo systems — the Polynesian core and the punitive column

- **Samoa**: the male *malofie* (pattern set called *pe'a*) runs hip-to-knee in dense geometric fields; women receive the *malu*. Work is done by *tufuga tā tatau* — hereditary master specialists with bone-tipped combs ('au) and mallet, assistants, and chant; the process is rank-bearing, expensive, and communal (Mallon 2002; Gell 1993). London Missionary Society pressure from the 1830s suppressed and displaced it; the twentieth-century revival is itself a named history — model this loop for any fictional mission field.
- **Aotearoa**: Māori *tā moko* is genealogy cut into skin — facial moko for men of rank, *moko kauae* (chin) for women; designs are so individualized that drawings of moko served as legal signatures (rangatira signed the 1840 Treaty of Waitangi with moko drawings). The *uhi* chisel leaves grooved incisions, not pricked lines. Robley (1896) is the classic source — and also documents the *mokomokai* (preserved heads) trade of the musket wars: genealogical skin becoming exchangeable wealth, the severest column-leak on record. Modern practice distinguishes *moko* (carried with the right to it) from *kirituhi* (Māori-style skin art without lineage claim) — a living example of an authenticity doctrine.
- **Marquesas and Hawai'i**: Marquesan tattooing approached total coverage, with pattern vocabularies documented by von den Steinen (1925–28); Hawaiian *kakau* fed the English word — "tattoo" entered English from a Polynesian form (Tahitian *tatau* is the usual donor) via Cook's 1769 voyage (OED s.v. *tattoo*). Loanword physics worth copying: the contact word for a body technique becomes the world's word.
- **Calibration — "sacred vs status" is a false fork**: Gell (1993) shows the two readings are one system. Operations ran under *tapu* (specialist, chant, seclusion, food rules — the rite column) while the finished skin declared rank, genealogy, endurance, and eligibility (the status column); theological and sociological interpreters simply stand at different ends of one protocol. Do not build "a sacred tattoo culture" or "a status tattoo culture"; build the protocol and let both emerge. Note also Gell's (1998) frame: the tattoo is part of the *distributed person* — it can be split off (heads taken, skin commemorated) and still act.
- **The punitive column**: Shang–Han China counted mò/黥 (face-inking) among the Five Punishments (Bodde & Morris 1967); Song military practice tattooed soldiers and exiled convicts on the face — the *Water Margin* heroes' facial marks are that institution fossilized in fiction; Yue Fei's back tattoo (盡忠報國) is attested in the Song History biography, while the mother-tattooing scene is later legend. Rome's *stigma* marked slaves, criminals, and (illegally) Christians; C. P. Jones (1987, *JRS* 77) is the standard study; a late-Roman law moved marks off the face (Code of Justinian (to-verify book)) — Christian scruple reaching statute.
- **The inversions**: Herodotus (5.6) reports Thracians ranking the tattooed noble over the unmarked commoner — status tattoo is not universal but is not universally low; Pazyryk frozen tombs carry figural tattoos on élite bodies (Rudenko 1970); Ötzi's joint-adjacent charcoal marks (61 groups per the 2015 re-survey (to-verify)) look therapeutic. Ainu women's mouth tattoos and Ryukyuan *hajichi* hand tattoos were suppressed by assimilationist Meiji policy (bans from 1871/1899, standard dates (to-verify)); Japan's 1872 tattoo ban (standard date; repealed under the Occupation, 1948 (to-verify)) pushed *irezumi* underground into the firemen's and criminal fraternities that still own its image.
- **Typology table** (mark a fictional system against every column):

| System | Who is marked | Column | Signature feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Samoan *malofie* / *malu* | men of standing; women | rite + status | comb-tapped by hereditary *tufuga*, chant protocol |
| Māori *tā moko* | rank-bearing men; women (kauae) | rite (genealogy) | chisel-grooved; doubles as legal signature |
| Roman *stigma* | slaves, criminals, some Christians | punishment | face-mark voids legal personhood (Jones 1987) |
| Chinese *mò* 黥 / Song soldier marks | convicts; drafted soldiers | punishment | among the Five Punishments; *Water Margin* fossil |
| Thracian (Herodotus 5.6) | nobles | status (inverted) | the unmarked man is of low birth |
| Pazyryk/Scythian | élite bodies | rite/status (contested) | figural animals on frozen skin (Rudenko 1970) |
| Ötzi's charcoal groups | one individual | therapeutic? | joint-adjacent placement (61 groups (to-verify)) |
| Ainu mouth / Ryukyuan *hajichi* | women | rite (marriage) | suppressed by assimilation-era state bans |

- **Craft rule**: permanence is the message. The tattoo is the only costume you cannot take off — which is why states choose it for punishment, gods for covenant, and careers (soldier, sailor, convict) as its accidental bearers. Ask of every marked body: who is licensed to mark, in what rite, and what does the mark do to the wearer's legal capacity?

## 4. Scarification — relief, lineage, and the keloid technology

- **Mechanics**: cuts are irritated (ash, plant juice, oil) and re-cut over months to build hypertrophic/keloid relief — scarification is *sculpture in skin*, slower than tattooing and darker-reading on highly melanized skin; this physiology is the standard explanation for the African/Islamic-region tilt toward cicatrization and the Polynesian tilt toward pigment — treat it as hypothesis-grade, not law.
- **Named systems**: Nuer *gar* — the six-line brow scar cut at manhood initiation, the visible boundary of age-set and personhood (Evans-Pritchard 1940); Dinka forehead marks likewise encode initiation stage (Lienhardt 1961); Yoruba *ila* facial marks identify lineage, and in the slave-raiding era are said to have served as ID for lost and kidnapped kin — scars as portable identity document; Sepik (New Guinea) initiation scarification reads the initiated back as crocodile hide. Aboriginal Australian cicatrices mark initiation in both ceremonial and everyday reading.
- **Calibration**: the tidy "one tribe, one scar pattern" maps are collector simplifications — patterns vary, drift, borrow, and go out of fashion like any dress system; and the "scar = African, tattoo = Polynesian" is a bracket, not a boundary (both techniques occur on both sides).
- **Craft rule**: scarification's variable is *pain endurance made public* — the initiation cut certifies the body under observation. Pair it with your liminality machinery (couples ritual-liminality.md): the cut is the moment the old social skin is removed and the new one is cut on.

## 5. Hair codes — the most regulable part of the body

- **Why hair**: it is visible, continuous, painlessly removable, regrows, and can be kept (relics, love-tokens, Victorian hairwork jewelry). Every regime that wants a cheap, legible, reversible punishment or loyalty test reaches for hair.
- **Four politics of hair**:

| Mode | Real exemplars | The rule it enforces |
|---|---|---|
| religious vow | Khalsa *kesh* (uncut, since 1699) + dastar; Buddhist *pabbajjā* head-shaving; clerical tonsure (Roman corona vs Celtic anterior style — the Whitby dispute of 664, Bede HE 3.25–26); Nazirite (Num 6) behind Rastafari locks | the body's growth belongs to the god/order, not the wearer |
| state loyalty | Qing queue — the 1645 edict's slogan "keep the hair, lose the head"; queue-cutting as treason, Taiping "long hairs," 1911–12 queue-cutting campaigns (Rhoads 2000); Meiji *danpatsu-rei* (1871) and the *zangiri* "cropped" head as the modern (anti-cropping riots reported from several prefectures (to-verify)) | hairstyle is a sworn allegiance — conquest and reform are dated on the head |
| rank & achievement | Mexica warrior hair: youths shaved, captives earned the long lock, élite "shorn ones" (cuāuhocēlōtl) wore a bound topknot; cowardice punished by shaving (Durán/Clendinnen 1991; punishment detail (to-verify)); Merovingian *reges criniti* — cutting a long-haired king's hair (e.g., Gregory of Tours on Chlodomer's sons) un-kings him | the head is a CV; shearing is demotion or deposition |
| lifecycle & mourning | Greek *koureion* — coming-of-age hair dedicated to Artemis/rivers; Achilles' hair-shaving for Patroklos (Iliad 23); Hindu sons' head-shaving at a parent's death, and widow-shaving in some castes; *opposite* Torah rule — no baldness for the dead (Deut 14:1); Hopi maiden whorls cut at marriage (Titiev 1944); married women's covered hair vs the prostitute's marked or bared hair in medieval city statutes (Karras 1996) | hair tracks the legal person: maiden, wife, mourner, initiate, corpse-warden |

- **Craft rules**: (1) one culture's mourning haircut is its neighbor's taboo (Hindu shaving vs Deut 14:1) — differentiation-by-taboo between adjacent cultures is cheap realism; (2) the haircut is the cheapest legal event a state can stage — use it for era seams (couples era-periodization-models.md): the queue edict and the 1871 edict each cut a nation's heads into a new calendar; (3) shaved heads read as slave, monk, convict, novice, or penitent in most traditions — decide which of those your world conflates.

## 6. Cosmetics and their vocabulary

- **Etymology anchors** (kotodama material): Greek *kosmein* "arrange, order" — the same root as *cosmos*: grooming is world-ordering, and Greek already split the field into kosmētikē (whole-body adornment) vs kommotikē (hair-dressing) (later lexicography (to-verify locus)). Arabic *kuḥl* (kohl) crossed into Latin/English both as *kohl* and — via the distillation sense — as *alcohol*: one cosmetic word became the word for purified substance itself.
- **Egypt**: kohl is galena/stibnite ground on cosmetic palettes (the Narmer Palette is a cosmetic palette); malachite supplied the green; analysis has found synthetic lead salts (laurionite/phosgenite) in Egyptian eye-makeup, and a claimed immune-health benefit remains speculative (Nicholson & Shaw 2000). Hathor is "Lady of Malachite" — the cosmetic and the goddess share a chemistry.
- **Rome/Medieval**: Ovid's *Medicamina* is a didactic cosmetic poem — your world's beauty manual as literary genre; *cerussa* (white lead) and *fucus* (rouge) are the stock word-pair; Tertullian's *De cultu feminarum* opens the moral-register war on paint that European languages never closed ("paint" as deceit is a register, not a description).
- **Japan/South Asia**: Heian *oshiroi* white, shaved-and-repainted *hikimayu* brows, *ohaguro* teeth-blackening (ferric acetate + tannin) as the adult/married woman's marker (Dalby 1993; Meiji edict against court tooth-blackening commonly dated 1870 (to-verify)); sindoor in the hair-parting and the bindi as Hindu married-woman markers whose *absence* signals widowhood — cosmetics as wearable marital status, removable at bereavement.
- **Calibration**: (1) lead cosmetics certainly contained lead; the claim that they lethally poisoned their wearers is plausible but dose-dependent and debated — write "the mask may have been killing her," not "the mask killed her"; (2) the famous "1770 English act" fining women for luring men via cosmetics does not appear in parliamentary statute records — treat it as apocryphal (beauty-history checks (to-verify)); (3) "painted = prostitute" is period-specific (Victorian England) and inverted elsewhere (Heian court, Sengoku firemen) — pin the norm to a time and class, never to humanity.
- **Craft rule**: build each culture's cosmetic lexicon in four strata — material word (kohl, ceruse), applicator/tool word (palette, brush, mirror case), occasion word (the bridal application, the mourning removal), and moral slur ("painted"). The strata date your scenes: a character who says the slur is telling you her era and faction.

## 7. Gesture and posture — engineered gradients and their failure modes

- **The toolkit**: Ekman & Friesen (1969) classify nonverbal behavior into *emblems* (culture-specific gestures with dictionary meanings — the conlang-adjacent category), illustrators, affect displays, regulators, adaptors; Morris et al. (1979) mapped emblem geography across Europe. Hall (1966) named proxemic distance bands (intimate/personal/social/public). These four labels are enough to script any fictional etiquette.
- **Engineered honorific gradients**: Japanese bow degrees (business-etiquette canon: ~15° eshaku, ~30° keirei, ~45° saikeirei); Thai *wai* and Khmer *sampeah* with hand-height levels keyed to relative seniority; the Chinese *kowtow* institutionalized as three kneelings and nine prostrations before the emperor. Posture gradients are honorific grammar — gradient depth encodes status distance exactly as speech-level morphemes do (couples sociolinguistics-registers.md).
- **The embassy scenes are canon**: Persian *proskynesis* — Greeks read prostration as god-honoring or slavery; Callisthenes' refusal before Alexander (Arrian 4.10–12) turned a posture code into a murder plot; Macartney's 1793 kowtow negotiation with Qianlong's court settled on a one-knee formula (accounts differ on details (to-verify)); Liutprand at Constantinople (968) documents Byzantine *adoratio*, gate-and-timing humiliations, and the mechanical throne — diplomacy as posture negotiation. Homage's *immixtio manuum* + kiss (Bloch) is the same grammar made feudal; the Judas kiss and the curule chair (who may sit) are its exploitation and its furniture.
- **Gradient table**:

| Gradient | Keyed to | Levels |
|---|---|---|
| Japanese bow | rank, formality, apology weight | ~15° / ~30° / ~45° (etiquette canon, not law) |
| Thai *wai* | relative seniority; monk > lay | hand height chest → nose → brow |
| Khmer *sampeah* | seniority | five levels by hand height |
| Qing *kowtow* | the emperor's unique status | three kneelings, nine prostrations |
| Byzantine *adoratio* | the sacral emperor | prostration + kiss of the purple |
| Feudal homage | lord–vassal bond | *immixtio manuum* + *osculum* |
- **Failure modes to mine**: emblem collisions (the Greek *moutza* open palm — its cinders folk-etymology unverified; nod/shake inversion reported from Bulgaria, drifting by generation); counting systems differ (Bede's *De temporum ratione* canonizes Latin finger-numbers; Japanese counting opens the palm 1–5 and closes 6–10); gaze, smile, and silence norms invert across cultures; military-salute and handshake origin stories are unverifiable folklore.
- **Calibration**: Ekman's universal-basic-emotions program is contested — Barrett et al. (2019) conclude expressions are far less universal and less readable than the pop version claims. For fiction that is good news: misreading a face is realistic; give your cultures real reading manuals and real misreadings. Juvenal's *verso pollice* (3.36) does not specify which way the thumb went — the gladiator-death gesture is genuinely unrecoverable, a standing lesson in how gesture evidence decays.

## 8. Calibration — contested, discredited, apocryphal

- **The barter myth**: there is no documented pure-barter economy from which ornament-money emerged (Humphrey 1985); shell, bead, and metal ornaments are *social* monies from the start. Never model a culture as having bartered before it wore wealth.
- **"Zulu love-letter" color dictionaries**: the neat fixed code-tables (white = love, blue = fidelity...) circulate in collector literature and postdate the classic ethnography (Krige 1936) — treat them as vendor canon; real bead message systems are local, negotiable, and contested.
- **Tattoo = criminal is not a constant**: Thracian nobles, Pazyryk élites, and modern revivals invert it; Edo-period Japan had firemen and courtesans wearing full work before the punitive image fossilized. Track which column a mark sits in *in your world's present tense*.
- **The 1770 English cosmetics act is apocryphal** (§6); **chastity belts are a nineteenth-century myth-machine** — surviving "medieval" examples are mostly fabrications (Classen 2007). Both are useful as in-world fake histories, exactly the kind your fictional antiquarians should peddle.
- **Salute, handshake, moutza origin stories**: folklore; **pollice verso**: ambiguous (§7); **lead-cosmetics lethality**: debated; **expression universality**: contested (Barrett et al. 2019); **climate-determinism for scar vs tattoo**: hypothesis-grade (§4).
- **Tonsure-as-slave-mark**: the reading that clerical shaving borrows the Roman slave's head ("servant of God" made visible) is a modern argument, not an ancient statement (to-verify) — usable as your world's scholarly dispute.

## 9. Applying it to a fictional culture — build sequence

1. **Draw the three-column ledger** (wealth / rite / punishment) for the culture's body codes; then design one leak: a punitive mark that becomes a badge, a ritual mark pawned, a wealth ornament that turns contraband.
2. **Choose the wealth-form** — weighed, counted, or inalienable. This single choice decides whether jewelry can settle a ransom, who must wear it, and what scales, bankers, and courts exist.
3. **Write the marking license**: who may mark whom (mother, guild, priest, state), with what tool ('au comb vs chisel vs blade), under what protocol (tapu, chant, seclusion, fee). Punishment-marking is always a state claim on the skin — even when contested.
4. **Run the ban loop in advance**: pick the banning agent (mission, shogunate, colonial office, health ministry), write the ban, then design what the ban produces — the underground style, the revival movement two generations later, the authenticity doctrine (moko vs kirituhi), and the fossil vocabulary that outlives the practice.
5. **Build the cosmetic lexicon in four strata** (material / tool / occasion / moral slur) and pin one marital or life-stage marker (ohaguro, sindoor, hikimayu) to a legal threshold.
6. **Engineer one posture gradient** keyed to your rank/kinship terms, and stage one embassy scene where the gradient is negotiated (proskynesis mode: refusal; kowtow mode: compromise; Byzantine mode: staged humiliation).
7. **Use hair as the era seam**: at least one conquest or modernization event in your history should be a hair edict, and at least one rebellion should have a hairstyle.
8. **Handoff to lore-shokunin**: deliver the code table (who wears/marks what), the assay (how a bystander verifies it), the sanction schedule (what happens to the wrong mark), and the identity documents your skins, heads, and hands constitute — method only; canon assignments are lore-shokunin's (lane rule).
