## 1. Three categories this entry keeps apart

A designer who merges these three gets a muddy grammar; a designer who keeps them apart gets three independent levers.

- **Evidentiality** marks the kind of evidence the speaker has for the statement: seen, heard, felt, inferred, told.
  S936
- **Epistemic modality** marks how far the speaker commits to the statement: certain, probable, possible.
  S938
- The SIL glossary entry states the same split and files evidentiality as a category whose primary meaning is information source.
  S940
- WALS ch. 77 builds the split into its coding rule: only markers that carry evidence, not degree of confidence, were counted, and English *must* fails the test because it still reads epistemically, while Dutch evidential *moeten* does not.
  S936
- de Haan's second argument is diachronic: evidential morphemes do not come from the same lexical sources as epistemic modals.
  S938
- **The boundary is contested, and the entry records both sides.** Tournadre & LaPolla argue that in Standard Tibetan evidential and epistemic auxiliaries are built from the same copulas and fuse into compound forms that carry either or both meanings; in Qiang, hearsay plus inferential stacked together marks extra uncertainty where hearsay alone does not.
  S939
- **Mirativity** (new or surprising information) is the third category. Tournadre & LaPolla reject reading the Tibetan sensory marker *'dug* as basically mirative: surprise is an overtone in some contexts, sensory access is the core.
  S939
- **Egophoricity** (personal knowledge, self-awareness) is a fourth axis that some systems grammaticalise beside source; see §4.
  S939

Design rule: decide the three (or four) axes separately, then decide which ones share morphology. Fusion is a choice with attested precedent, not a default.

## 2. The semantic space: what a system can distinguish

WALS ch. 77 counts grammatical evidentials only: affixes, clitics, particles and auxiliaries count; lexical adverbs (*reportedly*) and biclausal frames (*it is said that*) do not.
S936

Values of Map 77A as served on 2026-11-04, over a 418-language sample:
S936

- no grammatical evidentials: 181
- only indirect evidentials: 166
- both direct and indirect evidentials: 71

The value space, as the chapter defines it:
S936

- **Direct** (sensory) evidence: usually one general direct marker read as visual by default; some languages split off auditory (Koasati) or a non-visual sensory term (Tuyuca, where the evidential affixes also carry tense, person, number and gender).
- **Indirect** evidence, in two sub-kinds: **inferential** (from available physical traces; Khalkha *biz*) and **quotative / reportative / hearsay** (told by another person; Lezgian *-lda*). Many languages fold both into one general indirect marker (Dutch *moeten*).
- **Witnessed vs unwitnessed past** as a tense-bound pair: Turkish *-di* / *-miş*, Georgian, Komi-Zyrian, Haida, Ika.
- **The one-way implication.** No sample language has grammatical direct evidentials without grammatical indirect ones. A conlang with only a "witnessed" marker and nothing for hearsay or inference is therefore outside the sample's attested space — legal for fiction, but it should be a stated, motivated rarity.
- **Realis bias, with exceptions.** Evidentials cluster in realis and past contexts, yet the chapter shows an interrogative evidential (Barasano) and a future quotative (Tsova-Tush).

Design consequence: start from the indirect side. The cheapest attested system is one general indirect marker; add direct, then split indirect into inference vs report, then split sensory channels, in roughly that order of rarity.

## 3. Coding: where the marker lives

WALS ch. 78 maps the same 418 languages by morphological means, and states that the coding type reflects where the evidential came from historically (tense morphemes stay in the tense system, modal verbs stay modal).
S937

| Coding type (Map 78A) | Count in sample | Typical semantic load | Design consequence for a conlang | Attested anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no grammatical evidentials | 181 | lexical, optional attribution only | attribution is style; hedges live in adverbs and reporting verbs | S936 |
| verbal affix or clitic | 131 | any; the commonest strategy, on every continent except Africa | marker rides every finite verb; some clitics attach to any word class (Takelma quotative *-ihiʔ*) | S937 |
| part of the tense system | 24 | direct vs indirect, mostly past only | evidence and time fuse; the witnessed/unwitnessed past is a natural split (Turkish *-di* / *-miş*) | S937 |
| separate particle | 65 | almost always indirect only (exceptions discussed: Apalai, Lega) | a free quotative particle is attested; a free *visual* particle needs justification | S937 |
| modal morpheme | 7 | indirect, as one reading of a modal or irrealis form | evidential reading competes with an epistemic one; chiefly western European and some Australian (Gooniyandi) | S937 |
| mixed systems | 10 | particle plus affix, often quotative particle with sensory affix | two layers can carry two axes (Diyari quotative *pinti* beside sensory *-ku*) | S937 |

Coding generalisations worth enforcing in a design:

- Separate particles correlate very strongly with indirect-only meaning; the chapter treats the visual-particle cases as doubtful or explained by deictic origin (Lega *ampó* from a proximate pronoun).
  S937
- Tense-system evidentiality is mostly past-tense; Mingrelian, Svan and Tsova-Tush extend it to present and future, so a full-paradigm system is attested but marked.
  S937
- Partial grammaticalisation is a real state: Georgian has an indirect reading of the Perfect while the Aorist has not become a direct evidential. A conlang history can freeze at this half-way stage.
  S937
- Couple with linguistics/morphological-typology.md: an agglutinating, suffixing verb is the cheapest home for the commonest coding type; an isolating design will push the marker into particles and therefore, by the generalisation above, toward indirect meanings.

## 4. Beyond source: access, egophoric knowledge, and the anticipation rule

Tournadre & LaPolla propose a broader working definition: evidential marking represents source and access to information according to the speaker's perspective and strategy. They restrict "source" to verbal (reported) source and use "access" for sensory, inferential and other non-verbal ways the speaker reaches the information.
S939

Levers their Tibetic data adds to the WALS space:
S939

- **Egophoric / personal knowledge.** Standard Tibetan *yod* (egophoric) vs *'dug* (sensory) vs *yod-red* (factual) on the same existential sentence.
- **Endopathic access.** The sensory marker used with a first-person experiencer for hunger, pain or emotion; such statements are not shareable, so a confirmation tag sounds odd.
- **Epistemic authority.** Some languages bar a direct form for another person's inner state ("he is afraid"); only an inference from visible signs is licensed. The authors compare Japanese desiderative *-tai*.
- **The anticipation rule.** In questions the speaker selects the marker the *hearer* will need to answer: egophoric when asking about the hearer's own intentional act, sensory when asking an onlooker "what am I doing?".
- **Layered chains.** Each speaker in a reporting chain can mark access, formalised as S°: A(x) < S1: A(y) < S2: A(z); a hearsay suffix can sit on top of a sensory marker that belongs to the original reporter.
- **Memory and recency.** Long-stored direct experience may be reported with an indirect form, and Tibetan tag choice can track how active a referent is in the hearer's memory.
- **Dialect variation.** Spiti-Khunu-Garzha splits Standard Tibetan's sensory *'dug* into visual *'dug* and non-visual *grag* (from a verb "to sound"), making a four-way basic system out of a three-way one.

Design consequence: a "source-only" table (§2) under-specifies real systems. A culture design should state who may use the egophoric form about whom, and what the question form of each marker is.

## 5. Spread and history: why neighbours share systems

- WALS ch. 77 reports strong areal patterns: near-absence in Africa; indirect-only systems common in Europe (mostly Germanic, not English); direct+indirect systems clustered in the western United States, western Amazonia, the Caucasus and the Himalaya.
  S936
- Evidentiality diffuses easily between unrelated languages that share an area, though the chapter adds that possible spread is no guarantee of spread.
  S937
- The chapter suggests Turkish influence for Bulgarian, Macedonian and Albanian evidentiality, and Germanic influence for French.
  S936
- Sources of evidential morphemes in the opened material: tense morphemes, modal verbs, deictic elements, and in Tibetic verbs of sitting, shining/appearing and sounding.
  S937
  S939
- Tibetic evidential systems are reported as early as the 12th century, yet the modern systems do not directly continue the classical one; parallel development can use different lexical verbs for the same category.
  S939

Design consequence: for a fictional contact zone, evidentiality is one of the most plausible grammatical features to borrow along a trade or conquest frontier, and its coding type will betray the donor (a modal-verb evidential points to one ancestor, a tense-fused one to another). Couple with linguistics/language-contact.md for the contact scenario itself.

## 6. Cultural levers: what an evidential grammar does to a society

This section extends, and does not repeat, the inventory-size effects in semantics-pragmatics.md §4 and the sizing table in syntax-typology.md §4.

- **Strategy and lying.** Speakers with identical evidence can choose different markers, and grammatical evidentials can serve as a subtle tool for lying and manipulation; marker choice also tracks consensus, disagreement and politeness.
  S939
- **Genre.** Narrative, dialogue and procedure can trigger different series of markers, and a written literary register can be far poorer in evidentials than speech, as with Literary Tibetan.
  S939
- **States of consciousness.** Dreams, drunkenness, trance, revelation, illusion and remembering can call for distinct evidential series; the authors note this complicates Bible translation into such languages.
  S939
- **Intention.** In Qiang a direct visual evidential with a first-person actor can mark the act as unintentional ("I hit him, by accident") — the grammar of seeing doubles as a grammar of blame.
  S939
- **Attestation coupling.** In register-strata-attestation.md, assertives bind under a truth standard; an evidential language gives the attestation register a ready-made slot: which source tag a sworn statement must carry, and whether hearsay may be sworn at all.
- **Translation coupling.** In translation-protocol-fidelity.md, a note that upgrades its own epistemic type is a fidelity fault. Rendering a reported-evidential clause into English as a bare assertion is the same fault in grammatical form; render the source (lexically, "they say", or in apparatus), never silently drop it.

## 7. NON-CANON worked micro-design: the Tessaly reed-ferriers

NON-CANON. Fictional, invented here as a method demonstration only; no lore decision, no handoff status. The Tessaly are an imagined river-delta people who ferry goods and messages between towns.

Choices, each tied to a typological anchor:

1. **Semantic value:** direct + indirect, with indirect split into inferential and reportative — a "both direct and indirect" system, the rarer of the two attested evidential types, chosen because the culture's economy is message-carrying.
   S936
2. **Coding:** a mixed system — sensory and inferential as verbal suffixes, the reportative as a free clause-final particle. The particle is reportative, consistent with the particle-indirect generalisation.
   S937
3. **Egophoric slot:** a separate personal-knowledge suffix for the speaker's own intentional acts and inner states; for another person's fear or hunger only the inferential is licensed (epistemic-authority rule).
   S939
4. **Kept apart:** epistemic certainty is a separate set of modal adverbs, never fused with the suffixes, so "I saw it, but I may be wrong" is sayable.
   S938
5. **Anticipation:** questions to a ferrier about cargo she loaded herself take the egophoric suffix.
   S939

Invented forms (not a lexicon commitment): verb *rasse-* "arrive"; *-n* sensory, *-ke* inferential, *-o* egophoric; particle *wal* reportative.

- *barca rasse-n* — "the barge arrived" (I saw or heard it)
- *barca rasse-ke* — "the barge arrived" (wet ropes on the post; I infer)
- *barca rasse-n wal* — "the barge arrived, they say" (the reporter claims sensory access; the particle marks it as told)
- *na rasse-o* — "I arrived" (personal knowledge)

Culture consequences the design generates, not stipulated lore:

- A ferry-message is quoted with the original reporter's suffix intact plus *wal*, so a chain of custody is audible in the sentence (layered chain, §4).
- A dispute over a lost cargo turns on suffix choice: claiming *-n* for something only inferred is the recognised offence, and courts ask "which suffix?" before "what happened?".
- River-songs, a literary genre, drop the suffixes entirely, so archived songs are evidentially flat — a built-in source problem for later historians inside the fiction.

## 8. Pitfalls and refusals

- **Evidentials are not lie detectors.** They encode the source a speaker presents, not truth; speakers choose markers strategically and can lie with them. Never write a culture in which evidential grammar makes deception impossible or truth automatic.
  S939
- **WALS counts are sample-bound.** The 181/166/71 and 131/24/65/7/10 figures are counts over one 418-language sample at one data version; they are not world frequencies, population shares or probabilities for a fictional language, and the chapter's own "seem not to have" wording marks gaps in documentation as well as in grammar.
  S936
  S937
- **Do not port a real language's system wholesale.** Tuyuca, Tibetan, Turkish or Quechua markers come with their own tense, person, genre and history; copying a paradigm with renamed morphemes imports a living community's grammar as decoration and loses the internal motivation that makes a fictional system coherent. Borrow parameters (§2-§4), not paradigms.
- **Do not equate evidentials with epistemic modals.** An English-*must*-style form that still reads as probability is not an evidential by the WALS criterion; decide which you are building.
  S936
- **Do not read mirativity into every sensory marker.** Surprise readings may be contextual overtones of an access marker.
  S939
- **No cognitive determinism.** Nothing opened here shows that speakers of evidential languages are more truthful, more sceptical or more observant; Tournadre & LaPolla flag the acquisition question as open.
  S939
- **Refused as a source:** a stub reference page that defines evidentiality as a kind of epistemic modality and carries no references was opened and rejected this round (round-89 lesson Gate); it is not cited here.
