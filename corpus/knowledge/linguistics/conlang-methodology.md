# Conlang Methodology: Culture Brief to Reference Grammar

- topic: the full artlang pipeline — culture brief → phoneme inventory → phonotactics → morphology sketch → syntax skeleton → 300-word core lexicon → pragmatics — with iterative-deepening triggers and the documentation discipline that turns a sketch into an auditable reference grammar.

Attested artlangs are old — Hildegard of Bingen's 12th-c. Lingua Ignota is the earliest well-known one (Higley 2007) — but a *method* is modern. The pipeline below is the consensus craft of Rosenfelder 2010 and Peterson 2015, disciplined by the grammar-sketch checklists field linguists use (Payne 1997; Dixon 2010–2012).

## Pipeline at a glance

| Stage | Input | Output artifact | Sketch size | Gate to pass |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 culture brief | world need (from lore) | brief table: habitat, subsistence, rank, religion, neighbors, literacy, era | 1 page | every brief row has a design obligation (§2) |
| 1 phoneme inventory | brief + typological anchor | inventory table with IPA + rarity check | 10–30 segments | anchor family named; rarity justified |
| 2 phonotactics | inventory | syllable templates, cluster/stress/tone rules | 5–10 rules | 20 names + 10 toponyms build cleanly |
| 3 morphology sketch | brief + phonotactics | typology choice, alignment, categories, derivational kit | 1–2 pages | 10 scaffold sentences parse |
| 4 syntax skeleton | morphology | order, pro-drop, negation, questions, relativization, chaining | 1 page | scaffold set translates (§6) |
| 5 core lexicon | all above | 300 entries, field-weighted, etymologized | 300 words | Swadesh floor + brief fields covered |
| 6 pragmatics | rank/ritual from brief | register, honorific, evidential, taboo systems | 1 page | every rank/rite pair has a speech rule |
| 7 deepening | failures logged upstream | registers, daughter languages, full grammar | open | reopen rules followed (§8) |

## Stage 0 — the culture brief is a spec, not flavor

| Brief row | Design obligation |
|---|---|
| habitat/climate | landscape and weather lexicon density; direction/deixis habits |
| subsistence | craft lexicon; numeracy needs (fisher tallies vs palace taxes) |
| social ranking | honorific morphology and speech levels (§7) |
| religion/myth | taboo lexicon, ritual registers, evidentials: who may attest what |
| neighbors/trade history | loanword strata and sound substitutions (contact = APiCS 2013 data) |
| literacy/writing era | whether texts ever freeze the language (spelling drift stops) |
| era and history | how many sound-change generations to simulate |

Rule (Rosenfelder 2010; Peterson 2015): start from the society, not the phonemes. Okrent 2009 documents how the great failures of the genre — a priori philosophical languages — collapsed by ignoring how real languages live inside real communities.

## Stage 1 — phoneme inventory

- Baselines: consonant inventories cluster in the 19–25 bucket; vowel-quality modal is 5–6 (/a e i o u/); roughly 40% of the WALS sample is tonal, concentrated in East/Southeast Asia, West Africa, and Mesoamerica (WALS 1A/2A/13A; PHOIBLE).
- Rarity tiers (Ladefoged & Maddieson 1996; PHOIBLE): *common core* p t k b d g m n s l j w + 5-vowel set; *mid* v z ʃ tʃ ŋ ʔ, gemination, tone, uvulars, ejectives (ejectives cluster circum-Pacific, Caucasus, Africa); *rare* clicks (southern Africa), pharyngeals (Afroasiatic, Caucasus), retroflexes (South Asia, Australia), bilabial trills.
- Method: (1) name the anchor family first and write it into the grammar header (lane checklist); (2) copy a real inventory from the anchor, then move at most 2–3 segments, each rarity justified; (3) never assemble an inventory from favorite sounds — naturalness lives in the gaps as much as in the segments.
- Ritual-phonology precedent: Lardil's Damin, an Australian initiation register, famously used click consonants in a click-free continent (Hale's Lardil fieldwork; exact citation to-verify) — a rare attested case of a register with its own phonology, prime material for culture–language coupling.

## Stage 2 — phonotactics

- Fix: syllable templates (CV, CVC, CVN, (C)V(C)), cluster rules per position, gemination, stress assignment (weight-sensitive vs fixed), tone rules, morpheme-boundary effects (sandhi, hiatus bans).
- Tradeoff: rich morphology favors simple syllables (Turkish, Japanese); isolating languages often tolerate denser syllables (English, Cantonese). Decide the pair early, since Stage 3 depends on it.
- Generation trick (Rosenfelder 2010; Peterson 2015): script-generate ~500 candidate words, prune by ear, then re-extract the rules from what survived. The rules you cannot write down are exactly what a reader will hear.
- Test: write 20 personal names and 10 place names from phonotactics alone. If you reach outside the inventory, fix the inventory or fix the ambition. Names are the language's first public surface (couples `naming-systems.md`).

## Stage 3 — morphology sketch

| Type | Real anchors | Syllable habit | Craft cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| isolating | Mandarin, Vietnamese | dense syllables, tones common | grammar via order + particles |
| agglutinative | Turkish, Quechua, Swahili | simple CV(C) | long transparent words; regular paradigms |
| fusional | Latin, Russian, Arabic | clusters fine | portmanteau endings; irregularity |
| polysynthetic | Mohawk, Inuktitut | very simple syllables | whole-sentence words; heavy for dialogue scenes |

(Spectrum per Sapir 1921 ch. 6 — a serviceable checklist, not a law.)

| Alignment | Effect | Real anchors |
|---|---|---|
| nominative–accusative | S=A treated alike | Latin, Japanese |
| ergative–absolutive | S=P treated alike | Basque, Dyirbal |
| split-ergative | split by aspect/tense/person | Hindi (aspect) |
| tripartite | S, A, P all distinct | Georgian aorist slice |
| direct | by animacy/salience | English pronouns |
| Philippine voice-focus | verb indexes the chosen topic role | Tagalog |

- Also decide (Nichols 1986; Comrie 1989; Croft 2003): head- vs dependent-marking; inflectional categories (number, gender/noun-class, case, TAM, evidential, politeness); derivational kit (causative, applicative, nominalizer, privative).
- Noun classes range from 2 (French) to 10+ (Bantu). Designing a class for "sacred or mythically animate" things is a legitimate device — flag it as constructed; never claim languages *must* encode a mythic division.
- Irregularity budget: irregularity concentrates in high-frequency words (suppletive *go/went*); give the top ~50 words 10–20 irregulars so texts stop feeling synthetic (Haspelmath & Sims 2010).

## Stage 4 — syntax skeleton

- Word order (WALS 81A, n≈1377): SOV ≈ 41%, SVO ≈ 35%, VSO ≈ 7%; the rest are split or without dominant order. Greenberg-style correlations (adposition side, Gen/N, Rel/N) are usable defaults with documented area exceptions (Dryer 1989) — see calibration.
- Decide: pro-drop; copula strategy (zero copula? existential?); content-question formation; negation (particle / verb / double); relativization strategy (gap, pronoun-retention, internally-headed — Comrie 1989; Shopen 2007); clause chaining or switch-reference if the anchor family has it; available valency alternations (passive, antipassive, applicative).
- Scaffold test (one sentence per row, in the spirit of the Lingua questionnaire — Lehmann 1976; Comrie & Smith 1977): transitive; possession; negation; yes/no question; content question; relative clause; imperative; benefactive; two-clause temporal sequence; evidentially marked statement. Any row you cannot translate marks an incomplete sketch — that is the gate.

## Stage 5 — 300-word core lexicon

- Floor: the Swadesh 100 (Swadesh 1952; the longer list 1955) as basic-vocabulary floor — reweight, don't recite.
- Weight by the brief: a rice-delta culture needs dozens of water/agriculture terms before it needs "aunt"; rank and ritual fields expand; keep abstract philosophical vocabulary thin until deepening.
- Derivation-first vs coin-first: if the language has deep history, derive the lexicon from proto-roots through the numbered sound-change law list, mechanically (Rosenfelder 2010; his zompist Sound Change Applier automates this). If it must ship fast for scenes, coin directly and back-derive later (Peterson's screen workflow). Either way every entry ends with an etymology field.
- Frames, not atoms (Fillmore 1982): give each word its frame — "coast" implies a land/sea contrast; a commercial-event word implies buyer/seller/goods. Wierzbicka 1996 (NSM primes) is the opposite discipline: useful as a completeness check, poor as a lexicon generator.
- Frequency discipline: after writing any connected text, the word-frequency curve should bend Zipfian (Zipf 1949); use the text to rank which 50 words to deepen next. A 300-word language is a documented core, not a usable language — treat 300 as the floor for glossing the scaffold set.

## Stage 6 — pragmatics layer

| System | What it encodes | Real anchors | Coupling |
|---|---|---|---|
| speech levels / honorifics | rank reaches into morphology *and* lexicon, not just pronouns | Javanese ngoko–krama (Geertz 1960); Brown & Levinson 1987 | costume ranks map to speech ranks (`dress-through-eras`) |
| T–V / pronoun avoidance | power vs solidarity; who may be addressed at all | Brown & Gilman 1960 | deities never addressed in 2nd person; name taboo |
| evidentiality | grammar of how you know | Aikhenvald 2004 | testimony rules; myth narration uses one evidential only |
| avoidance registers | whole lexicon swap near taboo kin/persons | Dyirbal mother-in-law register (Dixon 1972) | mourning and liminal rites borrow it (`ritual-liminality`) |
| taboo deformation | euphemism feeds back into sound change | widespread; systematic in honorific systems | god-names truncate; royal body-part terms replaced per reign |
| conversational baseline | what ordinary talk presumes | Grice 1975 maxims | a register is a codified deviation from this baseline |

Color terms appear here only as vocabulary; the Berlin–Kay ladder is a default to draw from, contested as a strict law (see calibration); the cultural readings live in `color-symbolism.md`.

## Iterative deepening

| Symptom | Reopen | Action |
|---|---|---|
| a needed word cannot be built | phonotactics | widen one rule, log it, regenerate affected words |
| dialogue needs a subordinate strategy | syntax | add strategy; re-run the scaffold test |
| a new sound change is added for flavor | lexicon | re-run ALL etymologies through the law list; never hand-mutate |
| new rank/rite enters the brief | pragmatics + morphology | extend honorific morphology; add register lexicon slice |
| two texts disagree on a paradigm | morphology | the grammar doc is arbiter; fix the texts; log the ruling |
| deepening crosses ~500 years | diachrony | apply sound change, analogy, grammaticalization (Campbell 2021; Hock 1991; Rosenfelder 2012) — intelligibility drops fast; never let one static language span eras (couples `era-language-staging.md`, `historical-linguistics.md`) |

Discipline: reopen one stage at a time; every reopening is a LOG.md entry naming the motivating text; re-run that stage's gate. Deepening order: lexicon breadth → inflectional depth → diachrony → registers.

## Documentation discipline

Reference-grammar skeleton (mirrors Payne 1997 and the Lingua questionnaire; Dixon 2010–2012): front matter + typological passport → phonology (inventory with IPA, orthography mapping, phonotactics, prosody) → nominal morphology → verbal morphology → derivational morphology → syntax → pragmatics/discourse → lexicon → texts with interlinears → diachronic appendix → sources.

1. All interlinears follow the Leipzig Glossing Rules (MPI EVA 2008); maintain a local abbreviation table; any gloss not in Leipzig is defined in-file.
2. Typological passport in the header: one line per major feature (order, alignment, head/dependent marking, noun classes, evidentiality, register levels), each with its real-language anchor and its WALS/PHOIBLE link. This is the lane checklist item; it is what makes the language auditable.
3. Lexicon entry schema (fixed fields): headword | IPA | gloss | POS | inflection class | derivation (root + affixes) | register/status (plain/honorific/taboo/archaic/ritual) | etymology (proto-form → laws applied) | cultural note | status (sketch/settled/frozen) | version.
4. Change-law register: numbered, ordered sound laws. Every etymology must be mechanically re-derivable from proto-form through the current law list; adding a law means regenerating affected entries.
5. Version the language (v0.x sketch → v1.0 reference grammar). Frozen sections change only by logged decision; texts built on a superseded version get flagged for re-check (`consistency-verification-checklist.md`).
6. Give the language a working code: ISO 639-2/3 includes some conlangs (epo Esperanto, jbo Lojban, tlh Klingon) and reserves qaa–qtz for local use — use the reserve range; do not request a fictional-language code.
7. Citation norm: every "X is common/rare/universal" claim carries a live data link (WALS chapter, PHOIBLE; APiCS 2013 for contact/creole societies) or the tag `(to-verify)`.

## Calibration: contested and discredited

| Framework | Status | Craft ruling |
|---|---|---|
| strong linguistic determinism ("language forces thought") | discredited (Gentner & Goldin-Meadow 2003); weak relativity active | flavor of habitual attention; never "can't think X" |
| "no word for X ⇒ no concept X" (snow/Hopi genre) | discredited: Malotki 1983 (Hopi time), Martin 1986, Pullum 1991 | banned as worldbuilding logic; languages periphrase freely |
| Greenberg-style word-order universals | contested: sampling and area confounds (Dryer 1989; Evans & Levinson 2009) | weak defaults only; check WALS area-weighted data before asserting |
| Berlin–Kay color ladder as strict law | contested (Saunders & van Brakel 1997) | default ladder; variance lives in `color-symbolism.md` |
| universal sound symbolism (bouba–kiki) | lab-robust (Köhler 1929; Ramachandran & Hubbard 2001), corpus-weak (Blasi et al. 2016) | fine for coinage feel; not proof a language "must" sound soft |
| Pirahã exceptionalism (no recursion/numbers) | contested, unresolved (Everett 2005; Nevins, Pesetsky & Rodrigues 2009) | don't build a culture on it unflagged |
| mass comparison / Proto-World reconstruction | rejected as method (Ringe 1992; Campbell 2021) | allow only as in-world pseudoscience your scholars err with |
| "harsh consonants ⇒ warlike folk" | no scholarly basis; national-character stereotype | refuse; a language's temperament is an art choice, not a fact |
| politeness "universals" | scaffold contested (Eelen 2001) yet productive (Brown & Levinson 1987); Lakoff 1975 dated | use as scaffold; modernize gendered-register claims |

Old-but-serviceable checklists: Sapir 1921's typology spectrum; Hockett 1960's design features (displacement, duality). The Konstanz Universals Archive (Plank ed.) indexes the classic universals with sources. Treat all as heuristics, not laws.

## Handoff to lore (reference, never canon)

Ships to lore-shokunin: entity names with etymologies; register and taboo rules that constrain dialogue; ritual, calendar, and garment terms with derivations; era registers. Stays here: lexicon drafts, unreconciled variants, method notes, contested-source flags. Every handoff names its open questions; canon rulings are lore's (`lore-handoff-contract.md`). A language's facts enter the world bible only through that contract.
