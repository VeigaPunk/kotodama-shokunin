## 1. Why change is the tool

A language with no history reads as fake. History produces the artifacts readers
actually notice: dialects, register gaps, opaque spellings, false cognates,
archaic liturgy, names that don't fit the current phonology. Real families are
derived, not designed: one proto-system plus an ordered list of sound laws
mechanically generates plausible daughters (Campbell 2013, Hock 1991).

Deliverables (hand-off material, not canon): a family tree subgrouped by
shared innovations; a **law list** (numbered, ordered sound changes per
branch); a **strata table** (loan layers and old names keyed to dated events).

## 2. The comparative method (procedure)

William Jones (1786) observed Sanskrit, Greek and Latin "sprung from some
common source"; the method was systematized by Bopp, Rask and Grimm in the
1810s–1820s (Campbell 2013). Procedure:

1. Assemble candidate cognates **by meaning and form**, discarding chance
   lookalikes. Compare *correspondences*, not single words: one loanword can
   lie; a recurring set (Latin p/t/k : English f/θ/h across dozens of words)
   cannot.
2. Tabulate the correspondence sets per position (initial, medial, final,
   before/after each sound class).
3. Reconstruct proto-phonemes. Defaults: a correspondence of distinct sounds
   usually reflects one proto-phoneme; a proto-phoneme may merge in every
   daughter too (reconstruct it anyway if morphology or poetry demands it —
   Hock 1991).
4. Prefer the reconstruction that makes the changes **natural** (assimilation,
   lenition, palatalization — common cross-linguistically) and that respects
   the phonological system of the proto-stage.
5. Reconstruct morphology from paradigmatic alternations; irregular
   alternations in the daughters are fossil evidence of proto-phonemes.
6. Subgroup by **shared innovations**, never by shared retentions or
   lookalikes (Campbell 2013; Ringe, Warnow & Taylor 2002).

Decision table — what two similar words can mean:

| Evidence pattern | Verdict | Craft use |
|---|---|---|
| Regular correspondence across many word sets | cognates — common inheritance | reconstruct proto-form |
| Same form + same meaning, no law behind it | loan or chance | place it in a stratum instead |
| Shared oddity found only in 2 of 3 daughters | shared innovation | defines a subgroup |
| Archaic form surviving only in a peripheral dialect | shared retention | proves nothing about subgrouping |
| Alternation inside one paradigm (sing/sang/sung) | fossil morphology | strongest reconstruction evidence |

## 3. Regular sound change: Grimm and Verner as worked examples

The Neogrammarian hypothesis (Osthoff & Brugmann 1878): sound change is
regular and admits no exceptions — "apparent exceptions" are later laws,
loans, or analogy. Grimm's law (Grimm 1822; anticipated by Rask 1818): in
Proto-Germanic, Proto-Indo-European stops shifted wholesale:

| PIE | → PGmc | Example (Latin/Greek : English) |
|---|---|---|
| *p *t *k *kʷ | *f *þ *h *hʷ | *ped- → foot ~ pēs; *tréyes → three ~ trēs; *ḱm̥tóm → hundred ~ centum; *gʷih₃wós → quick ~ vīvus |
| *b *d *g *gʷ | *p *t *k *kʷ | *dwo- → two ~ duo; *ǵónu → knee ~ genu |
| *bʰ *dʰ *gʰ | *b *d *g | *bʰréh₂tēr → brother ~ frāter; *dʰugh₂tḗr → daughter ~ Greek thugátēr; *gʰóstis → guest ~ hostis |

Verner (1877) resolved the celebrated exceptions: Grimm's voiceless fricatives
(*f *þ *h *hʷ, and *s) became voiced (*β *ð *ɣ, *z) when the immediately
preceding syllable was **unstressed in PIE**. Proto-Germanic then fixed stress
on the initial syllable, burying the conditioning — the alternation became
opaque "grammar" (*grammatischer Wechsel*): English was/were (*was ~ *wēzun →
rhotacism), seethe/sodden, lose/forlorn. The textbook minimal pair:
*ph₂tḗr "father" (medial, unaccented → *fadēr) vs *bʰréh₂tēr "brother"
(accented → *brōþēr) — cf. Vedic pitā́ vs bhrā́tā, which preserve the PIE
accents (Ringe & Taylor 2014).

Lessons to reuse in a fictional family:

- One branch-wide law gives every daughter word a "family resemblance" no
  ad-hoc conlang achieves (kentum/centum vs hundred names the centum–satem
  split).
- Opaque alternations are history: keep *why* they exist in the law list even
  though in-world grammarians no longer know it.
- Genuinely contested: whether Kluge's law (nasal-infix preterites in
  Germanic) is real — Kroonen (2013) revives it, many remain skeptical.
  Don't rely on contested laws as load-bearing; mark them `to-verify` in
  hand-offs.

## 4. Analogy: where irregularities go to die

Sound change creates irregularity; analogy removes it. Four-part proportional
analogy (a : b :: c : x) remakes forms on a productive pattern (Hock 1991;
Campbell 2013): OE holp → helped; cow/kine → cows; Latin honos → honor
(rhotacism spread through the paradigm). Related moves: **leveling** (one
alternation in a paradigm wins), **contamination** (a form reshaped by a
neighbor), **folk etymology** (asparagus → sparrowgrass; bridegroom, where OE
*guma* "man" was opaque), **backformation** (edit < editor, burgle < burglar).

Frequency moderates it: high-frequency forms resist regularization longest
(Bybee 2001) — so keep your irregulars in the most common words (be, go, have).

→ Fiction: the conlang's "mistakes" are its future. Derive a stage, decide
which paradigm gaps children would have leveled, and the next stage writes
itself — with attested real-world precedent for every step.

## 5. The grammaticalization cycle

Grammaticalization (term: Meillet 1912): lexical items bleed into grammar
along recurrent paths; Hopper & Traugott (2003) state the cline
**content word > grammatical word > clitic > affix > zero**. Lehmann (1995)
formalizes the drift as parameter shifts:

| Parameter | Direction along the cline |
|---|---|
| integrity (semantic/phonological substance) | ↓ bleaching, erosion |
| bondedness (dependence on host) | ↑ loose syntax → affix |
| paradigmatic variability | ↓ optional → obligatory |
| paradigmaticity | ↑ free item → member of a closed paradigm |

Recurrent cycles (examples all attested; inventory: Heine & Kuteva 2002):

- **Negation cycle** (Jespersen 1917): plain negator → emphatic reinforcer →
  reinforcer alone → new negator. French *ne … pas* ("not a step"); *rien*
  < *rem* "thing"; colloquial French now drops *ne*.
- **Article cycle** (Greenberg 1978): demonstrative → definite article →
  generic noun-marker → new demonstrative needed (Romance *ille* → il/le/-l).
- **Future cycle**: volition or motion verb > future marker (English *will*
  < "want"; *going to* > gonna); Romance future < infinitive + *habēre*
  (cantāre habēō → chanterai).
- **Indefinite article**: numeral "one" > article (OE ān > a/an).
- **Reflexive**: body-part word > reflexive (Hebrew *ʾetmo-* "his-self"
  < *ʾetsem* "bone"; "body"/"head" paths — Heine & Kuteva 2002).
- **Perfective**: "finish/complete" verb > aspect marker (Mandarin *le*
  < *liǎo* "finish").

Calibration: **unidirectionality is a strong statistical tendency, not a
law.** Degrammaticalization exists but is rare and mostly small-scale (Norde
2009; contrast Haspelmath 1999's defense of irreversibility). Run cycles
forward freely; running one backward needs an explicit in-world mechanism and
a note that it is typologically marginal.

## 6. Dating language stages

Use **relative chronology** as the spine; absolute dates are anchors, not the
structure (Campbell 2013).

- Sound-law stratigraphy: a later change cannot have applied before an earlier
  one; loans that predate a law show the law's **input**, loans that postdate
  it show its output. This brackets everything.
- Loanword strata: each contact event leaves a layer with the phonology of its
  borrowing period (English: Latin *castra* → OE ceaster → -chester/-caster;
  Norse -by/-thorpe; Norman-French table words beef/pork/mutton beside Germanic
  cow/pig/sheep; Norse they/them replacing native hīe).
- Shared innovations date subgroups relative to each other; shared retentions
  date nothing.
- Attestation anchors (real-world calibration points): Hittite tablets from
  the Old Kingdom (~17th c. BCE); Linear B Greek ~1400–1200 BCE; Vedic and
  Gathic Avestan composed roughly in the 2nd millennium BCE but transmitted
  orally for centuries (dating contested); Elder Futhark runes from the early
  2nd c. CE. Mirror this in-world: a "first scripture" date is a stage anchor.
- Internal reconstruction can *predict* the unattested: Saussure (1879)
  derived missing consonants from the PIE vowel system alone; Hittite
  (deciphered 20th c.) turned out to preserve them as ḫ — the laryngeals.

**Glottochronology is discredited.** Swadesh (1955) assumed a constant
core-vocabulary retention rate (~0.81–0.86 per millennium); Bergsland & Vogt
(1962) showed retention varies enormously (Icelandic vs colloquial
Armenian). Bayesian phylogenetics (Gray & Atkinson 2003, Nature 426,
doi:10.1038/nature02029; Bouckaert et al. 2012) remains **contested**: Chang
et al. (2015, Language 91:1) argue ancestry-constrained models favor the
steppe hypothesis. Dispersal dates carry error bars of millennia — in-world
scholars should fight about this, as real ones do.

## 7. Constructing proto-languages and daughter languages

Procedure for a fictional family (the real comparative method run in reverse,
plus typological sanity):

1. Design the proto-language: phonology, core lexicon, a few irregular
   paradigms (put them in high-frequency words, per §4).
2. Per branch, write an **ordered law list** (L1, L2, …). Track
   **feeding/bleeding**: L2 may destroy (bleed) or create (feed) the
   environment for L3. Ordering is where chronology lives.
3. Derive daughter forms mechanically. Then apply analogical cleanup where a
   paradigm would be intolerably opaque.
4. Add strata: substrate loans (pre-law shapes survive as exceptions), elite
   or sacred borrowings (a liturgical language freezes an older stage), trade
   vocabulary. Each stratum carries the sound-shape of its period.
5. Cross-check: no daughter form may violate its branch's law list unless you
   have a named source — loan, analogy, dialect mixture, or a later law.
6. Subgroup the tree by shared innovations you actually wrote (step 2), not by
   visual similarity.

Pitfalls (all are ways conlangs die on inspection):

- Reconstructing from lookalikes instead of correspondence sets.
- **Reversing a merger.** Merged proto-phonemes cannot unmerge in daughters by
  internal means; "recovered" distinctions need morphology or borrowing
  (standard doctrine; Campbell 2013, Hock 1991). If the proto has one /a/,
  no daughter may secretly have two without an explanation.
- Uniform daughters: making every branch equally archaic or equally innovative.
  Real trees have lopsided branches (one daughter 3,000 years more
  conservative than its sisters — e.g., Lithuanian within Balto-Slavic).
- Forgetting contact. No language changes in isolation.
- Abusing the shared-retention fallacy to justify a subgroup in the world
  bible.

## 8. Calibration: discredited and contested material

| Claim/method | Status |
|---|---|
| Glottochronology as an absolute clock (Swadesh) | **Discredited** (Bergsland & Vogt 1962); lexicostatistics survives only qualitatively |
| Mass comparison / multilateral comparison without correspondence sets (Greenberg's method) | Not accepted by most comparativists as evidence for deep families (Campbell 2013) |
| Altaic as a genealogical family | Contested; mainstream treats Turkic/Mongolic/Tungusic/Korean/Japonic contact claims as stronger than descent claims |
| Nostratic, Proto-World | Speculative; not mainstream; do not cite as established |
| Neogrammarian "no exceptions" | **Both-regimes view**: regular for gradual phonetic drift, lexical diffusion (Wang 1969) for structural reassignments; Labov (1994) reconciles |
| Grimm's-law "exceptions" | Solved by Verner's law, loans, and *st*-clusters (*st* → *st*, not *sþ*) — the model case of an exception becoming a law |
| Strong linguistic relativity ("language X proves worldview Y" pop claims) | Not supported in its strong form; out of scope here but frequently smuggled into worldbuilding lore |

## 9. Application to a fictional culture

- **Build once, derive everywhere.** One proto + 3 daughters + 1 standard
  yields: dialect comedy, false friends (English *gift* vs German *Gift*
  "poison" — one root, split meanings), naming strata, opaque liturgy.
- **Names are fossils.** A personal name showing a pre-shift phoneme dates
  its family's settlement (real parallel: place-names in Norse -by vs OE
  -ford/-ham vs -chester). Keep a column in the strata table for name-shapes.
- **Divine names obey the laws.** PIE *dyēus ph₂tēr → Greek Zeus/Diós,
  Vedic Dyáuṣ Pitā́, Latin Iūpiter — one name set, three branch histories
  (Mallory & Adams 2006). Deriving god-names from the same law list as
  common nouns is the single cheapest authenticity win.
- **Taboo leaves traces.** PIE *h₂ŕtḱos "bear" was replaced in Germanic
  ("the brown one" → *bear*) and Slavic ("honey-eater" → Russian medved').
  Loss of a feared name is a myth-coupled sound change; couples to
  `knowledge/myth-symbol/`.
- **Frozen registers are time capsules.** A sacred language preserving older
  morphology (parallel: Church Slavonic, Latin liturgy, Quranic Arabic)
  generates in-world philology, schisms over recitation, plot misreadings.
- **Strata table template** (hand-off format for the world bible):

| Stratum | Anchor event (in-world) | Sound-shape markers | Vocabulary domain |
|---|---|---|---|
| S0 substrate | pre-settlement | violates branch law list | local flora, rivers |
| S1 founding | dynasty founding | pre-L5 shapes | kinship, law, gods |
| S2 high period | empire peak | post-L5, pre-L9 | administration, trade |
| S3 sacred freeze | scripture redaction | Stage-II forms | liturgy, oaths |
| S4 contact | neighbor empire | foreign clusters intact | prestige goods, war |

Coupling note for lore-shokunin: hand off the family tree, law lists with
phase numbers, and the strata table; never record canon from them. In-world
disputes over the proto's age mirror the real Anatolian-vs-steppe fight (§6).
