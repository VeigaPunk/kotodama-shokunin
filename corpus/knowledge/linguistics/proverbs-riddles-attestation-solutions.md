# Proverbs and riddles: attestation, editorial selection, and uncertain solutions

- topic: Deepening existing proverb/riddle knowledge through a dated-source dossier: distinguish an attested saying from its origin, a collection from the repertoire it selects, a transmitted riddle from an editor's reconstruction, and a proposed answer from an established referent.
- couples: linguistics/orality-formulaic-language; linguistics/semantic-fields-lexicon-building; linguistics/historical-linguistics; linguistics/writing-systems; lore handoff for era, community, performance frame, and canon ownership.

## 1. Scope and what this adds

The nearest entry, `orality-formulaic-language.md`, already addresses both genres: section 6 treats proverb use and situation classes; section 7 treats riddles and cultural taxonomy; sections 9 and 11 discuss textualization and canon boundaries. This dossier does not discover an uncovered genre, replace that entry, endorse its every comparative generalization, or add a new oral-tradition theory. Its narrower contribution is an evidence-handling layer for using dated collections without turning editorial decisions into facts about an entire speech community.

The three sources have different jobs. The encyclopedia supplies a particular 1911 description and its objection to a definition that omits popularity. The historical dictionary preface supplies its compiler's account of collection, dating limits, and selection. The riddle edition preface supplies its editor's account of solutions, collation, and marked interventions. All are evidence for those printed positions; none, on its own, establishes present scholarly consensus or an ancient performer's intention. The labels B in the anchors retain the assigned source classification without promoting these dated secondary/editorial accounts into direct field observations.

Design inference: keep three layers visible in every dossier: the wording actually available, the source's interpretation of that wording, and the designer's proposed use. An editor's statement that an answer is persuasive belongs to the second layer, not automatically to the first. A fictional institution inspired by that statement belongs to the third. An unexamined source, missing manuscript image, or unidentified variant remains a stated limit rather than a license to fill the gap.

## 2. Six design decisions

The following table contains design inferences, not sourced historical claims. Each row names a choice, its practical record, and a refusal condition. The dated evidence motivating these choices is isolated in the standalone anchors in the next section.

| Decision | Record to keep | Refusal or stop condition |
|---|---|---|
| Separate compact wording from evidence of uptake | Wording, named community, dated use-contexts, and provisional genre label | Do not call a newly coined maxim a popular proverb solely because it sounds memorable |
| Separate attestation from invention | Witness date, date basis, edition, locator, and a distinct unknown origin field | Do not relabel the earliest located occurrence as the birth of the saying |
| Expose the collection's selection boundary | Included forms, stated exclusions, compiler judgment, and unsearched material | Do not infer community absence from absence in a selective dictionary |
| Keep text and intervention distinguishable | Available text, editor's variant, typography or note marker, and provenance | Do not pass an editorial restoration off as an unaltered manuscript reading |
| Treat answers as attributed candidates | Each proposed referent, proposer or edition, clue fit, counterclue, and unresolved status | Do not turn an index of proposals into a unanimous answer key |
| Keep creative adoption outside the evidence layer | Fictional sense, speaker, era, competing uses, and lore approval state | Do not transfer a dated scholarly method into world canon without owner acceptance |

## 4. Attestation and selection: operating the dossier

Design inference: make a saying record before asserting a pedigree. Record its exact surface form, language or translation status, witness and locator, date and date basis, attributed situation, and what the witness actually establishes. A printed occurrence supplies evidence of that occurrence. It does not, without further evidence, identify first composition, establish uninterrupted oral transmission, or quantify how widely the phrase circulated. Keep an origin field explicitly unknown when only a witness date is available.

Design inference: give every proposed earliest date a search boundary. “Earliest located in the witnesses examined” tells another reader what could overturn it. “The proverb originated in year X” suppresses both the search boundary and the distinction between discovery and invention. A newly found earlier occurrence changes the attestation clock; it need not change a separate fictional origin date, which is not established by these historical captures in the first place.

Design inference: keep a collection-profile note beside extracts. A dictionary that omits material on propriety grounds or because its compiler judges it non-proverbial has a selection function. That function can be studied as editorial practice; it cannot be silently converted into a description of everything speakers said. A failed lookup therefore produces “not found in this collection under this search,” with variants and search terms recorded, rather than “not used by this community.” OCR adds a second possible reason for a missed lookup. Neither source silence nor a spelling mismatch establishes cultural absence.

## 5. Solutions and editorial text: operating the riddle record

Design inference: separate the riddle text, the edition's interventions, and the answer dossier. A useful answer dossier has one candidate per record: proposed referent; attributed proposer or edition; the particular textual clues it explains; remaining counterclues; dependence on a restored reading; and the status proposed, preferred-by-editor, disputed, or unresolved. These are workflow labels for this dossier, not a claim that the historical editor used this exact taxonomy.

Design inference: never infer italics from a plain-text extraction that does not preserve them. The preface's declaration of a marking rule identifies something to inspect in the edition, not permission to guess which words were marked. When the scan or typography has not been checked, write “editorial boundary not inspected.” If a preferred answer depends on one of those words, preserve that dependency instead of presenting the answer as independent evidence for the restoration.

Design inference: keep alternate answers even when one seems much stronger. The competing candidate may reveal a different semantic sense, an emendation dependency, or a clue left unexplained by the preferred reading. Counting answers in an index is not counting scholars who agree. The preface excerpts do not supply a solution for a particular numbered riddle here; no such solution is asserted by this dossier.

## 6. NON-CANON worked example: two senses, separate clocks

The entire settlement, wording, dates, speakers, object, and dispute below are invented design material. Nothing in this example is an attested English proverb, an Exeter Book solution, a proposed reconstruction of Old English, or approved world canon.

Suppose a fictional ferry town has the saying “A closed gate keeps two banks hungry.” Sense A is a practical warning against interrupting trade. Sense B is an ironic reproach used by a gatekeeper who is withholding passage from a rival. Preserve the same surface wording but give each sense its own situation and speaker record; the wording alone cannot decide whether a scene expresses cooperation or coercion.

Use four separate clocks. The witness clock is year 214, the date on an invented toll-book quotation. The reported-memory clock is a speaker in year 230 claiming that a grandparent used the saying; record both the report date and the claimed earlier use without treating the latter as independently observed. The editorial clock is year 266, when an invented school anthology omits coercive uses and prints only Sense A. The design-review clock is the date the author last checked the fictional dossier. None of these clocks establishes an origin year. This division is a design aid, not an extracted historical chronology.

Add an invented riddle: “I hold two shores while never leaving one post.” Candidate A is a ferry rope; candidate B is a chain attached to a river gate. Candidate A fits holding a connection across the water but may strain “one post.” Candidate B fits attachment but requires explaining “two shores.” Record both explanations and both failures. If a fictional editor restores a damaged word as “gate,” record the restoration separately; do not use the resulting answer as proof that the damaged word must have been gate.

The imagined anthology's omission of Sense B creates an editorial clock event, not the disappearance of Sense B from town speech. A scene could stage the resulting conflict: a pupil knows the printed counsel, a ferryman uses the reproach, and a magistrate insists the anthology fixes meaning. Those are creative options for lore review, not universal claims about schooling, dictionaries, oral communities, or riddling competitions.

## 7. Checklist before a usable handoff

1. Identify the nearest existing knowledge and state the narrower addition; do not sell this dossier as first coverage of either genre.
2. Give each historical factual assertion an exact body locator and an attributed speaker or editor; keep design inferences visibly labeled.
3. Separate the witness date from origin, reported-memory date, editorial date, and current access date. Preserve uncertainty in each field.
4. Keep the literal wording and each situated sense distinct. Do not infer community acceptance from brevity, wit, or one printed occurrence.
5. Describe the collection's stated exclusions and the actual search boundary. A missing lookup remains a bounded negative observation.
6. Keep transmitted text, marked editorial intervention, and proposed referent distinct. If typography was lost in OCR, do not invent it.
7. Give each candidate answer its clue fit, counterclues, and textual dependencies. Do not collapse attributed preference into consensus.
8. Label invented material NON-CANON, identify its era and speakers, and carry unresolved fields into the handoff rather than silently completing them.
9. Preserve source refusal reasons if a later access attempt fails; record a change from refused to accessible only when an actual reopened body supports that flip. No such independent access attempt or flip is claimed here.
10. Keep freshness paused under the inherited rule. A dated local capture review is not a freshness waiver, external admission, or validator exception.

## 8. Lore handoff and known limits

Design inference: hand lore-shokunin an option packet, not an inserted canon fact. The packet consists of surface wording; distinct senses and use-scenes; era and named community; witness ledger with date bases; collection-profile and exclusions; riddle candidate ledger; uninspected editorial dependencies; and a short list of decisions requiring ownership. In the worked example, lore must decide whether the ferry town exists, whether either sense is current, which institutions select the anthology, and whether the riddle ever receives an accepted answer. This knowledge dossier owns none of those decisions.

The dated sources justify only the scoped observations in the attested anchors. They do not establish global genre laws, reliable frequencies of proverb use, a full history of borrowing, the correctness of a particular riddle solution, or current scholarly agreement. These limits remain visible so that later inspection can refine a claim without turning a guessed history into an observation.
