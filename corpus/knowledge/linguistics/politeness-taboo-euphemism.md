## 1. The politeness framework stack

- Face (Goffman 1967): the public image a person claims in interaction; all interaction is ritual face-work. Brown & Levinson (1987) formalize: **positive face** = want to be approved of; **negative face** = want to be unimpeded. Any **face-threatening act (FTA)** — request, criticism, refusal — is weighed: Wx = D + P + Rx (social Distance + relative Power + Ranking of the imposition), each scaled culturally. Five superstrategies, in rising cost to the threat: bald on record → positive politeness (approbation, in-group markers) → negative politeness (hedges, deference, minimizing imposition) → off record (hint, irony) → don't do the FTA.
- Leech (1983) maxims (tact, modesty, agreement…) and Grice (1975) cooperation: politeness is the systematic *overriding* of clarity — useful as a drafting checklist, weak as theory (see Calibration).
- Ide (1989): two logics of politeness. **Volitional** (B&L): strategic, chosen, analyzable. **Discernment** (wakimae): grammatically prescribed fit to social variables — you don't "decide" to be polite, you conjugate it. Gu (1990): Chinese politeness runs a self-denigration maxim (debase self, exalt other) — face is relational, not individual.
- Matsumoto (1988) and Mao (1994): the B&L universal-face claim fails on Japanese/Chinese data — there, face is the acknowledgment of one's place in a group, and negative face (freedom from imposition) barely exists. B&L survive as a strategic toolkit and a heuristic formula, not as universal psychology (see Calibration).
- Discursive turn (Watts 2003; Kádár & Haugh 2013): "politeness" is first-order folk judgment — what participants and onlookers call polite — not an analyst's formula; politeness1 vs politeness2. Culpeper (2011): **impoliteness** is its own designed system (mock impoliteness as in-group bonding; strategic offence). Worlds need rude genres, not just polite ones.
- Design decision table — pick a culture's logic mix; it changes what "rudeness" even is:

| Politeness logic | Rudeness looks like | Enforcement | Port to fiction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discernment (wakimae) | a grammatical/category error (wrong honorific conjugation) | shame, instant audibility | politeness is grammar: children and outsiders make "mistakes", not choices |
| Volitional negative-face (B&L) | imposing, coercing directness | offence at the act, litigation, grudge | hedge-rich dialogue; bluntness = power display |
| Relational/self-denigration (Gu; Matsumoto) | failing to locate self vs other correctly | group sanction, loss of standing | apology and invitation scenes are rank ceremonies |
| Discursive (Watts) | whatever the onlooking community judges rude | reputation, moderation, mockery | politeness norms are contested in-world; metadiscourse is content |

## 2. Honorific systems as grammar: comparative data

- Axes (Comrie 1976): speaker→**addressee** (T/V, Korean sentence levels), speaker→**referent** (Japanese -si-, kin-term pronouns), speaker→**bystander** (audience-conditioned forms). Harada (1976): Japanese uchi/soto — you honor *out-group* people and *lower your own in-group*; honoring your own boss to an outsider is the classic blunder. A bystander axis makes eavesdroppers grammatical participants — cheap to build, high scene value.
- Comparative table:

| System | Mechanism | Granularity | Characteristic scene |
|---|---|---|---|
| Javanese | word-level register substitution: ngoko (plain) / madya (mixed) / krama (polite) / krama inggil (high-honorific vocabulary); a sentence is re-graded word-by-word live | fine, continuously negotiated | two speakers probing relative status by offering/withholding krama; wrong level = presumptuous or demeaning (Geertz 1972; Errington 1988) |
| Korean | two independent dials: addressee speech level (sentence endings — seven classical in grammars, four to six alive; hasoseo-che ceremonial → hae-che intimate) and referent honorific -si-; plus self-humbling | two-slot matrix | casual to the listener while honoring the person talked about; a mis-set ending reads as insult, comedy, or sudden intimacy (Lee & Ramsey 2000) |
| Japanese | five families (Bunkachō 2007): sonkeigo (raise other's actions), kenjōgo I/II (lower self/group actions toward other / toward own sphere), teineigo (neutral-polite), bikago (beautification); gated by uchi/soto | per-clause, per-referent | correct honorific, wrong in-group/out-group side (Harada 1976; Shibatani 1990) |
| Thai / Vietnamese | pronouns are kinship terms; I/you shift with age, status, intimacy; address is live negotiation and refusal of a status-raising term is a move (Cooke 1968) | continuous | a character promotes or demotes another mid-relationship by switching kin-term pronouns |
| T/V languages | pronoun pair; power semantic nonreciprocal (superior gives T, receives V), solidarity semantic mutual (Brown & Gilman 1960) | binary | nonreciprocal T as the period insult; mutual-T as egalitarian ideology |

- Honorifics enregister (Agha 2007): a form becomes "noble speech" / "servant speech" through metadiscourse — etiquette manuals, comedy, schooling. Irvine (1992): honorific ideologies naturalize a hierarchy; someone profits from the grammar. Ask who wrote the keigo handbook of your world.
- Construction kit for a conlang, in ascending complexity: (a) referent-raising affix on the verb; (b) humilific for self-directed action; (c) addressee index on sentence enders (Korean model); (d) bystander-conditioned alternation (Japanese model); (e) partial honorific lexicon — 40–150 doublets for the everyday verbs/nouns, not full vocabulary (Javanese model); (f) kin-term pronoun shifting (Thai/Vietnamese model). Combine two to four axes; a full stack like Javanese marks a steeply ranked society.

## 3. Taboo: domains, sanctions, what it does to the lexicon

- Steiner (1956): taboo is not "forbidden" but **charged** — a dangerous power managed by convention. Radcliffe-Brown (1939): what is ritually dangerous is ritually significant — taboo is a map of a culture's value nodes. Douglas (1966): abominations are "matter out of place" — purity rules encode cosmology.
- Frazer's two laws — contagion (contact transfers the charge) and similarity (resemblance transfers it) — are discredited as ethnographic history but survive as a usable **folk-model** of how taboo-speakers reason: name the dead and the dead hear; say the storm's name and it comes; the boat that says "pig" sinks (in-world belief, and in-world it should mostly *work* — see myth-symbol coupling).
- Domain inventory (seed 5–8): sacred words (gods, oath formulas — couples myth-symbol); death and corpses; disease; sex, body, excretion; food (species, preparation, fast days); names — the dead, and rulers (Chinese imperial naming taboo bìhuì 避讳: an emperor's personal-name characters vanish from print and office titles, so a dynasty's documents can be dated by which characters are missing; the mechanism is mainstream; specific renamed-title cases remain thinly documented); kin (mother-in-law avoidance languages, Dixon 1980); occupational (hunters and fishers rename the quarry and ban boat-words; specific regional cases to-verify).
- Sanction gradient — a culture exposes which institution backs each taboo: supernatural dread (self-enforcing) → kin/elder ridicule → community reputation → legal censor (obscenity law, blasphemy law, platform moderation). The blasphemy/indecency split is an era marker: religious censorship of oaths (medieval–early modern) gives way to sexual/scatological censorship (industrial–modern), and in-networked regimes to moderation filters.
- What taboo does to language: the dangerous word is *never deleted*, it is **replaced and preserved as a citation-form** — recited in glossaries, court records, censored editions, and grammar examples. Taboo words are affectively hot even for nonbelievers (Pinker 2007) — which is exactly why censors bother: the taboo outruns the belief.
- Calibrated aside: swearing tracks verbal fluency, not deficit — high taboo-word fluency correlates with high overall fluency (Jay & Jay 2015). The "cursers are inarticulate" folk model is false; port it as an in-world prejudice only.

## 4. Euphemism cycles and the treadmill

- Vocabulary (Allan & Burridge 1991): **euphemism** (shield), **dysphemism** (weapon), **orthophemism** (straight talk). Two motors: **fear-euphemism** (naming attracts the thing: death, disease, demons — circumlocution as prophylaxis) and **propriety-euphemism** (politeness: body, money, sex). Fear-ladders freeze easily (the substitute was never "polite", it was protective — it ossifies into a name); propriety-ladders churn as fashion.
- The **euphemism treadmill** (term coined by Pinker, popularized in Pinker 2007): a euphemism borrows the stigma of its referent; once the stigma bleeds through the word, it is replaced. Lifespan heuristic: one to two generations per rung. The treadmill measures ongoing social evaluation — it is not evidence that words "can't fix" anything (see Calibration).
- Documented ladders (comparative data; reuse the shape):

| Domain | Ladder (oldest → current) | Era readable from position |
|---|---|---|
| Death | die → pass away → pass / be lost; undertaker → funeral director; coffin → casket; "slumber rooms", "memorial parks" (Mitford 1963) | Victorian propriety → post-war industry → present |
| Disability | idiot/imbecile/moron (clinical IQ classes) → mentally retarded → intellectually disabled (Rosa's Law, P.L. 111-256, 2010) | each clinical upgrade became the next playground slur; position dates a text within decades |
| Toilet | privy → water closet/WC → toilet → restroom/bathroom/loo (etymology uncertain — OED records no settled origin) | technology + propriety strata coexist in one house's vocabulary |
| Sex work | brothel → massage parlor → "sex work" (coinage credited to Carol Leigh, 1979, coined as a panel title; Leigh 1997: 229; AP obituary, Nov 2022) | advocacy renaming as identity politics — an era lever |
| State violence | Schutzhaft "protective custody"; Endlösung; "dekulakization"; "collateral damage" (1991); "enhanced interrogation"; "special military operation" (2022); corporate "downsizing/rightsizing" | Orwell (1946): political language makes murder respectable; Sprachregelung = the officially prescribed line (Nazi-era term, now generic German) |

- Dysphemism runs the treadmill both ways: slurs are sometimes reclaimed as identity badges (Allan & Burridge 2006), and institution-words decay into pejoratives ("bureaucrat", "propaganda"). Design both directions.
- A world with algorithmic censors will breed **algospeak** — moderation-evasion coinages ("unalive", "seggs", leetspeak punctuation) that leak into childhood speech within a year (2020s, documented in journalism; a live, in-progress cycle visible in real time — good model for any censored medium).

## 5. Fossil records: how taboo leaves strata

Taboo never erases; it deposits. Four stratum types to seed per deep taboo:

1. **Frozen replacements in the plain lexicon.** PIE *h₂ŕ̥tḱos "bear" survives as Greek arktos, Latin ursus, Welsh arth; Slavic replaced it with medvědь "honey-eater" (Russian medved') and Baltic with lokys "licker"; Finnish runo-circumlocutions (Mesikämmen "honey-paw") for karhu (Mallory & Adams 2006). The hunter's euphemism became the species name — a fossil is a word whose etymology is a circumlocution nobody feels.
2. **Minced oaths.** zounds < God's wounds; gadzooks < God's hooks; blimey < (God) blind me; French sacrébleu/morbleu/parbleu < sacré Dieu / mort Dieu / par Dieu (rhyme replaced the holy word); Japanese chikushō < 畜生 "beast-rebirth" (a Buddhist curse, now mere "damn"). Minced oaths record *which class of word was dangerous, and when the censor moved on* — English swearing shifted from religious to sexual/scatological over the 19th–20th c. (standard observation in the swearing literature — Hughes 1991; Mohr 2013). "Bloody"'s etymology is genuinely obscure; "by Our Lady" is folk etymology (OED; see Calibration).
3. **Register stratigraphy from conquest.** English cattle are Saxon (cow, pig, sheep) but the meat is French (beef, pork, mutton); blunt Germanic vs clinical Latinate doublets (shit/excrement, sweat/perspire) are the Norman stratum heard on every playground (Baugh & Cable 1993). A conquest leaves a euphemism accent: the low words are the conquered's words.
4. **Ritual epithets, divine-name doubles, calendar erasure, legal fossils.** Greek Erinyes addressed as Eumenides "the kindly ones" (Aeschylus, Eumenides); Hades as Plouton "the wealthy one" — underworld gods are inverse-named. Hebrew YHWH (Tetragrammaton) unpronounced: Adonai in reading, HaShem outside prayer, written substitutions like G-d — a liturgical name-register (couples diglossia). German Mittwoch "mid-week" replaced Wodan's day under Christian pressure; Portuguese numbered weekdays (segunda-feira…) from ecclesiastical Latin feria, traditionally attributed to Martin of Braga (conventional attribution, not documentary) — a conversion reads straight off weekday names. Legal strata: Comstock Act (1873); FCC v. Pacifica Foundation, 438 U.S. 726 (1978) — Carlin's "seven words" as case law, not linguistics.

Design rule: for each deep taboo seed (a) one fossilized euphemism nobody analyzes, (b) one live ladder, (c) one minced oath, (d) one legal/liturgical fossil — four strata of visibly different ages. In-world philologists can then date documents by which rung they use.

## 6. Construction procedure

1. Choose the politeness logic mix (discernment / volitional / relational / discursive) and the sanction backing per taboo domain (priest, elder, censor, algorithm).
2. Choose which Comrie axes are grammaticalized; build the morphology/lexicon from the §2 kit. Binary T/V cultures are not less polite than word-grading cultures — they distribute the work differently.
3. Taboo inventory: 5–8 domains, each with its owner and sanction. Cross-tabulate taboo × institution; empty cells are missing institutions.
4. Run 2–4 euphemism ladders at deliberately different ages. Oldest rungs fossilize into plain words or minced oaths; newest are generation-marking slang. Record the ladder in the lexicon with era tags.
5. Write the metadiscourse: etiquette manuals, schoolroom rules, satire, censor lists, proverbs about other people's dirty mouths — this is what enregisters the system (Agha 2007). Decide who profits from the honorific ideology (Irvine 1992).
6. Stress-test scenes: a deathbed (fear-euphemism), a coronation oath (sacred words), a market haggling, a broadcast news bulletin (state doublespeak), a teenager quoting an elder (three ladders in one line). Score requests with Wx = D + P + R and check bystander cases — most drafts forget eavesdroppers.
7. Hand off to lore-shokunin: register grids, taboo lexicon, and ladder tables as era-anchored options; flag every to-verify before it touches the bible.

Worked micro-example: a sea-folk culture. The storm-god's true name is unspoken; the fossil "Wet Grandfather" is now just his name, and "tide's teeth" is a minced oath nobody parses (strata 1+2). Ship-crews ban land-animal words aboard — a live occupational taboo with substitutes like "hay-biter" for the mule. The court grades verbs Javanese-style ("salt-speech" vs "harbor-speech"), while the republic's ministry of the deep renames conscription "maritime contribution" — which town satirists already use sarcastically. Philologists date the republic's archives by which death-ladder rung its funeral rolls use.

## 7. Calibration: contested, discredited, commonly botched

- **Brown & Levinson as universals: contested.** Matsumoto (1988) and Mao (1994) show the negative-face construct fails for Japanese/Chinese; Ide (1989) shows discernment cultures don't run the strategy loop. Usable residue: the Wx formula and strategy ladder as a *scene-scoring heuristic*, not as in-world psychology or narratorial law.
- **Leech's maxims, CCSARP categories**: formalist and Anglophone-derived; drafting aids only. Blum-Kulka et al. (1989) is useful as a *coding scheme* for requests/apologies, not as a claim that all cultures request the same way.
- **Frazer's Golden Bough**: comparative ethnography discredited; the contagion/similarity laws survive as folk-model and as in-world belief. Do not build "real" magic-system mechanics on them without flagging.
- **Freud's Totem and Taboo** (1913, primal horde, patricide origin of taboo): discredited by anthropology and historical linguistics; keep only as a theory an in-world scholar might hold (and be wrong).
- **"Euphemism = lying / decadence / newspeak"**: reductive. Euphemism does real coordination work — respect, protection, solidarity; dysphemism is the moral failing in many contexts (Allan & Burridge 1991). Orwell and Lutz diagnose *political* euphemism, not euphemism as such.
- **"The treadmill proves language reform is futile"**: overclaim. Rosa's Law changes institutional reference and record-keeping; the treadmill shows evaluation is *continuous*, not that renaming never matters (Pinker 2007 is careful here; political readings that go further are the readers').
- **Folk etymologies swarm around taboo words**: "bloody < by Our Lady" (origin actually obscure), acronym etymologies for "fuck" and "posh" — all false. Taboo attracts false etymology; your in-world philologists should generate wrong derivations on purpose.
- **"Swearing = low vocabulary/self-control"**: false; taboo fluency tracks overall fluency (Jay & Jay 2015).
- **Carlin's "seven words" as a linguistic category**: it is comedy plus case law (Pacifica), not a list anyone's grammar recognizes.
- **Taboo-grammar-shapes-thought (strong Whorfian) claims**: weak evidence; run them as in-world ideology (couples myth-symbol), not narratorial fact.
