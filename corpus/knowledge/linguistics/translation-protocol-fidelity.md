## 1. What this entry licenses — and the three objects it keeps apart

- A **reference frame and a criteria set**, not a gate: this entry says what a gloss and a translation note are, what they can assert and license, and which ten checks a fidelity audit reads. It does not run the audit, register a command, or produce verdicts (§14).
- Three objects get merged constantly, and the merge is exactly how fidelity defects hide:

| object | what it is | where it lives | who owns its format |
|---|---|---|---|
| the **lexicographic gloss** | a language-internal description: one sense of a lexeme, interlinear or dictionary-style | the lexicon/grammar records | `corpus/knowledge/linguistics/conlang-methodology.md` :120,:122; `corpus/knowledge/synthesis/glossary-registry-format.md` :13 |
| the **apparatus translation note** | a metalinguistic act *about a rendering*: why this term was kept, translated, or narrowed, and what the choice loses | prefaces, footnotes, glossaries-as-apparatus, tooltips | `corpus/knowledge/synthesis/cultural-translation-protocol.md` :116-138 |
| the **equivalence claim** | an assertion that two expressions across varieties "mean the same" | anywhere a gloss quietly becomes a translation | nobody by default — this is where fidelity fails |

- The lexicographic gloss describes the **source** language; the apparatus note describes the **rendering decision**; the equivalence claim is a **thesis about two languages** that always needs evidence. A note that starts as (2) and ends as (3) — "X means *robe*" where the protocol licensed "we render X as *robe* because the office-collar distinction would otherwise be lost" — has laundered a decision into a fact. The four-way separation (`:16`) and the epistemic tags O/I/U/D (`corpus/knowledge/verification/verification-method-overview.md` :27-31) are the machinery that keeps them apart: a rendering choice is D (design), a reader's inference is I, the rendered text is the artifact, and only world-side claims are O — and U marks what the note honestly does not know.

## 2. What a gloss is: the metalinguistic layer

- A translation note is a **speech act about a speech act**: it has a speaker (an edition, a fictional translator, the designer), an utterance (the rendering), and a comment on the utterance. The pragmatic machinery applies unchanged: the note has felicity conditions of its own — it must be anchored to the occurrence it glosses (`:134`), spoken by an identifiable voice (`:118`), and it must do one dominant job (`:133`).
- **Which maxim the note sacrifices is itself evidence.** A Manner-flouting note (circumlocution for the term), a Quantity-flouting note (understatement of the loss), a Relation-flouting note (ritual circumlocution quoted without explanation) — the maxim a glossing culture breaks by default is a design lever (KTD-TF-S-0005 :86), and a fidelity reading records it, because a note that flouts Quantity about its own uncertainty is the laundering class of §4.
- **Deixis travels with the rendered voice.** A chronicle or letter keeps or shifts its deictic origo in translation (`:115`, `:119`); a note that quotes an in-world document is glossing a voice, not the designer's, and criterion C5 (attribution) reads the label on that voice.
- **A paired-lexicon culture makes glossing an in-world institution.** Where speech comes in register-paired doublets, "translation between in-laws" is a live scene, not a figure of speech (KTD-TF-S-0005 :106) — a packet whose apparatus never mentions how register-paired terms were rendered has an under-specified rendering convention (criterion C2), because the protocol requires exactly that declaration (`:36`).

## 3. The anatomy of a translation note

The protocol fixes the skeleton; this section fixes the audit fields derived from it. A note is auditable when six things are legible:

| field | the question | grounded in |
|---|---|---|
| provenance type | designer provenance, audience translation note, or fictional editorial apparatus — different labels, never blended | `:118` |
| speaker/edition | whose voice speaks the note | `:118`, `:132` |
| anchor | which occurrence it glosses; a fresh note only for a changed sense/era/problem | `:134` |
| rendering decision | retain, translate, transliterate, narrow — and why | `:36`, `:132` |
| loss/uncertainty | what the rendering gives up; the specific relationship left unrendered | `:132`, `:138` |
| evidence status | source or internal pointer; unresolved readings marked, never hidden | `:132`, `:136-137` |

- **The six fields are the completeness check (criterion C2).** The protocol's ledger minimum (`:111`) carries the same fields per term; a note missing the declared fields is a record-keeping defect regardless of its prose quality — the register-law lesson generalized: a field declared in the schema and left empty means the instrument that reads the block cannot see the omission (`corpus/knowledge/linguistics/register-strata-attestation.md` :218 records the exact shape).
- **Location is part of anatomy.** Required comprehension never lives behind an optional detour; trade-offs, competing readings, and apparatus-depth have assigned locations (`:122-130`). A note in the wrong location fails criterion C10 (skip-test) even if its content is perfect.

## 4. What a gloss asserts vs what it licenses

A gloss licenses a reader to infer exactly as much as its declared type carries. The classes below are the fidelity-relevant readings; each names the laundering it prevents.

| the note says | the reader may infer | the laundering when the line is crossed |
|---|---|---|
| "we render X as *robe*" | a rendering decision (D) | none — this is a note doing its job |
| "X means *robe*" | an equivalence claim (a thesis about two languages) | a decision laundered into a fact; equivalence always needs evidence |
| "X is related to Y" (etymology) | an O-claim about the built language's history | assertion without a locator; the protocol requires uncertain readings be marked (`:96`, `:137`) |
| "X is untranslatable" | nothing operational | banned in favor of naming which relationship, speech act, register, or material distinction remains unrendered (`:138`) |
| "the [fictional editor] calls it X" | a character's claim | apparatus read as narrator fact when the label is stripped (`:118`, `:129` in the overview's transmission sense) |
| "sources say X" | a mediated report | mediation laundered into self-description (`:206`); identify the mediation and its agenda |

- **Gloss bias is the corpus's own caution, generalized here.** "A shared gloss is not proof of identical meanings" and "read examples rather than treating English headwords as the senses themselves" (`corpus/knowledge/synthesis/symbol-lexicon-colexification.md` :49,:105) are stated for symbol comparison; criterion C3 applies them to every cultural gloss: **the gloss is evidence of the renderer's analysis, never of equivalence.**
- **The assert/license split has an evidential analogue.** Marking a reported source as witnessed is "grammaticalized lying" in the evidential literature (KTD-TF-S-0005 :82); a note that upgrades its own epistemic type — I into O, D into attestation — commits the same offence in prose. The overview's invariant names it (`:206`): no observation→inference laundering; no design→attestation laundering.
- **Absence of notes is not a defect by itself.** The tiers are disclosure levels, not a ladder every term must climb; background texture may stay T0-T1 without commentary (`:55`). Criterion C1 fires only where the packet's own protocol (or its consequences) requires a note and none exists — "does this term's tier hide a distinction the next necessary inference needs?" (`:56`).

## 5. Thick translation and the paratext carriers

- **Thick translation is the reference frame for C2**: an academic rendering that locates the text in its cultural and linguistic context through annotations and accompanying glosses (Appiah, at the corpus's registered status). Read through the protocol's calibration: it licenses *making context available*, it does not demand a note on every noun (`:27`); the fidelity question is whether the context a packet's own protocol declared is actually carried, not whether more apparatus would be nicer.
- **Paratext is where the context rides.** The carrier table a packet declares — and the audit walks — maps apparatus types to the protocol's note-location rules:

| carrier | typical load | fidelity reading |
|---|---|---|
| preface / front matter | rendering conventions, translator status, audience | the translation contract fields (`:34-40`) should be stated here once |
| glossary (as apparatus) | retrieval of established terms | supports memory, must not be the only introduction (`:122-130` row 2) |
| footnote / endnote | trade-offs, puns, register contrasts, competing readings | one dominant job (`:133`); anchored (`:134`) |
| interlinear / literal rendering | form-display for named text-types | format owned by `corpus/knowledge/linguistics/conlang-methodology.md` :120; the *claim* the literal carries is C3's |
| tooltip / hover (screen) | optional apparatus unless the interface requires it | must not be the only path to a consequential choice (`:142-146` delivery adjustments) |
| fictional editorial preface | a character's claims | attribution labels are load-bearing (C5); a fallible editor is a device, not provenance (`:118`, `:129`) |

- **Carrier ≠ evidence.** Genette's framework describes framing devices, not reader behavior (`:26`); a packet may place apparatus anywhere provided the causal path survives the removal test (`:190`).

## 6. Domestication/foreignization: a declared design axis, never a fidelity binary

- The pair (Venuti, at registered status) asks which institutions become deceptively familiar through target-language equivalents, and whose speech is marked as strange. The protocol runs it as one of the three named frameworks with its calibration attached (`:25`, `:27`): influential and contested, not a scoring system, and "more invented words" is not more respect.
- **Fidelity consequence:** the audit does not grade *where* on the axis a packet sits; it checks that the position is **declared** (rendering convention, `:36`) and **stable** (one audience-facing handle per referent until a scene-level reason, `:40`). An undeclared mixed strategy — foreignizing the conquered polity's kin terms while domesticating its offices — is not a defect in the mixing; it is a defect in the missing declaration (criterion C2), because the mixing is exactly where a reader's model of the culture gets built.
- **Typography is not the axis.** Consistent romanization with pronunciation help is the craft floor; decorative apostrophes and selective italics as foreignness-marking are a revision flag when they confuse referents (`:98`). The script half of romanization couples to `corpus/knowledge/linguistics/writing-systems.md`; this entry reads only the declared-convention side.

## 7. The interpreter/mediator: register, office, and power

- **The interpreter is a register office before a plot convenience.** When the H variety is dead or foreign, H becomes a professional secret and "interpreters, notaries, priests become a power class" (KTD-TF-S-0006 :74, on the diglossic pattern; Waquet 2003 on Latin, registered `:32`). A packet with a sacred or chancery H owes its apparatus an answer to: who can gloss H to L audiences, and with what declared loyalty?
- **The mediator's rendering is evidence with a viewpoint.** The corpus's civilization rows already treat it that way: assign languages per position, then test who needs an interpreter (`corpus/knowledge/civilizations/mamluk.md` :75); an interpreter's rendering is one audience's evidence layer, never the thing itself (`corpus/knowledge/civilizations/parthia.md` :116). Criterion C5 inherits this: a note in a mediator's voice records the mediation (whose agenda, which channel) — the protocol's caution is explicit (`:206`).
- **Legal and liturgical language resist paraphrase by design** (Tiersma, registered `:35`; reading at `:83`). A paraphrase-gloss of a term-of-art is a special licensing case: the note must say what the paraphrase gives up (the performative precision, the un-paraphrasable formula), or it silently licenses the reader to treat the paraphrase as equivalent — criterion C3 again, at its sharpest.
- **The mediation layer is where fiction's best material lives**: a gloss by a hostile mediator, a collector's filter, a translator with a doctrine — all legitimate, all requiring the label that keeps them out of narrator fact (`:118`, `:206`, `:209`).

## 8. The ten fidelity criteria — the decision surface a fidelity check reads

Each criterion names its evidence source in the packet's own declarations. The audit is **internal consistency**: a packet is read against the protocol *it* declares (the overview's internal-coherence rule, `:18`), never against real-world translation theory.

| # | criterion | the check | grounded in |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | note-presence where required | a term whose tier/contract requires context has apparatus, or the absence is declared | `:55-56`, `:122-130` |
| C2 | context-field completeness | notes and the ledger carry the declared fields (§3's six; `:111`'s minimum; `:34-40`'s contract) | `:111`, `:132` |
| C3 | assertion licensing | no gloss asserts equivalence, etymology, or meaning beyond its declared type; gloss bias recorded | §4; `corpus/knowledge/synthesis/symbol-lexicon-colexification.md` :49,:105 |
| C4 | translation/original separation | rendered wording never quoted as original wording; the rebus works in the declared stage, "not merely in the author's translation" | `:16`; `corpus/knowledge/verification/verification-method-overview.md` :111,:150 |
| C5 | apparatus attribution | every note carries its provenance label; fictional apparatus never reads as narrator or designer fact | `:118`, `:129` |
| C6 | mediation recording | mediated sources identified with channel and agenda | `:206` |
| C7 | era-of-rendering | the rendering's date is not the event's date; an editor's translation is not a contemporary witness | `:112`; `corpus/knowledge/verification/verification-method-overview.md` :129 |
| C8 | register equivalence | register-paired terms state how the pair was rendered; paraphrase-of-terms-of-art declares its loss | KTD-TF-S-0005 :106; `:83` (Tiersma reading) |
| C9 | name-first conformance | names anchor reference before etymology; glosses do not force history where reference-first was declared | `:83`, `:88` |
| C10 | skip-test survival | with apparatus removed, the causal summary survives on the default path | `:190`, `:122-130` |

- **Verdict mapping (borrowed, not redefined):** PASS — the criterion's predicate satisfied at declared scope with the required records present; FAIL — an observed contradiction (a laundering class observed); UNKNOWN — a required record missing or unparseable (a note with no anchor, an undeclared convention); N/A — the criterion demonstrably outside the packet's declared scope, exclusion recorded (`corpus/knowledge/verification/verification-method-overview.md` :93-97). Fail-closed holds: missing evidence never becomes the opposite finding (`:100`), and unresolved rows travel as (to-verify), never as silent passes (`:102`, `:137`).
- **Agreement is not fidelity.** If two readers agree on what a note says, that is E-shape agreement (`corpus/knowledge/verification/two-reader-interop-agreement.md` :29-35) — consistency, not correctness (`:23`), and explicitly not to be reported as verification (`:79`). A fidelity check and an interop check fail for different reasons; running one never discharges the other.

## 10. Worked micro-example (NON-CANON) — the criteria read over the protocol's own scratch demonstration

The protocol's §11 scratch demonstration (`:164-179`) is disposable teaching scaffolding, explicitly not a culture proposal (`:166`). Reading it through the criteria exercises the decision surface without touching any world:

- **The scene's three beats** (stable name + office; the garment made physically legible, then consequential; attributed conflicting readings, `:174-176`) establish reference-first — C9 conformant by construction (the protocol is its own best case).
- **The template note** — "Translator [edition]: retain [garment-term] because 'robe' hides the office-bearing collar distinction; the historical origin remains [evidence status]" (`:177`) — passes C2 (speaker/edition, term, rendering decision, loss, evidence status all present), C3 (it licenses "we retain X because…", asserts nothing about what X *means*), C5 (labeled as a translator's note, not narrator fact).
- **The narrative boundary is built in**: "If collar construction itself becomes decisive, put the necessary distinction in the narrative before the decision; this note is not a substitute" (`:178`) — that is C10 stated by the protocol itself: the note is optional apparatus; the causal path must not depend on it.
- **Planted defect 1 (row 3):** rewrite the note as "the [garment-term] means *robe*" — the rendering decision has become an equivalence claim with no evidence field; observed reading: FAIL on C3.
- **Planted defect 2 (row 7):** move the explanation into an in-world clerk's mouth and strip the attribution on reuse — the clerk's dispute becomes narrator fact; observed reading: FAIL on C5, and C6 if the clerk's institutional agenda goes unrecorded.
- **Planted defect 3 (row 2):** keep the note but drop its anchor and evidence-status field — the loss claim ("hides the collar distinction") now floats without its occurrence or its status; observed reading: UNKNOWN on C2 (missing records, fail-closed).
- **Receipt note (honest limit):** the demonstration is single-scene and template-shaped; it exercises C2/C3/C5/C9/C10 on one note and never exercises C6/C7/C8 — those criteria wait for a packet that actually declares mediated sources, era-layered renderings, or register-paired terminology.

## 11. Build order — how the criteria are applied (manual, no gate)

1. **Freeze the packet and its declared protocol version** (digest). The criteria are read against the packet's own declarations; a packet that declares no rendering convention makes C8 UNKNOWN for every register-paired term, honestly.
2. **Inventory the apparatus**: every note, glossary, preface, interlinear, tooltip, and fictional-editorial piece, each with its anchor and provenance label (§3's fields).
3. **Classify before judging**: each item's provenance type and epistemic content (O/I/U/D), per `:118` and the overview's tags.
4. **Run the ten criteria** per item; record per-criterion verdicts with locators, per the claim-card discipline (`corpus/knowledge/verification/verification-method-overview.md` :49-51).
5. **Negative controls — plant, and require the named verdict**: reword a note into an equivalence claim (row 3 → FAIL); strip a fictional-editor label (row 7 → FAIL); unanchor a note (row 2 → UNKNOWN). A criteria run whose controls do not fail is not a run.
6. **Legitimate exceptions**: a declared T0/T1 term with no note must pass C1 (`:55`); a deliberately puzzle-like apparatus with a designed reading path is legal when the packet says so (`:120`); a fictional editor's prejudice is legal *as labeled apparatus* (`:129`).
7. **Write the receipt**: per-note rows, verdicts, every UNKNOWN named, controls recorded — the overview's sign-off rule applies (`:163`): a prose walkthrough is not a reviewer sign-off.
8. **Route unresolved rows** through the (to-verify) triage; findings travel to the handoff; nothing becomes a world fact.

## 13. Calibration — what the frameworks are, and what a fidelity PASS is not

- **Thick translation (Appiah)**: a contextual, teaching-oriented argument; licenses making context available, never demands universal annotation (`:27`). Used here only as the frame for C2's completeness logic. **Not consulted this round**; page-level verification is the protocol's own record (`:4-5`).
- **Paratext theory (Genette)**: descriptive; says nothing about whether readers consult attachments (`:26`). The carrier table organizes where apparatus lives; it never predicts readers.
- **Domestication/foreignization (Venuti)**: influential, contested, ethical-historical; neither a binary nor a scoring system (`:25`). A declared axis only (DR-TF-06).
- **A fidelity PASS certifies the packet's bookkeeping, not truth**: internal coherence is not reference adequacy (`corpus/knowledge/verification/verification-method-overview.md` :18), and no PASS is ever a canon decision — lore-shokunin owns adoption (`:192`, `:209`).
- **No reader-cognition law is claimed anywhere**: the tiers, budgets, and the note skeleton are revisable craft heuristics (`:18`, `:58`), and this entry adds no empirical claim to them.
- **The interpreter section describes offices, not any real profession**: the civilization rows are reference exemplars (KTD-TF-S-0015); no real community's translating practice is described or adjudicated.
