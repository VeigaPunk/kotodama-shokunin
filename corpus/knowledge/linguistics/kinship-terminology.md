## 1. The six types and their exact merging fingerprints

- The typology is Murdock (1949), consolidating Morgan (1871). Each type is defined by which genealogical positions share a term. Read the fingerprint off the lexicon; infer nothing yet from it.
- Generational convention: G0 = ego's siblings; G+1 = parents; G−1 = children. "Skewing" = assigning a relative to a generation other than the genealogical one.

| Type | G+1 (parents/aunts/uncles) | Cousins | Sibling cell marks | Examples (standard) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eskimo (lineal; increasingly "Inuit" or "lineal" in modern literature) | F≠FB, M≠MZ, but one uncle word merges FB+MB (+ their affines), one aunt word merges FZ+MZ | one generic cousin word, undifferentiated by sex/side/age | sex only | English, Inuit-Yupik |
| Hawaiian (generational) | all of G+1 merged by sex only (M=MZ=FZ; F=FB=MB) | = siblings | sex + relative age + speaker's sex (Hawaiian: kaikuaʻana elder / kaikaina younger same-sex; kaikuahine man's sister; kaikuʻāne woman's brother — Pukui & Elbert 1986) | Hawaiian, Samoan, Tikopia (Firth 1936) |
| Iroquois (bifurcate-merging) | F=FB (same-sex merge), M=MZ; but MB≠F and FZ≠M (side split) | parallel cousins = siblings; cross-cousins distinct | sex (+age in many systems) | Seneca/Cayuga (matrilineal), classical Greek (textbook bifurcate-merging case) |
| Omaha | Iroquois base, patrilineage elaborated; matrilateral side skewed up a generation: **MBD → "mother" class** (Fox 1967; Lounsbury 1964) | skewed cross-cousin unmarriageable by terminology | sex | Omaha, Ponca, Osage; some West African patrilineal groups (Atlas-coded) |
| Crow | Iroquois base, matrilineage elaborated; patrilateral side skewed up: **FZS → "father" class** (mirror of Omaha) | the mirror-image cross-cousin unmarriageable | sex | Crow, Hidatsa; Trobriand (Murdock 1949); Chuuk (Goodenough 1951; Crow skewing confirmed, Hage & Marck 2002) |
| Sudanese (descriptive, bifurcate-collateral) | every position distinct: Latin patruus (FB) / avunculus (MB) / amitā (FZ) / mātertera (MZ); Arabic ʿamm / khāl / ʿamma / khāla | one word per cousin position | sex + side + affinity | Latin, Arabic, Sinitic (Feng 1937), biblical Hebrew |

- Frequency (counted 2026-10-17 from D-PLACE's digitization of the Ethnographic Atlas, EA027 Gray-corrected codings, 955 societies with data): Hawaiian ~35% > Iroquois ~27% > Eskimo ~12% > descriptive ~8.5% > Omaha ~8% > Crow ~6% > Sudanese ~1.6% — Sudanese is the rarest of the six named types, but the most frequent is Hawaiian, not Iroquois or Eskimo; the 186-society SCCS ranks the same (Hawaiian 32%, Iroquois 26%, Sudanese 1/172). Quote frequencies only from a sample you name.
- "Atom of kinship" (Lévi-Strauss 1949/1969): the minimal meaningful unit is not the nuclear family but four roles — father, mother, child, mother's brother. Any culture's lexicon confirms it: MB is always at least terminologically special. Cheap, universal worldbuilding anchor.

## 2. What each type encodes (and how much to trust it)

- Murdock's (1949) **terminological correlation**: terminology type co-occurs statistically with descent/residence/marriage. The correlations are moderate tendencies, not laws (see §3). Treat each row below as a prior, not a rule.

| Type | Typical (not necessary) package | Plot affordances |
|---|---|---|
| Eskimo | bilateral kindred, no unilineal groups; neolocal residence; autonomous monogamous nuclear family; foraging/market economy (Murdock 1949) | family isolates; thin in-law politics; inheritance by will or partible division; lineage-war stories unavailable |
| Hawaiian | ambilineal/cognatic kindreds, weak exogamy; **rank without terminological complexity**: hierarchy rides on sibling seniority and chiefly endogamy instead (Hawaiian piʻo sibling marriage as rank-making — Valeri 1985) | succession wars among kin all called "brother"; "everyone is a relative" mobilization politics; elders addressed by seniority terms |
| Iroquois | unilineal descent groups (either sex), lineage exogamy, often matrilocal; the Dravidian variant additionally encodes marriage in the terms (§5) | cross-cousin marriage alliance politics; parallel-cousin incest drama (a sibling word covers them) |
| Omaha | strong patrilineages, patrilineal exogamy, property in the male line; the skew absorbs the mother's lineage into parent-generation terms | MBD marriage is terminologically incestuous — expect it prohibited; lineage land politics; court genealogists |
| Crow | strong matrilineages; **avunculate**: mother's brother as guardian/funder, father as affectionate outsider (Radcliffe-Brown 1924) | MB-nephew patronage; husband isolated in wife's house; FZ as the wife-giver figure |
| Sudanese | state-level law, precise property/inheritance, often elite endogamy (Arab FBD marriage — Barth 1954); Sinitic tang/biao split encodes lineage exogamy in the lexicon itself (§5) | inheritance litigation; kin-calculation as a profession; address etiquette by exact degree |

- Hawaiian calibration case: stratified chiefdoms (Hawaii itself) run generational terminology — terminological simplicity does NOT signal egalitarianism. Never read "simple kin words = simple society."

## 3. The terminological correlation: one weighted vote, never a verdict

- **Anti-determinist tradition** (still correct): Kroeber (1909) and Lowie (1917) — kin terms are linguistic/psychological categories (age, sex, speaker-reference, formality), not transcriptions of social rules. People systematically apply terms to relatives the rules do not cover.
- Murdock (1949) tested the correlation statistically and got moderate co-occurrence, not determinism. Eggan (1950): terminology changes **more slowly than practice** — correlate via process and strata, not point-for-point.
- **Terminological lag** is itself evidence: a document using skewing terms for a society now described as bilateral marks a transition era. Industrial Japan/Korea keep stem-family-flavored address while terminology simplifies — practice moves, words trail (Shibatani 1990; Lee & Ramsey 2000).
- Goody (1983): the European shift toward Eskimo-type terms tracks the **Church's prohibited-degrees marriage law** breaking up agnatic marriage strategies — law, not subsistence, moved the lexicon. Contact-driven era markers beat ecology sometimes.
- Procedure: weight terminology as one vote among (a) residence, (b) inheritance, (c) marriage rule, (d) economy. A mismatch among them is a *feature*: assign it an in-world history (conquest, conversion, class divergence between elite and folk registers).

## 4. Generating systems instead of copying one (Lounsbury's rules)

- Lounsbury (1964) derives Crow/Omaha from an Iroquois base with formal rules; Goodenough (1956) and Lounsbury (1956) supply the componential method — every kin term is a bundle of features: ±generation, ±lineal, ±sex, ±cross, ±relative-age, ±speaker-sex, ±affinal, ±skew. Build your system as a feature matrix and it will be internally consistent for free.
- Rule kit (apply to an Iroquois base unless stated):
  1. **Bifurcate-merging**: same-sex parent's siblings merge with the parent (F=FB, M=MZ); opposite-sex keep distinct terms (MB, FZ).
  2. **Sibling-merging**: parallel cousins (FB sons, MZ daughters) receive sibling terms.
  3. **Cross-cousin distinctness**: MBD/FZD/MBD's brother etc. get their own sexed terms.
  4. **Omaha skewing**: relatives linked through a male on the matrilateral side are raised a generation (MBD → mother class; mother's brother's son's daughter likewise).
  5. **Crow skewing**: mirror — relatives linked through a female on the patrilateral side raised a generation (FZS → father class).
  6. **Affine-merging (Dravidian move)**: cross-cousins share terms with spouses/in-laws (encodes marriageability — §5).
  7. **Descriptive expansion (Sudanese move)**: cancel all merging; one term per genealogical position.
- Worked chain: base Iroquois → apply rule 5 → Crow-type matrilineal league → add uchi/soto-style doublets and an address layer (§7) → done: a consistent, non-copied system with a documented logic your in-world grammarians could also have derived.
- Derivation table for speedruns: want patriliny + land lineages → Omaha skew; matriliny + avunculate → Crow skew; maritime chiefdom + rank by seniority → Hawaiian + seniority terms; empire with law codes → Sudanese; diaspora traders / nuclear households → Eskimo.

## 5. Affinal logic: the marriage rule is usually IN the lexicon

- Two classical schools: **descent** theory (lineages reproduce themselves; Radcliffe-Brown & Forde 1950) vs **alliance** theory (lineages reproduce *through* exchanging brides; Lévi-Strauss 1949). Both are half-right; modern work uses both locally and models the household instead where descent fails (Carsten & Hugh-Jones 1995). For worldbuilding, alliance theory is the more generative fiction engine because it makes marriages into politics.
- **Dravidian encoding** (Dumont 1953; Trautmann 1981): in South Indian systems the cross-cousin is terminologically the spouse (MBD = wife-type term) — the marriage prescription is readable from vocabulary alone. Two lineages exchange daughters every generation; wife-giver and wife-taker statuses alternate.
- **Marriageability by lexicon, Chinese edition**: tang (堂, "same hall") cousins share the surname/lineage — unmarriageable; biao (表, "external") cousins are outside the lineage — marriageable (Feng 1937; Freedman 1958). The rule lives in the morphology of the kin words.
- **Arab FBD endogamy**: bint ʿamm (father's brother's daughter) marriage as the prestige default — close-kin terminology coexists with in-marriage, the opposite of lineage exogamy (Barth 1954). Two empires' worth of contrast from one cousin slot.
- **Levirate** (widow marries deceased husband's brother; Deuteronomy 25:5–10, Genesis 38) and **sororate** (widower marries wife's sister): the institution presupposes affinal terms that mark those substitutions — build the vocabulary first and the custom can appear in-story without exposition.
- **Joking vs avoidance relations** (Radcliffe-Brown 1940): affines get institutionalized permitted disrespect (joking) or mandatory distance (avoidance) — both are address phenomena, so they couple to politeness registers and give dialogue texture (mother-in-law avoidance registers across Aboriginal Australia, etc.).
- Wife-giver/wife-taker asymmetry: systems that remember who gives and who takes mark it asymmetrically in terms and deference; the debt outlives the wedding. Strong material for feuds and hostage-marriages.

## 6. Inverse method: the diagnostic checklist (lexicon → social evidence)

- Use forward (§1–4) to build; use this table backward — to audit your conlang for accidental claims, or as an in-world detective/philologist method: what does an old document's kin vocabulary reveal?

| If the lexicon has... | Infer | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| F/FB and M/MZ merged (Hawaiian G+1) | generational system; look for ambilineal kindreds and seniority rank | moderate |
| F=FB, M=MZ, but MB, FZ distinct (Iroquois) | unilineal groups likely; check for cross-cousin marriage | moderate |
| Cross-cousin shares the spouse term | prescribed/preferred cross-cousin marriage (Dravidian logic) | high |
| MBD in a mother/aunt class (Omaha skew) | dominant patrilineages; MBD marriage blocked | high (for lineage strength), low (for exact marriage rule) |
| FZS in a father class (Crow skew) | matrilineages; avunculate expectations | high |
| Every collateral distinct (Sudanese) | dense property law, codification, often endogamous elites | moderate |
| tang/biao-style lineage vs out-cousin split | surname-line exogamy with marriageable out-cousins | high |
| Elder/younger sibling words (kaikuaʻana; Korean hyŏng/nuna; Turkish abla) | seniority governance: succession, address etiquette by rank | high |
| Speaker-sex-marked sibling terms | gendered etiquette; speech norms differ by narrator sex | high |
| Nursery doublets (mama/papa vs formal parents) | intimacy domain exists — and **no historical information** (Jakobson 1960) | universal, evidence-free |
| Honorific kin doublets (JP otōsan address vs chichi humble-ref for own father) | uchi/soto politeness; kinship woven into deference grammar | high |
| Teknonymy/kunya (address as "X's mother") | parenthood as status transition; personal-name taboos | high |
| Avoidance/joking term pairs for specific in-laws | institutionalized affinal politics | high |
| Fossil skewing terms alongside bilateral practice | transition era; dateable strata (Eggan 1950) | moderate — assign the history |

## 7. Kin terms leaking into address: the borrowed-vocabulary layer

- **Reference ≠ address**: every system splits "my father" (reference) from "Dad!" (address), and the split is where class, intimacy, and era show (Brown & Ford 1961: English address ladder — kin title > title+surname > first name, ordered). Ervin-Tripp (1972) models address as ordered alternatives with occupancy rules; kin titles for non-kin occupy the solidarity/intimate slots.
- **Kin-term pronouns**: Thai/Burmese/Vietnamese pronoun systems are kinship terms; every conversation re-negotiates relative age and status (Cooke 1968 — politeness file §2 has the register detail). Cheapest deep-culture import for dialogue-driven worlds.
- **Fictive kin institutionalized**: compadrazgo/co-parenthood — godparent terms create binding obligations between families, not just sentiment (Mintz & Wolf 1950). Build a ritual that manufactures relatives and you get alliance tech plus vocabulary.
- **Teknonymy**: parents are addressed by the child's name ("X's mother") — Javanese stages (Geertz 1961); Arabic kunya (Abū/Umm + child's name) as honorific alongside the ism. A naming system can be a kinship clock: your status literally changes when you have a child.
- **Role-kin address**: Japanese senpai/sensei function as kin-class address terms in a vertical society (Nakane 1970); workplaces and guilds inherit family grammar. Guilds that call masters "father" are doing real kinship with borrowed words.
- **Coercive and post-coercive address**: auntie/uncle imposed across power lines (plantation-era address) persists as politeness default toward elders — Singapore English auntie/uncle is codified in the literature (Platt & Weber 1980); US South: whites' "aunt"/"uncle" were nonreciprocal address terms marking slave-owner status difference, while the same titles became fictive-kin respect terms inside Black communities (Gutman 1976); Anglo-Caribbean creoles extend auntie/tantie/uncle to non-kin elders (Mühleisen 2005). Address forced by hierarchy fossilizes into solidarity.
- Address system design: decide (a) which kin terms escape to non-kin address and in which direction (upward honorific, downward endearment), (b) what happens on refusal of an offered term (an auditable social event), (c) which terms a stranger may use at first contact without a broker.

## 8. Borrowing strata in kinship vocabulary (era markers)

1. **Ideology-driven register borrowing (Sinosphere)**: Confucian family law spread Sinitic kinship vocabulary into Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese — the borrowed stratum lives in writing, law, genealogy, and formal reference (JP fubo "parents", ryōshin; KR bu-mo; VN phu mu), while native terms keep the mouth and the home (chichi/haha; appa/eomma; bố/mẹ). Contact with a state scripture leaves a two-register kinship: spoken intimacy vs written law (Shibatani 1990; Lee & Ramsey 2000). Read the strata and you read the era of conversion.
2. **Nursery universals**: mama/papa-type syllables come from babbling phonology, are near-universal, and diffuse areally — they are NOT cognates and carry no genealogy (Jakobson 1960). Calibration trap: never use mama/papa as evidence of contact or descent between your languages; conversely, do not avoid them — they are realistic everywhere.
3. **Law-driven restructuring**: Church prohibited-degrees rules remade European marriage and thinned the terminological system (Goody 1983). A conversion or codification event can rewrite the kin lexicon within a few generations of documents — dateable by term choice.
4. **Coercive address spread**: kin titles imposed across master/servant or colonizer/colonized lines become in-group politeness after emancipation (§7). The insult of one era is the respect of the next.
5. **Intermarriage-trade seeding**: sustained neighbor contact loans kin vocabulary wholesale — Saisiyat took ~68 kin terms from Hakka (Kaybaybaw 2018, cited in Zeitoun & Goudin 2024); Mrkovići kin terminology shows Albanian calques and Turkish loan vocatives (Morozova 2019). The narrower "brides carry terms with dowries" vector is reported but not independently confirmed (to-verify) — pin a case citation per world before asserting it.

## 9. Construction procedure

1. Choose economy → residence → descent → marriage rule (or choose them directly). Deriving from economy is fine but flag it: the chain is Murdock's (1949) correlation, not causation.
2. Pick the terminological base type from §1; apply Lounsbury rules (§4) for internal consistency; record the feature matrix, not just the word list.
3. Build the term table: rows = genealogical positions; columns = reference form / address form / honorific-or-humble variant / possessive construction. Fill every cell; empty cells are decisions you have not made.
4. Add the address layer (§7): escape terms, refusal penalties, stranger protocols, teknonymy triggers.
5. Add register strata with era tags (§8): native intimacy stratum, borrowed law stratum, nursery stratum, fossil skewing terms for the previous social order.
6. Write the metadiscourse: proverbs about mothers-in-law, the inheritance manual's term definitions, the court genealogist's title, an etiquette book's address ladder — this is what makes the system feel discovered rather than declared.
7. Stress-test scenes: an inheritance dispute between a man and his "brother" (parallel cousin); a marriage negotiation where the lexicon shows who is wife-giver; a child addressing a stranger; a genealogy recited at court; an outsider misusing one term with visible consequences.
8. Hand off to lore-shokunin: term tables, rules, and strata as reference options; every to-verify flagged; canon decisions remain theirs per lane rules.

## 10. Worked micro-example

A matrilineal river-estuary trading league, built entirely from §4–§8: Crow skewing (FZS called "father"), avunculate economics (mother's brother funds voyages; the actual husband is a polite outsider in the household — Radcliffe-Brown 1924), marriage with FZD blocked by the terminology itself, so alliances channel to MBD with wife-giver deference terms asymmetrical to wife-taker ones. Address: every elder on the wharf is "mother's-brother" regardless of kinship; teknonymy starts the day you have a child, and guilds extend it to ship's-company membership ("Bittern's father" = captain). Registers: nursery doublets at home; the imperial lingua franca's kin words survive only in the trade cant and charter documents. Two centuries ago the dynasty went patrilineal for tax reasons — the skewing terms fossilized in the charters while practice moved on, so in-world philologists date documents by which cousin words they use (Eggan 1950 lag as a plot device).

## 11. Calibration: contested, discredited, commonly botched

- **Morgan's evolutionism (1871/1877)**: the promiscuity → group marriage → matriarchy → patriarchy → monogamy sequence, and Bachofen's (1861) matriarchy, are discredited (Boasian critique; Lowie 1917). Usable only as a theory an in-world 19th-century-style savant holds — and is wrong about.
- **"Classificatory terms prove group marriage/primitive communal marriage"** (Morgan, McLennan 1865): discredited. Terms are categories of age/sex/formality, not behavior records (Kroeber 1909; Lowie 1917).
- **Terminology ↔ organization point-for-point**: false; Murdock's own correlations are moderate. Counterexamples are routine (Hawaiian-type under stratification; Iroquois-type among bilateral groups). Weighted evidence only.
- **Alliance theory as universal engine**: Lévi-Strauss's elementary structures and the exchange-of-women model are contested — universality of prescriptive marriage challenged, the model feminist-critiqued (Rubin 1975); the Crow/Omaha-from-unilateral-marriage derivations failed test (Homans & Schneider 1955 vs Needham 1962). Residue: alliance *vocabulary* is still where your culture's marriage politics will be visible.
- **"Kinship" as a natural universal domain**: Schneider (1984) — the analytical category itself is a Western folk model; the field's "kinship crisis." This lane's residue is robust anyway: terminologies are linguistic data that exist in texts; projecting them onto "society" is the step that needs flagging.
- **Descent vs alliance as exhaustive**: superseded — house-society and practice models handle the residual cases (Carsten & Hugh-Jones 1995).
- **"Eskimo" as a label**: deprecated ethnonym for the peoples; modern literature says "lineal" or "Inuit type." Use the neutral label in your notes; let in-world scholars use whatever their era would.
- **Collected genealogies as objective data**: the genealogical method (Rivers 1910, "The genealogical method of anthropological inquiry", Sociological Review 3) collects genealogies, but genealogies are curated narratives serving present interests (Bohannan 1952, Tiv genealogies as charters) — in-world archives lie about descent on purpose.
- **Lag direction**: terminological lag usually means words trail practice, but loans can *precede* the institutions they name (borrowed law vocabulary imported before reform). Either direction is evidence of *some* change, not of which.
