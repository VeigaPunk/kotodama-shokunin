## Scope and calibration
- “Polynesian” names a related linguistic-cultural field, not one language, religion, wardrobe, or uninterrupted navigation school.
- Keep Tonga, Sāmoa, Hawaiʻi, Aotearoa Māori, the Marquesas, and Polynesian Outliers separately identified; borrow a mechanism, not an undifferentiated aesthetic.
- **Established:** comparative lexical relationships, barkcloth/plaited-mat distinctions, and documented contemporary navigation teaching.
- **Regional:** particular ritual entitlements, titles, genealogies, star names, tattoo practices, and cloth classifications.
- **Reconstructed:** protoforms and ancestral institutions inferred by comparison; these are not transcripts of ancient speech or eyewitness chronicles.
- **Contested:** precise settlement sequences, retrospective chronological readings of genealogies, and historical identification of some named stars.
- **Craft inference:** every proposed design procedure below; no proposal asserts what every historical Polynesian society did.
- **Important correction:** Hawaiian **kāpeʻa** denotes the **Southern Cross**, literally “the cross,” in Pukui–Elbert; it is not an established umbrella term for star paths.
- Exact antiquity of that designation, versus names recorded in other Hawaiian sources, is **(to-verify)**; a dictionary gloss cannot establish prehistoric use.

## Language family, sound history, and writing
- Polynesian languages belong within Oceanic, within Austronesian; Proto-Polynesian is reconstructed, not an attested written standard [Greenhill & Clark 2011; Kirch 2017].
- Geographic “Western/Eastern Polynesia” and linguistic subgrouping are different classifications; do not turn an island map into a complete family tree.
- Cognates demonstrate regular histories: Māori **waka**, Tongan **vaka**, Sāmoan **vaʻa**, Hawaiian **waʻa** all belong to the canoe set.
- Preserve local orthographies: Māori macrons mark vowel length; Hawaiian kahakō and ʻokina distinguish sounds; Sāmoan and Tongan glottal-stop conventions are not decorative apostrophes.
- POLLEX often doubles vowels for length, uses **g** for /ŋ/ and **q** for /ʔ/; its transcriptions need conversion, not unexamined copying into a modern name list.
- Modern Latin-based writing developed through differently dated encounters, missionary printing, Indigenous authorship, education, and government; literacy did not simply replace oral authority.
- Specify a fictional orthography's sponsor, teaching network, and treatment of inherited names; do not mistake spelling variation in older records for random pronunciation.
- A Rapa Nui-derived writing system requires a separate rongorongo evidence review **(to-verify)**; do not claim that its inscriptions supply deciphered navigation manuals.

## Proto-Polynesian-stage navigation lexicon
- An asterisk marks reconstruction, not a word directly recorded from a historical speaker; the following are inherited lexical sets relevant to the Proto-Polynesian stage.
- POLLEX's **AN/OC/PN** labels concern comparative reconstruction depth; an AN-labelled set is not evidence that the word originated specifically in Proto-Polynesian.
- Retain a narrow gloss first; add local metaphor, specialist meaning, and later semantic change only with separate evidence.

| POLLEX headword / working form | Checked gloss and selected reflexes | Coupling leverage |
|---|---|---|
| WAKA.A / *waka | Canoe; Māori waka, Tongan vaka, Sāmoan vaʻa, Hawaiian waʻa | Craft, crew, and identity can share vocabulary; do not assume all social senses are equally ancient. |
| FOLAU / *folau | Expedition by sea, sea voyage, travel by sea; Tongan and Sāmoan folau | Distinguish journey, fleet, vessel, and departure senses rather than collapsing them. |
| FETUQU / *fetuʔu | Star; Tongan fetuʻu, Māori whetū | An inherited generic word does not establish an inherited catalogue of identical constellations. |
| MATAGI / *mataŋi | Wind, breeze; Māori matangi, Hawaiian makani | Build environmental and sailing registers without assigning every wind a universal divine identity. |
| LAA.1 / *lā | Sail; Tongan lā, Māori rā | Separate sail technology from garment technology even when both use plant fibres. |
| MOANA / *moana | Sea beyond the reef, ocean; locally also other meanings | Reef/open-sea distinctions can organize work, danger, travel, and naming. |
| KAU-WAKA / *kau-waka | Canoe crew; Tongan kauvaka, Sāmoan ʻauvaʻa | Productive collective morphology can link vessel, labour, and affiliation. |

- Sources are the individual POLLEX entries linked below, not a free reconstruction from similar-looking modern words.
- **Counterexample to overextension:** POLLEX gives **KAU-MOANA**, sailor/mariner, a Central-Eastern label; do not silently promote that compound to Proto-Polynesian.
- **Counterexample to false certainty:** POLLEX marks the Māori **wharau** comparison under FOLAU as problematic; omit it from a clean sound-correspondence demonstration.
- Design a specialist lexicon around bearing, swell, current, reef passage, landfall, provision, and crew duty; these are proposed semantic slots, not unattested protoforms.
- Test landward/seaward, upwind/downwind, and rising/setting reference frames locally; do not substitute north/south/east/west everywhere.

## Star paths, kāpeʻa, and the attribution boundary
- Distinguish **horizon compass**, **ordered steering-star sequence**, **large sky mnemonic**, and **latitude/landfall clue**; these are related operations, not synonyms.
- Thompson describes a mental compass of star houses, continuous estimates of speed/direction/time, and switching among stars, sun, swells, and birds [PVS, “On Wayfinding”].
- His Hawaiian compass commonly presents 32 houses; the PVS comparison explicitly says Mau's named directions are not evenly spaced numerical divisions.
- Mau Piailug was a navigator from **Satawal in the Caroline Islands, Micronesia**, not a surviving Proto-Polynesian informant; credit the intercultural teaching relationship.
- The modern Hawaiian framework is skilled Indigenous renewal, not a defective imitation; its documented modern organization is also not automatic proof of prehistoric Hawaiian form.

| System or cue | Documented example | Methodological limit |
|---|---|---|
| Horizon houses | Thompson's Hawaiian star compass | A mental bearing framework, not a chart showing the canoe's absolute position. |
| Steering-star succession | Following successive rising/setting stars near a desired bearing | A course requires observation and correction; a star does not remain stationary overhead. |
| Large star lines | Thompson's four named sky divisions | Explicitly attributed modern mnemonic organization, not a universal ancient taxonomy. |
| Kāpeʻa / Southern Cross | Cross observation contributes to southern direction and latitude assessment | Distinguish the named asterism from the whole navigation method; orientation and observing time matter. |
| Swell and sun | Learned wave signatures, low-sun bearings | Wind waves, swell, current, and boat motion must not be treated as one cue. |
| Birds and target expansion | Land-associated birds; aiming for an island group before a particular island | Species, breeding behaviour, time of day, and geography can defeat a simplistic rule. |

- PVS names the four star lines **Ke Kā o Makaliʻi**, **Iwikuamoʻo**, **Manaiakalani**, and **Ka Lupe o Kawelo**.
- Their remembered figures include bailer, backbone, fishhook/fishline, and kite; PVS explicitly associates Manaiakalani with Māui's fishhook.
- That coupling of image, narrative name, sky configuration, and retrieval task is usable; copying the names does not reproduce a navigator's trained perception.
- Do not confuse these sky lines with identical bearing arcs or assume each is a sequence followed on one voyage.
- Modern house widths and degree equivalents are teaching representations; do not retroject degree arithmetic or uniform spacing into every school [PVS, “Star Compasses”].
- Different latitudes alter rising/setting bearings and visible sky; seasonal availability and changing course require more than a memorized pretty constellation.
- Lewis (1994) compares multiple Pacific systems; **etak** and other Micronesian models need their own attribution, not relabelling as generic Polynesian practice.
- **Fictional application:** define a sky, latitude range, route, seasonal window, and cue-failure scenario before inventing a navigator's chant.
- Give each mnemonic an operational job: recognizing a partially clouded sky, recalling a bearing, sequencing watches, or approaching a target region.

## Mythic and symbolic core: locally grounded, not a merged pantheon
| Regional reference | Attested conceptual material | Coupling to preserve / shortcut to reject |
|---|---|---|
| Hawaiian traditions | Kāne, Kū, Lono, Kanaloa; Hina, Pele, Māui and many local genealogical narratives [Beckwith 1940] | Divine relations, place, season, and particular narrative contexts; reject four gods as a complete universal chart. |
| Māori traditions | Ranginui, Papatūānuku, and genealogically related beings; whakapapa can connect human and other-than-human worlds [Roberts et al. 2004] | Relationships organize knowledge and obligation; reject “nature spirits” as merely interchangeable elemental mascots. |
| Hawaiian navigation renewal | Māui's fishhook named in the Manaiakalani teaching line [PVS] | A narrative figure can aid sky memory without proving that every fishing or sailing rite invoked Māui identically. |
| Tonga, Sāmoa, Marquesas, Outliers | Cognate names and shared motifs invite comparison, not automatic identity | Local pantheon detail and initiation narratives are thin in this entry **(to-verify)**; do not fill the gap with Hawaiian plots. |

- **Genealogy is not only human pedigree:** a genealogical ordering can join origins, places, plants, beings, rights, and duties; preserve the speaker's category rather than dismissing it as bad biology.
- **Mana/tapu are not game statistics:** authority, efficacy, restriction, sacredness, and social relations require local explanation; “magic power” and “taboo law” are insufficient universal translations.
- **Symbol ≠ dictionary code:** a repeated fishhook, bird, cross, or geometric mark need not carry one stable meaning across navigation, tattoo, cloth, and church contexts.
- **Analytical test:** record sign → medium → maker/user → setting → sanctioned interpretation → alternate reading; a shared shape is not proof of a shared cult.
- Beckwith is a historically important comparative compilation, not an unmediated statement by all Hawaiians; distinguish her synthesis from named narrators and source texts.

## Era model: stage changes without a single Polynesian clock
| Stage / approximate anchor | Coupled changes worth modelling | Evidence guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Lapita-associated settlement, second–first millennia BCE | Maritime movement, pottery, transported plants, networks | An archaeological assemblage is not a ready-made later Polynesian religion or costume set. |
| Ancestral Western Polynesian developments, first millennium BCE onward | Language differentiation, exchange, changing settlement and authority | Proto-language reconstruction and archaeological phases are not interchangeable dates. |
| Eastern Polynesian dispersals, late first–early second millennia CE | Longer routes, local ecological adaptation, divergent vocabularies | Exact island chronologies are debated; avoid one fleet-date explaining every population. |
| Aotearoa settlement, late thirteenth century CE | Temperate material constraints and different seasonal skies | Do not transplant a tropical tapa wardrobe unchanged into a colder environment. |
| Island-specific encounters and nineteenth-century transformations | New cloth, tools, Christianity, print, commerce, changing prohibitions | “Contact” was neither simultaneous nor the end of Indigenous agency. |
| Twentieth–twenty-first-century renewal and diaspora | Voyaging schools, tattoo revival/continuity, museums, synthetic tools | A renewed or globally circulating practice is not thereby inauthentic. |

- Anchors follow Kirch (2017), especially Polynesian origins/dispersals and political transformations; they are staging guides, not fine-grained dated chronicles.
- Do not turn “chiefdom → state” into an inevitable ladder; Tonga and Hawaiʻi are specific comparative cases, not every island's future.
- Exact dating of an individual costume, tattoo entitlement, or named navigation school needs local evidence **(to-verify)**, not interpolation from this table.

## Tapa economy and costume/material signature
- **Tapa/barkcloth** is made from inner bark processed into cloth; it is not woven yarn fabric. Paper mulberry is important, but breadfruit, banyan, and Hawaiian māmaki require regional attention.
- Operational chain: suitable plants → tended stems → bark extraction → region-specific soaking/fermentation and beating → joining/felting or pasting → decoration → storage/use/circulation.
- Do not require fermentation, one plant species, one joining method, or one colourant everywhere [Lennard & Mills 2020, material and technical chapters].
- In documented Tongan manufacture, pairs of women work at a **papa kokaʻanga**; **kupesi** rubbing patterns help compose and decorate a large sheet.
- Plain beaten strips **fetaʻaki** and finished/decorated **ngatu** distinguish production stages; do not erase makers' work by naming every stage “tapa.”

| Regional anchor | Material distinction | Economic / costume coupling |
|---|---|---|
| Tonga | Ngatu barkcloth; plaited mats are a separate technology | Large collaborative manufacture and ceremonial circulation can make labour, sponsorship, and rank visible. |
| Sāmoa | Siapo barkcloth; ʻie toga fine mats are not barkcloth | Tattoo-related exchanges may include fine mats; do not substitute “tapa payment” for every valuable [Mallon 2016]. |
| Hawaiʻi | Kapa, with distinctive techniques and locally available bast/dyes | Model named varieties, skill, texture, and use, rather than a generic brown tropical wrap. |
| Aotearoa Māori | Aute/barkcloth has a history, but does not describe the whole wardrobe | Harakeke-based textile traditions and temperate clothing needs require their own dated treatment. |

- **Economy framework:** track plant access, labour time, cooperative organization, finishing skill, custody, ceremonial gifting, reuse, and later cash markets separately.
- Cloth can furnish, wrap, clothe, commemorate, and enter ritual transactions; its value need not be reducible to either everyday utility or a fixed monetary rate.
- Avoid “women make decoration, men make politics”: the social life of cloth makes production and exchange politically consequential; local gender rules still need evidence.
- A prestigious sheet's size, finish, history, presenter, recipient, and occasion can matter without a universal motif-to-rank cipher.
- Costume choices should specify garment form, fastening, exposure, movement, occasion, and weather; barkcloth, plaited pandanus, feathers, and worked harakeke (New Zealand flax, not linen flax) are not one interchangeable material.
- **Fictional application:** an interrupted bark supply can change ceremonial obligations, imported-cloth prestige, artisan vocabulary, and marriage exchange together; these are design hypotheses, not historical claims.

## Chiefly rank tattoos: embodied relations, not heraldic decoding
- Separate **ascribed rank**, **title/office**, **achievement**, **service**, **ritual completion**, and **authorization to wear a design**; none implies all the others.
- Chiefs may be visibly marked, but the proposition “Polynesian tattoo = chiefly insignia” is false as a general comparative rule.

| Practice / reference | Useful distinction | Calibration |
|---|---|---|
| Sāmoan tatau: peʻa and malu | Historically gendered forms, skilled practitioners, relationships and obligations | Do not equate a peʻa with automatic possession of a matai title [Mallon & Galliot 2018]. |
| Māori tā moko | Identity, affiliation, status, embodiment, and contemporary cultural assertion | Neither facial coverage nor a copied motif is a safe public decoder of genealogy [Te Awekotuku et al. 2007]. |
| Hawaiian and Marquesan chiefly marking | Examine who could commission, receive, interpret, or restrict particular work | Exact period-specific entitlements and motif meanings are thin here **(to-verify)**; do not invent a universal chief's badge. |
| Contemporary diaspora practice | Ceremonial exchanges, commercial transactions, new tools and health requirements may coexist | Record change without making “unchanged tools” the authenticity test [Mallon 2016]. |

- **Fictional design record:** institution → eligibility → sponsor → practitioner training → body placement → occasion → duties → prohibited claims → consequences of misuse.
- Connect costly reception to household support and exchange networks; distinguish the wearer's endurance from the practitioner's expertise and the community's recognition.
- Do not repurpose real sacred/lineage-specific designs as a decorative asset library; invent a new visual grammar and new fictional authorization rules instead.
- Cross-medium coupling can be institutional rather than pictorial: cloth sponsors an event, speech recognizes a relationship, and bodily marking acknowledges duties without repeating one logo.

## Oral genealogy: whakapapa-style precision without false chronometry
- **Whakapapa** is Māori terminology, not a pan-Polynesian name for every genealogical practice; treat “whakapapa-style” as a comparative prompt, not a licence to rename others' institutions.
- Roberts et al. (2004) supplies a relational knowledge framework; Mahuika (2019) centres Indigenous oral history/tradition and the politics of who gets to define reliable knowledge.
- **Precision has several axes:** exact relationship, correct branch, place connection, name identification, performance context, and authority of transmission; calendar dating is a separate demand.
- A recitation may select an appropriate line without claiming to enumerate every descendant; a shorter performance is not automatically a corrupted longer one.
- Repeated names, alternative names, marriage links, adoption, and more than one relevant line demand discrimination, not a single chain of undifferentiated ancestors.
- Genealogies can ground social relationships and claims while also being debated; “politically important” does not mean either infallible or fabricated.
- Do not multiply a generation count by a fixed 25/30 years to manufacture settlement dates; telescoping, branch selection, and uncertain identifications need independent chronological checks.
- Exact local teaching, restriction, correction, and witnessing procedures require a named community/source **(to-verify)**; this entry does not claim one universal memory institution.

| Proposed fictional record field | Why it helps preserve precision | Historical claim? |
|---|---|---|
| Person identifier; spoken name; aliases; dialect/era | Separates homonyms and changed spellings | No: a design tool. |
| Relationship edge; relevant line; seniority context | Distinguishes descent from marriage, adoption, or affiliation | No: a design tool, not a claim about one Māori chart convention. |
| Place, voyage, garment, or other associated object | Tests material and narrative connections without equating them with proof | No: an integration check. |
| Reciter/custodian; audience; occasion; access status | Preserves provenance and obligations around transmission | No: a proposed accountability field. |
| Variant, source, disagreement, adjudication status | Retains contested branches instead of silently canonizing one | No: lore-shokunin decides fictional canon later. |

- Oral formula and repetition can support memory, but never claim a universal error rate or that meter alone guarantees historical truth.
- Living genealogies can be private or restricted; public accessibility does not erase custodial expectations. Use invented people and relationships for demonstrations.

## Naming practices and the coupling workflow
- Separate personal name, chiefly title, kin address, vessel name, place-name, star name, and recited epithet; the same string need not have the same referent or owner.
- Sāmoan matai titles should not be converted into hereditary European surnames; local title succession and naming rules need dedicated evidence **(to-verify)**.
- A Māori or Hawaiian name's dictionary segmentation is not a complete etymology; names can preserve older forms, events, wordplay, or explanations associated with particular custodians.
- A star's named story, a canoe's commemorative name, and a cloth's occasion can form a coherent semantic network without all being names for the same god.
- **Design order:** ecology/route → inherited words and sound changes → specialist register → mnemonic narrative → production/exchange → rank/authorization → naming/genealogy.
- **Stress test:** remove stars under cloud, disrupt the bark supply, or contest a title; identify which words, skills, exchanges, and authority claims now carry the burden.
- **Handoff:** give lore-shokunin alternatives, dependency notes, incompatible choices, and evidence status; do not write a canonical voyage, dynasty, pantheon, or costume decree here.

## What a worldbuilder can take
- A design dependency, not a costume collage: routes and ecology constrain specialist language; narrative images support recall; materials organize labour; recognition and genealogy authorize claims.
- Separate inherited roots from technical innovations; give new institutions old words with explicitly modelled semantic changes rather than fake ancient etymologies.
- Make prestige costly and relational through training, production, exchange, custody, and obligations; do not reduce chiefly rank to tattoo coverage or cloth colour.
- Let one underlying relationship appear differently in speech, a cloth presentation, an embodied mark, and a navigational mnemonic without requiring identical symbols.
- Deliver a non-canonical system sheet naming region/era inspirations, invented mechanisms, source confidence, unresolved choices, and consequences for the world bible.

## Provenance cautions
- Colonial voyagers, missionaries, collectors, translators, and later anthropologists selected what they recorded; compare accounts with Indigenous scholarship and maker/navigation testimony.
- Surviving museum cloth and published chiefly narratives are uneven samples; scarcity of dated chronicles does not mean scarcity of historical knowledge.
- Broad lexical cognacy is firmer than reconstruction of exact prehistoric star catalogues, tattoo codes, or ritual words; do not let confidence leak between these levels.
- Modern voyaging demonstrates trained capability under documented conditions, not that every ancestral migration used one identical school or route.
- Reject “primitive drift-only voyaging,” racialized lost-civilization explanations, and tourism's universal Polynesian symbol dictionaries as unsupported shortcuts; deliberate voyaging does not make every particular migration narrative verified.
- This entry is thin on Marquesan, Tongan, Sāmoan, and Outlier ritual detail, restricted navigation initiations, and exact tattoo/rank entitlements **(to-verify)**; retain those gaps rather than inventing chronicle evidence.
- Real custodianship and consent questions are not settled by a citation or a public-domain date; use the coupling methods to invent new worlds, not to claim ownership of living traditions.
