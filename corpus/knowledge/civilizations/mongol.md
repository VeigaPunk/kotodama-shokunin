## Scope and evidence grades

- **Attested:** a feature located in an identified text/object/record; this establishes an occurrence, not universality or literal truth.
- **Comparative:** a later/regional practice that can suggest a design mechanism, but cannot fill medieval gaps without an argument.
- **Design inference:** a proposed fictional dependency, not an assertion about historical Mongols.
- `(to-verify)` identifies a specific unresolved attestation, translation, distribution, or chronology; do not silently promote it to historical fact.
- “Mongol” can denote political allegiance, lineage, or a historical population; “Mongolic” denotes a language family. An imperial subject need not speak Mongolian.
- Keep the thirteenth-century plateau, Yuan China, western successor courts, Qing-era communities, and living Mongolian societies distinct. None is the timeless original of all the others.

## Era staging — institutions before costume silhouettes

| Stage | Language/writing and sacred authority | Material/logistical coupling | Boundary check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Late twelfth century–1206 consolidation | Genealogical claims, alliance speech, emerging imperial titulature; literacy acquired through contact | Belts, horses, and gifts enact personal relationships | The Secret History narrates this retrospectively; not a field recording |
| 1206–1260 imperial expansion | Mongolian chancery writing; interpreters connect different court languages; Eternal Heaven legitimates rule | Court households, gift robes, compulsory provisioning; Ögedei's reign, 1229–1241, is central to relay organization | SH §§279–281 describe a governing program, not every station's observed condition |
| Later thirteenth–fourteenth-century successor courts | Different religious/literary patronage; Uyghur-derived writing coexists with other documentary scripts | Court dress and transport administration adapt to local institutions and workshops | Yuan and western khanates are not one uniform costume or theology; exact local rules are not established by the sources inspected here (to-verify) |
| Sixteenth–eighteenth-century transformations | Renewed Buddhist patronage and literary standardization; later shamanic materials interact with Buddhist vocabulary | Monasteries, aristocratic households, regional dress distinctions | Heissig/Hesse are comparison leads; date each ritual inventory before reuse |
| Twentieth century and living traditions | Cyrillic institutionalized in Mongolia; vertical writing continues in Inner Mongolia and is also revived/promoted in Mongolia | Everyday, ceremonial, regional, and heritage uses of the deel diverge | Unicode/UNESCO establish plurality; present-day policy and prevalence require dated local checks |

- This is a working periodization, not a ladder from “primitive nomad” to “civilization.” Mobile pastoralism, urban production, and documentary administration can coexist.
- For a fictional era transition, name the changing institution: scribal school, patronage network, garment workshop, household levy, or relay jurisdiction.

## Language family, typology, and writing history

| System | Historical handle | Worldbuilding use / constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Family | Mongolian belongs to Mongolic; Khalkha, Buryat, Oirat/Kalmyk, and other varieties are not interchangeable | Separate language kinship from membership in an empire; use Janhunen's relevant chapter |
| Grammar | Predominantly suffixing morphology, case marking, verb-final structures, and vowel harmony; Street explicitly segments many SH morphemes | Generate naming and official vocabulary with repeatable morphology, not random consonant clusters |
| Contact | Uyghur scribal mediation does not make Mongolian Turkic; a shared script is not a shared family | Borrow scripts, administrative terms, and specialists independently of genealogy |
| Script ancestry | Early thirteenth-century Mongolian writing adapts Old Uyghur, itself descended through Sogdian from Aramaic traditions | Script transfer is a social chain of teachers and documents, not isolated genius |
| Direction | Vertical top-to-bottom columns advance left-to-right, inherited from Old Uyghur | Design scrolls, seals, plaques, and textile inscriptions with the correct reading order |
| Graph system | An alphabet with joined letters and positional forms; some distinct sounds share letter shapes | Readers need lexical and grammatical knowledge; ambiguity is not evidence of an unwritten language |
| Written/spoken distance | Later literary orthography is not a direct transcription of modern Khalkha pronunciation | Maintain separate spelling, reconstructed reading, and modern pronunciation columns |

- Court tradition credits the Uyghur scribe Tata-tonga with transmission/adaptation; Unicode's Old Uyghur encoding proposal calls this a “popular narrative” rather than a documented single event (Pandey 2019, L2/19-016, p. 2, inspected). Do not present a perfectly documented invention day.
- Yuan use of the Tibetan-derived ’Phags-pa script is a separate imperial experiment, not a developmental stage of the Uyghur-derived alphabet: it was devised by the Tibetan lama ’Phags-pa (1235–1280) at Khubilai Khan's behest and promulgated in 1269 as a new national script (Unicode Consortium 2025, ch. 14, §14.4); institution-by-institution distribution remains `(to-verify)` here.
- “Altaic” as an established genetic superfamily is not a safe premise. Similar word order and harmony can reflect contact; family relationships require historical evidence, not typological resemblance.
- Unicode's historical preamble uses broad “Altaic” language; use that source for encoding/layout, not as adjudication of the contested family hypothesis.
- Traditional vowel labels “masculine/feminine” do not establish noun gender, people's gender, or a cosmological law.
- **Production check:** joined glyphs, vertical layout, variant selectors, and suffix spacing need a real shaping font. Plain character substitution or rotating isolated Latin-like glyphs does not reproduce the script.
- Romanizations below are reference handles: `q/kh`, `γ/gh`, and `ǰ/j` vary by edition. A change of transliteration convention is not evidence of historical sound change.

## The Secret History — register without fake archaism

- **Transmission:** a Mongolian work survives through Chinese-character phonetic transcription, Chinese glosses/translation, and related textual traditions; the surviving transcription is not the lost Mongolian-script original.
- **Dating:** the rat-year colophon in §282 does not by itself choose a unique absolute year. Composition, additions, and redaction are disputed; do not label every speech “verbatim, 1240.”
- **Social location:** dynastic memory selects loyalties, grievances, genealogies, and exemplary deeds. It is exceptionally valuable without being a neutral census or transparent transcript.
- **Editorial caution:** Street marks what he *assumes* are poetic lines and discusses alliteration and quotation. Modern lineation is an analysis, not untouched manuscript punctuation.

| Register mechanism | Inspectable anchor | Reusable craft mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Genealogical opening | SH §1: Heaven-linked wolf/doe ancestry; ensuing descent lists | A genealogy makes an opening claim to standing; it need not explain the whole universe |
| Quoted counsel and repeated speech framing | Street's transcription/explanation, especially treatment of `ke(m)e-`, “say” | Distinguish narrator, reported speaker, and speaker-within-speaker; preserve who warrants the statement |
| Oath/alliance language | SH §§116–117, `anda`, renewed friendship and exchanged gifts | Spoken commitment, remembered reciprocity, and worn possessions can jointly bind an alliance |
| Petition and rewarded service | SH §203, Šigi Qutuqu's appeal and allocation of duties | A speaker enumerates past service to demand present office; register is political action |
| Administrative self-presentation | SH §§279–281, consultation, relay proposals, and Ögedei's good/bad deeds | Contrast a ruler's account of relief for subjects with the labor required to deliver it |
| Formulaic/parallel diction | Street's explicitly marked poetic passages; SH §279 repeats bodily-rest imagery | Repetition makes memory, emphasis, and moral evaluation audible without inventing “broken” speech |

- Cleaves's 1982 preface explicitly names the English Authorized Version as a stylistic model: “thou,” “hath,” and biblical cadence are translation choices, not Mongolian grammar.
- **Application:** define a household conversational register, alliance register, ritual address, and chancery order separately; decide which speakers can competently cross between them.
- Do not turn literary livestock imagery into a claim that every historical speaker lacked abstract political vocabulary.

## Heaven, earth, ancestors — sacred lexicon with time-depth

- **Core attestation:** SH §113 invokes powerful Heaven and Mother Etügen; §103 places Temüjin's survival in relation to Burqan Qaldun and records bodily ritual acts.
- **Political theology:** Eternal Heaven can authorize imperial success and commands; Rubruck's account of Möngke's letter to Louis IX shows this claim traveling through interpreters and writing.
- **Mythic descent:** SH §1's wolf and doe and §§17–22's Alan Qo'a narrative legitimate ancestry. They are not sufficient evidence for a universal wolf-god cult or a single “Mongol creation myth.”
- **Institutional plurality:** Rubruck encounters Buddhists, Christians, Muslims, and diviners within connected imperial settings. Accommodation of specialists is not proof of modern religious equality.
- “Tengrism” is a useful comparative umbrella, but can conceal local ritual and contemporary reconstruction. Medieval sources do not furnish a complete catechism or neatly bounded universal pantheon.

| Term / spelling family | Working gloss | Calibration and coupling |
|---|---|---|
| `tenggeri`, modern `tenger` | Sky / Heaven; also divine usages in particular contexts | Do not force every occurrence into a personal deity; SH and later classifications differ |
| `möngke tenggeri` | Eternal Heaven | Imperial legitimation; do not translate mechanically as the Christian God merely because a European author does |
| `Etügen`, `eke Etügen` | Earth / Mother Earth | SH §113 is an anchor; a complete biography or iconographic program is not supplied |
| `bö'e`, later `böö`; `udugan/udgan` | Shamanic specialist; female-specialist terminology in later sources | Standard comparative vocabulary; Middle Mongol female-shaman forms (jituxan, utugun) are documented in Starostin–Dybo–Mudrak (2003, entry *ŭdu); local gender distinctions remain `(to-verify)` |
| `qam/kam/cham` | A label Rubruck uses for diviners | A cross-language encounter term, not proof all Mongols used this word or that all ritual offices were equivalent |
| `ongon` | Consecrated presence, spirit, or representation in later descriptions | Heissig/Hesse comparison lead; regional senses and medieval continuity `(to-verify)`; not simply “idol” |
| `sülde` | Potency/spirit and associations with standards in different traditions | SH §201 has related vocabulary; later standard-cult equivalences require contextual checking `(to-verify)` |
| `oboo`, modern `ovoo` | Cairn/shrine in later and living practices | A place-based ritual institution, not a portable “generic pagan altar”; specific medieval continuity `(to-verify)` |

- **Ritual clothing anchor:** in SH §103, belt at neck and hat held in hand accompany reverence, facing the sun and repeated kneeling. Dress changes the grammar of a bodily act.
- **Power conflict:** the Teb Tenggeri narrative in SH §§244–246 is a lead for competing charismatic and dynastic authority, not evidence of a universally obedient “court wizard.”
- **Thin area:** regional spirit inventories, women's ritual offices, and everyday household observance are much less securely reconstructed here than elite narrative formulas.
- Later lists of ninety-nine heavens/deities and white/black/yellow shamanisms must be tied to a dated community and source; do not install them as an undisputed thirteenth-century chart.

## Symbolic system — anchor meanings to an event

| Anchor | Evidence and limit | Fictional design question |
|---|---|---|
| White standard with nine “feet”/tails | SH §202; exact translation/image reconstruction must be distinguished | Who can raise it, witness it, carry it, and authorize a successor? |
| Wolf/doe ancestry | SH §1; genealogy is not automatically species worship | Does an ancestral image legitimate a lineage, locality, office, or everyone? |
| Belt as relationship-object | SH §117 exchange; §103 ritual repositioning | Can the same object bind peers in one scene and signify humility in another? |
| Sacred mountain | SH §103, Burqan Qaldun | Does survival create a vow, protected landscape, pilgrimage, or inherited duty? |
| Inscribed credential | Rubruck's account of the gold tablet given to a Mongol envoy accompanying Theodulus | How does a small object compel distant people to provide transport? |
| Matched court colors | Rubruck, feast before Möngke's letter | Does collective appearance index attendance, patronage, or office rather than ethnic identity? |

- Color word, material color, ritual value, and political emblem are different systems. “Blue = sky” does not license “all blue garments are priestly.”
- Do not back-project the later Soyombo emblem into Chinggis's court. Verify any emblem's maker, first attestations, and political reuse separately.

## Deel and dress code — silhouette, occasion, entitlement

- **Contemporary reference:** UNESCO describes a long caftan-like garment with sash/belt, hat, and boots; it emphasizes ethnic-group differences, weather, lifestyle, and social situations.
- **Medieval anchor:** Rubruck describes right-side fastening, fur breeches, rich silk stuffing versus poorer linings, and a tall silk-covered `bocca` headdress for wealthy women.
- `deel`/`дээл` is a useful modern garment label; SH §114 also contains garment/material description. Lexical continuity does not prove an unchanged cut, collar, sleeve, or closure arrangement.
- Specify **the wearer's** right when diagramming fastening. Rubruck's contrast between “Tartars” and “Turks” is his comparison, not a universal ethnic law for all periods.

| Dress variable | What the evidence supports | What must not be assumed |
|---|---|---|
| Long wrapped/belted garment | A practical ensemble with seasonal and social variation | One standard national pattern across centuries and regions |
| Fur, wool/felt, silk | Different production chains and unequal access; Rubruck describes several uses | Nomads cannot have luxury textiles, or every decorated robe is royal |
| Sash/belt, `büse` as a historical handle | Worn object, gift, and ritual sign; SH §§103,117 | A buckle alone gives an exact civil-service rank |
| Hat and head arrangement | Rubruck differentiates girls and married/wealthy women's appearances | All women wore a monumental headdress, or modern wedding hair reconstructs the thirteenth century |
| `bocca` / conventional `boqtaγ`, `boghtaq` labels | Rubruck describes a light structured frame covered with costly silk (passage re-inspected 2026-10-17) | That his one word is a universal tailoring pattern, or that every region used the same form; the exact medieval lexical mapping of `bocca` to later `boqtaγ` labels remains `(to-verify)` |
| Court-issued clothing | Rubruck reports coordinated colors across successive feast days and gifts of gowns | A permanently uniform army or an empire-wide color-to-rank table |
| Ritual removal/repositioning | SH §103 gives an actual setting and sequence | Everyday etiquette can be deduced from this single exceptional act |

- **Dress-code worksheet:** record wearer, locality, date, season, activity, material supply, fastening, permission to wear, and consequence of misuse.
- Separate **documented rule**, **observed preference**, and **design inference**. A repeated portrait feature is not automatically sumptuary legislation.
- For a fictional courier, couple weatherproof layers and riding mobility to identification, credential storage, and access to replacement kit; these are design requirements, not an attested universal courier uniform.
- Do not invent a shaman's costume by adding antlers and a drum to a fashionable deel; use a dated practitioner's ensemble and its own account of materials and spirits.

## Yam — logistics as language and social obligation

- **Primary anchor:** SH §§279–280 proposes designated stations and personnel so envoys need not circulate through households for routine requisitions; §281 counts relay establishment among Ögedei's achievements.
- Cleaves (1982), pp.224–227, distinguishes station keepers, post-horse keepers, geldings, provision sheep, milch mares, cart oxen, and carts. Build a service ontology, not merely a word for “post.”
- Rubruck's journey supplies the counter-view: guides, interpreter problems, authority disputes, shortages, changes of route, and exhausted or unavailable horses. Norm and journey are different evidence types.

| Lexical handle | Operational distinction | Design use / warning |
|---|---|---|
| `jam/ǰam`, conventional English `yam` | Relay route/station/service vocabulary in medieval sources | Separate the network, individual station, and institution in the fictional lexicon |
| `jam(u)čin/ǰamučin` | Station keeper/personnel; Street's `jamuci+n` marks a plural | Distinguish staffing from the physical post |
| `ula'a`, written-language `ulaγa` family | Relay transport/remount provision and associated service | Not simply the generic word “horse”; precise document-level scope varies |
| `ula'acin` family | Cleaves renders the relevant SH personnel as keepers of post horses | Do not silently turn a personnel tally into a universal horse inventory |
| `elči` | Envoy/messenger | The bearer may need language skills and political accreditation, not just speed |
| `jarliq/ǰarliγ` | Sovereign order/decree | Authority travels as a speech act, document, and command to provision |
| `gerege`; comparative `paiza` | Credential-tablet terminology | Rubruck describes the object and its force; exact labels in each language/document `(to-verify)` |
| `aqta` | Gelding; SH specifies animals for post duty separately from other stock | Count usable remounts separately from all animals at a station |
| `örtöö` | Modern Mongolian station/relay term | Useful for later-stage comparison; do not insert the modern form into a medieval quotation |
| `mingγan` / edition variants | “Thousand” administrative-military grouping supplying duties in SH | An allocation unit is not proof of exactly that many present workers or households |

- **Service chain:** authorization → route assignment → station reception → mount/food allocation → onward travel → replacement/rest/accountability. This is an analytic decomposition of the evidence.
- SH §280's twenty post-horse keepers in Cleaves is a textual allocation, not an empire-wide observed constant; check the edition before quoting it as twenty horses.
- A messenger can continue on fresh horses; transferring the message to a new rider is a separate operating choice. Do not import Pony Express practice automatically.
- **Design inference:** transit time ≈ riding legs + station service + queues + weather/route delays. Remount demand depends on traffic, recovery time, fodder, and water, not only distance.
- Choose bottlenecks before claiming speeds: winter pasture, river crossing, escort, translator, authorization dispute, or missing supply. Do not copy a headline daily distance as a universal service guarantee.
- Relief from arbitrary requisition and compulsory staffing/provisioning can coexist. Ask which households bear labor, who can appeal, and who abuses privileged access.
- A tablet is neither a democratic passport nor proof that every merchant could use every station freely; privileges and limits need a dated jurisdictional source.

## Naming practices — personal name is not an entire identity schema

- SH §59 links Temüjin's name to the captured Tatar Temüjin Üge. Use the recorded naming event; “blacksmith” is not a secure universal explanation merely because `temür` means iron.
- Rubruck's explanation of Chinggis as “sound of iron” is a warning about folk etymology and mediated translation, not a derivation to repeat as fact.
- Distinguish personal name, lineage affiliation, title, epithet, and relationship term. `Chinggis` is titulary in context; “Khan” is not a modern inherited surname.
- `anda` identifies a sworn relationship; `noyan` identifies lordly/command authority; neither should be randomly appended as decorative name syllables.
- Auspicious vocabulary such as `Möngke`, “eternal,” is a comparative naming resource; religious and contact-language names require period/locality checks rather than one empire-wide list (Atwood 2004; detailed distributions `(to-verify)`).
- Later Tibetan Buddhist names and modern patronymic/administrative practices must not be pasted onto every medieval speaker. Protective/avoidance names likewise need individual historical attestations before dating them to the empire.
- **Fictional schema:** `[personal name] + [optional title]`, with lineage, parentage, sworn ties, and documentary aliases in separate fields; decide who speaks each field, in which register.

## What a worldbuilder can take

1. **Portable authority:** couple a legitimating formula to a scribal language, authenticated object, compelled service, and visible encounter etiquette; alter these together when the polity changes.
2. **Layered literacy:** let borrowed script expertise coexist with oral political memory and multilingual administration; never equate literacy with one ethnic identity.
3. **Materialized relationships:** give belts, robes, mounts, and credentials different social biographies; identical materials can signify gifts, duties, ritual acts, or office.
4. **Costed communications:** make the relay network consume horses, pasture, food, carts, staff time, and interpretation; narrate the supplying household as well as the rider.
5. **Situated sacred practice:** stage a vow, landscape, ancestor claim, or specialist's intervention before inventing a comprehensive pantheon.
6. **Handoff only:** send lore-shokunin an option, its causal dependencies, historical anchors, uncertainty tags, and alternatives. No invented person, deity, costume law, or chronology here becomes canon automatically.
