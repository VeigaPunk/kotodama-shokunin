## 1. Scope and evidence horizons
- Base horizon: approximately 250–900 CE, especially lowland court inscriptions, monuments, ceramics, and elite regalia. “Classic Maya” is neither one language community nor a single unified empire.
- Separate **attestation** (a text depicts or states something), **interpretation** (what that practice meant), and **analogy** (a later tradition helps formulate a question). An inscription’s divine claim is evidence for the claim, not proof of its supernatural truth.
- Preserve region, polity, medium, social position, and date with every borrowed feature. A painted vessel, a funerary deposit, and a royal portrait do not have interchangeable contexts.
- Later K’iche’ creation literature, Postclassic Yucatec books, colonial testimony, and living Maya traditions are distinct evidence horizons, not a continuous transcript of Classic religion.
- Framework here: **text–image–material triangulation**, constrained by **historical-linguistic comparison**. These are analytical procedures, not indigenous names for a Maya worldview.

## 2. Era model: stage institutions, not a vanished people
| Analytical stage | Approximate working range | What to stage together | Calibration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preclassic antecedents | Before c. 250 CE | Earlier monumental practices, greenstone prestige, and developing writing | Do not make Classic conventions spring into existence fully formed |
| Early Classic | c. 250–600 CE | Dynastic legitimation, increasingly regular inscriptional conventions, elite display | Local boundaries vary; KH itself uses 250–550 in one ceramic discussion |
| Late Classic | c. 600–800/900 CE | Rich courtly text/image ensembles; named rulers, kin, captives, and ritual performances | Royal documentation is unusually visible, not socially representative |
| Terminal Classic transformations | Broadly c. 800–1000 CE | Uneven changes in court patronage, settlement, monument production, and regional power | Overlaps neighboring labels; never a synchronous extinction date |
| Postclassic and colonial horizons | After c. 900/1000 CE | Continuing Maya societies and altered institutions; later manuscript traditions | The Popol Vuh’s surviving textual history belongs here, not in a Classic archive |

- Dates are working archaeological conventions, not self-named historical ages; verify the selected site’s sequence before assigning a costume or inscription style (KH; A, local details to-verify).
- Design use: allow an old ritual lexicon and heirloom ornament to outlive the court that first sponsored them. Monumental silence need not mean linguistic or population disappearance.

## 3. Language family and writing: speech inside images
- Mayan is a language family. Much surviving Classic writing is Ch’olan-based; HRS’s influential **Classic Ch’olti’an** model proposes an Eastern Ch’olan prestige language. It does not establish that every inscription’s author spoke one identical vernacular (KH §3).
- Yukatekan and other regional features complicate a uniform-language account. K’iche’, the highland language of the Popol Vuh, is not simply “Classic Maya written later.”
- Distinguish language, written register, script, and modern transcription. A shared script can circulate across courts without erasing speech differences.
- The script is **logosyllabic**: logograms write words/morphemes; syllabograms generally represent consonant–vowel sequences. Pictorial appearance does not make it a language-free system of ideas (KH §§7–9).
- Handbook example: `WITZ`, `wi-WITZ`, and `wi-tzi` can represent *witz*, “mountain.” A phonetic complement cues reading; it is not an additional spoken word.
- Final written vowels do not map mechanically onto pronounced vowels. Synharmonic/disharmonic spellings and reconstructed vowel complexity require linguistic analysis; do not manufacture a universal “delete every final vowel” rule.
- Glyph blocks often run in paired columns, read across then downward; internal sign order has conventions and exceptions. Layout, ligature, and allography can vary without changing the language (KH §§5–6).
- Editorial convention: uppercase logograms, lowercase syllabograms, then a separate linguistic transcription and translation. Keep these layers separate in a fictional inscription worksheet.
- Mayan apostrophes are not decorative: they distinguish sounds and reconstructed forms under specified orthographies. Preserve a source’s convention rather than sprinkling marks through invented names.
- Grammar matters: `u-` can mark a third-person possessor or an ergative argument. Its occurrence alone does not mean “the king did it”; analyze the noun or predicate it accompanies (KH §§15–16).

## 4. Decipherment: the Knorozov/Ventris parallel and its limits
| Dimension | Maya: Knorozov’s 1952 breakthrough | Linear B: Ventris’s 1952 breakthrough | Transferable procedure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bad starting assumption to escape | Treating the script as essentially nonphonetic | Excluding Greek as a possible underlying language | Let a successful analysis overturn inherited expectations |
| Productive opening | Reinterpret signs in Landa’s colonial “alphabet” as syllabic evidence | Relate patterned sign sequences to language, inflection, and place-name hypotheses | Form competing hypotheses with consequences beyond one attractive reading |
| System recovered | Mixed logograms and phonetic syllables | A syllabic script for Mycenaean Greek, alongside administrative signs | Classify the script before designing its spelling rules |
| Demonstration | Reuse proposed phonetic values in other spellings | Test proposed values against further tablets and Greek forms | Demand cross-context predictions, not picture resemblance |
| Corpus bias | Codices and royal/artifact texts differ by period and genre | Much evidence is palatial administration | Do not infer a whole society’s voice from one surviving genre |

- KH pp. 10–12 gives the pedagogical turkey/dog chain: old orthography `cu-tz(u)` for *cutz*, then `tzu-l(u)` for *tzul*. The reusable second sign is the test; illustration plus a guessed translation is not enough.
- KH explicitly simplifies this demonstration. Do not present its teaching sequence as an exact laboratory notebook or treat sixteenth-century Yukatek vocabulary as the language of every Classic inscription.
- The parallel is independent phonetic-hypothesis testing, not shared ancestry, contact, or the claim that both scripts have identical structures (K through KH; C).
- Decipherment was cumulative: Berlin’s emblem-glyph work and Proskouriakoff’s dynastic/date analysis were essential Maya contributions; Linear B likewise depended on work including Kober’s and Bennett’s. Avoid the lone-genius legend.
- Application: make a conscript’s sound values work on names, inflections, and nonroyal objects not used to invent the key. Keep unread signs genuinely unresolved rather than supplying a symbolic gloss on demand.

## 5. Long Count mathematics and interlocking calendars
- Source: KH, Appendix E, printed pp. 47–55. The ordinary numerical system is vigesimal; the calendrical count has a mixed-radix exception. **A tun is 360 days, not the 365-day Haab.**

| Conventional unit label | Days | Carry into next unit |
|---|---:|---:|
| k’in | 1 | 20 |
| winal | 20 | 18 |
| tun | 360 | 20 |
| k’atun | 7,200 | 20 |
| b’ak’tun | 144,000 | Higher periods continue |

- These are conventional scholarly labels; KH’s period-name table distinguishes Classic readings such as *winik*, *winaakhaab*, and *pik*. Do not automatically put textbook labels into a reconstructed Classic speaker’s mouth.
- For a five-place date `b.k.t.w.d`, total days `N = 144000b + 7200k + 360t + 20w + d`.
- Normalized lower-place ranges: `0 ≤ d ≤ 19`, `0 ≤ w ≤ 17`, `0 ≤ t,k ≤ 19`. Do not infer a hard thirteen-unit limit on the highest place or ignore longer mythological counts.
- KH’s worked example: `9.15.6.14.6 = 1,296,000 + 108,000 + 2,160 + 280 + 6 = 1,406,446` days.
- Carry tests: `9.0.0.0.19 + 1 day = 9.0.0.1.0`; `9.0.0.17.19 + 1 day = 9.0.1.0.0`.
- Numeral notation includes dots for units, bars for fives, and zero signs; head variants also occur. Zero notation and an ending/completion expression need contextual reading, not automatic graphic interchangeability.
- The **Tzolk’in** combines a continuously advancing number cycle of 13 with 20 day names: `lcm(13,20) = 260` distinct day designations.
- The **Haab** has `18 × 20 + 5 = 365` days: eighteen named twenty-day periods plus five additional days. Regular month positions conventionally run 0–19; the beginning can be expressed as the month’s “seating.”
- The **Calendar Round** pairs those cycles: `lcm(260,365) = 18,980 = 52 × 365 = 73 × 260` days. “52 years” here means Haab cycles, not fifty-two 360-day tuns or exact tropical years.
- A Calendar Round designation recurs; it cannot independently locate a unique event across centuries. Long Counts and historical context disambiguate it.
- Monumental **Initial Series** can anchor later dates; **Distance Numbers** state intervals, with grammatical indications of earlier/later relationships. Repeated cycles do not make historical chronology impossible.
- Gregorian conversion requires a declared correlation constant and calendar convention. KH uses modified GMT `584285`; never silently combine its modern dates with a differently correlated source.
- Thirteen b’ak’tuns equal `1,872,000` days. That is a numerical period boundary, not a built-in mathematical end of time or evidence for a popular apocalypse story.
- Application: give ritual specialists both recurrence and elapsed-time tools; distinguish festival scheduling, dynastic anniversaries, and historical dating instead of calling all three “the sacred calendar.”

## 6. Mythic/pantheon core: overlapping powers, local embodiments
| Figure or cluster | Securely usable starting point | Coupling to examine | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maize deity imagery | Agricultural life and renewal are central interpretive contexts (T; B) | Human bodies, regalia, maize, and precious greenstone | Do not automatically equate every Classic maize figure with one named Popol Vuh character |
| Chaak/Chahk, often transcribed Chaahk | Rain/lightning deity imagery; axe and water associations (B pp. 8–10) | Weather, crop success, divine weapons, named royal ancestry | Spellings and local manifestations need source-specific treatment |
| K’awiil / God K | Attested theonym; lightning-related imagery with a forehead element and often a serpent leg (KH dictionary; B p. 37) | Theophoric names, divine objects, bodily manifestation | “God K” is a research label, not an indigenous alphabetic classification |
| Solar deity / K’inich Ajaw | Solar imagery and an attested deity/title vocabulary (KH; B p. 45) | Sun/day vocabulary, royal titulature, pictured divine attributes | Solar references do not make every ruler or every solar-looking deity identical |
| Palenque’s GI, GII, GIII | A locally documented divine triad, named with modern scholarly labels (B pp. 37–45) | Local mythic genealogy, patronage, temple ensembles | Proposed equivalences to the Popol Vuh lightning gods are hypotheses, not settled pan-Maya theology |

- B’s useful comparative principle is that divine forms can overlap and multiply; its particular mappings across centuries are not all equivalent in evidential strength.
- Treat deity costume/embodiment as a ritual relation, not mere theatrical imitation. B p. 2 describes elite deity-costume performances; the exact ontology at a particular court needs its texts and images.
- Design question: which attributes distinguish an aspect, which identify a patron, and which a human performer may temporarily assume? Do not begin with a Greek-style fixed departmental roster.

## 7. Popol Vuh: creation, speech, and maize, with a chronological firewall
- The extant work is K’iche’ highland literature with a colonial textual history. PV dates its lost alphabetic antecedent probably to 1554–1558 and Ximénez’s copying/translation to 1701–1704; neither date makes the surviving manuscript Classic.
- Use Christenson’s translation headings and commentary; *Quiché* in an edition title and *K’iche’* in current usage are not two unrelated peoples.

| Narrative complex | Mechanism in PV | Coupling available to a designer |
|---|---|---|
| Council before creation | Framer/Shaper and other divine names/titles deliberate; effective speech brings forth earth | Authority can depend on naming, consultation, and ordered verbal performance |
| Animals | Creatures cannot supply the requested intelligible invocation | Voice and reciprocal recognition, not mere bodily shape, define successful creation |
| Mud person | An unstable attempted human fails | A body’s material affordances can be part of theology |
| Wooden effigies | Beings lack proper remembrance; household objects and animals participate in their destruction | Myth can make everyday treatment of things and dependents morally consequential |
| Hunahpu and Xbalanque | Twins face the lords of Xibalba through trials, deception, death, and transformative performance | Ritual competence includes recognizing a deceptive social script, not simply martial strength |
| Maize humanity | Yellow and white maize supplies human flesh; the account continues into ancestors and lineages | Subsistence, personhood, ancestry, and the legitimacy of named groups become one system |

- Named anchors include Xmucane and Xpiyacoc, Heart of Sky, Sovereign and Quetzal Serpent, the twins, and the lords of Xibalba. Translation titles and multiple names must not be counted mechanically as separate gods.
- PV is not merely a detachable creation tale: its genealogical and historical continuation matters to the social work of the narrative.
- Strong comparison: maize, divine action, and human sustenance recur in both later literature and Classic interpretation. Weak shortcut: assign the entire later plot, character list, or K’iche’ terminology to an earlier lowland court.
- Apply **controlled comparison**: specify the shared motif, the independent Classic evidence, the time/region gap, and competing explanations. B supplies testable proposals; it does not close that gap by assertion.
- Do not force the twins into a universal hero-journey template or collapse Xibalba into Christian hell. Colonial mediation must be considered without erasing indigenous authorship and continuity.

## 8. Symbolic system: relations rather than a decoder ring
| Sign/material relation | Evidential anchor | What must remain contextual |
|---|---|---|
| Jade ↔ maize, centrality, rulership, breath | T’s stated iconographic interpretation | Not every jade object expresses all associations simultaneously |
| Floral earflare ↔ emergence/passage of breath spirit | T’s abstract discusses floral flares, beads, and serpents | An interpretive association, not proof of a uniform wearer’s doctrine |
| Mountain/cave ↔ access to powerful beings and resources | B pp. 4–12; *witz* readings in KH | A cave is not automatically identical to the death-lords’ domain |
| Sun ↔ day and solar qualities | KH’s *k’in* and *k’inich* entries | Related words, titles, and iconographic uses still require distinct entries |
| Ordered space ↔ field/house/world analogy | PV’s opening and commentary; B’s comparative treatment | Do not convert one late explanation into a universal Classic orientation law |

- Keep **lexical meaning**, **visual resemblance**, and **ritual association** in separate fields. A mountain-face image can participate in both a readable sign and an architectural metaphor without either explaining every occurrence.
- Thirteen, twenty, four, and other recurrent numbers have demonstrable uses in particular systems; do not infer a single hidden numerology from their recurrence alone.

## 9. Costume/material signature: jade is a relation, not a rank chart
- Start with represented bodies, ornament placement, and object context; only then infer status or ritual meaning. T supports several jade associations, not a complete dress code with statutory permissions.

| Component/context | Reference knowledge | Design variable and evidential restraint |
|---|---|---|
| Jade earflares/earspools | T relates floral flares to breath and supernatural passage | Distinguish bodily placement, shape, and occasion; do not assign the same sense to every ear ornament |
| Beads and chest ornaments | Greenstone can join preciousness, bodily display, and rulership interpretations | Model inherited objects, repair, transfer, and the cost of acquisition, not just “green = noble” |
| Divine headgear and bodily attributes | B describes deities and elite embodiment through costume | Specify which element is an attribute, a wearable object, or an artist’s sign of transformation |
| Elaborate cloth in royal images | Carved clothing is evidence for depicted form and pattern | Exact fiber, weave, dye, and construction from relief alone remain (to-verify) |
| Funerary jade | T explicitly includes funerary use and breath-spirit interpretation | Grave placement does not prove that the same arrangement was daily dress |

- **Greenstone is not a mineral identification.** Do not label every pictured green ornament jadeite or every light-colored bead shell without an object analysis; particular material identifications remain (to-verify).
- Couple durable ornament to less durable attachments and clothing. Distinguish an object’s geological material, construction, depicted color, and attributed meaning.
- Costume change need not parallel linguistic change: a court may retain inherited ornaments while its spoken language and written title formulas diverge.
- Do not transplant modern community-specific Maya dress wholesale into Classic portraits, or substitute a generic feathered “Mesoamerican” costume assembled from unrelated peoples and eras.
- No universal, securely recovered Classic sumptuary statute is supplied here. Claims such as “only kings could wear jade” require independent evidence, not repetition of elite museum selection.

## 10. Bloodletting and ritual registers: do not flatten the vocabulary
- Royal self-offering belongs in a larger field of bodily discipline, divine/ancestral relations, costume, and public representation. It is not interchangeable with captive sacrifice or every other offering action.
- Case-study locator: Yaxchilan Structure 23, Lintel 24, associated with Ix K’abal Xook and Itzamnaaj Bahlam (numbered Itzamnaaj(?)/Kokaaj(?) Bahlam III in the Mesoweb record). Checked in this pass against the Mesoweb monument record [MB]: the caption gives 5 Eb 15 Mak (9.13.17.15.12), rendered as 28–29 October 709 CE across the records consulted, while the lintel itself is dated c. 725 CE — an event/carving-date distinction to keep; the lord holds a burning spear (*k’ahk’al jul*) and the text carries the carver Mo’ Chahk’s signature. Pictured equipment is described, not archaeologically recovered; do not fill gaps with a thorn-versus-obsidian story from memory.

| Register/action | Attested linguistic resource | What not to infer | Fictional coupling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discipline/penance | *ch’ahb*: KH dictionary gives “penance, fasting, sacrifice” | It does not lexically name bloodletting in every occurrence | One formal term can encompass several bodily observances |
| Formulaic paired expression | *ch’ahb* with *ahk’ab*, “darkness”; KH notes a possible strength/potency sense in the possessed couplet | Do not translate the pair by adding two modern dictionary glosses, or treat the proposed sense as certain | Formal couplets may carry meanings unavailable word by word |
| Scattering/sowing | *chok-*; KH’s Ixtutz Stela 4 example has *uchokow ch’aaj*, “he scattered droplets” | KH allows blood or incense-related interpretations of *ch’aaj*; scattering is not automatic proof of a bleeding body | Distinguish act, offered substance, agency, and ritual occasion |
| Court commemoration | Dates, named agents, titles, and witness expressions in KH’s worked inscription | A stone text is not a recording of everything actually spoken at the rite | Tie bodily action to authorization, witnesses, and historical memory |
| Ordinary conversation | Little direct vernacular dialogue is recoverable from this selected ritual evidence | Do not invent an attested commoner euphemism or priestly password | Any contrasting everyday register must be marked as new design |

- The point of a register contrast is social distribution: who can utter a title, commission a text, perform a discipline, or authorize a portrayal? Do not reduce register to ornate synonyms for blood.
- Keep act, caption, depicted apparition, and modern causal interpretation separate. Neither “all visions were hallucinations” nor “every serpent picture records a literal apparition” follows from the evidence.
- Application: design ritual obligations and contested readings of them, not an undifferentiated spectacle of violence. Gendered permissions require local evidence; one royal image does not establish rules for all men or women.

## 11. Naming practices: names, titles, and affiliations are different slots
- KH’s titles, kinship expressions, and Appendix K make a better starting point than a generator of exotic-looking syllables.

| Layer | Attested example/resource | Design lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Personal/theophoric name | *Bajlaj Chan K’awiil* in KH Appendix K | Divine names can enter personal names; do not turn a modern literal gloss into everyday address automatically |
| Rank/title | *ajaw*, “lord”; *k’uhul*, “holy/divine” | A title is not a hereditary surname or an ethnic label |
| Political affiliation | KH’s *k’uhul Mutul ajaw* example | Model a polity/dynastic title separately from the person carrying it |
| Kin relation | Maternal and paternal child expressions differ in KH | A genealogy needs the relation and source, not merely two adjacent names |
| Modern scholarly label | Translations, nicknames, and changing readings | Preserve aliases and edition history; matching English glosses do not establish identity |

- Name strings may contain divine, celestial, animal, object, or action vocabulary; derive candidates from a specified grammar rather than a bag of English noun translations.
- Numerals in names do not prove that all Classic people received automatic Tzolk’in birth names. Popol Vuh calendrical/theophoric naming belongs to its own language and textual horizon.
- Handoff ledger: `person-id | source spelling | normalized reading | name/title boundary | polity | kin relation | date | alias | uncertainty`. Keep unread portions unread.

## 12. What a worldbuilder can take
1. **Couple archive and authority:** choose what a court records, the spoken variety behind its prestige register, and who can challenge a carved historical claim. Do not equate written standardization with a uniform population.
2. **Couple arithmetic and ritual:** implement independent recurrence and elapsed-day systems; test carries and repeated dates before deriving anniversaries, titles, or period-ending obligations.
3. **Couple food and personhood:** derive a fictional creation logic from a culture’s actual subsistence materials, then ask what speech, remembrance, and reciprocity make a person socially complete.
4. **Couple costume and theology:** let an ornament’s placement, biography, and ritual use connect to attributed breath, ancestry, or renewal; decide who contests that interpretation rather than asserting a universal color code.
5. **Couple era and survival:** change institutions, patrons, and audiences at different speeds. Preserve the possibility of living communities after courts stop commissioning monuments.
6. **Keep the result a proposal:** send lore-shokunin an unbound reference/design packet with source-derived observations, attributed interpretations, invented rules, applicability ranges, and unresolved checks separated. Only lore decides canon.

## 13. Provenance cautions and thin areas
- **High confidence:** the logosyllabic principle, the handbook’s attested spelling examples, basic Long Count arithmetic, and the distinction between the Classic and colonial textual horizons. This does not mean every glyph has a settled reading.
- **Model-dependent:** the exact distribution of prestige Ch’olan versus vernacular features; deity identities across regions; breath-spirit iconographic readings; and the ontology of divine embodiment. Preserve attribution and alternatives.
- **Thin in this entry:** ordinary speech, nonelite ritual terminology, dress-law enforcement, actual textile recipes, and community-level variation. Chronicle evidence for Classic everyday voices is scarce relative to the selected court record; do not invent missing testimony.
- **Colonial mediation:** Landa’s elicitation and Ximénez’s transcription occurred under unequal colonial conditions. They are invaluable evidence, not transparent recordings of all earlier Maya thought; indigenous authors and living interpreters remain agents, not passive survivals.
- **Access limits:** KH, PV, and B were directly inspected; T’s argument is used only at the scope of its inspected abstract; HRS is mediated through KH; C and A were bibliographically checked, with no pretended full-text review. GV’s object-level checks now run through the Mesoweb monument record [MB], not the printed plate.
- **Discredited shortcuts:** pure-ideogram Maya, a timeless peaceful priest-astronomer society, ancient-astronaut authorship, 2012 mathematical apocalypse, and the disappearance of Maya people after the Classic are not usable scholarly premises.
- **Recheck trigger:** a final reconstruction of a real inscription, a specific costume, or a deity identity requires the relevant drawing/object, up-to-date reading, and local archaeological context.
