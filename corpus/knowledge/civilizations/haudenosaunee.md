## Scope and calibration
- Haudenosaunee is a living confederacy, not a vanished, homogeneous archaeological civilization; the six nations retain distinct languages, histories, communities, and institutions. [N09, p. 1]
- The original Five Nations are Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga, and Seneca; Tuscarora became the sixth in the early eighteenth century, conventionally dated 1722 in N09. Migration and incorporation should not be compressed into a single instantaneous event. [N09, pp. 1, 17]
- Distinguish political membership, language-family membership, clan affiliation, residence, and religious participation; these are not interchangeable classifications.
- **Established/public** below means supported by the identified public source, not permission to reproduce every associated image, name, or ceremony.
- **Source-specific** means retain the named narrator, nation, date, and translation; **contested** means competing interpretations must remain visible; **restricted** means do not turn the reference into reconstruction instructions.
- This is a craft reference, not an authority on contemporary citizenship, ceremonial access, or rights. No fictional names, historical events, or costume designs here become world-bible facts.

## Language family, grammatical resources, and writing
| Layer | Reference anchor | Craft consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Family | The six national languages belong to Northern Iroquoian; Cherokee belongs to the wider Iroquoian family but not the Confederacy. [M99; N09, p. 1] | Language kinship does not automatically imply political unity or the same religion. |
| Nation versus language | Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga, Seneca, and Tuscarora are distinct languages, not varieties of an undifferentiated “Iroquois language.” [N09, p. 1] | Select a particular language and community before investigating any form. |
| Polysynthesis | Iroquoian is a major comparative case; D24 treats pronominal prefixes, verb structure, and noun incorporation in Cayuga. [M99; D24, contents] | Build morphological dependencies, not English sentences with exotic spelling. |
| Form versus function | D24's preface distinguishes nouns, verbs, and particles as word classes from functions such as adjective, pronoun, or evidential. | An English dictionary heading is not automatically a native grammatical category. |
| Noun incorporation | D24 distinguishes incorporable/non-incorporable nouns and verbs, and incorporation in discourse. [D24, contents, chs. 14, 33] | Define licensing and discourse function; do not glue any noun onto any verb. |
| Person reference | D24 organizes prefix meaning by person, number, gender, clusivity, and participant role. [D24, contents, ch. 20] | Specify who is included in “we”; do not infer consensus politics from grammar. |
| Orthography | D24 separately treats spelling systems, a modified Henry orthography, vowel length, and accent. [D24, contents, chs. 2–3] | Preserve the selected source's marks; apostrophes and accents are not atmospheric decoration. |
| Documentation | Alphabetic texts and teaching grammars coexist with oral transmission and wampum records. [D24; N09, pp. 6–7] | Distinguish transcription of speech from an archive whose authority includes objects and custodians. |
- **Application:** use the contrast between grammatical category and communicative function to design an internally consistent fictional language; do not manufacture “authentic” Haudenosaunee words.
- Language renewal is contemporary work, not merely preservation of a dead past. The Confederacy language page documents renewal efforts, but its speaker counts are undated and must not be presented as current measurements. [HCL]
- Specific phoneme inventories, dialect forms, and productive name derivations not supplied here remain `(to-verify)` against a selected nation's linguistic resources; the Cayuga grammar (D24) is the one inspectable inventory in this entry's source set. Current vitality estimates need dated, community-based or survey data, not an undated institutional page.

## Great Law of Peace and the wampum archive
- The Great Law joins a founding history, political ethics, offices, deliberative relationships, and practices of condolence; it is not adequately represented by a freestanding list of statutes. [N09, pp. 2–7; HCF]
- The public founding account connects the Peacemaker, Aiionwatha/Hiawatha, clan mothers, chiefs, the Tree of Peace, and wampum strings explaining the laws. These names and spellings belong to that account, not a universal orthographic standard. [HCF]
- Treat the archive as **object + authorized narration + custodianship + occasion + continuing relationship**: an analytical model derived from N09 and HCW, not a claimed Haudenosaunee technical term.
| Archive component | Public evidence | Design question, not a cultural recipe |
|---|---|---|
| Material | White and purple shell beads, including quahog and whelk, circulated through Indigenous exchange networks. [N09, p. 6] | Who obtains the material, and what labor makes a record costly? |
| Record | Strings and belts document laws, political relationships, histories, and ceremonial obligations. [N09, pp. 6–7; HCW] | What kinds of commitment require a durable object? |
| Performance | The Confederacy describes training in interpreting belts and narrating their significance. [HCW] | Who can give an accountable reading, and before whom? |
| Authority | Wampum strings can credential chiefs, clan mothers, and messengers. [HCW] | How is the messenger distinguished from an impersonator? |
| Custody | Onondaga are keepers of the central fire and hold a central responsibility for wampum records in the Confederacy account. [HCW] | How do custody, succession, and restitution affect continuity? |
| Renewal | Wampum remains associated with raising a chief and thanksgiving, not only past treaty-making. [HCW] | How is an old relationship renewed rather than simply stored? |
- **Do not decode it as an alphabet:** neither a bead-to-letter cipher nor an unaided picture chart captures the documentation described by these sources. “Mnemonic” must not mean legally insignificant or incapable of preserving knowledge.
- **Not just money:** foreground Haudenosaunee diplomatic and ceremonial uses. Colonial monetization and manufacturing belong to another historical context; avoid turning either context into a universal definition of wampum. [N09, p. 6; HCW]
- **Not a waist accessory:** the diplomatic belts discussed in N09 are records, not clothing; distinguish them from bodily adornment made with shell beads. [N09, p. 6]
- Separate the age of a tradition, the date of a particular narration, the manufacture of a surviving belt, and a modern replica. None automatically dates the others.
- The public overview is not a complete belt catalogue or an authorized recitation of the Great Law; item-level readings and surviving belts' manufacture dates remain `(to-verify)`. Published retellings already disagree about the Hiawatha Belt's age, so cite the custodian's account or an object record, not a secondary retelling.

## Matrilineal clans, naming, and succession
| Distinction | Publicly documented relationship | Avoid this shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Descent | Children belong to their mother's clan. [N09, p. 4; HCC] | Treating clan as the father's surname or a freely selected emblem. |
| Residence | Historical marriage commonly moved a husband into his wife's longhouse without changing his clan. [N09, p. 4] | Equating residence, descent, and citizenship. |
| Responsibilities | A man retained responsibilities to his mother's family and his sisters' children. [N09, p. 4] | Assuming the resident household exhausts all kinship obligations. |
| Cross-national ties | People of the same clan can recognize relatedness across national boundaries. [N09, p. 4; HCC] | Making every nation a closed, unrelated population. |
| Names | Assigning names within the clan is among the clan mother's responsibilities. [N09, p. 4] | A random animal-plus-verb generator claiming cultural authenticity. |
| Office titles | Families hold hereditary rights to particular chief titles; clan mothers select chiefs and maintain their accountability. [HCF] | “Hereditary” meaning automatic eldest-son succession. |
| Removal | Clan mothers can remove a chief who fails his responsibilities. [N09, pp. 3–4] | Calling the office an unchecked monarch or a purely decorative honor. |
- **Matrilineal is not a total theory of power:** specify descent, appointment, residence, food provisioning, and deliberation separately rather than substituting the catch-all label “matriarchy.”
- Clan animals designate relationships and obligations; they are not evidence that everyone in a clan has an animal's personality, worships that animal, or wears its image daily.
- The broad public sources describe prohibitions on marriage within a clan, but local classifications and exceptions require nation-specific verification; do not generate universal marriage laws from one summary. [HCC]
- HCC's stated Oneida clan count conflicts with HCF's bear/wolf/turtle account. This entry deliberately does not reproduce the disputed count as settled fact.
- **Application:** keep separate fields for personal name, clan affiliation, office title, naming authority, and everyday/civil identification. This is a worldbuilding schema, not a claim that every person uses the same set of names.
- Clan-owned name inventories, reuse after death, name transfer, adoption, and gender-specific naming protocols require direct, nation-specific, community-authorized evidence `(to-verify)`; no reusable name bank is provided here, and collecting one is not this lane's task.

## Mythic core: use a named telling, not a blended pantheon
- **Anchor telling:** Amos Christjohn's public Oneida account begins with Sky World above a water-covered world; a woman falls, birds assist her, Turtle supports her, and Muskrat obtains earth. [O]
- In this telling she bears a daughter; the daughter later bears twins and dies. Crops grow after the daughter is buried. Do not silently substitute a version in which Sky Woman herself bears the twins. [O]
- The twins create and alter plants, animals, waters, and other features; their contest explains a differentiated, difficult world. Christjohn's account calls the right-handed twin Holder of the Sky and the other Flint, and supplies Oneida names. [O]
- This is **source-specific public narrative**, not a claim that all Haudenosaunee accounts share every genealogy, animal sequence, episode, or evaluation.
| Narrative domain | Useful distinction | Calibration |
|---|---|---|
| Sky Woman and animal assistance | World-making involves relations among beings, not only a solitary maker. | Grounded in O; do not turn every helper into a deity with a fixed portfolio. |
| Twins | Creation and alteration are narratively differentiated. | O uses moral contrasts; do not rename the pair “God and Satan” or impose a universal complementary-duality theory. |
| Three Sisters | Corn, beans, and squash are cultivated together and treated as gifts sustaining life. [N09, pp. 10–11] | Agronomy, reciprocity, and narrative connect; an exhaustive goddess genealogy is not established here. |
| Peacemaker history | Ending destructive conflict and establishing accountable relations belongs to political memory and sacred history. [HCF; N09, pp. 2–3] | Not simply a heroic adventure detached from law and living institutions. |
| Medicine beings | The mask policy identifies sacred medicines and obligations to community welfare. [S95] | Restricted context; do not extract a fantasy bestiary or catalogue of powers. |
- “Pantheon” is only an indexing convenience here. Creator, ancestors, animals, medicines, narrative protagonists, and clan signs are not automatically equivalent categories of “gods.”
- Storytelling teaches values and acceptable conduct; N09 also describes winter as an important storytelling season. A universal permission calendar for all narratives is not established here. [HCT; N09, pp. 5, 15]

## Longhouse cosmology: architecture, polity, and relationship
| Scale | Attested coupling | Worldbuilding method |
|---|---|---|
| Domestic | Bark-covered framed longhouses held related extended households, compartments, shared fires, and storage. [N09, p. 5; HCH] | Map actual household labor and shared resources before inventing a sacred floor plan. |
| Territorial/political | The united nations are figured as one longhouse; Mohawk guard the eastern door, Seneca the western door, and Onondaga keep the central fire. [HCS; N09, p. 1] | Let familiar architecture supply a political vocabulary without erasing constituent nations. |
| Ethical | The founding account binds peace to mutual responsibility, accountable leaders, and a shared council. [HCF] | Connect metaphor to procedures and obligations, not merely to a flag. |
| Cosmological | Public symbols include Sky World, Turtle, and the Tree of Peace; these belong to particular narrative and political contexts. [HCS; O] | Keep related images distinct rather than declaring every tree or roof a literal cosmic axis. |
| Contemporary | People live in varied housing; community longhouses continue civic, cultural, political, and ceremonial uses. [N09, pp. 8, 14] | A building's institutional role can persist after its residential role changes. |
- Do not equate the architectural longhouse, the Confederacy metaphor, and the historical religious usage “Longhouse” as if they were one unchanged institution.
- One dated religious reform is documented: the Seneca prophet Handsome Lake's visions from 1799 and the ensuing *Gai'wiio* code, whose emergence and transmission Wallace (1969) treats in the standard study. Christian community histories and the relationship between particular longhouse congregations and governments remain thinly covered here `(to-verify)` before staging them.
- **No invented correspondences:** the checked sources do not establish a universal mapping of roof smoke holes to stars, household compartments to heavens, or hearth count to a sacred number.
- The public Thanksgiving Address acknowledges human and more-than-human relationships and helps gather minds for an occasion; analyze its social function without copying it as fantasy incantation. [N09, introductory letter, p. 10]

## Symbolic system: meanings belong to institutions and occasions
| Sign | Publicly explained association | Coupling constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Hiawatha Belt | Connected white shapes on a dark field signify the original Five Nations; the central tree represents Onondaga. [N09, p. 7; HCW] | Not an interchangeable geometric border or a claim that the later sixth nation lacks membership. |
| Belt's territorial sequence | West to east: Seneca, Cayuga, Onondaga, Oneida, Mohawk. [N09, p. 7] | Distinguish geographic sequence from left/right in a rotated photograph. |
| White pine / Tree of Peace | Unity, shelter, and the Great Peace; the public account places buried weapons beneath it. [HCS; HCF] | Explain what peace requires; do not reduce it to generic “nature worship.” |
| Four White Roots | Paths in the four directions through which others may seek the peace. [HCS] | Directionality expresses relationship and invitation, not automatically four elemental gods. |
| Eagle | Watchfulness over peace and warning of danger. [HCS] | Keep this role separate from other eagle meanings in other nations. |
| Bound arrows | Strength through the union of nations. [HCS] | A shared motif elsewhere does not alone prove historical borrowing. |
| Deer / antlers | Hide, food, sinew, tools, and adornment; antlers also signify chiefly authority in the founding account. [N09, p. 12; HCF] | One material can participate in subsistence, craft, and office without a single universal meaning. |
| Bead colors | HCW associates white with peace and purple with serious or political matters. | Source-scoped guidance, not a complete color dictionary or white=good/purple=evil rule. |
- **Application:** record each sign as `form → context → authorized explanation → obligations → period/source`; do not assign meaning from appearance alone.

## Era staging: relational change, not a “primitive-to-modern” ladder
| Working stage | Language / institutions / material context | Staging discipline |
|---|---|---|
| Before sustained European trade | Agricultural communities, extended households, longhouses, hides/furs/fibers, and Indigenous exchange. [N09, pp. 5–6, 10–12] | Do not project a later costume assemblage or calendar date backward without evidence. |
| Seventeenth–eighteenth-century colonial encounters | Fur trade, diplomatic wampum, new metal tools, cloth, and glass beads; Tuscarora incorporation changes the Confederacy's membership. [N09, pp. 1, 6–8] | Imported materials can enter continuing local systems; they do not instantly erase identity. |
| Late eighteenth–nineteenth-century dispossession and rebuilding | Loss of land, movement among communities, and increasing use of single-family houses; trajectories differ by place. [N09, pp. 8, 17] | Show coercion and adaptation, not a voluntary universal abandonment of the longhouse. |
| Late nineteenth–early twentieth-century documentation | Photographs document varied dress, beadwork, public identities, and ordinary European-style clothing alongside regalia. [N09, pp. 4, 8, 12–13] | Date the photograph, maker, materials, and occasion; it is not a transparent precontact snapshot. |
| Living twentieth–twenty-first-century communities | Contemporary professions and clothing, community institutions, language renewal, and continuing material arts. [N09, pp. 13–15; HCL] | Continuity includes innovation and ordinary present-day lives, not perpetual historical reenactment. |
- **Founding chronology: contested — keep the disagreement visible.** HCF frames the Confederacy as existing since time immemorial; archaeological and historical argument has commonly placed formation around the mid-fifteenth century, while Mann and Fields (1997) argue for a 1142 founding from an eclipse record. Neither proposal converts oral history into a Gregorian date; cite the argument, not a settled year.
- For precontact dates and early ceremonial dress, the sources checked here do not supply a securely dated continuous chronicle. This is a limitation of this entry's evidence, not evidence that oral historical knowledge is absent.
- Local ceremonial calendars should follow community and seasonal conditions; differences between the public summaries must not be averaged into a fictional universal schedule.

## Costume and material signatures without freezing an era
| Publicly documented element | Coupled systems | Appropriate use |
|---|---|---|
| Hide garments, moccasins, leggings, skirts, dresses, shirts, and breechcloths | Animal processing, climate, work, and historical garment terminology. [N09, p. 12; HCCL] | Describe a dated ensemble and activity; not every person wears every element. |
| Quillwork, shell/wood beads, feathers, and painted decoration | Craft skill, materials, and expression. [N09, p. 12] | Distinguish technique from the meaning of an individual motif. |
| Trade cloth, glass beads, silk, velvet, and silver | Exchange, local making, changing aesthetics, and public regalia. [N09, pp. 12–13] | Trace material substitution and new designs, not “authenticity lost on contact.” |
| Gustoweh | Fitted feathered headwear with nation-associated feather number/placement and individual artistry. [N09, p. 13; HCCL] | Not a generic Plains-style warbonnet; do not invent a universal feather rank ladder. |
| Beaded regalia | Environment, cosmology, and clan imagery can enter particular designs. [N09, p. 13] | A motif's documented use is not permission to reconstruct every restricted object. |
| Contemporary everyday clothing | Public, domestic, professional, social, and ceremonial contexts differ. [N09, pp. 12–15] | “Regalia” and “costume” are not interchangeable descriptions of living ceremonial dress. |
- **Application:** make an ensemble record with period, community, wearer, occasion, maker, base material, acquisition route, decoration, and evidence status.
- Exact cut, construction, feather arrangement, gender/age conventions, and early dates for particular beadwork styles require object-level documentation — a dated catalogue record naming maker, materials, and occasion `(to-verify)`; this entry does not supply pattern templates.

## False Face ritual dress: public context and a firm boundary
- “False Face” is retained as the assignment's and historical literature's label; S95 discusses **medicine masks** and the societies responsible for them. These are not entertainment masks or a public costume category.
- The Grand Council's 1995 statement, signed by Tadadaho Chief Leon Shenandoah, identifies wooden and cornhusk masks, medicine societies, sacred duties, and community welfare. That is the public contextual limit used here. [S95]
| Question | Policy evidence | Consequence for this reference |
|---|---|---|
| Who holds authority? | Medicine societies and the Grand Council, not a collector or museum. [S95, “Authority over Medicine Masks”] | Outsider ownership or a catalogue entry cannot authorize reconstruction. |
| Are small/new/commercial examples exempt? | The policy calls all wooden and cornhusk medicine masks sacred regardless of size or age, and rejects commercial production. [S95, “Status,” “Sales”] | Do not invent a tourist-object exception as permission. |
| May images be used as design references? | The policy opposes public exhibition and exhibition use of reproductions, photographs, castings, or illustrations. [S95, “Exhibition”] | No mask images, facial pattern descriptions, replica instructions, or “inspired” ritual ensemble here. |
| Is old ethnography unrestricted knowledge? | S95 says information about medicine societies is not for general distribution and rejects outsider presentation of their significance. | Publication history does not settle cultural authority or access. |
| What about museum holdings? | S95 calls for return to the Grand Council for appropriate care. [S95, “Return”] | Treat custody and repatriation as living obligations, not lost-civilization salvage. |
- Complete ritual dress, materials-to-spirit correspondences, songs, medicines, making procedures, and ceremonial sequences are deliberately **not** reconstructed. Knowledge is thin here and authority is not ours; absence is not a gap to fill imaginatively.
- **Application:** learn that some material forms have restricted custodians and responsibilities. For fiction, invent an independent form and institutional logic instead of reskinning the recognizable mask tradition.

## What a worldbuilder can take
- **Multiscalar coupling:** a household model can organize kinship vocabulary, territorial metaphor, public deliberation, and architecture without making all four identical.
- **Distributed documentation:** durable objects, trained interpreters, succession, credentials, and repeated public relationships can form an archive without a bead alphabet.
- **Naming as governance:** who can assign a name or fill a title matters as much as the phonological shape; keep descent and appointment separate.
- **Material change with continuity:** altered cloth, tools, and bead supply can transform ensembles while offices, kin ties, and responsibilities continue or are renegotiated.
- **Variant-aware mythology:** choose a narrator and tradition before comparing motifs; a source-specific genealogy is more useful than a maximally inclusive “pantheon.”
- **Bounded access:** distinguish public teaching, office knowledge, and restricted ceremonial responsibilities; do not assume secrecy is an invitation to invent an exposed ritual.
- **Handoff to lore-shokunin:** supply proposed coupling rules, source identifiers, period/community scope, alternative interpretations, and unresolved checks. Lore alone decides and records fictional canon.

- **Colonial-source calibration:** mission reports, administrative records, museum catalogues, and collector ethnographies can preserve evidence while filtering it through translation, conversion agendas, dispossession, and unequal access. - **Reject false universals:** no “primitive animism → civilization” ladder, no automatic Great Goddess or Jungian-archetype explanation, no generic Native American costume, and no inference that shared motifs prove a single origin. These are methodological exclusions, not substitute explanations of the traditions.
- **Thin areas:** early absolute chronology, local name inventories, detailed religious periodization, original ceremonial ensembles, and restricted knowledge remain bounded rather than invented. Oral tradition, living expertise, archaeology, and written chronology answer different questions; compare them without demoting one simply because it is not a colonial document.
