# corpus/QUALITY.md — the admission bar

An entry is admitted into `corpus/knowledge/` only when it clears all of:

1. **Provenance** — every factual claim carries a cited, checkable source
   (author + year, standard edition, or stable ID). A claim the author
   cannot verify is marked `(to-verify)` inline — never laundered into
   assertion. Invented sources are a conformance defect, not a style issue.
2. **Calibration** — every framework entry names its status: mainstream /
   contested / discredited, with the reason. Pop-mythography
   (Campbell-as-universal, strong Whorf, glottochronology, Frazerian
   evolutionism) must be flagged as such on first use.
3. **Coupling** — the `- couples:` line names the systems/files the entry
   connects. An entry that couples nothing is reference, not craft; it must
   say so.
4. **Usability** — a worldbuilder can act on it: decision tables, typology
   tables, checklists, worked examples. No essays.
5. **Canon hygiene** — no entry decides what is true in any world. Canon
   recording and consistency verification belong to lore-shokunin; entries
   end at the handoff.

Rejections are kept with their rejection reason (see `lessons/`) — a
rejection is evidence of taste.

Source tiers (A/B/C, each scoped to a claim) are defined in `SOURCES.md`,
the lane's opened source registry; a tier-C row is a lead, never sole
support for an admitted claim.

6. **Source authority stays claim-scoped** — publisher descriptions and abstracts are tier B for substantive claims about a work, even when accurate; reserve tier A for sources primary or authoritative for the exact scope. Correct a filed tier with a superseding SOURCES row, never an in-place tier edit. Grounded in the reviewer disagreement and correction at `corpus/lessons/round-49.md#post-review-corrections` (S034, S038, S039 → S044–S046).

7. **Claims stay separable** — each round claim verifies on the base commit plus only the paths it lands itself. Its added lines may not cite an identifier another claim newly exports, nor name a path another claim newly lands; a counterfactual leg that quietly keeps the dependency (inline URLs standing in for the registry rows) or rewrites the other claim's bytes before checking does not count as verification. Sources a claim cites are that claim's lands. Run `tools/check-claim-separability.py` on the round manifest before dispatch. Grounded in the round-49 REJECTED-HONEST-STOP at `corpus/lessons/round-49-verifier-spotcheck.md#adjudication` (C cited the registry S created). A claim's declared exports name the exact id block it owns before dispatch — the ids it creates, or a block reserved to it that no other unit assigns and whose unused ids stay unassigned — never an open range: loop S000-S999 against each export and the matched set must equal the declared block. Grounded in the round-55 closure's routed defect 1 (`corpus/lessons/round-55-verifier-spotcheck.md#5-routed-defects-lane-fixes-next-round`, R55-M5: an S285-S999 export matched O's fixture id S900 and the declared check exited 1) and shown able to fail by `.ufo/waves/round-56/build/CriticSeparabilityExports.md` §1-§2 (the round-55 manifest exits 1, the exact range exits 0; a round-56 tool line naming S360 yields an O->C finding while S358 and S419 do not).

8. **Declared inputs run before dispatch** — every declared counting or acceptance command runs once on its exact declared input before the round dispatches, and the receipt is committed with the declaration. An input-shape error (missing manifest keys, an unreadable file, unrecognized arguments) fails the declaration, not the round: correct the declared command or input and re-run before dispatch; never substitute a different input after measuring. A not-yet-landed path is the only expected error before landing. A closure checks this from committed bytes: each command in the dry-run receipt matches its declaration verbatim and exits 0 or stops only at an absent land. Grounded in the round-50 Decision at `corpus/lessons/round-50-verifier-spotcheck.md#decision` (Ca's frozen leg on `reports/round-50/claims.json` exited 2, `SEPARABILITY_INPUT_ERROR manifest is missing base, schema`) and mistake R51-M1 at `corpus/lessons/round-51.md#mistakes`.

9. **Dry runs are checked both ways** — supersedes criterion 8. Every declared counting, acceptance and separability command must appear in the pre-dispatch receipt, and every receipt command must match a declaration verbatim. Expected pre-landing results are named in the receipt: an absent land (exit 2, the land named), or an after-value comparison failing with the before value printed. A checker that stops at its first absent land leaves its later legs unevaluated, and the receipt says so. Grounded in the falsify finding at `.ufo/waves/round-51/falsify/ReviewQuality8.md` and mistake R51-M5 at `corpus/lessons/round-51.md#mistakes`: round 51's receipt omitted the two manifest acceptance commands, which a two-way check flags.

10. **Sources are opened by ordinary means** — a row may record a source as opened only when it was reached by a plain request, a public copy, an archive capture or a library scan that a fresh session could repeat. A row whose own cells record getting past an access control (a headless browser or other browser-automation tool through a bot challenge, a solved captcha, a bypassed paywall, login or geo block) counts as unopened: its status is `rejected`, a superseding row keeps the reason and the flip, and no knowledge entry cites it. A challenge met and then routed around by an ordinary copy (for example a Wayback capture) is not a bypass, and the row should say the challenge was met and not passed. Check: `python3 -B tools/check-source-access.py` exits 0 (status leg and cited leg; `--selftest` holds its controls). Limits, stated: the checker sees only bypasses a row reports in its source, anchor or teaches cell, matches citations by S-id only, and does not test that a superseding row exists; a reviewer re-opens a sample. Grounded in the round-51 outside spot-check at `corpus/lessons/round-51-verifier-spotcheck.md#discernment-spot-check` (S094 disagree: HTTP 403, Cloudflare challenge, opened via headless browser) and mistake R52-M1 at `corpus/lessons/round-52.md#mistakes`; shown able to fail on the round-51 tip `2b956d7` (`reports/round-52/telemetry/prereq-access-check.txt`: 6 findings, exit 1) and on the round-52 base `147cfde` (5 cited findings, exit 1; ReviewCriterion10). Third-party journal or book scans served without stated permission are not ordinary public copies: accept a scan only when its host page names a licence or permission covering it, or the copy is the publisher's, sits in the author's institutional repository, or is the digitising library's or archive's own scan; a university or library host name alone does not establish permission, and a scan whose cover carries a database's personal-use terms (for example a JSTOR cover) fails unless the host states its own permission. The check is a reviewer's (host page and scan provenance); the access checker cannot see it. Grounded in R56-M4 at `corpus/lessons/round-56.md#mistakes` (two third-party JSTOR scans filed as public copies, rejected before landing) and the round-56 spot-check (`corpus/lessons/round-56-verifier-spotcheck.md` §3, both rejects confirmed); drafted by seat ScribeQuality10 (`.ufo/waves/round-57/build/ScribeQuality10.md`), which applies it to the two rejects (fail) and to S375 and S381 (pass).

11. **Each claim clause is shown able to fail** — adds to criterion 9, which checks that the receipt and the declaration agree; this criterion checks that the declared commands can fail at all. The declaration lists each claim's clauses (a count, a path, a form such as `path:line`, an index row, a status, a `used-by` cell, "one new entry"), and a non-author seat checks the list against the statement's words before dispatch. A clause no command checks mechanically (a topic, "on X as method", what a source carries) is listed as review-only and is never covered by a weaker stand-in predicate such as a `- topic:` pattern. Each other clause names the declared counting or acceptance command that checks it, and that command reads the exit status of every tool it runs: no pipe without `set -o pipefail`, and no `$(…)` or `|| true` that hides a status. Controls run the declared argv verbatim from the root of a scratch copy. A green control is a copy where every clause holds, and there the command exits 0; a stand-in landing is allowed in the copy only and is never landed. A red control is one per mechanical clause: it changes that clause alone with the nearest near-miss, not an easy break (a `dir/` token for a `path:line` clause, not a missing file; a second added file with an existing one removed, so the count still holds), and the command exits non-zero, printing that clause's leg as failing and every other leg as passing. The base run does not count as a control: there every clause is false at once. Where the statement ranges over rows that exist at base, the before-value matches the predicate applied by hand to those rows. Check from committed bytes, before dispatch: the clause list in the declaration, the control script that builds each copy, its receipt in a file criterion 9's two-way check does not read (no `$ ` prefix), and the sha256 of each instrument it ran; a closure replays a sample. A clause with no control is unverified, not passing. Shown able to fail (receipt `reports/round-53/telemetry/criterion11-controls.txt`, made by `reports/round-53/criterion11-controls.sh`): round 51's D argv exits 0 with one anchor replaced by `linguistics/`, and round 52's C argv exits 0 with the index row removed (the sweep exits 1). Round 53 does not meet this criterion: its declaration `60f1d2a` carries only the all-false base run, its per-clause controls ran in the build wave and after landing, and its after-landing receipt has no control for C's S187 floor or `mined` status or for D's SID_BAD. It binds from round 54's declaration. Grounded in mistakes R52-M3 and R53-M2 (`corpus/lessons/round-52.md#mistakes`, `corpus/lessons/round-53.md#mistakes`): round 51's D counter and round 52's C acceptance could not fail on a clause their statements made, and in round 53 the build-wave critic found two C clauses `accept-c.py` does not check (`.ufo/waves/round-53/build/CriticClaimSizingC.md:12-13`). That makes round 53 the third consecutive round of the class, which the round-52 closure said trips the drift sentinel (`corpus/lessons/round-52-verifier-spotcheck.md:57`).

12. **Re-test stated rejection grounds** — a reject or not-registered reason that names a tool, gives a measurable result (a word count, a locator failure, an HTTP error) or asserts a source property a tool can check names the re-test tool and carries its verbatim output in the lesson's Gate line. Without that output the reason is unverified and cannot justify the decision. Grounded in the round-56 koon1244 reason ("no quotable passage", contradicted by quotable JSON fields) and the round-57 CODH reason ("below the 8-word minimum", contradicted by four quotes that each locate) (`corpus/lessons/round-57-verifier-spotcheck.md` §3 and §5 item 1); drafted by seat ScribeQualityC12 (`.ufo/waves/round-58/build/ScribeQualityC12.md`).

13. **Unreachable live sources are handled before data** — a predicate with a live-fetch leg declares before data how an unreachable page is treated: list an archived capture of that same page in the row, or define a bounded allowance for that fetch class with its own control; the treatment is frozen and never relaxed after data. Grounded in `corpus/lessons/round-58-verifier-spotcheck.md` §5 item 5 (S510/S511 returned HTTP 403 and the no-slack predicate failed on those fetches); round 59's FETCH-59 (`reports/round-59/rowcheck.py`) is the first application. Drafted by seat ScribeErrataQuality59.
