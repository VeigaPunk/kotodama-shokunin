# To-verify triage: confirm, downgrade, or excise coupled cultural claims

- topic: A lane-local procedure for resolving uncertain reference claims without laundering speculation into linguistic-cultural knowledge or fictional canon; includes risk triage, evidence tests, review cadence, and an append-only resolution ledger.
- sources: Martha Howell and Walter Prevenier (2001), *From Reliable Sources: An Introduction to Historical Methods*, Cornell University Press; Samuel S. Wineburg (1991), “Historical Problem Solving: A Study of the Cognitive Processes Used in the Evaluation of Documentary and Pictorial Evidence,” *Journal of Educational Psychology* 83(1): 73–87, https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.83.1.73; Michel-Rolph Trouillot (1995), *Silencing the Past: Power and the Production of History*, Beacon Press (20th-anniversary edition, 2015, foreword by Hazel V. Carby); First Archivist Circle (2007), *Protocols for Native American Archival Materials*, version dated 9 April 2007; Digital Inquiry Group (n.d.), *Historical Thinking Chart*. Consulted surrogates and access limits are identified in §12; operational thresholds below are this lane's policy, not findings attributed to these works.
- couples: language family, writing and naming — `../linguistics/{historical-linguistics,writing-systems,naming-systems}.md`; myth and symbol — `../myth-symbol/{comparative-frameworks,motif-index-usage,symbolism-synthesis-method}.md`; era and costume — `../culture-material/{era-periodization-models,dress-through-eras,ritual-garments,sumptuary-law-rank-display,material-culture-inference-method}.md`; brace groups expand to filenames relative to this entry. Sibling interfaces: chousa-shokunin for specialist source investigation; lore-shokunin for world-bible decisions, never direct canon edits by this lane.

## 1. Boundary and calibration vocabulary

- This is a reference-maintenance protocol, not a historical truth engine, general research service, or permission to amend sibling records.
- The procedures describe future authorized reviews; this entry does not adjudicate or modify any already-seeded claim.
- Keep three independent fields: **evidence state** of the real-world reference; **design use** permitted for an invented mechanism; **canon disposition** owned by lore-shokunin.
- A well-supported historical claim is not automatically approved fictional canon; an approved fictional choice does not become evidence about a real tradition.
- `(to-verify)` means a named factual question remains unanswered. Attach it to the smallest uncertain proposition, not to a whole culture or bibliography.
- **Located**: a bibliographic record or lead exists; this is not evidence that its text supports the claim.
- **Inspected**: the relevant passage, object record, image, or linguistic datum has been examined, with a locator and edition.
- **Scoped-supported**: inspected evidence supports the exact restricted wording; confirmation is bounded and revisable, not certainty.
- **Contested**: inspectable scholarship disagrees about a specified proposition; preserve the disagreement, not a false consensus.
- **Design-only**: an invented option or outside analytical lens, carrying no claim that a real people practiced or believed it.
- **Unknown**: evidence is unavailable, thin, restricted, or beyond current language/tradition competence; unknown does not mean false.

## 2. Intake: freeze the claim before seeking an answer

1. Assign a stable `TV-<year>-<sequence>` identifier; save the original wording, file/section locator, and content fingerprint or dated snapshot reference.
2. Atomize bundles: “clan X spoke language Y, worshipped Z, and wore red” creates separate language, cult, costume, and relationship claims.
3. Write a falsification question: what observation would refute or materially narrow this proposition?
4. Specify people/community, locality, period, social position, genre, and occasion; use `unknown` rather than supplying plausible missing values.
5. Classify the assertion: observation, reported belief, translation, reconstruction, comparative interpretation, causal explanation, or design suggestion.
6. Record why it was flagged: missing citation, inaccessible passage, doubtful translation, conflicting chronology, contested framework, or unsupported cross-system inference.
7. List downstream consumers: name lists, myth tables, symbol palettes, era charts, garment vocabulary, and any already-sent handoff.
8. Set an owner, priority, next evidence action, and calendar review date; a naked marker without a research question is not a usable queue item.

| Priority | Trigger in this lane | Immediate containment |
|---|---|---|
| P0 — contain now | Fabricated citation, misquotation, sensitive/restricted content, demeaning generalization, or a false claim already circulating in handoffs | Stop reference reuse; notify the integrator/recipient with the claim ID; preserve only appropriate audit metadata |
| P1 — before dependent work | Family/script conflation; pantheon equivalence; causal symbol-to-dress rule; incorrect era boundary supporting several outputs | Quarantine the inference and dependent derivations; review before another handoff |
| P2 — next maintenance pass | Isolated gloss, uncertain object identification, incompletely located source, nonessential chronology | Retain an inline marker and scoped question; no promotion to settled reference |
| P3 — optional lead | Attractive comparison or design analogy with no factual dependency | Keep in a clearly labeled lead/design queue, not in an attested-example table |

## 3. Inspect evidence: named methods, limited warrants

- **Source criticism** after Howell and Prevenier (2001): distinguish authenticity, transmission, purpose, and what a source can actually establish. An authentic chronicle may still misrepresent others.
- **Sourcing, contextualization, corroboration** associated with Wineburg (1991), operationalized here through the Digital Inquiry Group chart: identify who made the evidence, situate its production, and compare relevant accounts.
- **Close reading**, also in the chart: inspect qualifiers, reported speech, audience, and genre; do not quote a sentence while dropping the restriction in the next sentence.
- **Silencing audit** after Trouillot (1995), via the consulted CUNY summary: ask what is lost at source creation, archive assembly, narrative construction, and assignment of historical significance.
- **Community-specific stewardship**: First Archivist Circle (2007) emphasizes consultation and culturally responsive treatment of Native American archival materials. Its scope is not a universal license or a substitute for other communities' protocols.

| Evidence class | Inspect and record | Does not establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Linguistic grammar/dictionary | Language/variety, edition, example/entry, transcription system, elicited versus attested form | A language family from shared script; an entire social institution from one gloss |
| Manuscript, inscription, chronicle | Witness, edition, passage, date of composition/copy, translation, speaker and genre | Literal historical truth of a myth; everyday speech from a ceremonial formula |
| Oral account/performance | Authorized public documentation, named setting, collector/interpreter, transmission and variant information where supplied | Timeless uniform belief; a precise origin date merely from the date of recording |
| Artifact/catalogue/image | Accession/catalogue identifier, find context where known, date range, restoration, image versus object distinction | Everyday dress from a divine image; rank or ethnicity merely from shape/color |
| Law or prescriptive text | Jurisdiction, date, class addressed, exceptions, enforcement evidence if claimed | That everyone obeyed a ban, or that the banned practice was universal |
| Specialist synthesis | Author's scope, cited evidence, treatment of alternatives, publication context | That every cited primary witness has been independently inspected in this review |

- For every source: author/date/title/edition, exact page or stable section/object identifier, access route/date, and the proposition supported or contradicted.
- Distinguish reading the original from reading an edition, translation, abstract, review, or catalogue. Cite the consulted intermediary explicitly.
- A search snippet, model response, or publisher blurb is a discovery aid, not passage-level verification of a cultural claim.
- Map dependence: translations of one inscription, editions of one manuscript, and websites copying one catalogue are not independent witnesses.
- Do not demand an arbitrary citation count: one inspected object record can support a narrow description; many copied assertions cannot support a universal claim.
- If a source is inaccessible, record the failed route and seek a legitimate accessible edition or specialist synthesis; never claim to have read the unavailable text.

## 4. Decision procedure: exactly three resolution outcomes

| Outcome | Necessary condition | Live reference wording | Ledger obligation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Confirm** | Relevant evidence inspected; exact proposition and scope supported; material alternatives addressed | State only what the evidence warrants, retaining period/region/register and any scholarly disagreement | Record passage/object, reasoning, residual limits, reviewer, and reopen trigger |
| **Downgrade** | Original exceeds evidence, but a narrower supported claim or explicitly non-empirical design use remains useful | Replace universality/causality with a supported local description, attributed account, hypothesis, or design-only mechanism | Preserve old/new wording and explain the lost warrant; identify any still-open factual question |
| **Excise** | Claim is contradicted, citation fabricated, conflation irreparable, or unsupported factual material has no safe useful remainder | Remove it from usable reference and handoffs; preserve an appropriate audit tombstone | State whether contradicted, unverifiable, or unsuitable to retain; never equate removal with proof of nonexistence |

1. First check whether the cited work exists and whether the locator actually contains the asserted material. Bibliographic correction alone does not confirm the substantive claim.
2. Compare evidence to the atomized wording, including quantifiers: `some`, `official`, `recorded in`, `always`, and `caused` are different claims.
3. Ask whether a minimal restriction repairs the claim: locality, date, genre, class, occasion, source attribution, or a weaker relationship verb.
4. If repairing the wording would answer a different question, create a linked child claim rather than pretending the original was confirmed.
5. A supported statement that “author A reports X” confirms the report, not X's truth or the supernatural event described.
6. If the factual basis fails but the mechanism is useful, detach the real-culture attribution and label the remainder design-only; do not disguise it as an ethnographic hypothesis.
7. If neither confirmation nor responsible downgrade is available, excise from usable reference or keep the investigation **open and quarantined** with `(to-verify)`.
8. Open/quarantined is a queue state, not a fourth resolution verdict. A deadline, failed search, or exhausted time budget never forces confirmation.
9. Close only after the authorized live wording, ledger event, dependency notice, and review conditions agree; otherwise the resolution remains proposed.

## 5. Coupling audit: evidence for nodes is not evidence for arrows

A language, myth, symbol, garment, and date can each be correctly described while their proposed connection is invented. Review both each item and every asserted relationship.

| Coupled claim | Required cross-check | Safe fallback when the link fails |
|---|---|---|
| Language family ↔ script | Separate genealogical classification from script use, literacy domain, and borrowing history | “Uses script S in context C”; leave genealogy separately sourced |
| Divine name ↔ pantheon role | Distinguish name cognacy, translation equivalence, local cult, and historical syncretism | Retain a dated attested epithet/report; do not merge deities automatically |
| Motif ↔ shared ancestry | Compare variants, context, transmission possibility, and the basis of any diffusion argument | “Comparable motif”; index similarity without claiming common origin |
| Color word ↔ symbolic value | Verify lexical sense separately from ritual interpretation and social setting | Keep the gloss and the local symbolic reading as separate records |
| Symbol ↔ costume/rank | Look for contextual interpretation, wearer/occasion, medium, and prescriptive versus observed usage | Describe the image/object; mark meaning or rank linkage `(to-verify)` |
| Era label ↔ cultural package | Check local periodization and separate dates for speech, cult, craft technique, and administration | Use overlapping dated trajectories rather than one synchronized “civilization stage” |
| Naming practice ↔ costume group | Test whether a name denotes lineage, office, locality, exonym, or later label; independently establish garment association | Retain name structure and material description without inventing group identity |

- A confirmed node does not upgrade an uncertain relationship. Record each disputed arrow as its own claim ID.
- Review downstream transformations: an uncertain gloss can contaminate a theonym, which then appears to validate an otherwise unsupported garment symbol.
- Preserve divergence: an inscription's language, a wearer's identity, an object's production place, and a findspot need not coincide.
- Where handoff tags such as HARD/SOFT/DOCTRINE/DESIGN appear in other notes, do not treat them as evidence grades or independent canon approvals; include this protocol's explicit evidence state.

## 6. Era staging: keep the clocks apart

| Clock | Question | Common false repair |
|---|---|---|
| Claimed practice/event | When is the activity said to occur? | Substituting the reign assigned by a much later narrative |
| Composition/performance | When was this wording or recorded performance produced? | Calling the recorded version unchanged since its narrated beginning |
| Surviving witness/object | When was this copy, depiction, garment, or artifact made? | Dating all represented customs to the object's manufacture |
| Collection/excavation | When and under what conditions was evidence collected? | Treating the collector's terminology as ancient self-description |
| Analysis/edition | When were translation, classification, or period boundaries proposed? | Presenting a modern analytical era label as an indigenous category |

- Store date ranges and uncertainty; identify calendar conversions. An unverified conversion stays `(to-verify)` rather than receiving false precision.
- Earliest inspected attestation is not an invention date; latest inspected attestation is not proof that a practice then vanished.
- For fictional staging, derive separate language-change, institutional-reform, symbol-reinterpretation, and textile-production timelines as design options; convergence must be chosen, not presumed from the historical reference.

## 7. Contested scholarship, colonial filters, and thin knowledge

- Review the proposition, not the reputation of an entire framework. A plot generator may be useful without validating its claimed cultural universality.
- “Contested,” “unsupported here,” “superseded,” and “discredited” are not synonyms. A claim of scholarly rejection also requires identified criticism and scope.
- Do not confirm either “all myths follow one template” or “every part of this school is worthless” from a seed file's confidence alone; inspect the particular claim and counterarguments.
- Colonial, missionary, court, and elite records are situated evidence: record interpreter, administrative/religious purpose, imposed categories, and whose testimony is missing where documented.
- A colonizer's translation of a local power as “god,” “chief,” or “devil” does not settle the indigenous category; the translation linkage remains `(to-verify)` until supported.
- Bias is not automatic falsity, and a community-authored account is not automatically uniform or timeless. Preserve attribution, disagreements, and the actual question each source can answer.
- Sparse chronicles do not demonstrate absent history, costume systems, names, or institutions. Write “not established by the evidence inspected,” naming the inspected corpus.
- State competence limits explicitly: `no original-language competence`, `only translated elite chronicles inspected`, or `community/region/period coverage thin`; name the missing evidence rather than inventing a connecting custom.
- Public accessibility is not blanket authorization for reuse. If sensitive/restricted material or community protocols arise, hold reuse and route the issue to the appropriate operator/custodian; this lane makes no rights ruling.
- Do not reproduce restricted details in the audit trail. Keep minimal non-sensitive metadata and a referral status; access restriction is not evidence that a claim is false.

## 8. Review cadence and stop rules

These intervals are local maintenance defaults, not a research finding or a scheduled automation job. Assign actual dates and an owner when a live claim is opened.

| Moment | Required action |
|---|---|
| Intake, before reuse | Classify risk, contain P0/P1 dependencies, preserve original wording, and assign the next action |
| Each active work session | Inspect P0/P1 items touching the current packet; do not let a queued correction lag behind another handoff |
| Every seven days while lane work is active | Review open P1/P2 items by risk and age; record evidence gained, decision proposed, or specific blocker |
| Every thirty days while active | Reconcile orphan markers, overdue rows, unresolved disagreements, excision tombstones, and stale source links |
| Before each lore handoff | Recheck every reference actually reused, its qualifications, dependencies, and unresolved IDs, regardless of calendar age |
| On new evidence or correction | Reopen affected decisions immediately; append a new event and notify known downstream recipients |
| On resuming dormant work | Review overdue items once against current evidence; do not manufacture missed historical reviews |

- After two consecutive scheduled reviews without new evidence, choose a sharper question, request specialist investigation, or defer with a concrete blocker and revisit condition.
- Deferral keeps the item open/quarantined; an unsupported example may instead be excised for nonuse with an explicit “not disproved” reason.
- A closed confirmation has a review trigger, not an invented expiry of historical truth: new edition/translation, revised dating, relevant critique, or expansion beyond the original scope.
- P0/P1 closure requires an identified second reader competent for the relevant issue; without one, retain “proposed” and the containment. Do not invent a reviewer or self-certify their review.
- Minimal queue health: open IDs by priority, overdue IDs, dependency notices outstanding, and resolutions reopened. A falling marker count alone is not success.

## 9. Resolution ledger: schema and append discipline

- Keep ledger events within the authorized lane record; this entry supplies the schema, not a new file mandate or permission to alter other records.
- Give each event its own ID; preserve stable claim IDs across reopening. Current state is the latest explicitly superseding event for that claim, not a rewritten historical row.
- Record proposed and executed actions separately. A decision written here does not prove a seeded entry or recipient's packet was corrected.
- Store the smallest sufficient quotation only when actually inspected and appropriate to reproduce; otherwise give a precise locator and neutral paraphrase.

```yaml
claim_id: TV-<year>-<sequence>
event_id: <claim-id>/E<sequence>
supersedes_event: <earlier-event-id-or-none>
reviewed_at: <date>
owner: <identified-reviewer>
second_reader: <identity-and-review-pointer-or-not-yet-reviewed>
original: <exact-claim-and-original-location>
scope: <community-locality-period-register-occasion>
claim_kind: <observation-report-translation-reconstruction-interpretation-cause-design>
question: <what-needs-checking-and-what-would-change-the-decision>
evidence: <full-citation-edition-locator-access-date-and-consulted-intermediary>
independence: <witness-dependencies-and-relevant-counterevidence>
limits: <language-competence-coverage-access-and-bias-limits>
queue_state: <open-quarantined-proposed-closed-reopened>
outcome: <confirm-downgrade-excise-or-unresolved>
replacement: <exact-supported-wording-design-only-note-or-removal-tombstone>
rationale: <why-this-evidence-warrants-this-scope-and-not-more>
residual_questions: <linked-open-IDs-or-none>
dependencies: <affected-files-name-rules-myth-tables-era-costume-and-handoffs>
action_evidence: <dated-authorized-edit-or-notice-pointer-or-not-executed>
next_review: <actual-date-or-event-trigger>
reopen_if: <specific-evidence-that-would-change-the-verdict>
```

- `unresolved` is no resolution outcome: it prevents an open investigation from masquerading as a closed verdict.
- A downgraded factual remainder with missing support retains its inline `(to-verify)` and stays open; a separately supported narrower child claim can close independently.
- An excised claim receives a tombstone pointing to its ID and reason, so it cannot quietly re-enter through another comparison table.
- Correct mistaken ledger events by appending a superseding event; retain the original except where sensitive content requires an authorized protective treatment.

## 10. Worked decisions — illustrative, not a record of corpus edits

| Hypothetical review input | Evidence test/result | Decision and permitted reuse |
|---|---|---|
| “All members wore the sacred sash” | Suppose an inspected catalogue documents one dated ritual depiction, not population-wide practice | Downgrade to the depicted wearer/occasion; keep population usage `(to-verify)` and separately quarantined |
| “Language A belongs to family B because it uses script S” | Script evidence contains no genealogical demonstration | Excise that inference; retain independently supported script use; family assignment `(to-verify)` |
| “This translation renders title T as ‘fire keeper’” | Suppose the actual identified translation/entry has been inspected and agrees | Confirm only that translation in that edition; no automatic confirmation of etymology, office powers, or ritual costume |
| “Every hero cycle has the same stages” | Template matching supplies no adequate cross-cultural warrant | Excise the universal factual assertion; optionally preserve a detached, design-only plot exercise |
| “No surviving source describes the garment” | Reviewer has searched only translated court chronicles | Downgrade to “not found in the specified inspected chronicles”; broader absence `(to-verify)` |
| A source page cannot be opened | Bibliography is discoverable but the relevant passage is uninspected | Leave open/quarantined; access failure cannot confirm or disprove the cultural claim |

No live resolution is claimed by these examples; real closure requires populated evidence, identity, action, and review fields.

## 11. What a worldbuilder can take

1. Select a coupled reference question, not an entire “civilization package”: for example, whether a naming rule and a ritual garment were linked in a particular setting.
2. Build a coverage card: language family and writing; myth/pantheon; symbols; local era model; costume/material techniques; naming practices. Mark unexamined dimensions as gaps, not implied matches.
3. Resolve the separate facts and each connecting inference using §§3–7; retain differences between regions, classes, occasions, and dates.
4. Extract a mechanism as an option: naming authority, material scarcity, ritual access, or regime reform. State which links are evidenced and which are invented for design.
5. Generate alternative couplings and counterexamples: the same office may retain its name while its clothing changes, or share regalia while using different liturgical languages. These are design probes, not historical assertions.
6. Hand lore-shokunin a reference-only packet: exact scoped claims, source/locator chain, evidence states, design-only options, open IDs, and change/reopen notices.
7. Leave cultural membership, official names, sacred truths, era boundaries, and canon acceptance to the world-bible owner. No protocol verdict decides them.
