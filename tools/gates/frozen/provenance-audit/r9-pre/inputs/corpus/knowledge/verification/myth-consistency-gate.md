## 1. Gate contract and evidence status

- This is a **manual review specification**, not a registered executable fleet gate. Its outputs are findings, comparison records, and unresolved options; it cannot certify an unreviewed bible or create canon.
- source: the editions in §10, distinguished from the lane-authored workflow below.
- verified-at: 2026-10-17 — institutional catalog/publication pages checked where linked; full books and every entry were not exhaustively consulted.
- recheck: when the index edition, source translation, culture/era scope, pantheon inventory, or symbol grammar changes; check any `(to-verify)` claim before making it load-bearing.
- **Unit of review:** one proposal revision × culture/community × locality × era × narrative or ritual register. A whole civilization is not one uniform witness.
- **PASS:** all required checks evaluated, no unexplained violation, and every deliberate exception has a bounded record.
- **FAIL:** a demonstrable violation of the proposal's declared rules; cite both incompatible assertions and their overlapping scopes.
- **HOLD:** essential inputs, provenance, or interpretation are missing; state exactly what evidence would permit evaluation.
- Evaluate each of P/M/E/S separately. Overall: FAIL if any check fails; otherwise HOLD if an essential check is unevaluable; otherwise PASS. Never average away a failure.
- PASS means internally reviewable under the stated assumptions, **not** historically authentic, ethically cleared, or accepted as canon by lore-shokunin.
- Treat numerical quotas and culture-wide distribution claims in generation notes as design hypotheses, not scholarly laws or automatic gate requirements.

## 2. Minimum review packet

| Record | Required fields | Why the gate needs it |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | proposal revision, community, place, era interval, register, intended exceptions | prevents comparing incompatible contexts |
| Role ledger | dependency, local category/title, agent or non-divine mechanism, cult/story status, uncertainty | tests coverage without imposing a universal pantheon |
| Narrative ledger | tale/variant ID, teller or institution, genre claim, ordered events, conditions, consequences | distinguishes plot logic from motif decoration |
| Index concordance | local ID, candidate index entry, edition, entry wording/location, match limits | makes comparative claims checkable |
| Layer ledger | narrated time, composition hypothesis, surviving witness, performance/reception, material dependencies | separates chronology from retrospective representation |
| Symbol ledger | sign form, context key, domain readings, material carrier, authorized users, change history | makes collisions reproducible |
| Evidence ledger | author, year, edition/translation, passage or object ID, provenance, confidence, access limits | distinguishes attestation from inference |

- Label every assertion **attested**, **scholarly inference**, **design constraint**, **proposal**, or **unknown**. A fictional proposal needs a derivation, not a forged historical citation.
- For an absence claim, record the corpus searched, languages, genre coverage, and search limits. “Not in this packet” is not “unknown to this people.”
- Do not expose restricted ritual knowledge to fill a table. Mark an access boundary and choose a less extractive comparison.

## 3. P — Pantheon role coverage, not archetype completion

Versnel (2011) makes Greek divine complexity a warning against neat slot systems. Use a **bipartite role–agent map** as designer tooling: agents can share functions, and one agent can carry several.

| Dependency to ask about, if relevant | Coverage evidence | Legitimate non-slot answer |
|---|---|---|
| Subsistence, water, weather | ritual appeal, seasonal story, household practice, declared material mechanism | impersonal process; no divine provider |
| Authority, oath, conflict settlement | invocation, sanction, assembly myth, legal custom | ancestors, institutions, or deliberately secular procedure |
| Mortality, burial, continuity | destination/process for the dead; who performs or refuses rites | no judge; multiple destinations; unresolved doctrine |
| Reproduction, care, household continuity | local kin terms, household rites, narratives of dependence | distributed spirits or human obligations |
| Craft, exchange, travel, danger | occupational practice or story where the proposal makes these important | borrowed patron; diffuse protection; no patron |
| Cosmic origins and boundaries | explanation, refusal to explain, competing accounts | no creator; origins outside ordinary causal categories |

- Start from the proposed society's dependencies and claims, **not** a mandatory sky/earth/war/love roster. The rows above are prompts, not universal religious needs.
- Mark each relevant role `ASSIGNED`, `SHARED`, `NON-DIVINE`, `EXPLICITLY UNEXPLAINED`, or `UNKNOWN`; give the reason and its scope.
- `SHARED` is not a defect: record jurisdiction, epithet, locality, occasion, or acknowledged rivalry. Do not split one deity merely to satisfy a spreadsheet.
- Separate **story presence**, **cult participation**, **institutional office**, and **analyst's category**. A narrated being need not have a known priesthood or festival.
- Test a practical request: which address-form, intermediary, rite, or refusal would the proposal license? Do not require a fixed number of epithets or rituals.
- Check names against the language ledger: local title versus personal theonym; inherited form versus loan; taboo substitute versus translation. Opaque names do not by themselves date a cult.
- **MC-P01 → FAIL:** the proposal requires an exclusive agent for a critical rite but assigns none, while also denying any substitute.
- **MC-P02 → HOLD:** a role is marked absent solely because the surviving elite text does not mention it.
- **MC-P03 → PASS with exception:** overlapping divine jurisdictions are explicit, scoped, and predict how a disputed invocation is handled.
- For non-pantheonic systems, mark the deity roster `NOT APPLICABLE` with an explanation; still review whatever cosmological or ritual agents the proposal actually claims.

## 4. M — Motif coherence against ATU and motif indexes

| Instrument | Unit it compares | Appropriate use | Invalid inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uther, ATU (2004 or named later edition) | recurring tale types summarized as plot configurations | locate comparative variants and bibliographies | every myth must fit an ATU type |
| Thompson (1955–1958) | indexed narrative elements across several genres, including myths | retrieve comparable incidents, objects, beings, relationships | a motif code proves identical meaning or common ancestry |
| Local/regional catalog or documented corpus | traditions within a stated collection scope | recover genre and distribution hidden by a broad index | a collection is the entire culture's narrative repertoire |
| Lane event/constraint graph | proposed actors, events, permissions, costs, consequences | test the fictional narrative on its own declared premises | graph consistency proves historical authenticity |

- Record **edition + identifier + checked entry + source witness**. Keep a candidate comparison distinct from a verified identification; use `(to-verify)` for an unchecked code or title.
- Uther's 2024 revision exists; do not silently read a 2004 reference as a checked 2024 entry. Editions are not interchangeable just because an identifier looks familiar.
- Do not invent an official ATU or Thompson number for a fictional tale. Give it a local ID; write `cf. ATU …` only when the comparison is justified.
- A motif is not a complete tale type. A shared magical object is insufficient to identify a plot; a shared type need not preserve every incident or interpretation.
- Mythic material may share incidents with indexed folktales, but ATU is not an exhaustive myth taxonomy. Thompson's coverage also exceeds its mythological A chapter.
- Build the event graph: **actor → action → enabling condition → cost/prohibition → result → social interpretation**; record variant-specific changes rather than flattening them.
- Test repeated taboos and powers across narratives: who may breach them, what consequences follow, and whether exceptions are authorized, contested, comic, or unexplained.
- A myth's taboo need not be a rule of ordinary conduct. Establish whether narrators endorse, invert, ridicule, or merely describe it before comparing it to custom.
- Compare reward, punishment, marriage, kinship, and afterlife claims within matched registers. Official theology versus a joke can intentionally disagree.
- **Checked comparison:** Ashliman's “Name of the Helper” groups type-500 tales in which discovering the threatening helper's name defeats its claim; the page supplies multiple tellings (§10).
- **Application:** compare a proposed name-escape tale's bargaining and discovery sequence, not merely the fact that a name is secret. A priestly avoidance-name system is a different claim requiring its own evidence.
- **MC-M01 → FAIL:** the same variant says a captor's power ends when a captive speaks its true name, then shows that qualified act leaving the power intact without an exception.
- **MC-M02 → HOLD:** a claimed index match has no checked entry, edition, or narrative witness. Preserve the local narrative without pretending the match is verified.
- **MC-M03 → PASS with exception:** contradictory outcomes belong to recorded variants with different tellers, ritual settings, or in-world theological positions.
- Shared types or motif strings alone do **not** establish borrowing, date, origin, kinship, or cultural sameness. Contact claims need independent transmission evidence.
- Never fail a culture for lacking the “expected” amount of war, captivity, trickery, or fate motifs; collection bias and genre selection can dominate apparent distributions.

## 5. E — Era alignment: compare layers, not one date

| Clock | Record | Do not equate it with |
|---|---|---|
| Narrated epoch | the time the tale claims its events occur | the date of composition |
| Formation hypothesis | proposed composition, inheritance, adaptation, or redaction interval | a securely observed first origin |
| Surviving witness | manuscript, inscription, object, recording, edition and date | the age of every element in it |
| Performance/institution | who tells or enacts it, when, under which authority | the practices of all earlier communities |
| Reception | later translation, revival, illustration, costume reconstruction | an unchanged ancient meaning |

- **First attestation is a limit on present evidence, not proof of invention then.** Separate an attested terminus from a conjectural history; preserve date ranges and uncertainty.
- For fictional chronology, compare literal manufactured objects with the setting's own documented availability, import route, repair skills, and access restrictions.
- A miraculous object need not be locally manufacturable; mark it as such and test the miracle's declared limits. Do not use “mythic” as an excuse for accidental material contradictions.

| Staging situation | Required coupling check | Acceptable explanation |
|---|---|---|
| A later teller describes remote founders | vocabulary, script, tools, textile depiction | modernization, translation, allegory, or literal-period reconstruction |
| A cult changes political patronage | titles, divine ranking, emblem ownership, festival sponsorship | dated reform, local resistance, competing authority |
| A myth travels across a contact boundary | names, loanwords, reinterpreted kinship, garments, symbols | specified carriers and adaptation; uncertain route marked unknown |
| Ritual dress preserves an older form | weave, cut, dye, maintenance, terminology | retained manufacture or deliberate reconstruction, not assumed timelessness |
| Script or sound change alters a sacred word | historical form, current reading, written convention, wordplay | archaism, respelling, borrowing, loss, or later reinterpretation |

- Homophone symbolism must work in the **claimed language stage**. A modern pun does not automatically explain an old emblem; a later reinterpretation can be explicitly valid.
- Dye names, poetic color words, actual pigments, and surviving textile color are different evidence. Do not infer an exact cloth recipe from a divine epithet.
- **MC-E01 → FAIL:** a literal local robe requires a material that the same revision excludes from production, trade, heirloom survival, and supernatural provision in that era.
- **MC-E02 → HOLD:** a late chronicle is the sole witness, but the proposal asserts its regalia and divine offices were unchanged centuries earlier.
- **MC-E03 → PASS with exception:** a later performance intentionally gives an old hero contemporary regalia, and the representation layer is labeled accordingly.
- Changes propagate: recheck the theonym, its epithets, symbolic carriers, name-derived personal names, vestments, and calendar references after any era revision.

## 6. S — Symbol-domain uniqueness without false universals

Turner's **multivocality** cautions against treating ritual signs as dictionary substitutions (1967, ch. I “Symbols in Ndembu Ritual”; restated with the book's page loci in Turner 1973, *Science* 179: 1101–1102, article text consulted 2026-10-17). Uniqueness below is a design-schema rule, not a claim that real cultures assign one meaning per symbol.

- Normalize a sign as **form + medium/material + community + place + era + register/occasion + audience/office**. Bare “white,” “serpent,” or “star” is usually an inadequate key.
- For each normalized key, require one editorial entry defining the primary domain or an explicitly bounded multivalent reading. Store subordinate associations and the evidence or design rationale for them.
- A multivalent entry must list its poles and contextual cues; “it means anything” is not a resolution. If a requested unique reading remains undecidable, HOLD that use-case.
- **Several signs may share a domain:** synonymy, redundant insignia, and deity association are not automatic collisions. Require a visible contrast only when a scene or institutional rule depends on distinguishing them.
- **One sign may carry several linked readings:** the required uniqueness is one bounded editorial record per context key, not an exclusive meaning across every use of the sign.
- Record sign bundles separately: bird alone, bird with crown, and bird woven only on an officiant's hem can have different institutional readings.
- Give each exception a mechanism: contextual metonymy, analogy, deliberate inversion, homophony, historical event, contact, factional dispute, or unknown. These are candidate analyses, not universal transformation laws.

| Apparent collision | Diagnostic question | Gate action |
|---|---|---|
| White marks burial and investiture | same community, era, material, occasion, audience? | preserve contextual polysemy if distinction is documented |
| Two gods carry the same animal | shared cult association, lineage claim, borrowing, or contested emblem? | record the relation; do not force exclusive ownership |
| One emblem authorizes incompatible exclusive offices | can an observer distinguish office, carrier, or occasion? | FAIL if the declared exclusivity is genuinely violated |
| A sign's meaning changes after conquest | are validity intervals and surviving old uses recorded? | accept dated drift; flag an unexplained retrojection |
| Dress color is interpreted by a wordplay | does the required pronunciation/reading exist in this stage? | HOLD the derivation until the linguistic link is checked |

- **MC-S01 → FAIL:** the same normalized key has incompatible exclusive defaults and no bounded ambiguity or exception record.
- **MC-S02 → HOLD:** “universal meaning” substitutes for evidence about the relevant community and period.
- **MC-S03 → PASS with exception:** several domains are deliberately condensed in one ritual sign, with documented readings and limits on disambiguation.
- Follow `../myth-symbol/symbolism-synthesis-method.md` for construction records, but do not mistake its designer-side primary-domain discipline for a discovered law of symbolism.

## 7. Reference calibration packet: Archaic/Classical Greek traditions

This is a bounded comparison, not a model of every Greek community. Burkert (1985) and Versnel (2011) supply the religious baseline; do not treat an epic, a city cult, and a philosopher as interchangeable witnesses.

| System | Reference anchor | What the gate should ask |
|---|---|---|
| Language family + writing | Greek is Indo-European; alphabetic Greek in these periods; Bronze Age Linear B is a separate earlier witness | which dialect, script convention, and transmission layer is being compared? Attributing a region's letterforms requires the epichoric-script reference (Jeffery 1990), not a generic “Greek alphabet” |
| Mythic/pantheon core | Olympian gods, heroes, varied local cults; names and cult settings matter (Burkert 1985) | is a literary relationship being mistaken for a universal ritual jurisdiction? |
| Symbolic system | divine attributes and cult settings coexist with complex, context-sensitive accounts of divine power (Versnel 2011) | does a proposed emblem comparison identify its specific object and setting? Iconographic identifications should be sought in LIMC / Digital LIMC before being asserted |
| Era model | modern “Archaic/Classical” labels identify scholarly periods, not one ancient community's cosmic calendar (Judson 2025; Morris 1997) | are narrative age, witness date, and local reckoning kept separate? Civic dating formulas (eponymous archon years, Olympiad reckoning) require Bickerman (1980) |
| Costume/material signature | draped garments conventionally identified as peplos, chiton, and himation; wool/linen distinctions require object-specific evidence | do not reconstruct one timeless “Greek costume” from unlike media; the Met survey essay was consulted 2026-10-17 (peplos: heavy wool, overfold *apoptygma*, shoulder pins; chiton: lighter, usually imported linen, girded), object-level evidence still required per garment |
| Naming practices | Oxford LGPN describes productive combinations of nouns, adjectives, and verbs, with strong local variation | do deity-derived names, name elements, local distributions, and date claims have independent attestations? |

- What a worldbuilder can take: **local cult title + narrative variant + dated material witness**, rather than “one god = one portfolio.” Copy the coupling problem, not a shuffled roster of Greek names.
- Provenance cautions: surviving literary and monumental evidence is uneven, often elite and institutionally selected; silence is not a census of household practice.
- Coverage limit: this packet does not reconstruct particular mystery rites, local dress regulations, or every dialect's sacred naming practice. Each of these needs its own witness-level study; a general reference cannot clear them, so treat any specific reuse as requiring that witness rather than as cleared by this entry.
- This entry's direct tradition coverage outside the Greek example is thin. No assertions about Maya, Yoruba, Indigenous American, Norse, or other traditions should be inferred from the gate's generic categories.
- For any added civilization comparison, supply the same systems, named witnesses, date limits, and access/provenance cautions; do not fill missing chronicle evidence by analogy alone.

## 8. Execution and finding format

1. Freeze the proposed revision and scope; list excluded communities, periods, registers, and inaccessible sources.
2. Read the packet in §2. Missing essential fields produce HOLD, not invented defaults.
3. Run P, M, E, S independently; give each finding a stable ID, assertion pointers, scope, evidence, verdict, and repair options.
4. Compare cross-links: a divine-name change must not silently leave the old pun, garment emblem, or rite chronology active as an exclusive default.
5. Rerun affected checks after edits; preserve variant records and earlier evidence rather than deleting disagreement to obtain PASS.
6. Hand lore-shokunin the findings and options, with a named reviewer and unverified list. Lore chooses acceptance, rejection, or canon interpretation; this lane does not.

Finding record: `ID | revision | P/M/E/S | scoped assertions | expected rule | observed conflict/gap | source status | FAIL/HOLD/PASS | repair options | reviewer`.

- A repair option may split scope, change a proposed rule, add an explicit exception, or seek evidence. Never fabricate a chronicle, etymology, motif code, or ritual to close a finding.
- Re-run stimulus: the reviewer changes the era or locality in a finding; if the supposed contradiction disappears, reassess it as a scope mismatch rather than a universal defect.

## 9. Non-canon review fixtures and calibration limits

These are isolated test assertions, not fictional-world facts or new canon.

| Fixture input | Expected result | Observable reason |
|---|---|---|
| One divine agent handles rain and judgment; proposal permits overlapping roles | P: PASS | coverage does not require separate gods |
| Proposed tale uses a local ID; nearest ATU match is unknown | M: PASS for local coherence; comparison not asserted | absence from an index is not a contradiction |
| Proposal claims a verified official motif code but supplies only an unchecked recollection | M: HOLD / MC-M02 | provenance cannot support the claimed match |
| Local garment needs excluded material with no allowed supply route | E: FAIL / MC-E01 | stated availability and literal manufacture conflict |
| Same sign has different readings in burial and enthronement, each scoped | S: PASS with exception | context keys differ and multivocality is bounded |
| Same sign/office/era/context both grants and forbids exclusive authority without an exception | S: FAIL / MC-S01 | normalized key returns incompatible defaults |

- Calibration: ATU and Thompson are valuable finding aids, not neutral samples of all human narrative. Collection history, translation, genre selection, and European-centered classification affect retrieval.
- Colonial or missionary records can preserve indispensable testimony while imposing alien genres, divine equivalences, or moral categories. Record the mediator and seek local-language/community scholarship; do not simply discard or endorse the record wholesale.
- Campbell-style monomyth checklists, fixed Jungian symbol dictionaries, and universal stage ladders are **not evidentiary gates**. Their use as generators cannot prove historical distribution, chronology, or emic meaning.
- Genealogical, diffusionist, and social-function interpretations require separate arguments; a tidy match is not a demonstration. A missing witness remains unknown, especially where chronicles are scarce.

## 11. What a worldbuilder can take, and provenance cautions

- Take the role–agent map, local narrative IDs plus checked concordances, layered chronology, and context-keyed symbol ledger as a reusable method.
- Make purposeful variation legible: a rival cult, late costume, borrowed epithet, or inverted ritual may pass when its scope and consequences are explicit.
- Do not turn comparison tools into ancestry tests, sparse evidence into universal absence, material motifs into exact dates, or designer-side uniqueness into a claim about real minds.
- Carry unresolved claims as `(to-verify)` with the needed witness and its limits. General references cannot clear an unexamined local claim.
- Deliver methods, reference anchors, findings, and repair options only; world-bible adoption and all decisions about what is true in-world remain with lore-shokunin.
