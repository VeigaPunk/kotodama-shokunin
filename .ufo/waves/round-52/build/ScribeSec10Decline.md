| Decline driver | Mechanism | What a designer decides |
|---|---|---|
| Roads and motor vehicles | the separation that made a long signal worth making disappears; on La Gomera, new roads and paths "removed the necessity and practicality" of the whistle (NEW-ScribeSec10Decline-4) | the terrain where the channel still pays |

**Perception and learning.** Whistled Turkish was comprehended with "symmetric hemispheric contributions", a relative increase in right-hemisphere encoding against the spoken-language asymmetry (NEW-ScribeSec10Decline-6). Untrained listeners still place whistled Spanish vowels into vowel categories above chance: 53% correct for Spanish, 55% for French, 43.5% for Standard Chinese, against 87.5% for a trained La Gomera whistler and a 25% chance floor (NEW-ScribeSec10Decline-7). A designer therefore sets a training curve and a tolerance for beginners, not a switch.

**What a fictional surrogate's history must record.** The year practical use stopped and why (road, telephone, emigration, school language); the decades when the code was heard only at school or on stage; who kept it and who was allowed to learn it; what the revived register actually is (a school register, a stage register, a working register); and how the world's conditions at each date change the channel's reach. Coupling stays one-way: the transmission ladder and its failure modes are owned by `linguistics/language-shift-obsolescence-revival.md:130` and `:142`, the whistle-and-drum register by `linguistics/phonetics-phonology.md:197-199` and `culture-material/music-instrument-lexicon.md:98-100`, the broadcast layer by `linguistics/orality-formulaic-language.md:114` and `:124`, the music hook by `linguistics/sound-symbolism.md:121`. **Typical failure:** a surrogate that survives only because it was taught — every speaker fluent in it, none needing it, and a public that has forgotten the sound it replaced.

## 4. Findings, conflicts, and one mined mistake

1. **The PMID in the brief is wrong.** The brief named the Güntürkün, Güntürkün & Hahn 2015 *Current Biology* paper via PubMed 25732019. That PMID is "Classroom research and child and adolescent development in South America" (Preiss, Calcagni & Grau, *New Directions for Child and Adolescent Development* 147:85–92) — a different paper on a different topic. The correct record for "Whistled Turkish alters language asymmetries" is **PMID 26294179**, opened and quoted above. Recorded as this seat's mined mistake; the L1 should not copy the brief's identifier into any row or lesson.
2. **Live UNESCO ICH element pages answer plain requests with a bot-challenge page** ("What code is in the image?") on this host. Both element pages used here were therefore read through **Wayback captures** (20241226111216id_ and 20231219145618), which QUALITY criterion 10 accepts as an ordinary route. The periodic-report PDF and the BOC page returned content directly. No challenge was met with and routed around inside a control; the challenge page was simply not the source used.
3. **The two UNESCO elements differ in kind, and the section must not blur them.** Silbo Gomero is on the Representative List (inscribed 2009) and its UNESCO text asks for more transmission work; the Turkish whistled language is on the Urgent Safeguarding List (inscribed 2017) and its text names mobile phones as a key threat. Both rows are filed separately with their own scopes.
4. **The revival routes are documented at the level of instruments, not of effect.** The Canarian order and the Türkiye report document curricula, accreditation, course modules and festivals; neither supports a claim that school teaching restored daily use. The drafted row says exactly that ("home transmission — the corpus's own named failure mode", citing `language-shift-obsolescence-revival.md:142`), which is why the section reports the school route as a mechanism to design with, not as a solved case.
5. **Naive-listener evidence is bounded.** The Meyer et al. figures are vowel categorization, not comprehension of whistled messages; the drafted paragraph says "place whistled Spanish vowels into vowel categories" and not "understand whistled speech". The brief's alternative source, "Perception of whistled Turkish by Turkish speakers" (2023), was not opened and is not cited.
6. **The in-corpus partial owners were not restated.** Each of the five cited partial-owner lines is referenced by path and purpose only; §9 adds decline drivers, revival routes, perception/learning and the fictional-history checklist.
