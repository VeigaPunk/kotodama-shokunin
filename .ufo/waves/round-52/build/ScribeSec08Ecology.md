## 7. Ecology and function

| Condition that favours a surrogate | Cited case | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Mountain, ravine and dense-forest terrain | Silbo Gomero, La Gomera (inscribed 2009): a range of "up to five kilometres" with hamlets in the ravines (NEW-ScribeSec08Ecology-1, -2); whistled languages are "mostly used in mountains or dense forests" (NEW-ScribeSec08Ecology-7) | Set the reach budget before the phonology: the distance decides how many contrasts the channel can carry |
| Dispersed settlement | Turkish Black Sea villages, whose houses are "remote and inaccessible from one another" (NEW-ScribeSec08Ecology-4) | Map the settlement scatter first; the channel exists exactly where face-to-face contact fails |
| Herding and farming at distance | Turkish whistling now survives in "a few words spoken by shepherds"; Banda-Linda drums reach people "working their farms" (NEW-ScribeSec08Ecology-4, -6) | Give the channel a working vocabulary (weather, livestock, harvest) before any elegance; the register link itself is owned by phonetics-phonology.md:197-199 |
| Hunting | Drum messages organise "assistance after a hunting accident" and carry instructions when "someone gets lost in the bush" (NEW-ScribeSec08Ecology-5, -6) | Write the emergency set as fixed formulae; a free-text channel fails under panic |
| Courtship and private errands | Kuşköy whistled "akşama bize gel" ("come to our place this evening"), whistled by a girl (NEW-ScribeSec08Ecology-8); routine courtship use (to-verify) | Separate public calls from private ones: the same channel that reaches a village also exposes the sender |
| Announcements and alarms | Turkish whistling carries "warning and SOS or calling for help", for example "There is a forest fire!" (NEW-ScribeSec08Ecology-4) | Fix the alarm set as stock phrases and decide who may send them |
| Ritual and praise | Banda-Linda drums announce "the investiture of a chief" and "the consecration of new drums" (NEW-ScribeSec08Ecology-5) | Tie the surrogate to one rite and one prestige genre; praise poetry itself stays with orality-formulaic-language.md:124 |
| Secrecy from outsiders | Drum tone codes keep their "mysteriousness" for listeners "unacquainted with it" (NEW-ScribeSec08Ecology-6) | Decide the audience: outsiders barred means a restricted code and restricted bearers; outsiders welcome means a small public code |

The typical failure is a surrogate designed for the wrong distance: built for the village it breaks in the ravine, built for the ravine it dies when the settlement concentrates, as the Turkish whistled language lost its daily function once mobile phones arrived (NEW-ScribeSec08Ecology-3, -4).

### The forest drum case, stated precisely

The assignment asked for "one ethnographic source for a forest drum case". The case offered here is the **Banda-Linda drum language of the Central African Republic**, documented by Arom 2007 (row 5) and Cloarec-Heiss 1999 (row 6). The forest grounding is the documented emergency function, not a generalisation about forest peoples: Cloarec-Heiss §2.1.2 lists among the situations that require drum language "accidents (e.g., on hunting expeditions or when someone gets lost in the bush)". No claim is made here that the Banda-Linda live in rainforest, that their drums are the only channel, or that any other forest community uses a comparable system — those would need their own sources.
