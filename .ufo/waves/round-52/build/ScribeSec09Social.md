## 8. Who may speak: social control of the channel

A surrogate is never a neutral wire: in every attested case below the channel is an office, a rule or a school, and the design consequence column is what a designer must fix (design frame, **H**).

| control | attested case | design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| court and royal office | Yorùbá (south-west Nigeria) drumming families carry the hereditary title *baale* or *aare onilu* (chief drummer) in patronage with *oba* (sacred king) families (NEW-ScribeSec09Social-1); in Akan (Ghana) practice a master drummer is "appointed Royal Court Drummer to the high chief of the Aburi-Akuapim" at seventeen (NEW-ScribeSec09Social-2), and Aburi-Akuapem court drummers are described as resisting change of repertoire (NEW-ScribeSec09Social-3) | make the channel an office with a title, a patron and a duty of announcement; an unnamed skill has nobody to answer for it, and a court posting can be lost |
| historian and praise poet | the same Yorùbá offices keep the ruler's lineage history and the praise names of the ruler and his ancestors, delivered in the *oriki* genre, greet visitors at the palace gates and direct the ruler's processions (NEW-ScribeSec09Social-1); the Àyàn lineage of Ọ̀yọ́ heralds the king's arrival and titles its masters *Otun onilu Alaafin* (NEW-ScribeSec09Social-4) | bind the surrogate to a text corpus — genealogies, praise names — and decide who owns that text; praise that nobody may revise is propaganda, not memory |
| gendered or age-restricted use | in Yorùbá drum poetry "no woman is found and even there has not been women apprentice for drumming", while recent formal training has produced women drummers (NEW-ScribeSec09Social-5); the Àyàn line is patrilineal, though female master drummers now exist (NEW-ScribeSec09Social-4); in Kuşköy (Turkey) a woman still learned the whistle language from her grandparents (NEW-ScribeSec09Social-6) | fix the default speaker set; a rule that bars half the speakers is a dated design choice, and lifting it is a historical event, not a correction |
| secrecy and eavesdropping | in Yorùbá practice only initiates may beat the *ìpese* ensemble and only initiates understand it (NEW-ScribeSec09Social-5); the older Yorùbá drums carry restrictions the dùndún does not (NEW-ScribeSec09Social-1); in Kuşköy "The only thing you never whistle is your love talk" because "you'll get caught" (NEW-ScribeSec09Social-6) | design an audience ladder — initiates, elders, office, public — and a content taboo list; since an overheard surrogate is a plot instrument, choose inaudibility deliberately rather than by accident |
| training and apprenticeship | Yorùbá drumming is "a family profession, which requires skill and perfection that cannot be attained without proper tutelage" (NEW-ScribeSec09Social-7); an Àyàn novice works up the whole dùndún drum family before "graduating" to *Ìyáàlù Dùndún* (NEW-ScribeSec09Social-4); La Gomera mandated the Gomeran whistle in primary and secondary schools in 1999 and requires accredited teachers (NEW-ScribeSec09Social-8) | choose the transmission mode — household, guild, school or state — because that choice decides whether the channel outlives one generation |

Status: the case column rests on the eight sources opened on 2026-10-22 (source tiers B, and A for the ministry record); the design-consequence column is the lane's heuristic (**H**, `../verification/calibration-registry.md:35-39`). The drum-and-whistle register couplings stay with `phonetics-phonology.md:197-199`, `../culture-material/music-instrument-lexicon.md:98-100`, `orality-formulaic-language.md:114,:124` and `sound-symbolism.md:121`; this section owns who holds the channel, not what the channel encodes.
