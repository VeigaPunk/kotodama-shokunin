## Status of the calibration item: contested, and the brief's date is wrong

**Correction to the brief.** There is no joint Witton and Hing paper of 2016. The joint critique is
**2024**, *Interdisciplinary Science Reviews*, DOI `10.1177/03080188241255543`. The paper itself
says what 2016 refers to: "Here, building on arguments outlined by Witton (2016), we apply this
analytical approach to Mayor's Protoceratops–griffin geomyth" (NEW-ScoutGriffinFossil-2, Introduction,
paragraph beginning "In this spirit"). So 2016 is an earlier solo Witton argument, cited by the joint
paper, not the calibration source. Anyone writing "Witton and Hing 2016" into a registry row would
be writing a citation that does not exist.

**What each side states, in its own text.**

- The publisher, on the book it sells, asserts the derivation without hedge: the legend "sprang from
  tales first told by Scythian gold-miners", and Mayor "convincingly shows that many of the giants
  and monsters of myth did have a basis in fact" (NEW-ScoutGriffinFossil-1, publisher's description,
  first paragraph).
- The authors of the critique call the same link "uncompelling", say it ignores the established
  history of griffin art, and file it as an "ex post facto geomyth" (NEW-ScoutGriffinFossil-2).
- Neither opened page records a reply by Mayor, and I did not open her book, so I cannot state her
  answer. What I can state is that the book remains in print from Princeton University Press with the
  publisher's unhedged description attached (NEW-ScoutGriffinFossil-1).

For the entry this is the usable shape of "contested": the dispute is not about whether griffins
existed but about **what kind of inference an origin story is allowed to be**, and the critique's own
test — geography, art history, textual reference, anatomy, materiality — is a portable method for a
worldbuilder inventing a monster origin.

**Caveat the L1 must not miss.** The paper is internally inconsistent about the date of its oldest
image: the body says "oldest griffin-like imagery of 4th millennium BCE Asia and Egypt", while the
Figure 2 caption for the same image says "one of the oldest known griffin artworks, from 4th century
BCE Mesopotamia" (both read; locations in NEW-ScoutGriffinFossil-2's locators). Do not let an entry
sentence carry a precise date from the caption. Say "fourth millennium BCE" and attribute it, or say
"one of the oldest" without a date.

**Gap this pair cannot close.** Neither page glosses what `gryps` means or gives its etymology. An
entry sentence about what the word means needs a different row (a lexicon or the classical passage in
translation). The paper quotes Herodotus 3.116 and 4.27 in Godley's 1920 translation, so the entry can
still cite the primary text directly at tier A from Perseus or LacusCurtius.
