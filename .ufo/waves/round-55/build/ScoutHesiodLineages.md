- Heroes close the list — Heracles with Iolaus "destroyed with the unpitying sword" the Hydra that Hera had nursed — so the genealogy doubles as a deed-list, and a worldbuilder must decide whether monsters exist to be killed or to be avoided (NEW-ScoutHesiodLineages-1, lines 315–318).
- The catalogue ends with a colophon, "This is the offspring of Ceto and Phorcys", so the record's form is a family register, not a bestiary entry with habitat and remedy (NEW-ScoutHesiodLineages-1, lines 335–336).

draft sentences:
- The Greek makes the name public property: "τὰς δὴ Γραίας καλέουσιν" has gods and men as its subjects, so a culture can treat a monster's name as ordinary common speech rather than as a secret or a taboo (NEW-ScoutHesiodLineages-2, lines 271–272).

- Her body is stated as a formula, "ἥμισυ μὲν νύμφην … ἥμισυ δ᾽ αὖτε πέλωρον ὄφιν", which names the halves by kind and lets the category word cover a part as well as a whole (NEW-ScoutHesiodLineages-2, lines 298–299).
- The Chimera is built by position, "πρόσθε λέων, ὄπιθεν δὲ δράκων, μέσση δὲ χίμαιρα", so the poem offers a slot grammar — front, middle, rear — that a worldbuilder can reuse for any three-part being (NEW-ScoutHesiodLineages-2, line 323).
- Typhon's parentage is reported with φασι, "they say", so the poem marks one link as hearsay; a worldbuilder deciding how authoritative its own monster catalogue is can copy that hedge or refuse it (NEW-ScoutHesiodLineages-2, line 306).
