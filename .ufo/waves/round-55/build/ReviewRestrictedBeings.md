## Rules the L1 must apply to the restricted-beings section

**What the entry MAY say.**

1. Name the rule, never the content. Where a community has published a rule, the rule is the
   whole of what a world document may take (precedent: `culture-material/masks-masquerade.md:69`).
   State the rule's existence, holder and scope exactly as published, cite it, and stop —
   NEW-ReviewRestrictedBeings-2 is the model form ("certain restrictions and so this is as much
   as we can say").
2. Attribute to named nations and named places, never to "Native American folklore" or an
   "Aboriginal" generic. Ngāti Naho and Karutahi, Diné and witchery, Aṉangu and Tjukurpa —
   the being's name, community and place are part of the claim (NEW-ReviewRestrictedBeings-3, -4).
3. Record the public/public-reported norm as a fact about circulation, with its speaker
   (NEW-ReviewRestrictedBeings-1, Denetdale and Yazzie as named Diné voices).
4. Treat place-tied guardian beings as legal actors: the Ngāti Naho taniwha case ran inside an
   ordinary highway-planning process and the Aṉangu climb closure became EPBC-Act enforceable
   (NEW-ReviewRestrictedBeings-3, -4). This is the section's usable design lever: an access-status
   field, a holder-of-authority field, and a place-field on the bestiary record — not a list of
   "banned monsters".
5. Reuse the corpus's own access ledger: `civilizations/algonquian.md:189` already prescribes a
   `feature | source/community/date | public/restricted/unknown | invention boundary` ledger; the
   entry should point to that column vocabulary rather than invent a second one.
6. Point one-way to partial owners without restating their registers (claim review-only clause):
   `civilizations/dine-navajo.md:15,68,99` (public accessibility is not permission; no chantway or
   being rosters; never instructions for invoking beings), `civilizations/algonquian.md:17,196`
   (living communities remain authorities; unknown access stays unknown, not "open"),
   `civilizations/australia-aboriginal.md:45,126-128` (do not copy a local ancestral being into a
   fantasy bestiary; do not recover withheld detail), `civilizations/maori.md:109-111` (tapu/noa,
   kaitiakitanga as relationship, not "environmentalism"), `civilizations/haudenosaunee.md:132-141`
   (False Face boundary table), `civilizations/hopi.md` (katsina permissions), `civilizations/igbo.md`
   (mmanwụ restricted associations), `synthesis/source-discipline-calibration.md:191-192` (do not
   export registry labels into those traditions; record named positions, not a single verdict).

**What the entry MUST NOT do.**

1. MUST NOT cite S265 or S266. The RANGE leg requires every cited S-id ≥ S285; citing the
   Haudenosaunee medicine-mask policy or the Hopi CPO row would fail the claim outright.
   Reach those policies through in-corpus path pointers (`haudenosaunee.md:139`, masks entry §4),
   which carry no S-id.
2. MUST NOT narrate the restricted content: no anatomy, no ceremonial sequence, no name-calling
   procedure, no "how to defeat/cure" mechanics, no image description for any being under a
   published or insider-stated restriction.
3. MUST NOT launder an outsider depiction into the tradition: horror-film, creepypasta, comic or
   television windigo/skinwalker forms (antlers, lair-haunting cryptid, "skinwalker ranch") are
   pop-mythography and must be flagged as such on first use (QUALITY 2), with
   NEW-ReviewRestrictedBeings-5 as the harm citation.
4. MUST NOT present "Windigo Psychosis" as a real diagnosis: it is a discredited/contested
   psychiatric category documented by NEW-ReviewRestrictedBeings-5 (p. 109); state it only as
   such (QUALITY 2 status naming).
5. MUST NOT write a bypass recipe: no "ask respectfully and you may learn it" procedure, no
   altered-detail workaround (precedent `dine-navajo.md:95`); the honest move for a worldbuilder
   is to design an original being, not to file the real one under a changed name.
6. MUST NOT turn restriction into exoticism: no "swamp creature" framing, no "forbidden secrets
   list"; a restricted being is a holder of community authority, not forbidden loot.
   (The BBC-style headline for the Ngāti Naho case — "Maori swamp creature delays road" — is the
   register to avoid; unopened, noted below.)
7. MUST NOT declare canon: the section hands lore-shokunin the access-status and holder fields
   plus these pointers; it decides nothing about what any fictional world contains (QUALITY 5,
   `synthesis/worldbible-integration-contract.md` owns the handoff format).
8. MUST NOT write "the community forbids X" where the evidence is a reported account: Te Ara is
   tier B for iwi positions; write "reported" at that strength (QUALITY 6).
9. MUST NOT write an S-id range (e.g. "(S303–S304)") — the RANGE leg's RED-RANGE-SPAN control
   rejects it; cite rows singly.

## Calibration notes

- The section's status axis (QUALITY 2) is public/restricted/unknown, not real/fake — mark
  circulation status, never truth value.
- "Monster" is an etic bucket; for a community's being, name what the community names it
  (taniwha as kaitiaki, windigo as the Algonquian tradition's own term) before any "monster" gloss.
- Windigo spellings differ by nation (NEW-ReviewRestrictedBeings-5, p. 104: witiko/wihtikow Cree,
  wintiko/wiindiigo Ojibwe); prefer the source-community spelling, mirroring the corpus
  Tjukurpa/Tjukurrpa/Jukurrpa rule (`australia-aboriginal.md:38`).
