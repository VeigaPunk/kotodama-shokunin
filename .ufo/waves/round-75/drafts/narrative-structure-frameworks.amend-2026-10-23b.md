## 1. The 1966 article names three levels, and credits each

The enumeration is one sentence: "On propose de distinguer dans l'œuvre narrative trois niveaux de description" — 11 words, printed p. 6 (S885). The three are then named with their owners: "le niveau des « fonctions » (au sens que ce mot a chez Propp et chez Bremond)" — 17 words — "le niveau des « actions » (au sens que ce mot a chez Greimas lorsqu'il parle des personnages comme d'actants)" — 20 words — and "le niveau de la « narration » (qui est, en gros, le niveau du « discours » chez Todorov)" — 19 words, all on the same page (S885).

Two consequences follow, and both correct the staged text this draft replaces. First, the article names three levels, not four; the four-item list that circulates in the literature is the article's classes of *one* level, which is the subject of §2. Second, the article presents the list as provisional: the same page calls the proposed levels "un profil provisoire, dont l'avantage est encore presque exclusivement didactique" (S885).

## 2. The functional level divides into four classes

The classes are stated in one sentence on printed p. 11: "Noyaux et catalyses, indices et informants (encore une fois peu importe les noms), telles sont" — 15 words — "les premières classes entre lesquelles on peut répartir les unités du niveau fonctionnel" — 13 words (S885). The parenthesis matters: the names are offered as labels, not as doctrine.

The page also draws the distinction inside the second pair: "Les indices impliquent une activité de déchiffrement" — 7 words — while "les informants apportent une connaissance toute faite" — 7 words — and the informant is characterized as "un opérateur réaliste", which the sentence grounds with an example: "l'informant (par exemple l'âge précis d'un personnage) sert à authentifier la réalité du réfèrent" — 14 words (S885).

The rule a plot specification needs is on that page too: "une unité peut appartenir en même temps à deux classes différentes" — 11 words, with the whisky-in-an-airport-hall example following it (S885). The mixed unit is the normal case, not an anomaly.

The noyau/catalyse criterion is not on p. 11 — the page opens after it — but on printed p. 10, in one sentence: "Disons qu'on ne peut supprimer un noyau sans altérer l'histoire, mais qu'on ne peut non plus supprimer une catalyse sans altérer le discours." — 23 words (S885). That is the test, and it is a deletion test.

## 3. The five codes are named on a reading sheet of *S/Z*, not on the 1966 pages read here

The document reached through the Geneva bibliography is labelled on its own page: "Fiche de lecture", signed "(DKW, mai 2000)", on *S/Z* (S891). It is a reading sheet about the 1970 book, not an excerpt of it, so the staged description of it as "the accessible French excerpt of the primary text" is not what the page supports (S891).

The sheet enumerates: "cinq grands codes que vont rejoindre tous les signifiés du texte" — 11 words — and then names them, glossing each: "le code herméneutique (l'énigme à déchiffrer)", "le code sémique (champ thématique)", "le code symbolique (l'antithèse)", "le code proaïrétique (code des actions et des comportements)", "le code gnomique (sagesse, savoir, code culturel)" (S891).

Two label corrections follow, both visible on the quoted page. The staged English list said "semantic" where the sheet says *sémique*, and "cultural" where the sheet says *gnomique*, with "code culturel" as its gloss (S891). And the staged claim that the codes are "a set of simultaneous voices" is not what the sheet says: it says they are "le lieu de disparition des voix dont est tissé le texte", the place where the voices disappear, not a chorus of them (S891).

## 4. A peer states the relation, and states it as partial

The relation is put on the record by a semiotician: "ces cinq codes sont en partie une reformulation des quatre niveaux du récit individués dans l'« Introduction à L'analyse structurale des récits » (1966) : fonctions, actions, indices et informants" — 30 words, printed p. 58 (S892). The word that carries the claim is *en partie*.

The same passage states which code does which work: "Le code proaïretique est en quelque sorte une synthèse des fonctions et actions" — 13 words, same page (S892). So the proairetic code is not a fifth level; it is a synthesis at the level of the first two.

The peer's own enumeration names the five under two different labels than the reading sheet's: "proaïretique, sémantique, culturel, herméneutique" — 4 words — with the fifth broken across a line in the PDF text layer as "symbo- lique" (S892). That is why this entry keeps both spellings and claims only the five-part structure.

The peer's own four-item list compresses the article's two lists into one: fonctions, actions, indices and informants are, in the 1966 text, three levels plus four classes, as §1 and §2 above show (S885). The compression is the peer's own, and it is why this entry states the relation as partial rather than as a renaming.

## 5. What the lane does with it

Attribute the five codes to *S/Z* and the levels to the 1966 article, and never to the same list: the 1966 article carries three levels and four classes, and no five-code enumeration was located on any page read for this file (S885).

Specify a plot against the four classes before adding detail: the deletion test separates what the story cannot lose from what the discourse cannot lose, and a unit that passes both tests is the mixed case the article says is normal (S885).

Treat the code vocabulary as a checklist of simultaneous registers rather than a sequence: the relation the peer states runs from the levels to the codes, and the proairetic code folds two of the levels together instead of adding one (S892).
