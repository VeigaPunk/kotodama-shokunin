## 1. Todorov: three categories, and time as the seam

The page opens mid-sentence on the three axes of the literary narrative: the time of the narrative, "où s'exprime le rapport entre le temps de l'histoire et celui du discours" — 13 words, printed p. 139 (S886). The other two are named in the same sentence: the aspects, "la manière dont l'histoire est perçue par le narrateur", and the modes, which depend on the type of discourse the narrator uses to make the story known (S886).

The time problem is stated as a dissimilarity between two temporalities: "Le temps du discours est, dans un certain sens, un temps linéaire, alors que le temps de l'histoire est pluridimensionnel." — 20 words, same page (S886). From it Todorov derives the necessity of breaking the "natural" succession of events, and says the author usually does not try to restore it but uses temporal deformation for aesthetic ends (S886).

His historical claim, in full: "Les formalistes russes voyaient dans la déformation temporelle le seul trait du discours qui le distingue de l'histoire" — 18 words, p. 139 (S886). The paragraph that closes the Vygotsky quotation draws the consequence: "la nature des événements compte peu, seule importe la relation qu'ils entretiennent" — 12 words, same page (S886). That is the sharpest design rule this amendment carries: a plot is an order of relations, not an inventory of events.

Calibration: what the page attributes to the Formalists, Todorov adopts and illustrates with Vygotsky's 1925 *Psychologie de l'art*, quoted at length on the same page (the transcription renders the title as "Psychologie de Vart") (S886). The claim is a lineage statement made in 1966, not a survey of what the Formalists held; the entry cites it as Todorov's account.

The page after carries the forms: "L'enchaînement consiste simplement à juxtaposer différentes histoires" — 7 words, p. 140 — and "L'enchâssement, c'est l'inclusion d'une histoire à l'intérieur d'une autre" — 9 words, same page (S886). A third form is named there too (the transcription mangles the word as "Y alternance"), and the same page runs the detective-novel example: the puzzle novel starts from an end and arrives at a beginning, the noir reports threats first and corpses last (S886).

The aspects are the perception axis: "l'aspect reflète la relation entre un il (dans l'histoire) et un je (dans le discours), entre le personnage et le narrateur" — 21 words, p. 141, where the classification is credited to Pouillon and adopted "avec des modifications mineures" (S886).

## 2. Greimas: one message, two isotopies, and an equivalence to establish

Greimas starts from Lévi-Strauss's three components of myth — *armature*, *code*, *message* — and asks what each contributes to an interpretation (S887, p. 29). The unit he interprets is the narrative as such: "Le récit, pour avoir un sens, doit être un tout de signification et se présente, de ce fait, comme une structure sémantique simple." — 23 words, p. 29 (S887).

The message is where the two-reading claim sits. The transcription carries it in two pieces, because it breaks the word *isotopies* across a line: the message "se situe, lui aussi, sur deux iso- topies à la fois" — 11 words, p. 30 — and it "donne lieu à deux lectures différentes, l'une sur le plan discursif, l'autre, sur le plan structurel" — 16 words, same page (S887). The definition he gives on the same page is the one to design against: "par isotopie nous entendons un ensemble redondant de catégories sémantiques qui rend possible la lecture uniforme du récit" — 18 words (S887).

What the description then has to do is not to choose between the readings but to line them up: the problem "est celui de l'équivalence à établir entre les lexemes et les énoncés constitutifs des séquences narratives et les articulations structurelles des contenus qui leur correspondent" — 22 words, p. 31 (S887).

And the page qualifies its own model, which is why the entry does not repeat the staged phrase "the two must be set equivalent" as an identity: "Une telle conception du message qui serait lisible sur les deux isotopies distinctes et dont la première ne serait que la manifestation discursive de la seconde, n'est peut-être qu'une formulation théorique" — 31 words, p. 31 (S887). It may hold for a sub-class, folk tales; myths are instead marked by "l'enchevêtrement, dans une seule narration, des séquences situées tantôt sur l'une, tantôt sur l'autre des deux isotopies" — 17 words, same page (S887).

## 3. Barthes: levels of description, and two readings of the same units

The concept the 1966 article installs is stated in one sentence: "Ce concept est celui de niveau de description" — 8 words, p. 5 (S885). The levels stand in a hierarchical relation, and the rule that follows is the load-bearing one: "aucun niveau ne peut à lui seul produire du sens" — 10 words, same page — because "toute unité qui appartient à un certain niveau ne prend de sens que si elle peut s'intégrer dans un niveau supérieur" — 21 words (S885).

The consequence for reading is on the same page: understanding a narrative means "projeter les enchaînements horizontaux du « fil » narratif sur un axe implicitement vertical" — 14 words (S885). The closing page names the two readings exactly: "la dystaxie oriente une lecture « horizontale », mais l'intégration lui superpose une lecture « verticale »" — 17 words, p. 26 (S885). And the double membership that makes the two readings necessary is stated there too: "très souvent une même unité peut avoir deux corrélats, l'un sur un niveau (fonction d'une séquence) l'autre sur un autre (indice renvoyant à un actant)" — 25 words (S885).

The article credits its own borrowings on p. 5, which is what lets this entry place it in a set rather than in a vacuum: the theory of levels is attributed to Benveniste, the mythème to Lévi-Strauss, and Todorov is named for two grand levels, "histoire" and "discourse", the latter carrying "les temps, les aspects et les modes du récit" (S885). So two of this amendment's three articles meet on the page being quoted, and the third is cited there as the source of the aspects-and-modes list (S885).

## 4. What the three articles do together, and where they differ

They are three cuts of one problem, and the cuts are not interchangeable.

Todorov's seam runs between story and discourse in time (S886).

Greimas's runs between a discursive plane and a structural plane of content, with a stated equivalence to build between them (S887).

Barthes's runs between levels of description, each level integrating units from the one below (S885).

The practical rule that follows for a world bible: fixing one axis fixes nothing about the others. A culture's story can be specified for its temporal deformation and still have no stated content structure, and vice versa; the two failures look identical in a draft and are repaired differently.

The other rule is a warning about attribution. Greimas's own page states the debt rather than hiding it: the three components are Lévi-Strauss's, and the armature definition is offered as not contradicting "la formule générale du mythe proposée naguère par Lévi-Strauss" (S887). Flattening the three articles into "structuralism says" loses exactly the disagreements — the myth interleaving against the neat two-plane reading is one of them — that a designer needs when choosing which frame to copy. That is this entry's reading of the three texts, not a claim any of them makes.
