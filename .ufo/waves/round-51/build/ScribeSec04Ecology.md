     should be classified as potentially endangered."

```
## 4. Community ecology and transmission

A signing community forms where deafness is concentrated and interaction is daily: a village with hereditary deafness, a residential school, or a Deaf family. Design these six conditions as dials:

| Condition | Attested case | Worldbuilding lever |
|---|---|---|
| High incidence, hereditary deafness | Adamorobe >10% in 1961, ~2% by 2001 (NEW-01); Bengkala's recessive gene, ≥7 generations (NEW-03); Martha's Vineyard (NEW-02) | village signing emerges at high rates; mobility dilutes them |
| Shared village signing | Martha's Vineyard hearing and Deaf grew up signing together (NEW-02); Adamorobe hearing villagers sign, fluency tracks contact (NEW-01); Bengkala ~1,200 hearing signers (NEW-03) | a bilingual village, not a separate Deaf world |
| Schools as transmission sites | most Deaf children learn their sign language only after entering school; ~5% are born to Deaf parents (NEW-01) | a school generation can shift the language (AdaSL → GSL) |
| Deaf-of-deaf families | family transmission is the minority route (NEW-01); Adamorobe's former chief barred deaf-deaf marriage (NEW-01) | a founder-family effect powers the founding myth, not the main channel |
| Attitudes | oralism spread after the 1880 Milan congress (to-verify) — history, not law | the hearing majority's ideology sets status and schooling choices |
| Endangerment of small sign languages | AdaSL potentially endangered (NEW-01); Kata Kolok threatened by Indonesian Sign Language shift and marriage-pattern change (NEW-03) | pressure points: school generation, prestige sign, demographics |
```
