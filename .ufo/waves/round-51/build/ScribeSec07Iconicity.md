```
## 7. Iconicity and arbitrariness

Iconicity in sign is **patterned**, not pantomime: signers organize iconic form into lexical strategies whose distribution differs even between unrelated sign languages (NEW-KotodamaR51a3.ScribeSec07Iconicity-1). Design rule: pick a strategy — handling, instrument, or shape — by what the culture treats as salient, then expect conventionalization: historically, ASL signs tend to drift toward arbitrariness (NEW-KotodamaR51a3.ScribeSec07Iconicity-2), and every iconic form is motivated but not determined (NEW-KotodamaR51a3.ScribeSec07Iconicity-3) — a young sign language reads more iconic than an old one.

| Strategy | What the form depicts | Design use |
|---|---|---|
| Handling | the hand holding or manipulating the tool (NEW-KotodamaR51a3.ScribeSec07Iconicity-1) | action/verb scenes; agent focus |
| Instrument | the handshape stands for the tool's own shape (NEW-KotodamaR51a3.ScribeSec07Iconicity-1) | noun lexicon; tool categories |
| Shape | the hands trace the referent's outline — ASL, Danish and Chinese TREE are equally iconic yet trace differently (NEW-KotodamaR51a3.ScribeSec07Iconicity-3) | depicting constructions; spatial layout |

Refuse the myths: sign is not mime — iconic forms are conventional, standard lexicon (NEW-KotodamaR51a3.ScribeSec07Iconicity-3) — and there is no universal sign: strategy distributions differ across three unrelated sign languages (NEW-KotodamaR51a3.ScribeSec07Iconicity-1). Build the manual modality like any conlang; the spoken-side three-way map (arbitrary/iconic/systematic) stays in `sound-symbolism.md:32`, its signed-channel note at `sound-symbolism.md:124`.
```
