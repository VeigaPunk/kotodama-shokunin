```
## 1. The register property: a sign language is a full language in the visual-manual modality

**Master claim.** A sign language is a full language in the visual-manual modality: it has its own lexicon and grammar, is acquired natively by deaf children of signing communities, and does not derive from any spoken language (NEW-1; NEW-3). Stokoe's 1960 analysis first brought "within the purview of linguistics a virtually unknown language" and showed signs compose from a finite inventory of form aspects — tabula (location), designator (handshape), signification (movement) — as phonemes compose words (NEW-1). Four load-bearing choices, in order:

1. **Modality** — manual and non-manual articulators (hands, face, gaze, body) carry the linguistic load; the channel sets the craft constraints, not the grammar's depth.
2. **Sublexical structure** — meaningless form units combine into meaningful signs; without this duality there is only a repertoire (NEW-1; NEW-3).
3. **Community transmission** — the language lives in deaf and mixed communities that transmit it to children, including emergent village/shared sign systems (NEW-1; NEW-2).
4. **Independence from speech** — grammar is invented in the modality, never translated from a spoken language's morphology; codes that graft onto a spoken language fail the test (NEW-1).

| Test | Pass when | Fails as (owner) |
|---|---|---|
| **Productive grammar** | morphemes combine by regular rules; new complex signs form freely | an emblem repertoire: fixed gestural glosses with dictionary meanings (`adornment-body-codes.md:79`) |
| **Natively acquired** | deaf children acquire the system as a first language from signing adults | a manual code of a spoken language: word-order-signed systems with no generation of native signers (NEW-1) |
| **Primary medium** | carries the full communicative load face-to-face, unsupported | a writing system: a secondary notation of a language (`writing-systems.md:11`) |

Iconicity, simultaneity and spatial grammar are resources of the modality, not signs of deficiency — a worldbuilder writes the register's grammar first, then lets gesture borrow from it, never the reverse (NEW-3). Terminology: signers, deaf or Deaf community, sign language — never "deaf-mute" or "hearing-impaired" as labels (lane rule).
```
