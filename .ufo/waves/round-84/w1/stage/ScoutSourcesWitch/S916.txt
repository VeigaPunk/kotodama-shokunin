2. WHAT THE ROW CAN AND CANNOT TEACH
   The Bull (Summis desiderantes affectibus, 9 December 1484) is the constitutive document: it recites the
   alleged crimes and their vocabulary, delegates coercive jurisdiction, removes appeal, and orders the
   Bishop of Strasbourg to publish it. That covers the accusation grammar, the vocabulary set, and the
   enforcement leg of the institution. It does not contain an evidentiary rule.
   The root page's Part III contents is a complete procedural schema in outline: 35 questions under three
   heads, the judge's "Eleven Actions", pages 194-271 — initiating a process, the number of witnesses,
   whether mortal enemies may testify, imprisonment, the place of detention and torture, the several forms of
   sentence, appeal. That covers "the proof technology" at the level of sequence and page, with no text.
   The missing piece is the same one the round needs most: the Malleus' own interrogation-and-confession text.
   It is not on this site. Supply it from S914 A2 (which names the Bull and states the torture-to-confession
   mechanism) rather than pretending the Malleus text is present.

4. ACCESS LIMITS AND CAVEATS
   — The root URL is a cover page: 2174 words, no body prose, body subpages 404. Any citation of the root as
     "the Malleus" is unsupportable; cite the Bull for text and the root for the contents schema.
   — The Bull is Summers' abridgement and prints literal ellipses at three points: "... as well as animals of
     …", "... without let…", "Non obstantibus … Let no man therefore …". A quote that crosses one of those
     marks is not a continuous span. The excerpts above stop before every ellipsis.
   — The Bull page has NO Wayback capture. Do not claim one. The root's only capture (2024-12-22) is a
     compressed 21515-byte record and pre-dates the 2025 revision; it cannot corroborate the live bytes.
   — The proofread pages for the Bull are level 3 ("proofread, but not validated against the page image" in
     Wikisource's scale). For a document this short and this heavily transcribed elsewhere the risk is low,
     but the row should not claim scan validation.
   — Parsoid injects unexpanded template payloads into the rendered HTML at the <pages> transclusion point,
     so a naive stripper emits JSON around the Bull text; strip payload spans or read the three Page:
     subpages (Page:Malleus maleficarum translated by Montague Summers (1928).djvu/52, /53, /54).
