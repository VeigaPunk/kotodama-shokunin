2. WHAT IT TEACHES (for the entry's scopes)
   The article is the entry's spine for four of the six scopes, and it is the only one of the four rows that
   states the institutional thesis in one sentence.
   — vocabulary set: hexe, lamia, striga, Unholde, malefica, incubus/succubus, sortiariae, "white witchcraft",
     "black magic", the "evil eye", the "ligature" (impotentia ex maleficio) — all defined in place.
   — accusation grammar: "From the 7th century onwards women and priests figure largely in the accusations of
     witchcraft"; the three-period scheme (to 1230 doubt; 1230-1430 scholastic fusion; 1430 onward persecution)
     and the sentence that the modern idea is "largely a learned product".
   — proof technology + confession: the Malleus passage below (questions under torture, admissions "described
     as voluntary"); the trial-by-water and witch-pricking list for England; "the valueless declaration of the
     victims of torture".
   — slander law: the church/secular split ("a canonical sin, or superstition" against "a violation of the civil
     rights of others"), and the note that the crime "is unimportant save where maleficium is combined with
     treason".
   — social functions: the estimate of victims (100,000 to several millions), the Inquisition's role, and the
     rise of the critical spirit (Weier, Scot, Bekker, Spee).
   It does NOT give: a single trial transcript, any statute text, or any accusatory formula in the accusers'
   own words. Those come from S916 (the Bull) and S917 (Upham's records).
