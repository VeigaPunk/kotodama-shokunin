Note that the end of the word “Heaven” and the entire word “Earth” are placed in square brackets to indicate that they are modern restorations of parts of the text that are no longer legible. The restoration of the word “Heaven” is extremely secure: the text preserves the letters שמי, the first letters of the Aramaic word for “Heaven” (cognate to Hebrew שמים [ shamayim ]), so that only the last letter is missing. Based on that, restoration of the word “Earth” is very reasonable, because it forms a natural pair for “Heaven.” The presence of another pair, “day and night,” in the preserved text that follows further supports this. The name “Esarhaddon’s Succession Treaty” is assigned to the text in the edition by Simo Parpola and Kazuko Watanabe, Neo-Assyrian Treaties and Loyalty Oaths, State Archives of Assyria 2 (Helsinki: Helsinki University Press, 1988), 28–58 (No. 6). This designation best reflects the text’s purpose. The first edition of the text used the name “The Vassal-Treaties of Esarhaddon,” because all the copies name foreign, conquered vassal governors as parties to the treaties. See D. J. Wiseman, “The Vassal-Treaties of Esarhaddon,” Iraq 20 (1958): 27. However, the language of the treaties makes it clear that the entire Assyrian population, and not just the vassals, were subject to similar terms.
