articleBody"> THE LITERARY ASSEVERATION IN IRISH Liam P. Ó MURCHÚ In Éigsev ol. XVII (1977-8) 237-64 I published a series of three poems which I termed dearbhaithe or asseverations, the social and cultural context of which is worth investigating. The poems were composed around the year 1781 by two Co. Clare poets Tomás Ó Míocháin and Séamas Mac Consaidin. The poets in these compositions purport to act as protagonists for the three people for whom they wrote them -a married couple and a third party, Doiminic do Búrc, who swears that he has not had a liaison with the married woman, Murainn Ni Aodha, the wife of one Siomon Mac Ceorais. The latter, in nine stanzas, counters that he is painfully aware of his wife’s entanglement with do Búrc, and finally, through the words of the poet, the accused adventuress, Murainn Ni Aodha, swears that no adultery occurred between her and do Búrc, in terms, however, that indicate a greater anxiety to clear do Búrc’s name than to establish her own innocence. In a note that Eugene O’Curry wrote some 70 years after the composition of the poems, he confirms the historicity of the parties involved in the contention : ‘the above humorous, but vulgar piece’, he says referring to the last piece in the series, ‘as well as the other two which precede here a few pages, were written about the year 1781. Doiminick Burke was a horse jockey, blind of an eye; and Fergus Mac Feoras, or Birmingham, was the keeper of a Shebeen House at Belatha near Dunbeg on the Malbay Coast, Co. Clare. Eugene O’Curry in a great hurry, 1851’. (Maynooth Mss. C 15, 204). In my edition I referred to some other examples of the literary asseveration : two are Ulster compositions which Breandán Ó Buachalla included in his edition of the poems of Cathal Bui Mac Giolla Gunna, but, as Professor O Buachalla points out, internal evidence would indicate that they were composed in the second half of the 18th century when the poets to whom they are ascribed in the manuscripts were no longer living. A Co. Cork example of the literary asseveration can be pinned down in time and place with some precision : it was composed in Kanturk between the years 1772-80 (23 I 26, 168).
