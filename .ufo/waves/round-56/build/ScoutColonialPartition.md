# ScoutColonialPartition — the end of the Kongo kingdom as an era boundary (claim C, source seat)

Round 56, 2026-10-22. Leaf seat; no subagents, no background jobs, no installs (curl, pdftotext and python3 were already present; nothing installed or upgraded). Five rows opened by ordinary means. The question: how the Kongo kingdom ended as a political era — the 1884-85 Berlin framework, the three-way colonial division, the kings under Portuguese suzerainty, and the 1913-14 revolt and suppression of the kingship. Rows 1-4 carry the core sequence (clientage, revolt, suppression, titular revival); row 5 carries the Berlin mechanics and the correction that the conference did not itself partition Africa.

teaches: Royal legitimacy in the late Kongo kingdom rested on reciprocity: a king installed with Portuguese support in 1911 was toppled by his chiefs after forced labour broke that contract.
draft sentences:
- Make the king a client before he is a subject: Pedro V sought Portuguese troops in a succession dispute in 1860, and the resulting clientage was consolidated when official colonial rule was established in 1888. (NEW-ScoutColonialPartition-1, §1)
- Let royal legitimacy run through the chiefs: the December 1913 rising was a rebellion against King Manuel Kiditu, blamed for the loss of peace, while the few white officials in the capital were not treated as important targets. (NEW-ScoutColonialPartition-1, §22)
- Give the recognised chiefs an office: about fifty chiefs had been formally recognised in 1912 as primeiro chefe (mfumu antete) to collect taxes and raise labour, and they used that standing to refuse the quotas. (NEW-ScoutColonialPartition-1, §25)
- Date the attack on the sacred capital: on the morning of 10 December 1913 Álvaro Buta and his allies launched the first large-scale assault on São Salvador in more than half a century. (NEW-ScoutColonialPartition-1, §27)
- Stage the succession as a negotiation: after Pedro Mbemba's death the chiefs wanted a king who could read and write and negotiate with the Portuguese, and they installed the mission-educated Manuel Kiditu in 1911. (NEW-ScoutColonialPartition-1, §32)
- Keep the labour machinery concrete: recruitment for Cabinda's plantations was the underlying cause of the 1913 rising, and the hut tax pushed men into contracts. (NEW-ScoutColonialPartition-1, §7)

## Observed, inference, gaps

Observed (page-level, all five rows):
- The kingdom's dependence on Portugal is dated to 1860 in two independent rows: Pedro V's request for troops in a succession dispute and the clientage that followed (row 1, §1), and a Portuguese military occupation of the court with an imposed vassalage treaty (row 2, abstract paragraphs "Notre thèse" and "Certes").
- Row 1 dates the consolidation of "official colonial rule" to 1888; row 4 dates official colonization of Angola to 1885. The two dates mark different things in the sources (a client relation hardened, a colonial map drawn) and should not be merged into one event in the entry.
- Row 4 states the three-way division of Kongo society after 1885 in one sentence: Mbanza Kongo in the Portuguese colony, part of its founding population in the Belgian Congo, another part in the French colony. It says "Congo belga", the post-1908 name; row 5 gives the 15 November 1908 takeover date, so 1885-1908 scenes should say Congo Free State and later scenes Belgian Congo.
- Row 5 carries the conference mechanics: negotiations from 15 November 1884, final acts on 26 February 1885, free trade and navigation on the Congo, effective occupation, and no article on Leopold's Association or the Congo Free State. Its explicit correction is that Africa was not partitioned at Berlin.
- Row 3 carries the office's reclassification: under the Republic the post of king of Congo became officially a people's judge (juiz popular), while most people still treated Manuel Martins Kidito as king. It also quotes the 1914 request of the Governor of Congo (AHU, Angola, Pasta 999): let there be no more "king of Congo" authority at São Salvador, allow no further election public or secret, and send the throne regalia to Luanda's ethnographic museum.
- Row 3 quotes a 1914 Portuguese official, Faria Leal, saying the Berlin Conference had carved up the kingdom's domains. That is a contemporary official's narration of the loss, not a statement of what the Act says.
- Row 2 states the end in two places: the 1914 suppression of the royal institution after the colonial conquest is "the clearest chronological marker" of the kingdom's end, and the "total suppression of the kingship" is dated 1914.
- Row 1 gives the revolt's course: chiefs rose in December 1913 against Kiditu, the first large attack on São Salvador came on the morning of 10 December 1913, and the king's legitimacy was expected to rest on harmony and reciprocity with the chiefs.
- Row 4 dates the insurrection to 1913-1914 and gives its two aims: depose Kiditu or replace the Portuguese staff at the court with Kongo, and hold the king responsible for the forced labourers. Row 3 dates the revolt to 1914. Row 1 puts its outbreak in December 1913.
- Row 3 records the titular revival: Johnny Lengo was crowned Pedro VII in 1923, imposed by the Portuguese outside the traditional election rules.

Inference (mine, flagged as such):
- [INFERENCE] The conventional "abolished in 1914" formula is a compression of an administrative process: the opened sources show a governor's 1914 request to end the office and election, and a historian's statement that the royal institution was suppressed in 1914. No opened row contains the legal instrument itself, so the entry should say "suppressed in 1914" or "the office was ended after the 1913-14 revolt", not "a decree of 1914 abolished the kingdom".
- [INFERENCE] "Berlin divided the Kongo kingdom" is a retrospective over-reading that was already current among Portuguese officials in 1914 (row 3). The division the rows document is the 1885-onward colonial map (row 4) and the treaty-by-treaty claims the conference's effective-occupation rule unleashed (row 5). A worldbuilder staging a single conference-room partition is staging the later story, not the record.
- [INFERENCE] The royal title's survival after 1914 is a separate fact from the kingdom's political end: row 3 has a 1923 coronation, so a worldbuilder can keep a ritual kingship in being while the polity is gone, but should not present that king as sovereign.

Gaps and disagreements between rows:
- The legal instrument of the 1914 suppression (a decree, a ministerial order or portaria, with number and date) is in no opened row. Row 3's 1914 document is a governor's request; row 2 asserts the suppression as fact without citing the instrument.
- The date of the titular successor Álvaro XV (commonly 1915) is not in any opened row; row 3 only records that Pedro VII was elected in 1923 after the death of "D. Álvaro Negingu". The 1915 date therefore remains unsourced by this seat.
- "Tulante", the title carried by Álvaro Buta in row 1, is not glossed by any opened row. Nor is the relation between "Tulante" and the office of spokesman explained.
- The name varies across the rows: Kiditu (row 1) and Kidito (row 3); Buta (row 1) and Mbûta (row 4); Kongo and Congo; Kôngo in row 4. The entry should pick one form for the king and one for the leader and note the variants once.
- The specific bilateral treaties that drew the Kongo boundaries after 1885 (Portugal with the Congo Free State and with France) are not in any opened row; row 4 gives only the resulting map, row 5 the conference's rule.
- Row 5 is a survey chapter, not Kongo research: it should carry only the conference mechanics and the historiographical correction, not Kongo chronology.
