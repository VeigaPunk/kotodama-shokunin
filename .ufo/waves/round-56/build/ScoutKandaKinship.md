### NEW-ScoutKandaKinship-1

citation: MacGaffey, Wyatt. 2003. "Crossing the River: Myth and Movement in Central Africa." Paper for the international symposium *Angola on the Move: Transport Routes, Communication, and History*, Berlin, 24-26 September 2003; symposium-archive PDF, no DOI.

url: https://archiv.zmo.de/angola/Papers/MacGaffey_(29-03-04).pdf

route: direct plain request (curl), HTTP 200, `application/pdf`, 15 pages, read with `pdftotext -layout`; no challenge, no login, 2026-10-22. The PDF's first page carries the author's copyright notice asking permission for further circulation; the file is publicly posted by the symposium archive.

kind: paper

tier: B — a specialist's conference paper, not peer-reviewed, and its thesis is argued against Hilton and Vansina, so it is authoritative as the statement of a contested position, not as settled Kongo social history.

scope: the paper's own argument about what kanda and ngudi a nkazi mean, how titles were acquired, invested and passed on in the Lower Congo and Kongo, and its description of Kongo society as a network of patrilateral links between matrilineal nodes, including its caution against reading clan organization back into earlier centuries. Not a source for royal chronology, the six provinces, the mani titles, or the sixteenth-century court.

quotes:
- p. 10, section "Matrilineal Descent as Political Process": "kanda means “group or category,” as in makanda ma nza"
- p. 11, same section: "the term ngudi a nkazi, “mother’s brother,” can refer to a man who is not a member of the speaker’s matrilineal clan"
- p. 13, section "Magical Linkages": "to belong to the kingdom, one acquired a title in exchange for tribute, and equipped oneself with appropriate insignia"
- p. 13, same section, block quote of a tradition: "I have already paid for the title, so let it be given to my sister’s son Na Mpyoso Nsakala Nangudi."

teaches: Kikongo kanda means group or category before clan, mother's brother is a role wider than the matrilineal clan, and titles came by tribute plus insignia.

draft sentences:
- Before making kanda the technical word for one descent group, note that in Kikongo it means "group or category", with makanda ma nza for "the peoples of the earth", and the paper says it also denotes clan without being a kinship term (NEW-ScoutKandaKinship-1, p. 10).
- Let a mother's brother be a role rather than a blood line: the paper says ngudi a nkazi can name a man outside the speaker's matrilineal clan who may have no genealogical relationship at all, so let the label mark a relationship rather than a pedigree (NEW-ScoutKandaKinship-1, p. 11).
- Design office as acquired: to belong to the kingdom, one acquired a title in exchange for tribute and equipped oneself with appropriate insignia, so a world can make a title something acquired and equipped, not only inherited (NEW-ScoutKandaKinship-1, p. 13).
- Make investiture a journey: when a chief died and was replaced, ritual retraced the route to an earlier settlement, now a cemetery, where investiture took place or the insignia were obtained, so each office can be tied to a place and a road (NEW-ScoutKandaKinship-1, p. 11).
- Let a woman transact a title she cannot take up: in a tradition recorded in the 1930s, Na Bikadyo brings goats, chickens, money, leopard's teeth, a leopardskin, bracelets, anklets and a buffalo-tail whisk to Mbanza Nkazi to buy a chiefship, and, forbidden to show herself, asks that it be given to her sister's son (NEW-ScoutKandaKinship-1, p. 13).
- Keep both sides in the design: the paper calls the structure of Kongo society a network of patrilateral links between matrilineal nodes, so a world that runs everything through the mother's line loses the father-side links that hold the nodes together (NEW-ScoutKandaKinship-1, p. 11).

### NEW-ScoutKandaKinship-2

citation: Pereira, Luena Nunes. 2013. "Religião e parentesco entre os bakongo de Luanda." *Afro-Ásia* 47: 11-41; Universidade Federal da Bahia, SciELO article id `SwYN8cRNkbYcgJggNm4HPYv`, ISSN 0002-0591 (print), 1981-1411 (online).

url: https://www.scielo.br/j/afro/a/SwYN8cRNkbYcgJggNm4HPYv/?format=pdf&lang=pt

route: direct plain request (curl), HTTP 200, `application/pdf`, 31 pages, read with `pdftotext -layout`; no challenge, no login, 2026-10-22; citation metadata confirmed on the landing page (HTTP 200, `text/html`, 86,303 bytes).

kind: paper

tier: B — a peer-reviewed open-access ethnography of Bakongo kinship in Luanda; strong secondary for the terms and the rules people state, but its evidence is twentieth- and twenty-first-century, not the kingdom period.

scope: twentieth- and twenty-first-century Bakongo kinship as described from Luanda and from the earlier literature the author cites: the kanda definition, exogamy, the mvila distinction, nzo houses, nkazi authority, nephew inheritance, father-son grants of land and insignia, and the changes attributed to mission, colonial law and urbanisation. Not evidence for the sixteenth- or seventeenth-century kingdom, and the author's own warning about the matrilineal label applies.

quotes:
- p. 19, section "O parentesco entre os bakongo de Luanda": "A Kanda é o grupo de parentesco organizado em linha materna, descendente de uma antepassada comum."
- p. 19, same section: "Empiricamente, a Kanda costuma estar associada ao “clã” (mvila), embora aquela faça mais referência ao grupo local do que o clã"
- p. 20, same section: "o tio materno ou tio-avô materno, o membro mais velho da Kanda (nkazi)"
- p. 22, section "Relação pai filho (tata-mwana) e a importância do pai": "o sobrinho mais velho herda do tio materno"

teaches: The kanda is the exogamous matrilineal descent group descended from an ancestress, its eldest man is the nkazi, and the eldest nephew inherits the maternal uncle's office.

draft sentences:
- Make the kanda the exogamous unit: it is the matrilineal descent group descended from a common ancestress, and it defines the block outside which marriage partners must be found (NEW-ScoutKandaKinship-2, p. 19).
- Separate kanda from mvila: kanda is usually glossed as "clan" but refers more to the local group, while mvila names the wider descent category and does not imply exogamy, so give the world two levels of belonging and two words (NEW-ScoutKandaKinship-2, p. 19).
- Add a house level if you need one: the older literature the article cites has nzo, houses that divide the kanda into three sections and regulate access to land, although the article says this level is no longer invoked in Luanda (NEW-ScoutKandaKinship-2, p. 19).
- Put everyday authority in the oldest man: the article describes a family headship centred on the maternal uncle or great-uncle, the eldest member of the kanda, called nkazi (NEW-ScoutKandaKinship-2, p. 20).
- Run succession from uncle to sister's son: the eldest nephew inherits from the maternal uncle, while a father separately gives his son land and the insignia of rule, so pair matrilineal office with paternal patronage (NEW-ScoutKandaKinship-2, p. 22).
- Expect the literature to look more rigid than practice: the article says that categorising Kongo society as matrilineal imposed a certain rigidity on the definition of the system, while its own account shows variation inside it (NEW-ScoutKandaKinship-2, p. 21).

## Findings for integration

- Orthography: MacGaffey prints `KiKongo` and lower-case `kanda`; Pereira prints capitalised `Kanda`. Normalise the entry to `Kikongo` and `kanda`, and keep each source's spelling inside quotations.
- The two rows pull in different directions and the entry should say so. MacGaffey argues that matrilineal descent is unstable and that its supposed importance is largely a European preoccupation; Pereira describes a matrilineal kanda with exogamy, inheritance and authority, while also warning that the matrilineal label made the system look rigid. Mark the question **contested**, not settled.
- Keep the terms apart: `kanda` (local matrilineal, exogamous group), `mvila` (wider descent category, not exogamous), `vumu` (lineage or barrio, descent to the fourth generation, which the article says regulates inheritance rights), `nzo` (house, three per kanda in the older literature), `nkazi` (the eldest man of the kanda; maternal uncle or great-uncle), `ngudi a nkazi` (mother's brother). Do not let the English word "clan" stand for all of them.
- Title mechanics usable from row 1: a title has (a) a right acquired by payment or tribute, (b) a set of insignia, (c) an installation that retraces a route to an earlier settlement or cemetery, and (d) a named heir, here the sister's son. That sequence is a ready-made worldbuilding checklist; keep each step separately visible.
- Gender and office: MacGaffey's footnote calls the menstruation episode "a folkloric cliché in Central Africa that explains why women are not chiefs" (p. 13, n. 22), while the same story has a woman pay for a chiefship and direct it to her sister's son. Keep both the exclusion and the transaction visible rather than flattening them into one rule.
- Gaps this seat does not fill: the six provinces, the `mani` titles, the `Ntotila` title, and royal succession at Mbanza Kongo. The Pigafetta/Lopes, UNESCO and Afonso-letter rows from sibling seats should carry those, and this report makes no claim about them.
- Checked and not used as a row: Laman, *The Kongo I* (1953), archive.org item `gub-0128614`, a public scan in the `opensource` collection (not lending-restricted), opened at OCR level and searched. Its preface states that the people mainly described are the Sundi, not the Kongo, and Volume I as digitised is clan tradition and material culture rather than a systematic kinship chapter; with the seat's two-source cap already met, it is left as a lead. The book also uses `Nkazi a Kongo` as a clan name, a caution for any entry that glosses `nkazi` only as "mother's brother".
