# ScoutDiogoCaoContact — Portuguese contact, 1482-1491, sources for C (Kongo kingdom entry)

Seat scope: Diogo Cão's voyages and the stone padrões, the Ielala river inscriptions, the 1491
baptism of Nzinga a Nkuwu (João I). Three sources opened by plain requests, no access control
met. No tool installed or upgraded.

Verification note: the 1877 volume below was read from its OCR text layer, and every quoted
passage was then checked against the scanned page image (`/page/nNN_w1200.jpg`), so the quotes
are the printed page, not the OCR. The printed page number is the offset `n - 7` (n182 = p. 175,
n13 = p. 6, n17 = p. 10), which is how the locators below were fixed.

### NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-1

citation: Jordão, Levy Maria, Visconde de Paiva Manso. *Historia do Congo (Documentos)*. Obra posthuma publicada pela Academia Real das Sciencias de Lisboa. Lisbon: Typographia da Academia, 1877. Internet Archive item `historiadocongod00jorduoft`.
url: https://archive.org/details/historiadocongod00jorduoft — text read at https://archive.org/download/historiadocongod00jorduoft/historiadocongod00jorduoft_djvu.txt ; pages checked as images at https://archive.org/download/historiadocongod00jorduoft/page/n182_w1200.jpg (likewise n13, n17).
route: direct plain request, HTTP 200, text/plain, 1,002,207 bytes for the text layer; page-image requests HTTP 200, image/jpeg (about 440 KB each). Access date 2026-10-22.
kind: book (documentary edition of primary texts)
tier: A — the Academy edition prints the documents themselves, not a narrative about them: the 1512 manifesto of D. Afonso (doc. IX, which the editor takes from Damião de Góis's *traslado*, described in the footnote as word for word), the 1512 charter of arms (doc. X, from an Évora manuscript, which the editor notes differs from doc. IX), and a 1624 letter to Manuel Rodrigues (from Torre do Tombo, Corpo Chronológico I, maç. 117, doc. 16, per the edition's footnotes). Tier A scoped to the printed text; the editor's 1877 footnotes are B and are marked as editorial where used.
scope: the Kongo court speaking in its own words in 1512 (royal style and titles, the date it gives for the Portuguese teaching mission, the king's account of his own baptism) and a Kongo churchman's dynastic list of 1624 (king list, election of kings by the great men, the baptism, the king's later return to the old cult). Not the Portuguese voyages, not the padrões as objects, not Kikongo grammar.
quotes:
- p. 6, doc. IX (Manifesto of D. Afonso, 1512), opening sentence: "dom Affonso por sua graça, Rei de Manicongo, e senhor dos Ambudos"
- p. 10, doc. X (Carta d'armas, 1512): "no anno do nascimento de Nosso Senhor Jesus Cristo de 1487"
- p. 175, letter of 1624, dynastic list: "baptisando-se em 3 de maio do anno do Senhor de 491, chamando-se D. João"
- p. 175, letter of 1624, dynastic list: "os grandes do Reino, usando da sua posse, elegerão outro filho bastardo"
teaches: A Kongo king styles himself Rei de Manicongo and senhor dos Ambudos, and a 1624 Kongo list has the great men elect kings and dates the baptism to 3 May.
draft sentences:
- A Kongo king's own 1512 manifesto opens with the style Rei de Manicongo and senhor dos Ambudos, so treat "Manicongo" as part of the royal style a herald proclaims, not as a personal name. (NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-1, p. 6)
- The same king's 1512 charter of arms says the Portuguese king sent teachers in 1487, four years before the baptism the 1624 list gives, so stage early Kongo dates as a band: the documents disagree and one of them is wrong by years. (NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-1, p. 10)
- The 1624 list puts the baptism on 3 May, with the king taking the name D. João from a Franciscan who was the first pastor of that church, so conversion is a court ceremony with a new name to use in later charters. (NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-1, p. 175)
- The same list says the great men used their right to elect a king to choose a bastard son over the legitimate heir, so succession is an election among titled men and the designated heir can be set aside. (NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-1, p. 175)
- The list also says the baptized king later turned back to the old cult and died outside the church, so stage conversion as reversible royal policy, not a one-way change of religion. (NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-1, p. 175)
- The list names each king twice, Nginga ancu who became D. João and Nbenba a Nginga who became D. Afonso, so a character carries a Kongo name and a baptismal name and charters may use either. (NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-1, p. 175)

### NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-2

citation: Museu da Sociedade de Geografia de Lisboa, object record "Padrão de Santo Agostinho" (no inventory number shown on the page), Lisbon, viewed 2026-10-22.
url: https://museu.socgeografialisboa.pt/objectos/padrao-de-santo-agostinho/
route: direct plain request, HTTP 200, text/html, 197,712 bytes. Access date 2026-10-22.
kind: record (holding-institution collection record)
tier: A — the holding museum's own catalogue record for the object it curates; authoritative for this piece's identity, material, date band, size and provenance history. It is not a source for the voyages' chronology and says nothing about the Congo mouth.
scope: the surviving Diogo Cão pillar from Cabo de Santa Maria: lioz stone, dated 1482-1483, pillar height 216 cm, column 169 cm, head 46x33x33 cm, column diameter 73 cm, about 300 kg; raised to mark the Portuguese arrival on that stretch of coast, replaced at the site by another commemorative mark in 1892 and the original brought to Lisbon.
quotes:
- object data, date field: "1482-1483"
- object data, material field: "Pedra (lios)"
- description, first sentence: "Marco comemorativo erguido por Diogo Cão no Cabo de Santa Maria"
- description, second sentence: "Em 1892 foi substituído por um outro marco comemorativo e o original trazido para Lisboa"
teaches: The Lisbon Geographical Society holds a 1482-1483 lioz pillar raised by Diogo Cão at Cabo de Santa Maria, replaced on site in 1892 and brought to Lisbon.
draft sentences:
- The record dates the pillar to 1482-1483 and names Diogo Cão as the man who raised it, so give the coastal pillar a two-year band in a timeline rather than a single founding date. (NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-2, object data, date field; description)
- It is lioz stone, 216 cm as a pillar and about 300 kg, so a padrão is a column a boat can carry and men can raise in an afternoon, not a building. (NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-2, object data, material and dimensions)
- The record says the mark was replaced on site in 1892 and the original brought to Lisbon, so a story set after 1892 has another mark on the coast and the original in a museum. (NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-2, description)
- The record states the pillar marked the Portuguese arrival on that part of the West African coast, so stage it as a claim-stone planted where a crew first landed, whose meaning depends on who finds it. (NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-2, description)
- No record for the Congo-mouth pillar (São Jorge) appears on the museum site, so do not cite the museum for the river mouth; the only lead seen for it is a 1932 periodical. (NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-2, absence: /objectos/padrao-de-sao-jorge/ returned HTTP 404)

### NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-3

citation: Lacerda, Teresa. "Rocks Ielala". *Encyclopaedia of Portuguese Expansion* (EVE), CHAM – NOVA FCSH / Universidade dos Açores, 2009; English translation by Maria das Mercês Pacheco.
url: https://eve.fcsh.unl.pt/en/places/rocks-ielala
route: direct plain request, HTTP 200, text/html, 21,526 bytes. Access date 2026-10-22.
kind: web (academic encyclopaedia entry)
tier: B — a named scholar's summary entry in a university reference work, with a named translator; it reports the rocks and their reading but is not the epigraphic edition, gives no letter-by-letter transcription, and cites no inscription corpus.
scope: where the Ielala rocks are, that the carvings are attributed to the crew of Diogo Cão's second fleet, and the page's English rendering of the three rocks' texts (king, captain and companion on the first; two names on the second; four names on the third).
quotes:
- opening description: "Ielala rocks, situated on the left bank of the Zaire River, close to the Ielala Falls, approximately 150 km from the river's mouth"
- second sentence: "inscriptions made by the crew of the second fleet captained by Diogo Cão"
- first-rock passage: "Here came the ships of the enlightened King João II of Portugal Diogo Cão Pêro da Costa"
- second-rock passage: "Álvaro Pires and Pêro Escobar"
teaches: The Ielala rocks about 150 km up the Zaire carry second-fleet inscriptions naming João II, Diogo Cão and Pêro da Costa, plus crew names.
draft sentences:
- The carvings sit on the left bank of the Zaire about 150 km from the mouth, so the Portuguese went past the estuary to the first falls: put the contact zone at the rapids, not only at the coast. (NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-3, opening description)
- The page attributes them to the crew of Diogo Cão's second fleet, so an inscription upstream belongs to a later voyage than the coastal pillars and should be staged as a second arrival. (NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-3, second sentence)
- The page renders the first rock as naming King João II, Diogo Cão and Pêro da Costa in one line, so a carving can carry king, captain and companion together as one public claim. (NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-3, first-rock passage)
- The second and third rocks carry only personal names, Álvaro Pires, Pêro Escobar, João de Santiago, Diogo Pinheiro, Gonçalo Álvares, João Álvares, so crews carved their own names beside the royal title. (NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-3, second- and third-rock passages)
- The English line is credited to a named translator and the page offers no letter-by-letter transcription, so anyone quoting it quotes a modern rendering, not the carved letters. (NEW-ScoutDiogoCaoContact-3, translation credit)
