# ScoutCosmogramCalibration — claim C source report (cosmogram calibration)

Result: two sources opened, both from the 2011 *Historical Archaeology* forum "Crosses to Bear: Cross Marks as African Symbols in Southern Pottery", both read as PDF text in this session. They form a matched pair. Gundaker defends the dikenga as an analytic frame and quotes Thompson's formulation; Steen attacks the inference from cross and X marks on southern colonoware to a Kongo cosmogram. Together they document the contest at the point where a worldbuilder is most tempted to over-read a mark.

Status: contested; the two sources state the disagreement in these terms. Gundaker treats the dikenga as real but denies that it is a unitary symbol or that any rendering is "pure"; Steen calls the North American cosmogram attribution "simply an idea, a theory" with no direct, unambiguous evidence. Neither source denies that Kongo people used cross-shaped signs: Steen explicitly allows that the reading might be true. The direct MacGaffey critique of Thompson and Fu-Kiau was not opened (see unopened), so the entry should attribute the contested status to the sources actually cited, not to a general scholarly consensus.

Provenance note: both PDFs carry a cover page stating that the Society for Historical Archaeology gave permission to post the article copy for educational use; both bodies are the published journal typesetting. Neither is a shadow-library copy. The tier lines below stop at what these articles themselves argue.

## What this pair establishes for the entry

The pair supports a two-sided sentence and nothing stronger. Supporting side: Kongo cosmogram readings exist in scholarly use; Gundaker describes the dikenga as a relational system rather than a logo, calls it an enduring moral compass, ties the yowa to Lemba initiation and membership, and quotes Thompson's formulation in a peer-reviewed forum. Critical side: the archaeological extension of the reading to North America is explicitly contested, the evidence is hedged, and the critic prefers a Christian explanation for the South Carolina examples.

Gap on the calibration's third term: the Kalunga line is only named as "the Kalunga waters" in this pair, and neither source defines its direction or sets out the four solar stations. A row that defines the line or the four moments must come from another source; do not stretch these two to cover it.

What the pair does not establish: that Thompson or Fu-Kiau invented the diagram, that the dikenga is late or inauthentic, or that Kongo people did not use cross-shaped signs. Steen allows the cosmogram reading might be true; Gundaker treats it as an enduring moral compass. A direct critique of Thompson and Fu-Kiau's historiography is MacGaffey 2016, which was not opened here.
