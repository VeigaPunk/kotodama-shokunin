## Cautions for the entry (observations, not row claims)

1. **Three work counts on one institution's pages**: 134 (exhibition text), over 170 (catalogue description), 146 (a reviewer quoted on the MetPublications page). Any sentence in the entry that gives a number must name which object count it means.
2. **The cosmogram reading is asserted, not argued.** The Met's crucifix essay states the Four Moments of the Sun reading flatly. The wave brief calls these readings contested; **no source opened this session carries the contest**. If the entry says "contested", it needs a row that actually states the dispute, otherwise write it as "the Met reads the cross as ..." and leave the contest out.
3. **Nomenclature drift inside one museum**: `nkisi` (object titles), `minkisi` (plural, nkisi essay), `minkondi` (catalogue description), `power figure` (English gloss in every title). The entry should pick one term per register and list the others as variants; the museum itself never uses the word fetish in these pages.
4. **Dates move between record and essay**: object 318324 is dated 16th–17th century in the title block and called fifteenth-century in its own essay; object 320053's captured 2021 page says `19th century` while the live API now says `ca. 1880–1900`. Cite the capture date with any object date.
5. **The conversion date and name are thin here**: the Met writes only `Nzinga` and `1491`; it gives no baptismal name and no succession detail. Those belong to another row (Thornton's chapter is listed in the contents but its text was not opened).
6. **What these pages cannot carry**: province names (Mbata, Soyo, Nsundi), titles (mani, ntotila, mwene), nzimbu shell money, the Antonian movement, and the colonial partition. None appears on any page opened here. Five rows of museum text do not make a polity; the entry needs those seats' sources for offices, provinces and money.
7. **Where the outsider's record over-reads**: the Met files the crucifix under `Kongo peoples; Kongo Kingdom` with no workshop or patron, and reads a European-modelled corpus as Kongo work without naming the Kongo carvers; the anonymous `Kongo artist and nganga` credit is the museum's convention, not a person. Use it as evidence of a role, not of an individual.
