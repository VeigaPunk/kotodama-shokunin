draft sentences:
- Kongo kept no written records before Diogo Cão's 1483 visit, so a worldbuilder staging the founding era must present it as reconstruction from oral tradition and archaeology, not as recorded history (NEW-ScoutThorntonOrigins-1, p. 89).
- Because the traditions are themselves interpretative histories, the entry should separate what a tradition preserves as memory from the later explanatory narrative built around it (NEW-ScoutThorntonOrigins-1, p. 89).
- The first explicitly historical description, Duarte Lopes' account as revised by Pigafetta and published in 1591, has no account of the foundation and no king before João I Nzinga a Nkuwu, and Pigafetta added editorial emendations to fit Kongo to European ideas of Africa, so the entry should mine it for provinces rather than chronology (NEW-ScoutThorntonOrigins-1, p. 102).
- Mbata joined Kongo by its own will in this tradition and kept the privilege that its ruler was chosen by the king of Kongo from Mbata's own royal line, with Mbata's rulers succeeding in Kongo if the royal line failed (NEW-ScoutThorntonOrigins-1, p. 103).
- In 1514 Afonso I described Dom Jorge, the Mwene Mbata, as the head of his kingdom, and in 1529 João III of Portugal wrote that Mbata's ruler was the first voice of Congo and that no king could be made without him, which is the succession rule a worldbuilder should build around (NEW-ScoutThorntonOrigins-1, p. 103).
- The recorded name form is Lukeni lua Nimi, Lukeni son of Nimi, and Thornton reads it through a naming practice that adds the father's given name to the son's, a pattern a worldbuilder can reuse for dynastic names (NEW-ScoutThorntonOrigins-1, p. 105).

kind: paper
tier: A. Open-access, peer-reviewed article by the specialist, and the current published statement of his revised reconstruction of Kongo's origin; authoritative for exactly the chronology and constitutional reading the entry stages.
scope: Thornton's revised origin thesis (conquest of an older Mpemba), the changing origin narratives as constitutional statements, Kongo titles and settlement terms, and the late date of kanda clans.

quotes:
- p. 2, on the historiography of origin traditions: "traditions of origin in Kongo changed much more substantially over time."
- p. 2, after comparing da Pavia's 1710 and Cavazzi's 1687 founders: "These two accounts represent constitutional statement that evolved alongside the kingdom itself."
- p. 6, on the revised origin: "I proposed that the “origin of Kongo” should be reconceptualized as the “conquest of Mpemba.”"
- p. 8, on clans: "They were not, as Cuvelier had originally proposed, ancient entities that existed before Kongo emerged."

teaches: Thornton now derives Kongo from the conquest of an older Mpemba and reads each origin story as a constitutional statement of its own era.

draft sentences:
- A worldbuilder choosing a founding story must choose which era's version to dramatize, because the 1687 Cavazzi founder is a ruthless killer of his aunt while the 1710 da Pavia founder is a wise blacksmith who settles disputes (NEW-ScoutThorntonOrigins-2, p. 2).
- Thornton reframes Kongo's origin as the conquest of an older Mpemba, a major polity with three levels of authority probably founded in the first half of the fourteenth century (NEW-ScoutThorntonOrigins-2, p. 6).
- Mbata's role in Kongo's elections diminished over time and the tradition evolved to accommodate the change, so the entry should date its constitutional claims rather than treat them as one fixed rule (NEW-ScoutThorntonOrigins-2, p. 3).
- Kongo kings appointed and dismissed officials down to the sub-province level at will, and noble income rested on revocable revenue shares (rendas) rather than private land, which is the administrative model a worldbuilder should copy (NEW-ScoutThorntonOrigins-2, p. 8).
- Kanda clans were not primordial units that predated Kongo; they emerged in the collapse of central authority in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, so a worldbuilder should not project modern clan structure back onto the founding era (NEW-ScoutThorntonOrigins-2, p. 8).
- Settlement terms run from villages (mavata) grouped under a capital (mbanza), with the higher political orders called wene and their holders mwene, a title whose semantic field included dispensing justice (NEW-ScoutThorntonOrigins-2, p. 9).
