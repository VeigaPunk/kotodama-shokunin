teaches: The Kikongo Language Cluster is a continuum of closely related Bantu varieties, not one language, and its four subgroups predate the Kongo kingdom.

draft sentences:
- Kikongo is a cluster, not a single language: the authors name the Kikongo Language Cluster and call it a disparate continuum of closely related Bantu languages (NEW-ScoutKikongoClassification-1, p. 87).
- Inside that cluster they separate four subgroups, North KK, East KK, South KK and West KK, so a world bible that says only "Kikongo" leaves the speakers' variety undecided (NEW-ScoutKikongoClassification-1, p. 146).
- The four subgroups were already in existence about 1,500 years ago, so the founding of the kingdom cannot be used to explain them (NEW-ScoutKikongoClassification-1, p. 146).
- Kingdom expansion did reshape the map once, producing a convergence zone called Central KK where South KK, East KK and North KK met north and south of the unnavigable Congo (NEW-ScoutKikongoClassification-1, p. 147).
- A single H16a label covers four separately named varieties, South Kongo including Kisikongo, Mboma, Songo and Solongo, so an outside list that treats the label as one people flattens them (NEW-ScoutKikongoClassification-1, p. 103).
- Guthrie's H16 codes are geo-location shorthands with no classificatory value, so H16 should never be written as a family or an ethnicity (NEW-ScoutKikongoClassification-1, p. 106).
