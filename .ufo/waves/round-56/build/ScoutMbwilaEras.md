## Observed, inference, gaps

Observed (page-level, both rows):
- Randles dates the battle 29 October 1665 at Ulanga near Ambuila and counts 400 nobles dead, 98 of them members of the administrative corps (ch. X § 13); the king's head was carried to Luanda and buried in the chapel of Nossa Senhora da Nazaré, and the booty included the crown Innocent X had given D. Garcia II in 1648.
- Randles says the victors did not occupy the country and that the Portuguese court disliked the victory (§ 14-15); a general peace followed in 1667 (§ 16).
- Randles names the two royal clans as Ki-mpanzu and Ki-mulaza (§ 22), and has three kings claiming sovereignty at São Salvador, Kibango and Bula, with Soyo long independent and holding fifty cannon by 1711 (§ 19).
- Batsîkama names the field offices in Kikongo: Na Mpânzu (chefe de guerra), Ma Mbâmba, Kibênga (general), Mwêne Nsoyo for the Soyo ruler (pp. 19-20, 23).
- Batsîkama states Mbanza Kongo was without government from 1667 and that kings were made at Bula or Kibangu (p. 24).

Inference (flagged as mine, not the sources'): the familiar formula "sacked in 1678, abandoned until 1709" compresses two different claims. Randles has the city burned and its inhabitants absent only "until 1703", with no recovery afterwards; Batsîkama has a king installed there in 1678. A worldbuilder should stage 1678 as a disputed year (burning, occupation, or both in sequence) rather than a clean abandonment.

Gaps:
- No source opened here names the agent of a 1678 "sack by Soyo"; Randles attributes the burning to the fallout of Daniel's own campaign, and Batsîkama has Daniel entering as king. The exact day of the 1678 burning is not given by either.
- The 1709 date is not monolithic: Randles gives it as definitive; Batsîkama's footnote-level gloss shifts the same conquest to 1712. Any entry sentence on 1709 should keep that hedge or attribute it.
- The 1703 return date (Randles) is the one detail most likely to be flattened into "abandoned until 1709" by a later summary; it is worth keeping if the entry stages the city's repopulation.
