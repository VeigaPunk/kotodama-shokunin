# Prayer and petition

- topic: prayer as a petitionary address register
- sources: S010 S011 S012
- verified-at: 2026-11-04
- recheck: 180d

## 1. The address

A prayer opens by naming the deity in the vocative and stating the petitioner. The vocative formulae vary by rank and by occasion, and the petitioner names a claim on the god.

## 2. The petition

The petition states a want in a fixed order: address, reminder of past favour, request, promise. The votive promise binds the petitioner to a return and is the enforceable half of the exchange.

## 3. Fixed and extempore

Fixed prayer is recited by rote and its wording is protected from change. Extempore prayer is composed at the moment and its authority rests on the speaker rather than the text.

## 4. Efficacy

A prayer claims efficacy through the address, the promise or the speaker. The claim is graded in the culture: some prayers are held to compel, others only to petition.

## 5. Table

| element | fixed | extempore |
|---|---|---|
| address | rote | improvised |
| petition | set | composed |

## 6. Language choice

The language of prayer is chosen by the address: a god may be addressed only in a sacred register. S010 S011 S012 supply the comparative cases and each claim above is bounded to them.
