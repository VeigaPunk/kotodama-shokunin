```markdown
## 6. Language shapes gesture

| How the language packages an event | What the language does | What the speakers' hands do | Design consequence (lane heuristic) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Manner and path in one clause | "English typically encodes Manner in a verb and Trajectory in a preposition or a verb particle"; "All of them used one clause to encode Manner and Trajectory" (NEW-ScribeSec06Packaging-1) | one gesture that shows both, with "Manner and Trajectory are simultaneously realized" (NEW-ScribeSec06Packaging-1) | Fix the clause count before drawing any gesture |
| Manner and path in two clauses | "Japanese and Turkish typically express the two pieces of information with two separate clauses" (NEW-ScribeSec06Packaging-3) | "Compared to English speakers, Turkish and Japanese speakers were more likely to have Manner Only and Trajectory Only gestures" (NEW-ScribeSec06Packaging-1) | Two clauses mean two gestures, not one sweep |
| Path in a satellite, manner in the main verb (satellite-framed) | "Speakers of satellite-framed languages such as English express the path in a so-called satellite, like up, and manner in the main verb" (NEW-ScribeSec06Packaging-2) | "the gestural representations of the same events differ across languages when they are encoded by different syntactic frames" (NEW-ScribeSec06Packaging-2) | Name the framing type in the design record |
| Path in the main verb, manner outside it (verb-framed) | "the verb root at once expresses both the fact of Motion and the Path"; Manner or Cause then goes in "an independent, usually adverbial or gerundive type constituent" (NEW-ScribeSec06Packaging-4) | the same event is gestured differently in a satellite-framed and a verb-framed language (NEW-ScribeSec06Packaging-2) | Name the framing type in the design record |
| A word the language does not have | English has the intransitive verb swing; Japanese and Turkish "do not have any equivalent intransitive verb" (NEW-ScribeSec06Packaging-3) | Japanese and Turkish speakers drew "straight gestures" that did not show the arc trajectory, English speakers "arc gestures" that did (NEW-ScribeSec06Packaging-3) | A missing lexical item quietly deletes the gesture for it |
| The same language, two clause choices | "English speakers were more likely to express manner and path in a single gesture when they described manner and path within a clause" (NEW-ScribeSec06Packaging-3) | the hands follow the clause the speaker chose this time, not a fixed habit (NEW-ScribeSec06Packaging-3) | Let a scene change the clause and the gesture changes with it |
| The same element left out in both languages | English and Turkish speakers who said only manner or only path gestured alike (NEW-ScribeSec06Packaging-2) | "gestural information was found to fit the semantic encoding of the event rather than compensate" (NEW-ScribeSec06Packaging-2) | Do not use gesture to carry what the lexicon refuses to carry |
| What the sentence never said | the direction of the event was never verbalized (NEW-ScribeSec06Packaging-1) | "the same Trajectory Only gestures encoded the direction of the event that was never verbalized" (NEW-ScribeSec06Packaging-1) | Check the hands against the clause before adding a gesture |

**Design consequence (lane heuristic).** Decide the lexicalization pattern first — at the syntax-skeleton and core-lexicon stages (`conlang-methodology.md:76`, `:82`) — and the speakers' hands follow it; the typical failure is a gesture system that contradicts its own language: a two-clause event drawn as one English-style sweep, or a scene whose hands carry a path the lexicon has no word for.
```

## 6. Conflicts, corrections and deviations (reported verbatim for the canonical judge)

1. **The brief's Talmy attribution needed a source of its own.** The brief asked for "satellite-framed vs verb-framed languages (Talmy)" and named only the two gesture papers as sources. Neither gesture paper is Talmy. The Talmy 1985 chapter was therefore opened as a public copy (row 4) so the framework rests on its own primary source; the gesture consequence still rests on the gesture papers. The chapter is cited only for its wording, and both of its quotations were read from the printed page image because the copy's OCR layer misprints characters (it renders "pattern" as "pattem" and "the" as "&e").
2. **The public Talmy copy is a two-up scan with a faulty OCR layer.** The Arizona file carries printed pp. 58–149 (two printed pages per PDF page), so no part of the chapter is missing; what is unreliable is its OCR text layer, which renders "pattern" as "pattem", "the" as "&e" and "conflates" as "codates". That is why both Talmy quotations were read off rasterised images of the printed pages rather than from the extracted text, and why a checker should read them from the page image too.
3. **Kita 2009 is cited by the accepted manuscript's pagination, not the journal's.** The open copy is the accepted version, whose pages run 1–40; the journal issue runs 145–167. The registry anchor names the accepted manuscript pagination so a checker can find the line, and the row scope says which it is. A checker who wants journal pagination must open the publisher version, which this round did not do (no paywall bypass was attempted).
4. **The English one-clause/two-clause row is a secondary claim.** Row 6 of the table rests on Kita 2009's report of an experiment by other authors that was not opened this round; the row draft scopes that sentence at tier B through the review. The primary experiment (Kita, Özyürek, Allen et al. 2007) is on the MPI PuRe server but the copy served at `pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_57605_7/component/file_532173/content` is a page-image scan with no text layer, so it could not be quoted from and was not quoted from.
5. **"Rolling down" is not a slope in the 2003 study.** The brief described "English vs Japanese and Turkish speakers' gestures for 'rolling down'". In Kita and Özyürek 2003 the Rolling Event is a cat with a bowling ball in its stomach rolling down a street into a bowling alley (printed p. 22); the slope version is the paraphrase in Kita 2009's review (AAM p. 17). The drafted rows say "the Rolling Event" and quote the sources, so neither reading is asserted. The integrator should not merge the two events.
6. **The English and Turkish omission result is stated at one sample size.** The drafted row 7 says English and Turkish speakers who said only manner or only path "gestured alike"; the source states this for 20 English and 20 Turkish speakers over 10 motion events (p. 236). The row does not claim a general law.
7. **No partial owner was restated.** The draft contains no emblem taxonomy, no finger counting, no sign-language boundary and no frame-of-reference material; those stay with `adornment-body-codes.md:79` and `:92`, `numerals-and-measures.md:107`, `sign-languages-manual-modality.md:64` and `:161`, and `direction-symbolism.md:6` and `:36`.
