## 8. Social control: taboos, politeness, hand and status

Gesture is a register with rules: a culture's hands, targets, audiences and spaces are all licensed, and its politeness is visible in who may move and in front of whom (design frame, **H**).

| control | attested case | design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| the hand in giving and pointing | in Ghana "a set of conventions restrict the use of the left hand", and "Virtually all Ghanaians consider giving, receiving and eating with the left hand to be rude" (NEW-ScribeSec08Social-1, p. 61); of the 26 speakers who gave route directions, all acknowledged the general left-hand taboo and 16 also the taboo on direction-indicating left-hand gestures (NEW-ScribeSec08Social-1, p. 62); the corpus behind the study is Ewe route-description recordings in Ghana, non-elicited, with speech and gesture together (NEW-ScribeSec08Social-2); one community reports that "Giving, receiving, eating, and drinking with the left hand are considered rude by virtually all members of the community" (NEW-ScribeSec08Social-4, p. 22) | write the polarity as an institution: which hand gives, points, eats, receives and swears (`../myth-symbol/direction-symbolism.md:99`), then design the substitute forms the taboo forces — the reported respect position and the bi-manual or right-hand forms (NEW-ScribeSec08Social-1, p. 62) — or every scene breaks its own rule (design frame, **H**) |
| pointing at people, and at what is feared | a peer-reviewed cross-cultural study reports taboos "against left hand pointing, index-finger pointing, or pointing at people" and records that not pointing at people is "a taboo common, e.g., in Europe, the Americas, Japan", and that such taboos "are explicitly represented in folk theories of conduct and politeness" (NEW-ScribeSec08Social-3, pp. 700, 708); the MPI field manual lists as elicitation items that "in Laos it is said to be rude to point at people, but departures are common and do not usually attract sanction" and that "one should not point at a rainbow for fear of getting a bent finger as a result" (NEW-ScribeSec08Social-5, p. 94); one community adds a place-restricted taboo: left-handed residents "dare not to point their left hands at their father’s house" (NEW-ScribeSec08Social-4, p. 22); pointing at a rainbow is reported as "a foolhardy act" in Hungary, China, Mexico and Gabon, with the consequences "getting jaundice, losing an eye, being struck by lightning, or simply disappearing" (NEW-ScribeSec08Social-6) | give each taboo a body: what is pointed at, who may not point, and what the sanction is — and decide whether it is enforced or merely stated, since the manual's own question "Are pointing taboos actually followed or merely stated as rules?" is the design test (NEW-ScribeSec08Social-5, p. 94) (design frame, **H**) |
| gesture rate and size by status and setting | with a socially distant superior, 14 Korean and 14 Catalan participants "curtail gestures when interacting with a socially distant superior", producing fewer gestures, "reduced their gesture space" and less manner encoding (NEW-ScribeSec08Social-7); a study of Korean televised drama finds deference to status superiors marked by "suppression of gestures and touching" versus "frequent gestures and touching behaviors" with status equals (NEW-ScribeSec08Social-8; screen data, not field observation); by setting and community, "Taiwanese mothers produced gestures at a much higher rate (about three times more) than American mothers" and Mediterranean gesture is "more expansive, elevated, and on the lateral (transversal) plane" (NEW-ScribeSec08Social-9, pp. 27–28) | budget gesture volume, size and space per status pair and per setting, and make the hierarchy visible in the amount of body allowed; a single uniform rate erases rank (design frame, **H**) |
| gesture inside oaths and rites | the lane's binding-speech entry already owns the oath machinery and its channel rows, including the reddened ring on which oaths are sworn (`../myth-symbol/binding-speech-complex.md:108`, `:127`); a community's own rite rule excludes the left hand: "the left hand has no role to play in cultural and customary rites in the community when it comes to ritual performances" (NEW-ScribeSec08Social-4, p. 22) | decide the bodily act of the oath (which hand, which object) in the same design pass as the hand polarity; the gesture is the visible edge of the binding speech, so it cannot be left to the words alone (design frame, **H**) |
| who may gesture to whom | the hand rule is addressee-graded: exchanging money "with a trader or elderly person with the left hand is considered disrespectful and rude", and giving a gift with the left hand to one's own child "indicates that you suspect the child not to be your own biological child" (NEW-ScribeSec08Social-4, p. 22); the manual asks whether it is "impolite to point in the company of certain people" (NEW-ScribeSec08Social-5, p. 94); in the screen corpus, status superiors receive "suppression of gestures and touching" while status equals receive frequent gesture and touch (NEW-ScribeSec08Social-8) | write the permission matrix by addressee (elder, trader, kin, superior, equal) before writing the gesture inventory; a rule that ignores who is watching has no social meaning (design frame, **H**) |

Status: the case column rests on the sources opened on 2026-10-22 (tiers A and B; the K&E 2001 article itself is paywalled, so its study is reported here through the MPI's own annual report of the study and the MPI archive record of its Ewe corpus, and the article's publisher abstract is already drafted by ScribeSec04Pointing); the design-consequence column is the lane's own heuristic (**H**, `../verification/calibration-registry.md:35-39`); a cell whose community, sanction or data source the opened page does not name carries `(to-verify)`.
