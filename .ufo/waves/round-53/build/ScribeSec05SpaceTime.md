```markdown
## 5. Gesture in space and time

| Frame or time line the language keeps | Where the hands go | Design consequence (the designer's own heuristic, **H**) |
|---|---|---|
| Absolute cardinals (Guugu Yimithirr, Hopevale, Cape York) | Pointing keeps true bearings — "If one is talking about going to the beach and the beach is east, one points east" (NEW-ScribeSec05SpaceTime-1); the frame survives a change of language: "they gesture freely in a way that presupposes absolute orientation" (NEW-ScribeSec05SpaceTime-11); the language side is carried by `myth-symbol/direction-symbolism.md:36` | Fix the bearing set before writing an instruction: a hand meaning "to my left" is a contradiction in this world |
| The sky as a clock (Guugu Yimithirr) | "GY speakers frequently point to the part of the sky where the sun would be visible at a certain hour" (NEW-ScribeSec05SpaceTime-2) | Give the culture a horizon and a sun path, or the time-of-day gesture has nothing to point at |
| Topographic frame (Tzeltal, Chiapas; Tzotzil, Zinacantán) | Uphill and downhill carry the load (`myth-symbol/direction-symbolism.md:39`); Tzotzil speakers "also display precise bodily orientations to space" (NEW-ScribeSec05SpaceTime-3) | Keep the local axis local: the same phrase sends hands to different slopes in two valleys, and a gesture can be finer than the words for it |
| Static time line (Aymara, Andes) | "The space behind the speaker is the future, and the space in front of the speaker is the past" (NEW-ScribeSec05SpaceTime-4); the language-side claim sits at `linguistics/semantics-pragmatics.md:118` | Choose one time line and keep it in every scene: a gesture that puts the past behind contradicts the words beside it |
| Topographic time line (Yupno, Papua New Guinea) | "the past is construed as downhill, the present as co-located with the speaker, and the future as uphill" (NEW-ScribeSec05SpaceTime-6), in a language that "makes extensive use of allocentric topographic (uphill/downhill) terms" (NEW-ScribeSec05SpaceTime-7) | Let the valley write the timeline: a time line that ignores the terrain reads as foreign speech |
| Horizontal time line from script direction (English, Hebrew) | "English speakers represented earlier time-points by pointing to the left of a reference point, whereas Hebrew speakers pointed to the right of the reference point" (NEW-ScribeSec05SpaceTime-9); "Written languages appear to organize their readers’ attention in a vector from where text usually begins to where it usually ends" (NEW-ScribeSec05SpaceTime-10) | Decide whether the script has a direction: if it does, one gesture reads mirrored to readers of the other script |

The frame the language keeps decides where the hands go, and the failure is a hand that points in the writer's frame — a relative "to my left" inside a world of absolute bearings, an uphill hand on a flat map. Bilingualism opens the seam: "fluency in Aymara relates to frontward gestures when referring to the past, whereas fluency in Spanish relates to backward gestures" (NEW-ScribeSec05SpaceTime-5), so a mixed community needs a rule for which frame a scene is in.

The gesture space moves with the setting as well as the time line: indoors, "Yupno participants primarily produced past-category gestures pointing toward the houses’ entryways, and future-category gestures pointing away from them" (NEW-ScribeSec05SpaceTime-8). Keep the claim bounded — "the whole system of absolute orientation, the associated gesture and interaction system, is much more than merely a linguistic phenomenon" (NEW-ScribeSec05SpaceTime-12) — so a culture whose speakers cannot orient is a different culture, and its hands point relative (**H**, `verification/calibration-registry.md:38`; `linguistics/linguistic-anthropology.md:49`).
```
