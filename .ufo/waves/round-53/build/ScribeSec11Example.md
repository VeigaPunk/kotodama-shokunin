```markdown
## 11. NON-CANON worked micro-example

**NON-CANON.** The Vellmark people of the invented Vellmark uplands, their language, their gestures and their numbers (the upland terraces are nine) are invented for this worked example; nothing here describes a real community, and canon belongs to lore-shokunin. The language fixes space absolutely — uphill and downhill, never the speaker's left — so the frame register and its costs live at `../myth-symbol/direction-symbolism.md:36`, and co-speech gesture stays on the speech side of the manual boundary at `sign-languages-manual-modality.md:64`; it also marks motion in the verb rather than in an adverb, which frees the hand from carrying the path (the S-type/V-type contrast in Talmy's typology is one of the language characteristics McNeill names gesticular differences as tracing to, NEW-ScribeSec11Example-3; "verb-framed" is this entry's own label for the invented language, not the source's term). Walking the §10 decision table: §3 gives the Vellmark hand three jobs — the three quotable gestures below, the pointing form, and the accompanying motion; §4 takes the chin-and-lip point, since the vector of that point is set by the gaze and the chin raise is nearly always there, so a finger-free point suits a people whose bearings are never relative (NEW-ScribeSec11Example-1); §5 puts the time line on the vertical, past uphill and future downhill, because a vertical time axis is attested and its direction follows the language's own metaphor rather than a fixed human one (NEW-ScribeSec11Example-2), with the lane's own caution on vertical time metaphors at `semantics-pragmatics.md:118`; §6 keeps path in the verb, so the accompanying hand is free to mark attitude instead of direction; §7 fixes three quotable gestures, each with one stable form and one verbal gloss, culturally specific and translatable rather than universal (NEW-ScribeSec11Example-3), the wider emblem taxonomy being owned at `../culture-material/adornment-body-codes.md:79`; §8 sets one taboo — the promise gesture is not made toward an elder, and the sanction is social rather than legal (the sanction gradient lives at `politeness-taboo-euphemism.md:76`); §9 gives one learner's error — the beginner who says the past while the hand marks the future, so that gesture and words disagree, which is the mismatch the child-learning literature reports (design inference from NEW-ScribeSec11Example-4); the failure a designer must design against is the one §10 makes visible: a relative pointing habit or a borrowed time axis inside an absolute-frame language, where the gesture contradicts the words.

| Vellmark gesture (invented) | Gloss (English) |
|---|---|
| palm turned up, held out flat | "give it to me" |
| index finger to the lips, then to the listener | "I say this to you only" |
| closed fist at the shoulder, twice | "I swear it" |
```

## What the example decides, and the failure it names

The Vellmark walk fixes, in §3–§9 order: three gesture registers (§3); a finger-free
chin-and-lip pointing form whose referent is located by gaze (§4); a vertical time line
with the past uphill and the future downhill (§5); path carried by the verb so the hand
