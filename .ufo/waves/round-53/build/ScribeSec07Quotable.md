## 7. Quotable gestures and emblem repertoires

- **Boundary**: the inventory of emblems, their geography and their collision examples are `../culture-material/adornment-body-codes.md:79,:92-93`; this section owns only how a repertoire is documented and specified, and who may use it.
- **What a documented repertoire is**: Brookes's repertoire of quotable gestures among black urban South Africans in Gauteng province falls into three gestural types — lexical, holophrastic and concept (NEW-ScribeSec07Quotable-1) — and its published dictionary gives every entry an English gloss, the local term, a language label (that dictionary prints Is = Iscamtho, S = Sotho, Z = Zulu) and a body description (NEW-ScribeSec07Quotable-1); the entries reach greeting, money, eating, silence and insult (NEW-ScribeSec07Quotable-1).
- **How one is documented**: observations and video of spontaneous talk, elicitation interviews and a decoding test (NEW-ScribeSec07Quotable-1); in the wider literature, procedural inventories with a stated decoding threshold, observational inventories, and emblem dictionaries that are “usually light on methods” (NEW-ScribeSec07Quotable-2).
- **Two older lineages**: de Jorio's 1832 Naples treatise states its aim as “un saggio della mimica de' Napoletani” and prints 21 plates (NEW-ScribeSec07Quotable-6); Kendon's recordings near Salerno separate two gestures that carry emblem status from two that only mark discourse structure (NEW-ScribeSec07Quotable-3); Payrató's emblem has autonomy from speech, communicative purpose, illocutionary force, a semantic core and a social nature, and he calls emblematization a process beside lexicalization and grammaticalization (NEW-ScribeSec07Quotable-4).

| Repertoire size | Domains it must cover | Who may use which |
|---|---|---|
| a household set: a few dozen entries, greeting and insult (design frame, **H**) | insult emblems seem especially widely attested (NEW-ScribeSec07Quotable-2) | everyone, and outsiders pick them up by contact, because emblems are used without speech and travel across language barriers (NEW-ScribeSec07Quotable-2) |

| one register per trade or rite (design frame, **H**) | whatever a silence rule pushes into the hand (design frame, **H**) | specialists and initiates only (design frame, **H**) |
