| Index finger | US undergraduates: 450 of 496 points (91%), and 15 of 16 preferred manual pointing (NEW-ScribeSec04Pointing-3, p. 8, Table 2) | singles out one referent: “a speaker singles out an object which is to be attended to as a particular individual object” (Kendon 2004, 205, as reported in NEW-ScribeSec04Pointing-6, p. 146) | a default your culture borrows, not a law: where index pointing is claimed to be absent, “other forms of pointing (e.g., lip-pointing, open hand pointing, middle finger pointing) may be dominant” (NEW-ScribeSec04Pointing-4, p. 89) |
| Whole hand (flat hand, fingers together) | Northamptonshire (England) and Campania (Italy), in Kendon’s index-hand and flat-hand data (NEW-ScribeSec04Pointing-6, p. 145); Arrernte (central Australia) keep a “wide hand point” among six handshapes (NEW-ScribeSec04Pointing-6, p. 146) | a referent linked to the topic rather than the topic itself: “the object being indicated is not itself the primary focus or topic of the discourse but is something that is linked to the topic” (Kendon 2004, 208, as reported in NEW-ScribeSec04Pointing-6, p. 146) | give the flat hand a job the index finger does not do; a variant with no contrast of its own is a redundancy (**H**) |
