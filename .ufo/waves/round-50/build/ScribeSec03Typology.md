## 3. Pastoral systems typology: herd mobility as the design dial

**Master claim.** The herd's mobility pattern sorts the systems; settlement, labour and risk follow from it. Read the rows as dials inside one economy, not a ladder from "primitive" to "settled" (`mongol.md:40`).

| System | Mobility pattern | Settlement | Labour | Risk strategy | Status / cite |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nomadic** | whole household and herd move on a cycle that follows forage; no fixed base | portable dwellings and goods; the chest is "first and last furniture of the poor and the nomad" (`furniture-interior-lexicon.md:81`) | household herding; division by age and sex is culture-specific `(to-verify)` | mixed herd spreads species risk (`animal-symbolism.md:110`); movement is the hedge | B — [SRC: https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/items/9683d150-cd4e-4762-b879-72333e87c8ac \| "the ecology of nomadic pastoralism" (abstract, sentence 3)] |
| **Semi-nomadic** | part of household and herd travels; a base is held year-round | held base (village or winter camp) with satellite camps | base keeps the old, young and any crop plot; herd party travels `(to-verify)` | the held base is the store and fallback in drought | H — design dial between rows 1 and 3; no opened source defines the term `(to-verify)` |
| **Transhumance, vertical** | fixed seasonal cycle between lowland and highland pasture; routes fixed | two bases: valley village and summer hut `(to-verify)` | herd party travels, base keeps households; "greater herding labour required" | seasonal movement answers harsh climate and low pasture yield; "an ecological necessity" | B — [SRC: https://researchers.une.edu.au/en/publications/transhumance-livestock-mobility-and-mutual-bene%EF%AC%81ts-between-crop-a-5/ \| "routes for livestock movement are fixed" (abstract, sentence 2)]; vertical/horizontal split itself `(to-verify)` |
