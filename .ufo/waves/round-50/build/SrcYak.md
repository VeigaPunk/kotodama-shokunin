## What the primary row teaches C (section map)

- **§3 typology:** transhumance is the predominant management form, not one option among equals — warm-season high pastures with frequently moved campsites, cold-season lower pastures nearer the winter home; alpine areas run four-season (or three-season combined) grazing; the plateau has three systems (nomadic, semi-settled, small-holder) that *all* keep seasonal movement. Good for the vertical/horizontal transhumance rows and the "settlement" column.
- **§4 species/herd:** the yak as the cold-highland species; herd split into lactating cows, dry cows, replacement stock, steers (pack yak); yak and yak-cattle hybrids used for ploughing and carrying loads (ch. 8 Overview, para. 9).
- **§6 products:** the full chain in one authoritative place — raw milk to milk tea; butter churned in a wooden bucket or a hide bag, stored in calf-hide/rumen bags; skimmed-milk cottage cheese; Swiss-type cheese (Nepal); pre-winter slaughter, then freezing or drying, sausage and viscera as casings/storage; hides and leather; coarse hair and fine down to ropes, garments, tents; tail hair ceremonial/fly-whisk; dung as fuel and wall material; draught and pack use.
- **§7 wealth:** "many herders, perhaps most, still equate numbers of animals with wealth and status" (ch. 8 Overview, para. 4; verified substring) and the insurance logic of herd growth; pre-winter culling/counting as the annual accounting.
- **§8 tenure:** village grassland divided into warm-season and cold-season categories; state land with privately owned animals; several families or a village sharing pastures with rights fixed by agreement and penalties; fencing and household allocation as the recent change. Pair with Candidate 2 for the policy-pressure side.
- **§9 relations/labour:** herder control by voice and slung stones, night tethering for milking and predator protection, keeping the herd off another's territory; the seasonal labour calendar (births, shearing, milking/mating, culling/slaughter, winter shelter, spring supplementation).
- **§10 ritual:** ch. 12 supplies yak sacrifice at festivals, yak as dowry, butter sculptures as offerings, and the "god yak" release (the last quoted there from Miller et al. 1997 — nested quotation, attribute carefully).
- **§13 calibration:** (a) the "nomadic system … may account for no more than ten percent of the total yak population in China" is a **1999** estimate cited in ch. 8 (Dong et al.) — do not present pure nomadism as the typical contemporary plateau system; (b) ch. 8's own caution, "Generalizations about the management of yak and the uses to which yak are put are bound to be an oversimplification" (20 words, verified), belongs in the regional-model-discipline row; (c) Candidate 2's sedentarization judgement is an argument, not consensus.

**Living-practice flag:** ch. 12 describes Tibetan Buddhist and pre-Buddhist practice as living culture. The entry must use these as source-specific examples, not as a generic "pastoralists do X" law.

## Tier reasoning (QUALITY criterion 6)

Both rows are **B**. FAO is an authoritative publisher and this is FAO's technical reference on yak, but the quoted sentences are a synthesis of cited field reports and experiments (the text cites Wiener 1996, Long and Ma 1996, Miller 1990, Dong et al. 1999, etc.), not the authors' own primary measurement; the same applies to the Tashi Nyima case study, which is an argued policy review. Criterion 6 reserves A for sources primary or authoritative for the *exact* scope, and the r49 correction (S034/S038/S039 → S044–S046) established that a publisher's framing is not primary authority. I did not inflate either row. If L1 needs an A-tier plateau row, the honest route is a peer-reviewed primary study with a plateau sample, not this book.

## Mined-mistake candidate for `corpus/lessons/round-50.md` (hand to ScribeLesson/L1)

Two quotation errors in my own first draft were caught only by the mechanical substring check, not by reading:

1. I attributed "Butter, made in traditional fashion, is the main product from the milk in most places" to ch. 8; it is in **ch. 10**, Overview para. 2. Chapter drift within a multi-chapter web book is easy because the reader shows only one chapter at a time.
2. I drafted "allocating pasture to families" from the Tashi Nyima bullet; the source says "**allocate** pasture to families". A tense change inside quotation marks would have been a conformance defect.

Lesson: quote from the fetched text, never from memory of the previous read; run one exact-substring pass per quote before the row is pasted. This is the same class as the r49 SOURCES mistakes (a quote that reads right but is not in the source), and it is cheaper to catch than a reviewer rejection.
