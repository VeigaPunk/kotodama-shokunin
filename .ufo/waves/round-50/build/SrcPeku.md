## Precision traps for the C author

1. **Do not average the glosses.** AHD glosses the *root* "Wealth, movable
   property"; Pokorny glosses the *noun* as livestock. Writing "PIE \*peku-
   meant livestock and wealth" as if one source said both flattens a real
   difference. Say: the noun's reflexes cover livestock; the derivatives
   (pecūnia, pecūlium) carry the property/money sense; the chronology is
   reconstructed.
2. **Varro is testimony, not economic history.** "Cattle were the first money"
   is not asserted by any opened row. Varro (1st c. BC) says the ancients'
   wealth consisted in cattle; Lewis & Short repeats him. Keep it as Roman
   self-description and as a design rationale, not as a datum about the origin
   of money.
3. **A reconstruction is not a society.** Lexical derivation dates nothing and
   proves no demographic or archaeological fact. No opened row supports "the
   PIE homeland was pastoral" or any date for herding.
4. **EA004 measures dependence, not mobility.** Do not infer nomadism from it;
   the mobility typology needs its own opened source (still missing).
5. **Pure pastoralism is a tail, and Varro separates grazing from farming.**
   12/1,290 at 86–100% dependence; Varro himself treats crops and herds as
   distinct arts. Write mixed economies by default.
6. **Three notations for one phoneme.** AHD prints `*pek̑u-` (k + U+0311
   combining inverted breve), Wiktionary prints `péḱu` (U+1E31, k with acute),
   StarLing prints `pek̂-` (k + U+0302 combining circumflex). Pick one
   convention in the entry and say which source uses which; do not silently
   re-spell a quoted form.
7. **A citation line is not an open.** Wiktionary's references to De Vaan and
   Pokorny are not evidence that either was read; only the StarLing page was.
   This is the r49 laundering pattern in a new dress.
