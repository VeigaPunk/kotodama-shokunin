## 1. The register property: pastoralism is an argument about grass, water, animals and mobility

**Master claim.** A pastoral culture is not "herders who lack fields". It is a production system that reads rangeland as a moving schedule and holds the herd as the household's living capital: a stock that breeds, sells, lends and walks. Where the hunting register argues about the patch (`hunting-foraging-subsistence.md:27`) and the agrarian register about the field (`agrarian-field-system-lexicon.md:4`), this one argues about forage, water, and the animal that turns both into milk, wealth and movement. [SRC: https://www.fao.org/4/y2647e/y2647e02.htm | "pastoral society is, above all, driven by the nature and requirements of different species" | Overview] The forage side is one sentence: "The productivity of a pastoral livestock production system depends largely on animal management, availability of water and the distribution, productivity and quality of forage." [SRC: https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/0ce87f59-bca8-48ad-aa67-51fed1898f1f/content/x5552e03.htm | same sentence | §1.2, para 6]

**Distinct-register test.** Pastoralism needs medium population density plus extensive semi-arid rangeland; where hunting-gathering is easy, "there is little or no impetus to herd animals." [SRC: https://www.fao.org/4/y2647e/y2647e02.htm | "there is little or no impetus to herd animals" | Classifying pastoral systems, para 2]. Four tests decide membership:

| Test | Pass when | Fails as (owner) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Herded capital**: stock is bred, held and culled as a standing store | "livestock are a proxy for wealth in pastoral society" [SRC: https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/0ce87f59-bca8-48ad-aa67-51fed1898f1f/content/x5552e03.htm \| same \| §1.3.2] | a hunt: `hunting-foraging-subsistence.md:36` ("not planted, herded or owned") |
| **2. Mobility tracks forage**: movement is the production logic, not an episode | transhumance is "the regular movement of herds among fixed points" [SRC: https://www.fao.org/4/y2647e/y2647e02.htm \| same \| Transhumance] | a fixed mixed farm: `agrarian-field-system-lexicon.md:62` owns the agropastoral row |
| **3. Labour-capped herd**: household labour sets the herd it can hold | herd size "determined by the labour needed to manage the herds and flocks" [SRC: https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/0ce87f59-bca8-48ad-aa67-51fed1898f1f/content/x5552e03.htm \| same \| §1.2, para 1] | enclosed ranching, "the land is individually owned and usually fenced" [SRC: https://www.fao.org/4/y2647e/y2647e02.htm \| same \| Enclosed systems and ranching] |
| **4. Distribution by rule**: animals move as gift, loan and exchange | "Livestock are also an important medium of social exchange" [SRC: https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/0ce87f59-bca8-48ad-aa67-51fed1898f1f/content/x5552e03.htm \| same \| §1.2, para 4] | a market trade: `trade-economy-lexicon.md:3` |

**Calibration** (QUALITY criterion 2):
- **Mainstream:** the four tests and the mobility typology nomadism → transhumance → agropastoralism (FAO 2001, Pastoral enterprises); the rainfall-driven forage logic.
- **Contested:** "pasture and water must be granted as rights" is Fratkin's policy stance inside the tragedy-of-the-commons debate, one side only. [SRC: https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/items/da4238e1-8a2f-49d5-bcaf-8259e0cc0b3b | "For the future, pasture and water must be granted as rights" | Abstract]
- **Discredited frame:** "Pastoralism was seen as an evolutionary stage in human history" (hunting → herding → farming). Replace with surplus logic: "Pastoralism develops from surplus." [SRC: https://www.fao.org/4/y2647e/y2647e02.htm | both quotes | History and origins, para 2]
- **Bounded:** the herd-inequality figure ("the richest 20% of households controlled 60% of the cattle") and the productivity data are one Kajiado Maasai study, 1981–85, in years the authors call "favourable." Quote place and date only. [SRC: https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/0ce87f59-bca8-48ad-aa67-51fed1898f1f/content/x5552e03.htm | "Conditions were favourable for livestock production during this period." | §1.1, para 3]
- **Herd is not always capital:** where smuggling or trade lead, "pastoralist economic dependence on livestock is slight." Decide per culture which store holds the wealth. [SRC: https://www.fao.org/4/y2647e/y2647e02.htm | same | Pastoralism and trade]
- **(to-verify):** Dahl & Hjort 1976, *Having herds*, the herd-growth source behind the household formulation, is cited through Bekure §1.2; the book is unopened. Khazanov's comparative survey is the long-form anchor: reviewer's "the first comparative survey of pastoral societies that can claim to be truly comprehensive" [SRC: https://uwpress.wisc.edu/Books/N/Nomads-and-the-Outside-World | same | Reviews of the first edition]; body unopened.
