## 7. Herd as wealth and exchange — bridewealth, loans, herd-splitting, raids, and the cattle-complex critique

**Master claim.** A herd works as three instruments at once: marriage currency, loanable capital and risk buffer. Design each transfer by what moves, who gains rights, and what it insures; "cattle wealth" is not one value.

| Transfer | What moves / who gains | Design rule | Calibration and source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bridewealth | stock from groom's kin to bride's kin; claims on wives and children | incidence follows descent: highest in patrilineal and double-descent groups, lowest in bilateral [SRC: https://hraf.yale.edu/ehc/documents/337 \| "The incidence of bridewealth decreases along a kin group scale"] | B — Goody's Ethnographic Atlas hypothesis, bounded to its sample; not a universal law |
| Stock loan | a cow lent to another herder; borrower keeps early calves (habbanaae type) | redistributive loan is not a herding contract (wage in milk or grain) [SRC: https://openknowledge.fao.org/…/x5439e03.htm \| §3.14 "many examples of livestock 'loans'"] | B — Sahel secondary review; Dupire 1962 (to-verify) |
| Herd pooling and division | joint herd sent with one or two members; animals spread across sites | pooling saves labour; division needs labour a household may lack [SRC: FAO ch.3 \| §3.12, §3.16 "herd division"] | B — insurance logic rests on Dahl & Hjort 1976, cited via FAO §3.05 (to-verify); Galaty 1981 "herd dispersion" (to-verify) |
| Raid or theft | stock taken to rebuild after loss | a fast restock route; coercive form of exchange, chosen by relative power [SRC: FAO ch.3 \| §3.15 "alternative means"; https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/items/d5a32be5-e9df-40b9-b659-d134705afd64/full \| "the more specialized mobile pastoralists become, the more dependent"] | B — Khazanov's thesis from a catalogue abstract; detail (to-verify) |

| Claim | Status | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| Cattle as the single value organising all life (Herskovits 1926 "cattle complex") | C — value-complex, not a production budget; primary unopened (to-verify) | list each instrument above and what it secures; cattle idiom is a register, not an economy |
| Secondary-products revolution: milk, wool and traction spread together | C — Marciniak 2011 says the shift is "far from direct and straightforward" [SRC: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10963-011-9045-7] | staged, regional, with faunal profiles |
| Pastoralists are self-sufficient outside the herd | R — herds need grain, metal and markets; Khazanov reads raiding as one such exchange | write the dependence the herd creates |
| Nuer cattle "the idiom of all value" (`trade-economy-lexicon.md:69`) | T — in-corpus assertion; Evans-Pritchard 1940 unopened here | cite only after the ethnography is opened (to-verify) |

Anchors (in-corpus, path:line): `hunting-foraging-subsistence.md:116` (meat as currency — the herd case is firmer, living capital), `inca.md:131` (a non-market transfer creates allegiance), `scythian-saka.md:70` (pastoralism is not absence of farming), `finnic-sami.md:150` (no fleet-wide "reindeer revolution"), `egypt-ancient.md:128` (cattle counts are not automatic annual censuses), `dine-navajo.md:125` (introduced sheep are not unchanged antiquity), `india-vedic.md:107` (cattle gifts as ritual correspondence), `slavs-early.md:98` (Volos as cattle god in oath sanctions).
