```markdown
## 8. Tenure, commons and mobility rights

A herd move claims three things: a route, a well and a pasture. Each is a rule with an owner, not a landscape feature. Mobility types stay at `corpus/knowledge/culture-material/hunting-foraging-subsistence.md:96`; the water-right chain stays at `corpus/knowledge/culture-material/agrarian-field-system-lexicon.md:44,:90`.

| Claim object | Tenure form to design | Evidence and calibration |
|---|---|---|
| Seasonal route / winter pasture | user right of passage, not property; the bottleneck is access (`corpus/knowledge/civilizations/mongol.md:166`) | [SRC: FAO-ALIVE §6.3 p.10 "access and user rights (not necessarily property rights) to critical grazing and water resources"] B, SSA-bounded |
| Well / water point | a turn plus a maintenance duty, agreed with users (`agrarian-field-system-lexicon.md:90`) | [SRC: FAO-ALIVE §6.4a p.10 "Infrastructure needs concern mostly water"] B; well-turn customs (to-verify) |
| Common pasture | rights tied to village or manor, owed in labour (`agrarian-field-system-lexicon.md:56`) | single European case; [SRC: FAO-ALIVE §2.2a p.2 "The encroached rangelands included the better dry season grazing areas with easier access to water."] B: the first land lost |

Hardin vs Ostrom: calibrate the access state, do not pick a winner.

| Model | Proposition (located) | Status | Design use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hardin 1968, open-access commons | [SRC: HARDIN-1968 §"Tragedy of Freedom in a Commons" p.1244 "Freedom in a commons brings ruin to all."] | R as universal; C as description of unregulated access | a designed collapse only where access is genuinely open |
| Hardin's remedy | [SRC: HARDIN-1968 §"Mutual Coercion" p.1247 "mutual coercion, mutually agreed upon by the majority of the people affected."] | C: the remedy is itself a tenure rule | a herders' rota with sanctions they chose |
| Ostrom 1990, managed common-pool | [SRC: OSTROM-1990 blurb p.1 "common-pool problems sometimes are solved by voluntary organizations rather than by a coercive state."] | B for named cases (meadows, irrigation, fisheries); design principles (to-verify) | turn-rota, fines and monitors as institutions |
| Fratkin 1997 review | [SRC: FRATKIN-1997 abstract "the “tragedy of the commons” debate"] | C: the debate is live in pastoral governance | an in-world argument between herders and officials |

| Driver | Mechanism | Evidence and calibration |
|---|---|---|
| State settlement policy | [SRC: FAO-ALIVE §2.1 p.2 "Governments are sometimes promoting settlement to intensify and commercialize animal production."] | B, SSA |
| Forced move | [SRC: FAO-ALIVE §2.1 p.2 "Involuntary settlement of pastoralists by Governments has also been reported because of dam construction, famine, and civil war."] | B |
| Privatization / colonial land law | [SRC: FAO-ALIVE §3.2c p.3 "Land became either owned by the State or was privatized to serve colonial settlers and/or national elites."] | B; European enclosure history (to-verify) |
| Sedentarization as endpoint | settlement is local history, not a fixed stage (`corpus/knowledge/civilizations/finnic-sami.md:150`; `corpus/knowledge/civilizations/dine-navajo.md:125`) | C; never write settlement as the endpoint of mobility |

Handoff (lore-shokunin): who holds each route, well and pasture; sanction and fine names; whether any enclosure law is in-world fact. Decided there, not here. Sahelian well and rota customs remain (to-verify).
```
