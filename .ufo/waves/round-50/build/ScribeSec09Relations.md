```
## 9. Pastoral–sedentary relations: interdependence, trade, tribute, conquest and raiding

**Master claim.** A herding economy is non-autarkic by construction: it must acquire grain, handicrafts and metal, and it sells livestock and secondary products (wool, hides) that sedentary economies value. Every pastoral–sedentary relation is a designed exchange with a power term attached, not a default state of war or peace. Build it as a row, name the lever each side holds, and let the neighbour's strength set the form. Status: non-autarky is **mainstream** [K19][D87]; the claim that nomadic political complexity is caused by sedentary states is **contested** [B20][N23].

| Relation | What moves, and who holds the lever | Design check | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interdependence | livestock and secondary products out; grain and crafts in; sedentary merchants carry the goods, no professional nomad trader class | who carries the goods and who sets the price? | mainstream — non-autarky [K19] |
| Trade under pressure | sedentary state opens markets only to subordinates; nomads force opening by arms | the licence and its price term are the plot | attested in detail (China–Xiongnu treaties; Uyghur–China forced trade) [K19] |
| Tribute and gift | dependent cultivators deliver grain and slaves; gifts recruit followers | tribute is an income line, not a one-off levy | attested (Scythian, Xiongnu, Türk); silk-roll figures (to-verify) [K19] |
| Tolls and passage | polities sell safe passage; caravans pay in food, cloth, coin | the toll rate sets trade volume | attested (Ibn Fadlan's account, 922) [K19] |
| Raiding | livestock and people, not grain, are the typical target; rival polities raid caravans | raid targets follow the form of wealth | attested as pattern [N23]; origin argument (to-verify) |
| Conquest state | nomadic empire extracts tribute and controls transit; statehood forms against a powerful neighbour | the state needs an external rival; name it | **contested** — Barfield frontier thesis [B20] vs inter-nomadic-dynamics critique (to-verify) |

- **Bias flags.** Chinese dynastic histories are the main outside witness to steppe politics: indispensable, not neutral (`corpus/knowledge/civilizations/turkic-steppe.md:193`). Reject any ladder from "raider" to "civilized state" (`turkic-steppe.md:194`; `civilizations/mongol.md:40`; `civilizations/arabia-pre-islamic.md:174`). "Barbarian" is a calibration defect: describe the neighbour by its trade role and its power.
- **Complexity follows the neighbour.** Where rivals were stateless (East Africa), pastoral polities stayed decentralised; the largest steppe polities arose against China [N23]. Write the neighbour's power first, the nomad's organisation second.
- **Coupling.** Exchange instruments: `culture-material/trade-economy-lexicon.md:131,145` (name the instrument after the risk-bearer, e.g. caravan-share). Agropastoral manure-for-stubble contracts: `culture-material/agrarian-field-system-lexicon.md:62`. Meat-for-crops with farming neighbours: `culture-material/hunting-foraging-subsistence.md:116`.
- **Handoff.** Lore-shokunin owns which tribute route exists, which raid happened, and who holds the frontier; this section supplies relation types and checks only.
```
