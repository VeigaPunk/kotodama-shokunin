citation: Ben Elliott and Chantal Conneller, 2021 (issue dated 2020), "Masks in context: representation, emergence, motility and self", *World Archaeology* 52(5): 655–666, editorial introducing the special issue "Masks"; DOI 10.1080/00438243.2020.1996770

kind: paper (journal editorial; read as full text on the archived journal page)
tier: B — peer-reviewed journal editorial by the special-issue editors: authoritative for how that volume frames masking, secondary for every ethnographic case it summarizes, and not a statement by any community
scope: the analytical distinction between masks that disguise a body and masks that transform one; the claim that a transformative mask does not deceive its audience; the volume's non-representational reading ("what they do rather than what they depict"); the explicit refusal of a universal definition of masks
quotes:
- "Power and representation", ¶1: "Many of the authors take a non-representational approach to masks – seeking to explore what they do rather than what they depict."
- "Transformations", ¶1: "draw an ontological distinction between the ability of masks to conceal or disguise a body"
- "Transformations", ¶8: "The audience is not ‘deceived’ by the mask – its relationship to the wearer is openly presented as part of the performance"
- "Conclusion", ¶1: "the search for universalist definitions of what masks are and what masks do is fundamentally futile"

teaches: masking is not one act — a world must choose per culture whether its mask hides a person or produces a being, and that choice changes what the audience is owed.

draft sentences:
- Decide first what the mask does to its wearer: the editors separate ontologies in which a mask conceals or disguises a body from ontologies in which it transforms one, and treat these as different world rules, not as stages of the same belief (NEW-ScoutMaskTheory-1, "Transformations", ¶1).
- If your masks transform, write the audience as un-deceived: the editors describe a rite where the wearer's human face is shown to the audience and the relationship between mask and wearer is openly performed, so the transformation is staged rather than hidden (NEW-ScoutMaskTheory-1, "Transformations", ¶1, ¶8).
- Do not design masks as pictures first: the volume's contributors ask what masks do rather than what they depict, which means the mask's job in a scene outranks its iconography — the editors' own examples are ancestor-making by painted mummy faces (¶4) and masks that assemble cattle, human bodies and the divine (¶8) (NEW-ScoutMaskTheory-1, "Power and representation", ¶1, ¶4, ¶8).
- Refuse a single definition for your world: the editors conclude that searching for universal definitions of what masks are and what they do is futile, so give each culture its own answer and let the answers conflict (NEW-ScoutMaskTheory-1, "Conclusion", ¶1).

teaches: the secret in a masking society is a rule about who may know, and access is graded by category, so a world's masks come with an access ladder rather than a single ban.

draft sentences:
- Budget the whole apparition, not the face: in the surveyed systems a mask is the entire costumed appearance — headpiece, costume, ornament, whatever is carried — with the unseen dancer, the dance routine and the music added, and swapping accessories can make it a different mask (NEW-ScoutMaskTheory-2, "The African Masking Configuration", ¶2).
- Put masking at the passages that change status: these rituals cluster in initiation and death, appear only occasionally in healing, rarely in sacrifice, hardly ever in agricultural or rain rites, and never in marriage or birth (NEW-ScoutMaskTheory-2, "The African Masking Configuration", ¶3).
- Write the secret as a right, not a fact: what is held secret is that the wearers are costumed men, a secret the author calls as public as it is obvious, and the uninitiated are said to have no right to that knowledge (NEW-ScoutMaskTheory-2, "The African Masking Configuration", ¶4).
- Grade the ladder instead of banning everyone: postmenopausal women are described as unafraid and familiar with masked figures, and "sisters of the mask" feed the dancers as partial initiates, while ranked societies divide initiates into echelons by staged revelation — decide which categories your world places where (NEW-ScoutMaskTheory-2, "The Masking Option", ¶3–¶4).
