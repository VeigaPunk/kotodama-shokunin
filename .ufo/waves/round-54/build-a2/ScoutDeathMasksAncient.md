# ScoutDeathMasksAncient — funerary masks as portrait or idealised face

Assigned question: what do holding-museum records say about two funerary-mask
objects — the gold mask from Mycenae known as the "mask of Agamemnon", and an
Egyptian cartonnage mummy mask — regarding date, who the mask covered, and
whether it is a portrait?

Round 54, lane `kotodama-shokunin`, base `260c68d`, access date 2026-10-22.
All quotes below were verified as exact substrings of the bytes fetched in this
session (2026-10-22); the Greek description quotes are the record's own text and
the bracketed English renderings sit outside the quotation marks.

Result in one line: the Mycenae record gives a dated, single-grave, cord-tied
gold mask whose face it reads as individual likeness while keeping the heroic
name conventional; the British Museum record gives an anonymous Egyptian
cartonnage mask whose gold face and wide-open eyes are described as vitality,
whose period label is explicitly uncertain, and whose surface carries funerary
text and gods.

  (NEW-ScoutDeathMasksAncient-2, Cultures/periods; Production date)
