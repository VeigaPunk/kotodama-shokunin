# ScoutSowei — Sande sowei / ndoli jowei helmet masks (claim C source scout)

Seat: KotodamaR54a2.ScoutSowei. Assigned unknown: the sowei or ndoli jowei helmet masks of
the Sande society (Mende, Sierra Leone and Liberia) — who may commission, carve, wear, see or
hear the mask, what the mask is said to be, when it appears, what it licenses, what is
restricted. Four sources opened this session by plain request, all HTTP 200, access date
2026-10-22; no browser, no captcha, no login, no paywall, no installs or upgrades.

Result: four registry-ready rows. Three of them (1, 3, 4) carry the social architecture —
commissioning, making versus wearing, seclusion, lodge structure, public appearance — and the
fourth is a holding museum's own catalogue reading of the mask type. What the rows do **not**
carry, and the entry must not invent:

1. No Sande or Mende community statement was opened. Every sentence about what the mask "is"
   here is either a researcher's report of what she was told and allowed to do (row 1) or an
   outside institution's interpretation (rows 2, 3, 4).
2. No opened row states a rule that non-members may not see the masker at the public
   performance. The rules the rows carry are narrower and are about the bare headpiece:
   Sande women let a woman researcher see and measure headpieces, sometimes in an enclosure
   men could not enter, while villages keeping stricter protocol showed only the fully
   masked and costumed figure.
3. The sowei masker is not documented here as speaking in a disguised or translated voice.
   Phillips describes attendants' "musical, high-pitched chanting of her name", not the
   masker's own speech. Do not transfer the voice-disguiser material (ScoutMaskVoice) onto
   sowei.
4. "Mende male artist" is a catalogue attribution convention (row 3 captions). No opened row
   says how a carver is chosen, trained or paid, so the entry must not describe a guild.

### NEW-ScoutSowei-1

- citation: Phillips, Ruth B. 2009. "The Mask Stripped Bare by its Curators: The Work of Hybridity in the Twenty-First Century." *Les actes de colloques du musée du quai Branly Jacques Chirac* 1 (2009), doi 10.4000/actesbranly.336. Article body read in full; notes and references present.
- url: https://journals.openedition.org/actesbranly/336
- route: direct plain `curl`, HTTP 200, 141,215 bytes, server-rendered HTML. A first request sent with a browser user-agent string returned HTTP 500 with a 1,618-byte error page; the same URL fetched with a plain request (no user-agent header) returned the article. No challenge, cookie, login or paywall. Quotes re-checked against the saved bytes, with spaces introduced by inline italic markup removed.
- kind: paper (conference-proceedings essay, open access)
- tier: A for Phillips's own report of her 1970s fieldwork in Sierra Leone — the translation problem, what Sande women allowed her to see, and the chief's answer. Not A for Mende belief in general: the row is one researcher's account of what was said to her, and Phillips herself limits it to "public discourse".
- scope: what the carved headpiece is called and how it relates to costume and spirit in Mende usage as Phillips reports it; the two conditions under which Sande women let her see headpieces; the refusal of a partial display. Not a general account of Sande initiation.
- quotes:
  - paragraph 2 (beginning "My focus on the carved headpiece"): "The Mende, like most African peoples, do not have a separate word for “mask,”"
  - paragraph 2: "the phrase that most closely translates the English “sowei mask” is sowo wui (“the head of sowei”)"
  - paragraph 2: "the masked being’s identity as an ngafa, or spirit, as separable from the material and mechanical components"
  - paragraph 2: "the ndoli sowei could only be seen when fully and correctly garbed"
- teaches: the headpiece is a "head", not a separable artwork, and a society can require the whole costume before the being is present at all.
- draft sentences:
  - Decide what your language calls the thing before you name the office: Phillips found the Mende had no separate word for "mask", and that the closest phrase for a sowei mask is *sowo wui*, "the head of sowei" (NEW-ScoutSowei-1, ¶2).
  - Decide whether the mask is an object or a presence: to speak of the headpiece apart from the costume, or of the *ngafa* (spirit) apart from the impersonator, "implies a distinction which the Mende do not verbalize in public discourse" (NEW-ScoutSowei-1, ¶2).
  - Split the access rules by object and by figure: Sande women let Phillips see and measure bare headpieces, "sometimes in an enclosed area that men could not enter", while villages keeping stricter protocol brought out only the fully masked and costumed figure (NEW-ScoutSowei-1, ¶2).
  - Let the mask be a head, not a prop: the chief who arranged her meeting said the ndoli sowei "could only be seen when fully and correctly garbed", and asked her whether she could go out without her head (NEW-ScoutSowei-1, ¶2).

- quotes:

- draft sentences:

  - Separate the office from the person inside it: the name is *sowei* or *ndoli jowei*, "the Sande leader" or "the expert leader who dances", and the mask is "worn exclusively by women, a rarity in African masquerades" (NEW-ScoutSowei-2, catalogue text, first paragraph).
  - Fix the calendar of appearances: the museum lists important funerals among the occasions, with the masker most publicly visible during girls' initiation into Sande (NEW-ScoutSowei-2, catalogue text, second paragraph).
  - If the face is a lesson, choose the lesson: narrowed eyes and a composed, half-smiling mouth are read as the poise and self-control the young women are expected to assume (NEW-ScoutSowei-2, catalogue text, third paragraph).
  - Design the mask to be a role model, not a portrait: as "manifestations of the guiding spirit of Sande and potent role models", the museum says, the masks reinforce what the initiates learned and codify community values (NEW-ScoutSowei-2, catalogue text, second paragraph).

### NEW-ScoutSowei-3

- citation: Curnow, Kathy. *The Bright Continent: African Art History*, chapter 3.4 "Art and Youth Initiation". Cleveland State University Libraries open textbook (Pressbooks), CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; author and licence read from the book front page. LibreTexts copy of the same chapter.
- url: https://human.libretexts.org/Sandboxes/admin/The_Bright_Continent_-_African_Art_History_(Curnow)/03:_Themes_in_African_Art/3.04:_Art_and_Youth_Initiation (book front page used for the author and licence: https://pressbooks.ulib.csuohio.edu/bright-continent/)
- route: direct plain `curl`, HTTP 200, 196,825 bytes on the LibreTexts copy; book front page HTTP 200, 142,823 bytes. No login, no challenge.
- kind: book (open textbook chapter)
- tier: B. An academic art historian's teaching synthesis with figure captions and a cited researcher (Boone); secondary, and its UNESCO membership and excision statistics are second hand in this row.
- scope: the Sande/Bondo society's place among initiation societies, the female water-spirit masquerader and her role across initiation, masks catalogued to male Mende artists while the wearer is a woman, facial features as teaching, and the *gonde* clown's reuse of retired masks.
- quotes:
  - section "Sande and Bondo/Bundu of Sierra Leone and Liberia", paragraph on the masquerader: "its tutelary female deity, a water spirit, is personified by a masquerader who is also female"
  - same section, next paragraph: "escorts the initiates to their bush camp, keeps them company during their months of excision and training, and brings them back into town"
  - same section, paragraph on beauty as behaviour: "ears are larger than mouths so that graduates might listen more than they speak"
  - same section, paragraph on the *gonde*: "The gonde mask is usually an old sowei mask that has suffered termite or other damage and has been retired from its original function."
- teaches: a women's society can put a female spirit in a female performer who works through the whole initiation, while the masks it wears are catalogued to male artists and can be demoted to clowning when damaged.
- draft sentences:
  - Make the masked spirit female and benevolent: Curnow calls it a regional exception that "its tutelary female deity, a water spirit, is personified by a masquerader who is also female", unlike the feared men's-society masker (NEW-ScoutSowei-3, §"Sande and Bondo/Bundu of Sierra Leone and Liberia").
  - Give the masker a job across the whole rite, not only the parade: she escorts the initiates to the bush camp, keeps them company through training, brings them back to town, and may also dance at civic occasions (NEW-ScoutSowei-3, same section).
  - Separate who carves from who wears: the captions credit the masks to "Mende male artist" while the performer is a woman, so making and wearing are two different qualifications (NEW-ScoutSowei-3, fig. 325 caption).
  - Give each feature a rule: following Boone, Curnow reports that "ears are larger than mouths so that graduates might listen more than they speak", with broad foreheads for honesty and generosity and downcast eyes for modesty (NEW-ScoutSowei-3, same section).
  - Plan the afterlife of a retired mask: the *gonde* clown wears an old, damaged sowei mask retired from its first function, or a newly carved asymmetrical grotesque, inverting the dignified type (NEW-ScoutSowei-3, same section).

- url: https://sierraleoneheritage.org/v12.6/glossary/word.php?id=sande (companion: https://sierraleoneheritage.org/v12.6/glossary/word.php?id=sowei)

- kind: web (project glossary)

- scope: the society's purpose and seclusion practice, its non-centralised lodge structure, the rank term *sowei*, and the public masker's name, costume and stated ideal. Not an account of initiation rites and not evidence about restricted knowledge.
- quotes:
  - "Sande" entry, main text, first sentence: "Mende name for a women's society that is responsible for the preparation and education of girls for their role as adult women."
  - "Sande" entry, main text, paragraph on organisation: "Sande is not a centralised institution, but takes the form of numerous more or less independent local lodges with common practices and traditions."
  - "Sande" entry, main text, paragraph on the masker: "who wears a black-dyed wooden helmet mask above a costume of black-dyed raffia and cloth"
  - "Sande" entry, main text, paragraph on the masker: "The mask is an idealised image of female beauty and dignity."
- teaches: the masker is one public face of a decentralised society of local lodges whose business is girls' education, and the mask is a black wooden helmet over a black raffia costume.
- draft sentences:
  - Design the society around a curriculum: Sande is defined as a women's society responsible for preparing and educating girls for adult roles, with initiates in seclusion in the society "bush" under mature women (NEW-ScoutSowei-4, "Sande" entry, main text).
  - Do not give it a headquarters: the glossary says Sande "is not a centralised institution" but many more or less independent local lodges with common practices, so rules can differ lodge by lodge (NEW-ScoutSowei-4, "Sande" entry, main text).
  - Keep rank in the terminology: higher-ranking members are called *sowei*, the masker is the *ndoli jowei*, "the dancing sowei", and the same word is applied by extension to the helmet mask (NEW-ScoutSowei-4, "Sande" and "Sowei" entries, main text).
  - Build the costume as part of the mask: the masker wears a black-dyed wooden helmet above black-dyed raffia and cloth, and the glossary calls the result "an idealised image of female beauty and dignity" (NEW-ScoutSowei-4, "Sande" entry, main text).
