read as two independent witnesses to the same sentence.

Finding in one line: the Nuo material gives a mask tradition whose masks are carved types
with read-off features, are consecrated into power, live in a trunk or attic for most of
the year, come out for a fixed stretch of the first lunar month, are carried and arranged by
assigned or hereditary officers, and are counted back in at the end; the Himalayan
material gives the same worn object under a monastic institution, male or monastic
performers, a shrine-to-courtyard reveal, and an explicit denial that it is entertainment.

### NEW-ScoutNuo-1

citation: Chen, Yuxuan; Suvimolstien, Chusak; Khochprasert, Jantana, 2025, "A Study on the Cultural Connotations and Heritage Development of Nanfeng Nuo Dance Masks", International Journal of Sociologies and Anthropologies Science Reviews 5(1): 853–864, doi 10.60027/ijsasr.2025.5485.
url: https://so07.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/IJSASR/article/download/5485/4266/53623
route: direct, HTTP 200, PDF, 628,245 bytes, 2026-10-22
kind: paper
tier: B — peer-reviewed open-access article, but its mask typology is quoted from Zeng (2019) and its carving materials from Zeng, Wang and Li (2006), so both are second hand in this row; its own contribution is a field visit, photographs and the synthesis.
scope: what Nanfeng Nuo masks are taken to be, what they are carved from, how faces are made readable, the six-type mask roster, and the state of transmission in 2015.
quotes:
- p. 856, section 2 "Nanfeng Nuo Dance Masks": "Artisans primarily use willow and camphor woods, shaping various forms through meticulous carving techniques."
- p. 856, same section, reporting Huang Chaobin: "allowing viewers to discern the characters' identities, genders, ages, and personalities"
- p. 857, section 3 "The Role of Nanfeng Nuo Dance Mask": "epidemic-exorcising deities, folk deities, Daoist and Buddhist immortals, legendary heroes, mythical animals, and secular figures"
- p. 859, section 1.1: "These masks are endowed with mystical powers through specific rituals and consecration ceremonies"
teaches: the mask is a legible type rather than a portrait, carved from named woods, consecrated into power, and chosen from a six-part roster that mixes liturgical and everyday figures.
draft sentences:
- Decide first what the mask is: here it is a carved type whose features let a viewer read role, gender, age and temperament, not a portrait of an individual (NEW-ScoutNuo-1, p. 856).
- Fix the wood and the finish if masks must be made in your world: Nanfeng carvers are said to use willow and camphor, painting and varnishing after carving, which sets weight, grain and surface (NEW-ScoutNuo-1, p. 856).
- Give consecration its own scene and officer: the authors report masks endowed with power through specific rituals and consecration ceremonies, so the mask before and after that rite are different objects in play (NEW-ScoutNuo-1, p. 859).
- Treat wrath as a function, not decoration: the authors report a formula in which fearsome and ugly faces are carved because they drive evil away, ugliness set against ugliness, so the horror of the face is the mechanism and not a taste (NEW-ScoutNuo-1, p. 859).
- Populate the mask rack from the reported six-part roster — plague-expelling deities, folk deities, Daoist and Buddhist immortals, legendary heroes, mythical animals and secular figures — and notice that one cabinet holds the liturgical and the everyday (NEW-ScoutNuo-1, p. 857).
- Budget transmission: 136 troupes counted in Nanfeng in 2015 with about half active and one nationally recognised inheritor means a tradition can keep its objects and lose the skill to use them (NEW-ScoutNuo-1, p. 853).

### NEW-ScoutNuo-2

citation: Chen, Limin, 2020, "Study of Nanfeng Nuo Dance in Jiangxi Province Under the Influence of Farming Culture", Proceedings of the 4th International Conference on Art Studies (ICASSEE 2020), Advances in Social Science, Education and Humanities Research 469: 475–479, Atlantis Press, CC BY-NC 4.0.
url: https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/125944384.pdf
route: direct, HTTP 200, PDF, 589,212 bytes, 2026-10-22
kind: paper
tier: B — conference paper, descriptive; its calendar and sequence details are the author's field report and the county annals, not an argument, and the English is unrevised, so quote it narrowly.
scope: when the Nanfeng mask cycle runs, the named stages of two village programs, who holds authority over a troupe, and where the masks are kept and displayed.
quotes:
- p. 476, §A "Shiyou Nuo dance": "From the lunar new year to the 20th day of the first month, its structure and program can be divided into six parts"
- p. 476, same paragraph: "stepping on the stage, having the exorcise, performing Nuo, searching Nuo, yuan Nuo and the sitting"
- p. 476, same section, on authority: "The village is dominated by the Wu family, and still maintains the hereditary system of the headman"
- p. 477, same paragraph: "There is a small attic on the altar to store the holy box and props for Nuo mask."
teaches: the mask's year is fixed and short, the rite is a named sequence, authority is an inherited office, and the mask has a storage address distinct from its performance address.
draft sentences:
- Fix the mask's season before its character: the Nanfeng cycle runs from the lunar new year to the 20th day of the first month in Shiyou and to the 19th in Shanggan, so the mask is a calendar object first (NEW-ScoutNuo-2, p. 476).
- Write the rite as an ordered program, not a mood: Shiyou's six named stages run from stepping on the stage through exorcising, performing, searching and yuan Nuo to the sitting, which gives a worldbuilder a start that opens the year and an end that closes it (NEW-ScoutNuo-2, p. 476).
- Put the mask under an office: the village's hereditary headman of the Wu family runs the troupe's affairs, so custody is inherited rather than hired (NEW-ScoutNuo-2, p. 476).
- Give the masks two addresses: dozens are worshipped on the altar during the Spring Festival, and the holy box and props are stored in a small attic at that altar for the rest of the year (NEW-ScoutNuo-2, p. 477).

### NEW-ScoutNuo-3

citation: Zhang, Jiahao, 2025, "Faith, Function, and Transformation: A Study of the Nuo Culture in Pingxiang, Jiangxi", Pacific International Journal 8(6), doi 10.55014/pij.v8i6.931.
url: https://www.rclss.com/pij/article/download/931/702
route: direct, HTTP 200, PDF, 201,497 bytes, 2026-10-22
kind: paper
tier: B — single-author article in a small open-access journal; three months of fieldwork and 32 interviews are reported, so its own observations count as first-hand, but its symbolic readings are interpretation and should be attributed.
scope: how a mask is thought to work on its wearer, the four-act shape of a Nuo rite, lineage-based transmission of the institution, and the closure of some rites even to a researcher.
quotes:
- p. 101, section "Belief System", symbolic components: "When performers don them, they temporarily take on divine agency—an act of embodiment central to ritual efficacy."
- p. 101, same section: "Through the acts of invitation, exorcism, performance, and dismissal, participants symbolically restore balance between humans and the spirit world."
- p. 100, section "Regional Characteristics of Pingxiang Nuo", item 2: "entire lineage groups were responsible for maintaining Nuo temples, sponsoring festivals, and training performers"
- p. 99, "Scope and Limitations": "some sacred rituals were not fully accessible due to restrictions"
teaches: the mask admits a presence for the duration of wear, the rite is a four-beat frame from invitation to dismissal, the institution belongs to lineage groups, and a closed portion is normal.
draft sentences:
- Decide what enters the wearer: performers are said to take on divine agency temporarily when they put the mask on, so the design question is what the mask admits and whether it can be put down again (NEW-ScoutNuo-3, p. 101).
- Use a four-beat frame — invitation, exorcism, performance, dismissal — and let each beat settle who may wear, see or handle the mask at that moment (NEW-ScoutNuo-3, p. 101).
- Make access lineal: whole lineage groups maintained the temples, paid for the festivals and trained the performers, so membership runs by descent and sponsorship rather than by talent alone (NEW-ScoutNuo-3, p. 100).
- Keep one room closed as a design fact: the author, who observed eight ritual events, reports that some sacred rituals were not fully accessible, which shows a closed portion is normal and not an accident (NEW-ScoutNuo-3, p. 99).
- Do not let the mask be only a sign: practitioners are reported to treat these figures as participants that act on cosmic order, which rules out a purely decorative reading of the worn object (NEW-ScoutNuo-3, p. 101).

draft sentences:
- Set the mask against entertainment: the museum states cham is not meant as entertainment even though it is a spectacle, which settles what the audience is doing there (NEW-ScoutNuo-5, History section).
- Give the dance a stated purpose in the community that hosts it: it is performed to purify and protect the monastic village community and to attune its energies with the land (NEW-ScoutNuo-5, History section).

- Make weight and training part of the mask's description: the masks are heavy, the dances must be exact, and the dancers must therefore be strong and well trained (NEW-ScoutNuo-5, Watch a Cham Dance section).
- Build the costume as an index rather than a frock: the museum's Mongolian example is silk, bone and metal, with an apron embroidered with skulls, bone-bead strings and boots ending in a makara (NEW-ScoutNuo-5, costume caption).

draft sentences:
- Make the mask set property that travels: villagers fetch the mask trunk from their temple or borrow it from another clan and carry it to their own ancestral hall (NEW-ScoutNuo-6, Guichi section, para. 2).
- Assign handling to one office: a specially designated person arranges the sacred masks in a prescribed manner, so touching is a role and not a right of the crowd (NEW-ScoutNuo-6, Guichi section, para. 2).
- Write reception as a material obligation: every household makes offerings and offers chicken blood to welcome the masks (NEW-ScoutNuo-6, Guichi section, para. 2).
- Make inventory a ritual act: after the performance the masks are carefully and cautiously counted and put back into the trunk (NEW-ScoutNuo-6, Guichi section, para. 2).
- Close the cycle at a shrine: at the fifteenth of the first month the whole clan carries the trunk to Qingshan Temple to worship the Nuo gods, so the set returns to the place that authorises it (NEW-ScoutNuo-6, Guichi section, para. 3).

## Gaps these rows do not fill

- Funerary masking: no row here touches it. Nothing in this report should be used for a
  funeral-mask sentence.
- Masquerade societies with graded ranks or entry fees: not covered. Nuo access is
  described as lineage, heredity and one designated office, not as a graded society.
- Licensed voice: none of my sources says a wearer speaks as the spirit or is licensed to
  say what he otherwise could not. Nuo drama has spoken parts, but I hold no quote that
  makes the mask a speech licence; that sentence needs a source another seat opens, or an
  inline (to-verify).
- Prohibition on seeing: no row states that a Nuo mask may not be seen by outsiders. The
  only closure evidence is custody (trunk, attic, designated arranger) and Zhang's note
  that some sacred rituals were closed to him. Do not upgrade that into a secrecy rule.
- Restriction of cham masks: neither the UNESCO page nor the Rubin page says cham masks are
  restricted tantric objects. The Met record that might show a festival context was not
  opened (see below), so no restriction claim on cham is supported here.
- Regional caution: rows 1 and 2 are Nanfeng (Jiangxi), row 3 is Pingxiang (Jiangxi), row 6
  is Guichi (Anhui). Keep the place names on the sentences; none of these authors claims a
  nationwide rule.
