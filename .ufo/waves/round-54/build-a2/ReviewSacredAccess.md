## 3. Rules the L1 must apply (mechanical)

R1. **Status split per object, per community.** Every mask tradition named in the entry gets its access status from an opened source of that community or its holding institutions — never inferred from market availability, museum display, age, or another community's rule (`real-world-inspiration-boundaries.md` §1: publicly viewable ≠ unrestricted; unknown `(to-verify)` is not permission).

R2. **Name in the community's terms.** Say "Hopi Katsina Friends" for the sacred objects and never call them "masks" in the entry's own voice (NEW-1, p. 2); separate Katsina (spirit) / dancer / tihu (carved figure) (NEW-8, note 1); say "Kokko" for Zuni beings and do not import Hopi terms, rules, or permissions (NEW-8); say "Gule Wamkulu" (dance) ≠ "Nyau" (society) ≠ "Chewa" (people) (NEW-5); "medicine masks" for Haudenosaunee wooden and corn husk masks (NEW-3).

R3. **No image reproduction for restricted classes.** The entry must not embed, link, or describe where to view images of: Haudenosaunee medicine masks (exhibition, photographs, reproductions, illustrations all barred by policy, NEW-3) or Hopi Katsina Friends (media request to refrain from publishing images, NEW-1). Public-facade traditions (Gule Wamkulu, Sande masquerade) are described as performances; the entry still cites rather than republishes imagery.

R4. **No construction or empowerment detail beyond public statements.** No making-rites, consecration, feeding, or medicine knowledge for Haudenosaunee masks, Katsina Friends, or Kokko. The Haudenosaunee policy itself states there is no proper outside way to explain the masks' significance and that society information is not for general distribution (NEW-3). What a source states publicly may be cited; what it does not state is `(to-verify)` or omitted (QUALITY 1).

R5. **Ceremony interiors stay closed.** Describe who performs, when, and for whom from public records; never narrate initiation content, kiva/Ca'lako-house interior knowledge, or society teachings (NEW-8: Ca'lako knowledge "reserved for only a select few"; NEW-3). Apply the corpus rule: do not expose restricted ritual knowledge to fill a table (`myth-consistency-gate.md` §2).

R6. **Observation is not permission; prior publication is not present permission.** Cite HCPO's list of violations (photographing dancers' items for sale, publishing ceremony pictures, duplicating dolls from observed dancers, NEW-2) and Columbia's withheld-despite-published watercolors (NEW-8) as the two canonical demonstrations. A "replica", "doll", or "souvenir" label establishes nothing about status.

R7. **Reuse the permission-boundary pattern, do not mint a new one.** Where the entry touches living restricted practice, route through `civilizations/australia-aboriginal.md:130-138` (describe the institution type; stop at a consultation/permission request; never produce a speculative substitute labelled authentic) and through `verification/real-world-inspiration-boundaries.md` for the borrowing screen. State the boundary in the entry (the bathing entry's closing-line pattern, `bathing-and-body-hygiene.md:142`).

R8. **Calibrate the secrecy axis per tradition, not as one bucket.** Four distinct public/restricted shapes, all source-backed: (a) image-and-explanation restricted, performance not at issue — Haudenosaunee (NEW-3); (b) name-and-image restricted, carved figures circulate in a market — Hopi (NEW-1, NEW-2, NEW-8); (c) objects-and-knowledge circulation controlled by a tribal institution — Zuni (NEW-7, NEW-8); (d) public performance with closed membership — Nyau/Gule Wamkulu (NEW-5) and Sande (NEW-6). Poro is attested here only at exhibition breadth (gbetu costume, NEW-6): keep Poro at that depth or flag deeper claims `(to-verify)`.

R9. **Gender and society facts stay precise and unexoticized.** Sande is a women's society whose leaders commission, keep, and maintain the masks, with male carvers possible (NEW-6); Poro is male (gbetu); Nyau is an initiated men's brotherhood inside a matrilineal society (NEW-5). Use community and people names precisely (Haudenosaunee, Hopi, A:shiwi/Zuni, Chewa, Mende, Gola, Vai, Kpelle); no "tribal fetish" register; describe restricted sacred objects only as far as the community's own public statements allow.

R10. **Canon hygiene and usability together (QUALITY 4–5).** Convert all of the above into a decision table for the entry — rows: who may see / who may wear / who may own / who may name / who may image / who may copy / who may sell / who may explain; columns: the four shapes in R8 — plus a worked non-canon micro-example, and a closing line stating no restricted real ceremony is described. Entry ends at the handoff; it records method and public record only.
