### S504
- source cell: U.S. Board on Geographic Names (U.S. Geological Survey), n.d., Guidance on Name Proposals — https://www.usgs.gov/us-board-on-geographic-names/guidance-name-proposals (direct, HTTP 200)
- url(s): https://www.usgs.gov/us-board-on-geographic-names/guidance-name-proposals
- anchor: §8 Can a feature be unnamed?: "Once named, a feature in the Geographic Names Information System (GNIS) can never be “unnamed”, only changed to another name."
- teaches: A feature that has received an official name may later be changed to a new name but never returned to having none.
- entry lines citing it (corpus/knowledge/linguistics/toponymy-place-names.md):
  - - The US naming board's proposal guidance says that once named, a feature in the Geographic Names Information System can never be "unnamed", only changed to another name (S504). Official names can be replaced but never deleted.

### S516
- source cell: Gillian Fellows-Jensen, 1995 (Dorothea Coke Memorial Lecture delivered at University College London 21 February 1994; published for the College by the Viking Society for Northern Research), *The Vikings and their Victims: The Verdict of the Names* — http://www.vsnrweb-publications.org.uk/Fellows-Jensen.pdf (direct, HTTP 200)
- url(s): http://www.vsnrweb-publications.org.uk/Fellows-Jensen.pdf
- anchor: p. 17 (PDF page 19): "the presence of numerous Scandinavian place-names in -bý points to the survival of an enclave of Danes here"
- teaches: A dense cluster of Scandinavian place-names ending in -bý in one district can show that an enclave of Danes survived there.
- entry lines citing it (corpus/knowledge/linguistics/toponymy-place-names.md):
  - - Fellows-Jensen reads numerous Scandinavian place-names in -bý in one district as pointing to the survival of an enclave of Danes there (S516). A dense cluster of one people's settlement ending can mark where that people stayed.
