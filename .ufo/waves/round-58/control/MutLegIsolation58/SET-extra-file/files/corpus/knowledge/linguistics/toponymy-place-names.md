# Toponymy: place names for worldbuilders

- topic: A compact toponymy reference for worldbuilders: feature names, historical layers, hydronyms, naming authorities, endonyms and exonyms; source-backed principles and a non-canon handoff checklist.

## Scope and calibration

This short reference is a control fixture, not round-58 claim data. Three Wikipedia pages supply explicitly secondary introductory evidence (tier C); one USGS page supplies a primary institutional example. The principles below are scoped tendencies and definitions, not substitutes for local scholarship. Design recommendations are inferences from those principles.

## Names and historical layers

- Treat each geographical feature as eligible for a toponym, not just settlements. (S500)
- Separate toponymy from the wider field of onomastics when scoping a naming brief. (S501)
- For a basic toponym, plan two elements before deriving its surface form; this is a common pattern, not a universal requirement. (S502)
- Keep a storyteller's place-name tale separate from a verified origin account; the article associates these narratives with poets rather than treating them as automatic historical proof. (S503)
- Give changing toponyms a chronology: languages, borders and political events need not move at the same time. (S504)
- For local usage, record residents' names alongside maps and histories instead of assuming the map exhausts the community's vocabulary. (S505)
- For each surveyed toponym, retain language, pronunciation, origins and meaning as separate fields alongside the actual feature it denotes. (S506)
- Use toponyms as one line of evidence for historical geography, not as a replacement for corroborating records. (S507)
- A fictional map can preserve settlement patterns and immigration layers in its toponyms; do not assign every surviving form to the current population. (S508)

## Naming authority

- Distinguish everyday geographical names from official names selected by a government board. (S509)
- For streets, consider political naming as an inscription of ideological views, following the interpretation attributed here to Pinchevski and Torgovnik, not a universal motive. (S510)
- Before geographical commemoration of living persons, decide who bears the risk of later disgrace; the article reports this practice as problematic, not automatically forbidden. (S511)
- Assign an institution a legal remit: Harrison's Executive Order gave the BGN authority to resolve unsettled geographic names, illustrating a concrete naming mandate rather than an assumed universal power. (S519)

## Water names

- Build hydronyms for lakes and swamps as well as rivers and seas, rather than treating the category as river names alone. (S512)
- Allow hydronyms to retain older linguistic layers after settlement changes; conservative water names need not match the incoming language. (S513)
- When designing survival layers, contrast conservative names of large rivers with the potentially less persistent names of small streams; this is a tendency, not a dating algorithm. (S514)
- A river hydronym can inherit a plain's toponym: the article gives the Liffey as an example, so derivation need not always run from water to settlement. (S515)

## Local and external forms

- Mark an endonym by an official or established language of the geographical feature's area, rather than assuming one local population has only one legitimate name. (S516)
- An exonym may be mediated by another language: English names for places in continental Europe often came through French rather than directly from a local endonym. (S517)
- Use Deutschland versus Germany and Germania as a concrete country-name contrast between an endonym and external-language exonyms. (S518)

## Design decisions and lore handoff

| Decision | Record before deriving a name |
|---|---|
| Referent | Feature identity and boundaries; do not confuse a river with its valley or town. |
| Structure | Candidate generic and distinguishing element, or an explicitly simplex form. |
| Strata | Language of coinage, later adaptations, documentary dates and uncertainty. |
| Speakers | Local forms, official forms and external-language forms, each with users. |
| Change | Institution, event and adoption lag; preserve contested or surviving alternatives. |
| Evidence | Separate attested etymology, community account and author-designed inference. |

Hand lore a feature identifier, era, language, pronunciation, derivation, local usage, official authority, competing forms and uncertainty. These are proposed options, never canon. For personal names use `naming-systems.md`; for sacred places use `../myth-symbol/sacred-topography-pilgrimage.md`; for divine names use `../synthesis/deity-name-derivation-pipeline.md`. Those owners are not overwritten by this place-name checklist.
