# Place names for worldbuilders

- topic: toponymy: place-name records, naming authorities and historical survey evidence for worldbuilders
- sources: individual source citations below
- couples: language history and maps

## Reference notes

- Federal geographic naming has a statutory standardization authority: the BGN dates to 1890, with its present legal form dating to 1947. (S500)
- GNIS anchors federal geographic nomenclature in a national standard rather than a private naming convention. (S501)
- GNIS inventories roughly one million geographic names, spanning populated places as well as natural features such as lakes and ridges. (S502)
- The Domestic Names Committee administers place names for the United States and dependent territories through GNIS. (S503)
- A named GNIS feature retains some name: replacement is permitted, whereas returning it to an unnamed state is not. (S504)
- EPNS organizes its principal research project as a survey of English place names. (S505)
- Digitised English Place-Name Society material reproduces survey volumes dating from 1925 through 2009, not a newly authored replacement survey. (S506)
- Founded in 1923, the English Place-Name Society set out to survey the country's names comprehensively. (S507)
- The EPNS county survey links linguistic origins to England's place names; a 1923 foundation preceded DEEP digitisation. (S508)
- Whanganui River illustrates an official place-name spelling change: the added h was approved in 1991. (S509)
- Official place names require formal agreement, and government documents must adopt those authorized forms. (S510)
- The gazetteer retains original Māori place names when they are known, alongside official and unofficial records. (S511)
- Place-name proposals can cover New Zealand features and offshore islands; the public can submit proposed naming choices. (S512)
- Official names support Antarctic navigation and emergency response through maps and charts. (S513)
- The NZGB provides a digital list combining unofficial and official place names, useful for separating legal status from mere attestation. (S514)
- Feature-name changes reach the geographic names board through proposals from members of the public. (S515)
- Federal, tribal and local partners feed edits and new features into GNIS; staff validation precedes publication. (S516)
- GNIS error reports go to its manager, where data experts investigate the records and validate corrections. (S517)
- GNIS preserves historical names and features even after their physical disappearance or the end of active usage. (S518)
- Region boundaries reflect application-specific choices; the geographic names board does not standardize their extents in GNIS. (S519)

## Worldbuilding handoff

Keep local usage, official status, historical spellings and institutional jurisdiction in separate fields of a fictional gazetteer. Give the map maker the recorded forms and the naming body's remit rather than treating present official spelling as the sole historical form.
