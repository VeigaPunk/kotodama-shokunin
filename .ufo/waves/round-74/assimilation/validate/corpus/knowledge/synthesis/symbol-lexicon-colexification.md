## 1. Scope: a fingerprint is a comparison, not an essence

- Unit of analysis: **community × language variety × era × register × medium**. A polity may contain several languages; a language may span rival religions, occupations, classes, and polities.
- “Fingerprint” here means a documented configuration of lexical and symbolic relations useful for differentiating fictional traditions. It is not a unique identifier of ethnicity, ancestry, intelligence, or personality.
- Colexification supplies candidate associations; text, performance, artifacts, and speakers' explanations determine which associations have cultural work to do. Do not infer a pantheon from a dictionary entry.
- Everything prescriptive below is **designer tooling**, not a universal law of language change. Fictional samples are diagnostic fixtures, not accepted world facts; lore-shokunin alone can adopt them.

## 2. Keep the relations distinct

| Relation | What establishes it | What it does NOT establish |
|---|---|---|
| Colexification | Two comparison concepts can be expressed by the same lexical form in a specified variety and context; declare the form-matching rule | A common etymology, a conscious association, or a doctrine |
| Polysemy | Related conventional senses of a lexical item, supported by usage and contextual analysis | That every sense is active whenever the item occurs |
| Homophony | Distinct items share pronunciation under a stated phonological analysis | A semantic bridge; separate spelling may expose the difference |
| Partial colexification / shared morphology | Forms share a constituent or derivational material; segment it explicitly | Full-form colexification; a substring match alone does not establish a morpheme |
| Symbolic association | An image, object, gesture, or material evokes a concept in an attested practice | That the object-name also lexicalizes that concept |
| Historical relationship | Dated attestations, cognates, or an argued etymology connect forms/senses | Present-day polysemy or the direction of change from an undirected network |

- François's colexification comparison deliberately avoids making the polysemy/homophony decision a prerequisite. Preserve a raw form-based result, then annotate its semantic interpretation rather than silently redefining the observation.
- Decide whether “same form” means phonological identity, orthographic identity, or a lemmatized citation form. Record inflectional normalization, tone, stress, vowel length, and word boundaries; changing these can manufacture or erase edges.
- Homophones may connect in a sound-based colexification extraction yet remain separate lexemes. Their symbolic usefulness can be high while their value as evidence of semantic similarity is low.
- A white garment associated with mourning need not share a word with death. Conversely, a conventional body-part metaphor need not produce a ritual emblem. Audit both directions independently.
- A character used for sound, an image of its referent, and a word written with that character are separate objects. Rebus writing and visual punning are not automatically polysemy.

## 3. Frameworks and their calibrated uses

| Framework | Practical use | Calibration |
|---|---|---|
| François (2008): lexical typology and colexification | Compare semantic packaging without assuming English word divisions are natural boundaries | A recurring association is a possibility, not a required conlang merger |
| Haspelmath (2010): comparative concepts vs descriptive categories | Keep analyst labels separate from a language's own lexical categories | A shared gloss is not proof of identical meanings |
| Concepticon, List et al. (2016) | Align elicitation glosses to explicit comparison sets | IDs facilitate comparison; they are not an inventory of universal mental atoms |
| CLICS³, Rzymski et al. (2020) | Inspect networks, underlying wordlists, language families, and source forms | A database edge needs lexical checking; no edge may reflect missing coverage |
| Jackson et al. (2019): emotion networks | Model local variation alongside broader constraints, including valence and activation | Study-level findings are not psychological diagnoses of individual speakers |
| Murck (2008): audience, obscurity, and visual allusion | Give public blessings and restricted criticism different decoding requirements | An elegant interpretation can remain uncertain; audience comprehension must not be assumed |

- A colexification network records witnessed links. A semantic map may instead hypothesize a smaller adjacency structure explaining distributions; do not treat every drawn adjacency as an attested same-word pair.
- A connected path is not identity: A–B in one language and B–C in another does not imply A–C anywhere. If one form genuinely lexicalizes A, B, and C, record all three witnessed pairs and retain that common witness.
- Force-directed layout, centrality, and community detection are descriptive choices. A visually central node is not automatically a sacred concept; a cluster is not automatically a native category.

## 4. Minimal ledger and typed-network recipe

Store these fields in the proposal itself; this is a schema, not a request to create extra files.

| Record | Required fields |
|---|---|
| Comparison concept | Local ID, definition, inclusion/exclusion examples; verified Concepticon ID only when actually matched |
| Lexical item | Lexeme ID, sense ID, original form/script, transcription, part of speech, variety, register, era, example context |
| Form identity | Phonological vs written vs lemmatized key; normalization rule; homophony/polysemy/uncertain annotation |
| Symbol occurrence | Motif/object ID, referent, proposed reading, medium, owner/user, occasion, findspot or collection identifier |
| Witness | Author/source, edition, page/entry/passage/object locator, date of witness, date of copying if different, access limitations |
| Interpretation | Relation type, emic explanation vs analyst inference, competing readings, confidence, disconfirming observation |

1. Choose a **comparison slice**, not “the entire language”: e.g. body/emotion terms in a particular register, plus dress motifs used by the same institution.
2. Build a bipartite lexical record: lexical forms → comparison concepts. Retain separate lexeme IDs when matching forms are homophones; keep examples for every proposed sense.
3. Project an undirected concept edge only when a common form witnesses both concepts in the chosen slice. Tag raw colexification edges as polysemous, homophonous, disputed, or not yet adjudicated.
4. Maintain typed cross-layer edges: motif → referent `DEPICTS`; referent → lexical item `NAMED_BY`; lexical item → concept `LEXICALIZES`; item → item `HOMOPHONE`; motif → concept `SIGNIFIES`; garment → motif `BEARS`. An image depicts a referent, not automatically its name.
5. Join the layers through attested names, explanations, inscriptions, performances, or artifact contexts. Mark an ungrounded cross-layer link **PROPOSED**, not **ATTESTED**.
6. Keep separate counts for forms, sources, varieties, languages, and families; duplicated dictionary editions are not independent cultural witnesses. Record whether counts describe attestations or opportunities to observe.
7. Attach every edge to era/register and its witnesses. Record rejected readings too, so a later compiler cannot restore them from a pretty network picture.

- Data states: **observed same-form pair / investigated but distinct / unknown**. Missing entries are unknown, not negative evidence; a partial wordlist is not a survey of everything speakers can say.
- For an aggregate comparison, denominator = varieties with usable evidence for both concepts, not all languages in the database. Report related varieties and area clustering rather than counting them as independent repetitions.
- Optional similarity diagnostic: restrict two slices to concept pairs adequately investigated in both, then compute edge-set Jaccard `|E1 ∩ E2| / |E1 ∪ E2|`; an empty union is undefined. Report excluded pairs and inspection criteria.
- Jaccard is designer bookkeeping, not a validated “cultural distance” scale. Identical scores can hide different edges, registers, and symbol functions; inspect the actual contrasts.

## 5. When does polysemy support a worldview inference?

| Evidence level | Warranted statement | Next evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Lexical observation | “This source gives one form for these senses” | Source-language examples; exclude glossing/normalization errors |
| Conventional usage | “This community/register repeatedly uses the association” | Independent genres or speakers; constructional restrictions |
| Symbolic deployment | “The association is recruited for this emblem, rite, name, or garment” | Contextualized artifact, performance, or explicit explanation |
| Local doctrine | “This named school/institution says the association describes reality” | Dated doctrinal witness; counterpositions; audience and authority |
| Wider practice | “This interpretation is supported across these sampled settings” | Evidence beyond the institution; variation and negative cases |

- These are escalating warrants, not a compulsory evolution sequence. A poet may invent an allusion without general lexicalization; a conventional idiom may persist after its doctrinal explanation is forgotten.
- Prefer “in these court petitions, memory is described through binding” to “these people think memory is a knot.” Specify what would falsify the larger claim before expanding it.
- Polysemy can preserve a familiar expression without an active belief. Historical origin, current conventional meaning, and a speaker's improvised explanation are different kinds of evidence.
- A word can package senses together while grammar, context, or paraphrase distinguishes them precisely. Shared vocabulary does not imply inability to distinguish concepts.

| Rival explanation | Cheap discriminator before claiming worldview |
|---|---|
| Inheritance | Compare related varieties and dated sources; do not count every descendant as a new invention |
| Contact or translation calque | Check donor language, bilingual institution, and earliest genre of use |
| Embodied/material analogy | Ask whether similar environments or ordinary bodily experience predict the link without shared doctrine |
| Source-language gloss bias | Read examples rather than treating English headwords as the senses themselves |
| Dictionary/editorial practice | Compare sense divisions, broad glosses, homonym numbering, and omitted register labels |
| Euphemism, pun, or name avoidance | Check who may use the form and whether another term survives in ordinary speech |
| Archive survival | Compare elite written evidence with domestic, oral, regional, and occupational evidence where available |

- Independence check: a catalogue, tourist account, and symbol dictionary repeating the same original explanation constitute a citation chain, not three independent witnesses.
- Jackson et al. (2019) support cultural variation and broader structure in emotion semantics together. They do not establish that a colexified pair produces identical emotional experiences or an immutable national character.

## 6. Bounded reference comparison: Chinese textual and late-imperial visual traditions

This is a comparison of particular evidence windows, not a single civilization-wide graph. Modern Mandarin readings below are pronunciation labels, not reconstructions of ancient or every regional speech form.

| System | Reference observation | Coupling discipline |
|---|---|---|
| Language family + writing | Chinese varieties belong to Sinitic within Sino-Tibetan; written Chinese is conventionally described as morphosyllabic, not a language of pure pictures; see `../linguistics/writing-systems.md` | Distinguish Literary Chinese, regional speech, and the reader's pronunciation; the empire is not one speech community |
| Lexical/philosophical core | 心, modern Mandarin xīn, is conventionally glossed heart/heart-mind; Mengzi 6A15 assigns reflection to the heart; 2A6 develops morally significant responses | The passages establish a local account of the heart's functions; they do not by themselves prove two discrete native senses corresponding to English HEART and MIND |
| Mythic/religious core | Ancestor veneration and popular gods associated with blessings, official rank, and longevity coexist with Buddhist/Daoist and Confucian traditions in the late-imperial reference material (Bartholomew 2013; SFO Museum exhibition text below) | Do not reduce these practices to one consistent pantheon or infer worship from a decorative wish alone |
| Symbolic system | 蝠 fú “bat” and 福 fú “blessing” support a sound-based rebus; Murck (2008), paragraph 9, documents the five-bat/long-life design in Cixi's birthday decoration | Two distinct items, not evidence that “bat” has the ordinary lexical sense “blessing”; check regional and historical pronunciation before extending the pun |
| Era model | Mengzi's Warring States intellectual context; later textual transmission/commentary; separate Ming (1368–1644) and Qing (1644–1911) decorative-art windows in Bartholomew's handout | Copy date, commentary date, object date, and a proposed meaning's first attestation must remain separate |
| Costume/material signature | Garrett distinguishes Qing rulers', consorts', officials'/merchants', women's, and lower-class dress; embroidered robes are one major surface for display, alongside accessories | Do not turn a court robe into generic “Chinese costume”; identify fiber, weave, construction, motif placement, wearer, and occasion on each actual object |
| Naming practices | Family and personal names are distinct slots; elite courtesy names and name avoidance add further layers; consult `../linguistics/naming-systems.md` | Exact permissions by status, gender, dynasty, and name type require primary-text locators (to-verify); an auspicious name is not proof of a lexical merger |

- **Textual transmission caution:** the received Mengzi was edited/commented upon after the philosopher's lifetime; the SEP discussion identifies Zhao Qi's second-century CE intervention. A translation is not an untouched fourth-century BCE speech transcript.
- **Semantic calibration:** HEART/MIND can be analyst distinctions imposed on a broader term. Record a colexification comparison only with explicit definitions and lexical witnesses; use the philosophical passage as additional doctrinal evidence, not circular proof of the initial segmentation.
- **Visual calibration:** Murck's paragraph 13 distinguishes spoken rebuses from character-component puzzles; her paragraphs 3–4 discuss changed readings of conjoined trees. Script, sound, and symbolic meaning can drift independently.
- **What a worldbuilder can take:** combine a broader lexical category with competing local explanations; let a homophone generate a wearable blessing without merging the lexemes; give learned readers access to allusions other audiences miss.
- **Provenance cautions:** Murck's paragraph 2 traces modern motif catalogues through outsider collections and earlier compendia; these need source tracing, not automatic rejection or uncritical adoption. Court objects and literati texts overrepresent patrons and educated men. Regional speech, ordinary wearers' explanations, women's voices, and local ritual transmission are thin here; no continuous chronicle has been assembled for them.
- Supplementary artifact-context source: **SFO Museum (2010)**, [“Hidden Meanings: Symbolism in Chinese Art” exhibition text](https://www.sfomuseum.org/exhibitions/hidden-meanings-symbolism-chinese-art-collections-asian-art-museum-san-francisco-chong). Its generalizations about population-wide recognition are not substitutes for reception evidence.

## 7. How to apply the method to a fictional culture

1. Declare the social and material slice: speakers, institution, neighboring varieties, media, textile/metal/dye capabilities, mythic authorities, and evidence the project actually needs.
2. Choose a small contrastive concept inventory: bodily process, kin relation, valued action, material, landscape, ritual category. Define each without requiring an English-sized word for it.
3. Draft candidate same-form pairings and explicit non-pairings. Use CLICS³ as a possibility search, not an approval oracle; an unattested pair can be proposed with a motivated route and an honest evidence label.
4. Choose the mechanism for each pairing: conventional metaphor, metonymy, contextual specialization, semantic broadening, contact/calque, homophony, or unresolved. Do not give every relationship the label “ancient belief.”
5. Design ambiguity management: ordinary context, compounds, classifiers, register, stress/tone, or alternate lexemes can disambiguate. Test mundane instructions as well as poetry.
6. Add a symbol only when a practice uses the association: a motif, oath gesture, ritual material, or naming formula. Record interpreters, occasion, and the reading's competitors.
7. Couple clothing through manufacture and access: can the sign be woven, embroidered, printed, cut, dyed, or attached at the proposed scale and cost? A lexicon entry cannot establish the technology.
8. Generate names through the language's existing morphology and social slots; distinguish a personal name, inherited clan emblem, garment label, and official title. Avoid making every name transparently translate the same national slogan.
9. Build contrast within the culture: a school, profession, generation, or neighboring variety can retain, reject, or reinterpret one edge. Variation needs social support, not random exceptions.
10. Supply alternatives and disconfirmation tests to lore-shokunin. Proposed coherence is not authority to declare what the world's inhabitants really believe.

## 8. Era staging: change edges, not just labels

The stages below are optional operations, not a universal march from “primitive” symbolism to rational language.

| Design operation | Lexical-network change | Myth/symbol/costume consequence to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| Contextual innovation | New use occurs in a narrow construction; do not yet mark an ordinary lexical sense | A ritual or poem may recruit the association before common speech does |
| Conventionalization | Repeated use supports an additional sense in one register | An institution may teach the reading or authorize a motif; document who resists it |
| Contact/translation | A donor pattern adds an edge, replaces an item, or supplies a competing term | Borrowed doctrine can coexist with local dress materials and altered motifs |
| Sound merger/split | Raw form matches appear or disappear; semantic relations need not change | A pun may become newly available, fail, or persist only in a ritual pronunciation |
| Political/material change | A title or material develops new senses; record old and new usage separately | New patrons, access rules, cheaper production, or prohibition can reassign a garment sign |
| Lexical replacement/fossilization | Everyday colexification disappears while an idiom or old name survives | Inherited insignia may remain intelligible only to specialists, or gain a folk explanation |

- Store each change as `earlier slice → later slice; witness/assumption; mechanism; affected edges; unaffected edges; surviving fossils; uncertainty`.
- Chronology test: did the proposed pun, doctrinal reading, garment technology, and naming permission coexist? If not, present alternatives or date the combination later; do not quietly retrofit the past.
- Absence of dated evidence establishes an uncertainty interval, not an invention date. Destruction, illiteracy, restricted literacy, and collection practices can all hide earlier use.

## 9. Diagnostic fixture: proposed links, not fictional canon

Treat these local IDs and forms as disposable testing inputs. They assert nothing about an existing fictional people or real tradition.

| Input | Correct ledger result | Incorrect inference to reject |
|---|---|---|
| Proposed form /sela/ conventionally covers BREATH and WIND | A candidate full colexification edge, pending the design's usage examples | Speakers cannot distinguish exhaling from weather |
| A ritual gloss calls a visiting spirit “wind”; ordinary speech has another spirit term | Mark a ritual metaphor or candidate register-specific extension; ask whether it is conventional | Add BREATH–SPIRIT automatically by transitivity |
| Distinct proposed lexemes /ka/ “reed” and /ka/ “ancestor” are homophones | Separate lexeme IDs plus a sound-match annotation | Treat reed/ancestor as semantically related senses without further support |
| A candidate cloth border depicts reeds to evoke ancestry through the pun | A proposed DEPICTS → NAMED_BY → HOMOPHONE → LEXICALIZES route, with textile feasibility unresolved | Claim reed-fiber cloth or an ancestor cult must exist because of the pun |
| A later pronunciation separates the two /ka/ forms | Remove the contemporary sound match unless a restricted reading preserves it | Erase inherited decoration or pretend every wearer knows the old wordplay |

- Required exercise: produce a weather report, a kinship introduction, and a garment-maker's instruction. Mark which context licenses each sense, metaphor, and motif reading; rewrite any ambiguity that prevents ordinary communication.
- Keep a plain alternative: the border could be a workshop mark rather than a sacred sign. The same shape is not enough to decide between those hypotheses.

## 10. Calibration failures and delivery checklist

| Tempting claim | Status and replacement |
|---|---|
| “No word for X, therefore no thought of X” | Invalid inference; check paraphrase, constructions, context, and sampling |
| “This etymology reveals the nation's original worldview” | Unsupported without historical and reception evidence; current use may be opaque |
| “One universal archetype fixes the meaning of this sign” | Discredited as a symbol-decoding shortcut; document local, dated uses instead |
| “All network clusters are native cultural categories” | Unsupported; compare algorithm settings, gloss definitions, and explicit classifications |
| “Ancient/Indigenous sources record a timeless whole culture” | Often filtered through colonial, missionary, elite, or later editorial categories; identify recorder and conditions |
| “The most connected word is the culture's holiest word” | No such inference follows; common form, broad glossing, and coverage can inflate degree |

- [ ] Every same-form edge has original forms, concept definitions, witnesses, register, era, and a declared matching rule.
- [ ] Polysemy, homophony, shared morphology, historical relationship, and symbolic association remain distinguishable; no transitive closure creates unwitnessed colexifications.
- [ ] Unknown data is not coded as lexical absence; comparison denominators and source dependence are explicit.
- [ ] Each worldview claim names its institution/audience and an alternative explanation; contradictory evidence is retained.
- [ ] Sound/script, mythic explanation, naming permission, material technology, and garment use coexist in the proposed era or are explicitly unresolved.
- [ ] Real references distinguish translated text, source passage, catalogue description, and modern interpretation; thin evidence carries `(to-verify)` or a named gap.
- [ ] Hypothetical fixtures are labeled as such; no graph, name, myth, garment rule, or interpretation is promoted to canon by this entry.
- Handoff to lore-shokunin: comparison-slice definition; lexical/symbol ledgers and typed edge list; dated change notes; naming/costume feasibility notes; rival readings; source locators; open `(to-verify)` items; decisions reserved for the world-bible owner.
