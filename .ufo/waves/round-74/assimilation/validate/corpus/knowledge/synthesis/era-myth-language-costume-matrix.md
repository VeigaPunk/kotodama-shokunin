# Era × System Matrix — Myth, Language, Costume, Symbol, and Calendar in Uneven Time

- topic: A reusable era-by-system worksheet: separate political periodization from changes in cult, speech, writing, naming, dress, signs, and timekeeping; record mechanisms, uneven adoption, and survivals. Includes a historical calibration profile and a filled fictional demonstration of drift, not world canon.

## 1. Matrix contract: an era is an address, not an explanation

- Rows identify dated or relatively ordered situations; columns hold system states. A coronation does not automatically change phonology, garment construction, or the length of the year.
- Keep **etic** labels, the analyst's “Era II,” separate from **emic** labels, a population's “Restoration.” A restoration is a political claim until its restored features are specified.
- Put geography and population in the row key: `era_id + interval + locality + institution/social group`. Split simultaneous court, port, household, and diaspora rows when their states differ.
- Distinguish **attestation date**, **proposed origin**, **adoption interval**, and **survival interval**. The oldest surviving garment or text is not automatically the first of its kind.
- Coupling means a traceable dependency or institutional connection, not “everything expresses one worldview.” Contradictory calendars and competing readings can be part of a coherent society.
- This matrix and its transition tags are this entry's craft synthesis; neither Braudel nor an Egyptian source supplied this exact worksheet.

## 2. Named frameworks and their limits

| Framework | Use in the matrix | Calibration |
|---|---|---|
| Braudel's event, conjunctural, and long-duration perspectives [B58] | Separate accession events, institutional reorganization, and persistent production/ecological constraints | An analytical distinction, not a schedule assigning every myth or language a fixed lifetime |
| Historical-linguistic relative chronology [C13; A13] | Order sound changes, loans, written attestations, and register splits before assigning absolute dates | One dynasty is not one language stage; sound change and script reform are different processes |
| Assmann's implicit/explicit theology distinction [AS01] | Separate practices and representations from literate theological argument | Elite theological change cannot stand for universal conversion; see [SZ03] |
| Garment construction versus representation [VE93; M531] | Separate excavated fiber/seams from depicted silhouette, pleating, and ideal bodies | A sculpted pleat is evidence of representation, not a recovered dress pattern |
| Parallel civil/religious calendars [P50] | Track date syntax, administrative count, and ritual timing independently | Parker's reconstruction is an argument; exact festival equations need period-specific evidence |

- Do not substitute a universal “primitive → priestly → rational” ladder for transitions. Literacy, polytheism, industrial production, and centralized rule do not form a single necessary sequence.
- A monomyth diagram, archetype dictionary, or universal color-meaning chart cannot supply missing historical cells. Use such devices, if at all, as explicitly invented in-world theories.

## 3. Cell grammar and evidence labels

Every cell expands into `state; users/place; authority; carrier; exception; evidence/status`.

| Column | Minimum contents | Coupling question |
|---|---|---|
| Myth | Cult actors/pantheon, cosmological claim, narrative or ritual variant, institution transmitting it | Who benefits when an epithet, ancestor, or ritual priority changes? |
| Language | Family/branch, spoken stage, register, script and medium; personal/divine/place-name rules | Does a new cult name follow vernacular changes, remain liturgical, or arrive as a loan? |
| Costume | Fiber, construction/silhouette, visible material distinction, wearer/occasion rule, production access | Can the economy make the mandated garment, and who can actually wear it? |
| Symbol | Sign/form, material carrier, situated meaning, audience, permitted alternative reading | Does its reading survive a change in language, ownership, medium, or ritual context? |
| Calendar | Cycle arithmetic, epoch/year count, observation rule, festival anchor, authorized announcer | Does a new regime rename time, change its arithmetic, or merely move one festival? |

- **A — attested:** identify an object/text and what it directly establishes; a representation attests an image, not universal behavior.
- **R — reconstruction/interpretation:** name the scholar, evidential basis, and rival reading where relevant.
- **F — fictional stipulation:** a teaching assumption or design option, never a historical assertion or canon decision.
- **U — unresolved:** write `(to-verify)` beside the specific claim; include the missing witness or check. Unknown is not evidence of absence.
- Mixed cells need mixed labels. Do not let an attested royal decree silently upgrade an inference about household compliance to A.
- Store naming under Language even when it is also a cult or rank signal; link the same name identifier across columns rather than copying incompatible glosses.

`from → to | trigger | actor/resources | mechanism | first witness | adoption interval | resistant group | surviving carrier | evidence/status`.

| Transition tag | Mechanism to specify | A useful non-result to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| RETAIN | A practice continues through an external period boundary | New ruler, same cloth and calendar arithmetic |
| REKEY | Existing form receives a different interpretation or institutional owner | Same badge, different entitlement |
| REPLACE | A form/practice is displaced in a stated domain | New court dress does not imply new farm clothing |
| BORROW | Donor, route, adoption conditions, and local adaptation | Imported cloth need not bring imported gods |
| SPLIT | Users, regions, or registers diverge | Vernacular and liturgical names coexist |
| REVIVE | A later institution selects and reconstructs an earlier form | Archaic spelling need not restore archaic pronunciation |

## 5. Historical calibration profile: Egypt around the Amarna episode

This is a bounded reference case within the New Kingdom, especially the fourteenth century BCE and its Ramesside aftermath, not a complete Egyptian civilization survey.

| Required surface | Reference observation | How to record it without overclaiming |
|---|---|---|
| Language family + writing | Egyptian is a distinct branch of Afroasiatic; hieroglyphic and hieratic writing coexist across relevant periods [A13 §§1.1–1.3] | Allen uses the older label “Hamito-Semitic”; do not reproduce “Hamitic” as a valid racial population category or assume a simple script-replacement ladder |
| Language staging | Late Egyptian appears in writing around Akhenaten and becomes standard in the following dynasty; traditional Middle Egyptian continues in monumental/religious domains [A13 §1.2] | A written debut is not the birth of a spoken language; dynasty boundaries and register boundaries do not coincide |
| Mythic/pantheon core | Aten-centered royal theology and the post-Amarna theology of Amun are central to Assmann's argument; non-Atenist amulets/figurines at Amarna complicate universal replacement [AS01; SZ03] | Separate court theology, temple institutions, and household practices; debate over “monotheism” does not settle actual compliance |
| Symbolic system | Cultic images/objects can differ between royal and household contexts; non-Atenist objects survive within Amarna's settlement evidence [SZ03] | Shared locality does not require one exclusive emblem vocabulary; do not infer a complete clandestine belief system from one object |
| Era model | Amarna is a modern archaeological/historical label; Assmann distinguishes pre-Amarna, Amarna, and post-Amarna theological phases [AS01; SZ03] | These are analytical subdivisions, not proof that residents announced the beginning of an “Amarna era” |
| Costume/material signature | The broader Egyptian reference repertoire includes cloth and leather loincloths, dresses, bag-tunics, and headgear [VE93]; the specifically Amarna-attributed limestone fragment 21.9.531 depicts a pleated dress [M531] | Date individual garment types rather than assigning the whole repertoire to this episode; use the fragment for depicted pleating, not actual fiber, seam layout, universal transparency, or the invention of pleating |
| Naming practices | Theophoric names are one possible witness to personal religious relationships; Szpakowska invokes them against restricting personal piety to the Ramesside age [SZ03] | Record divine element, bearer, date, context, and legal/address use; royal names alone cannot establish ordinary naming rules |
| Calendar | Civil and religious timekeeping require separate tracks; Parker reconstructs a civil calendar alongside lunar religious calendars [P50] | Do not infer a leap-day reform from a theological reform; this entry asserts no specific Amarna festival-to-civil-date equation — [P50] was inspected at catalogue level only |

- **Demonstrated drift:** a shift in official theology, an expanding written language stage, retained older written registers, and nonexclusive household cult evidence need separate timelines [A13; AS01; SZ03].
- **Contested:** Assmann's sharp placement of personal piety in the Ramesside age; [SZ03] identifies earlier expressions and the danger of defining the phenomenon through late phraseology.
- **Thin here:** nonelite self-explanations, regional adoption intervals, the full local name distribution, and exact festival scheduling. These sources do not supply a continuous chronicle of every household's response; mark those cells U rather than inventing it.
- **Source bias:** elite monuments select ideal bodies and official claims; surviving objects reflect excavation and collection histories. Egyptian testimony is not interchangeable with later Greek accounts or colonial-era reconstructions.

## 6. Filled example: a disposable fictional worksheet, not canon

**All worksheet states in §§6–8 are F.** “Worksheet A,” its eras, institutions, words, and laws are invented solely to demonstrate the method; they identify no existing world-bible culture and establish no truth about a living tradition.

- Substrate: a river plain with local flax, an undyed linen-wrap baseline, and later access to upland wool. No garment is licensed solely by its symbolic desirability.
- Language: fictional branch A of fictional family R; a second contact group supplies wool cloth, but its family relationship is unknown, not inferred from trade.
- Baseline naming: given name + parent-name phrase; divine names occupy a distinct liturgical register. Writing is a locally invented alphabet; its capitals below are transliteration conventions, not historical scripts.
- Mythic baseline: a reed-associated guardian and household ancestors; their relationship can be renegotiated without replacing every story.
- Calendar assumption: this fictional planet's seasonal year is exactly 365.25 local days; the initial civil count is 12 × 30 days + 5 closing days. These are stipulated numbers, not an assertion about Earth's tropical year.
- Era ranges are designer coordinates, not a universal tempo of cultural change. In-world year counts can differ from these coordinates.

## 7. Make the drift reproducible: laws, names, and carriers

| Item/domain | E0 | E1 | E2 | E3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary word “reed” | /ˈsapa/, SAPA | /ˈsafa/, SAPA | /saf/, SAF | /saf/, civil archaizing SAPA |
| Ordinary word “cloth” | /ˈmapa/, MAPA | /ˈmafa/, MAPA | /maf/, MAF | /maf/, MAF; not every word archaized |
| Guardian's liturgical name | /ˈsapa/, SAPA | Retained /ˈsapa/ in taught temple register | Same taught register; vernacular gloss uses /saf/ | Same taught register; antiquarians dispute the civil spelling policy |
| Personal-name record | Given + parent | Same slots; optional cult-derived given names | Household byname added to tax record | Administrative slots persist despite restoration rhetoric |

- **Ordered law list, ordinary inherited vocabulary only:** L1 `/p/ > /f/ between vowels`; then L2 `unstressed final /a/ > ∅`. Both words obey both laws; invented additional roots must pass the same environments.
- **Ordering check:** if L2 happened first, /ˈsapa/ would become /sap/ and the intervocalic environment would disappear; /saf/ therefore requires the stated order in this toy derivation.
- **Register exception is explained, not excused:** the taught temple name is continued access to an older register. Do not silently grant every divine or aristocratic name immunity from ordinary sound change.
- **Naming check:** a tax byname is not automatically hereditary, exclusive, or used in address; state each later transition separately.
- **Symbol check:** “reed” as plant, guardian name, tax seal, guild license, and loyalty tie has multiple institutional readings. Shared form creates disputes, not a single meaning for all users.
- **Arithmetic check:** compare equal year-counts: four civil years contain 1,460 days, four stipulated seasonal years 1,461; at 240 years the totals differ by 60 days. Adding leap days stops further average rate drift but does not itself align the already-offset new year.
- **Carrier check:** spelling can travel in copied charters; a garment through manufacture or inheritance; an oral rite through practitioners. Do not let a textile survive centuries merely because a written formula can.

## 8. Coupling ledger: explain links without synchronizing everything

| Seam | Explicit causal path | Lag, resistance, or unchanged system |
|---|---|---|
| E0 → E1 | Levy office finances red braid, authorizes seal, and commissions guardian redaction; records prefer inherited spelling | Household cult continues; calendar arithmetic RETAINED; L1 is not attributed to a royal decree |
| E1 → E2 | Wool contact enables a new garment construction; treaties license an epithet and guild mark; civil clerks standardize current spellings | Temples keep older register and count; sound-law chronology and textile import chronology are independently specified |
| E2 → E3 | Restoration court selects archived spelling, levy-era myth, and undyed ceremonial dress as its antiquity package | No reverse sound change; wool trade and leap rule continue; village variants expose selective memory |

- For a real reference case, this ledger requires witnesses. Similar timing alone does not prove that a calendar reform caused a language change or vice versa.
- A fictional causal path needs incentives and resources too: who pays for copies, cloth, training, observatories, and enforcement; who benefits from keeping the old practice?
- **Counterfactual test:** remove the court reform. Could the sound change or wool import still happen? If yes, retain it as an independent trajectory, not a compulsory consequence.

## 9. Applying the worksheet to a fictional culture

1. Choose the focal region and users before selecting era labels; create parallel rows for meaningful contemporaneous disagreement.
2. Establish a baseline for every column, including language family/writing/naming and the material supply chain, not just a pantheon list and a costume silhouette.
3. Pick a boundary witnessed by a change of institution, contact route, production, or self-description. Explain which columns it does **not** change.
4. Give each changed cell a mechanism, adoption interval, dissenter, and surviving carrier. Copying old spelling, teaching old speech, and preserving an old garment require different explanations.
5. Run phonology through common words and names; keep script, spoken form, translation, and folk etymology in separate fields.
6. Calculate calendar rates and phase offsets; distinguish accession-year renaming, month-name replacement, leap rules, and festival rescheduling.
7. Stage a cross-era misreading: a clerk hears a new pronunciation under an old spelling; a guild badge is mistaken for royal clearance; a ceremony falls on different days in temple and civil counts.
8. Offer lore-shokunin the matrix and alternatives, not an asserted world history. Preserve open decisions and evidence limits alongside the proposed coupling.

## 10. Handoff and acceptance checks

- [ ] Every row has an interval, locality/group, and reason for the boundary; broad “ancient/medieval/modern” labels are not doing causal work.
- [ ] Every column has a state and A/R/F/U status; unknowns carry `(to-verify)` locally rather than disappearing into a footnote.
- [ ] Family, spoken stage, writing system, register, and naming slots are distinguished; no dynasty-to-language or script-to-language identity assumed.
- [ ] Mythic claims identify who transmits them; “official,” “widely practiced,” and “universally believed” are not treated as synonyms.
- [ ] Costume claims distinguish fiber/construction evidence from representation and law; material access and selective enforcement are specified.
- [ ] Calendar arithmetic, epoch, festival anchors, and adoption geography are separable; a restored name does not conceal changed machinery.
- [ ] At each seam, an audit records both changes and retentions; REVIVE identifies its selected source era rather than claiming timeless authenticity.
- [ ] Handoff contains matrix, transition ledger, law/name tables, source pointers, unresolved claims, and alternative interpretations; canon admission remains lore-shokunin's decision.

## 11. What a worldbuilder can take

- Take **uneven time**: dynasties, speech, written registers, fibers, cult institutions, and feast rules can have different boundaries without making the world inconsistent.
- Take **institutional carriers**: a seal, copied charter, taught divine name, garment workshop, and calendar office explain how couplings spread and why some persist.
- Take **contextual polysemy**: the same reed can mean passage at home, clearance in court, and jurisdiction in a guild; record audience and occasion instead of forcing a universal dictionary.
- Take **selective revival**: a convincing restoration can preserve contemporary grammar, imported wool, and new leap arithmetic while claiming ancient names and ceremonial wraps.
- Take mechanisms, not a composite “Egyptian-looking” culture. The filled worksheet is a disposable test fixture; no fictional polity, deity, costume law, or historical event is proposed as canon.

## 12. Provenance cautions

- Verification date: 2026-10-17. Publisher excerpt [A13 §§1.1–1.4], original article [B58], full review [SZ03], institutional calendar record [P50], clothing edition records [VE93], and object record [M531] were checked; this is not a claim that every cited book was read in full during compilation.
- [AS01] supplies a contested interpretive model; [SZ03] is a named scholarly challenge, not a reason to discard all of it. Earlier theophoric names or household amulets do not by themselves recover a complete theology.
- [P50]'s publication record verifies the work and describes its reconstruction, not every chronological inference. Calendar origins, lunar synchronization details, and exact Amarna festival dates are not established by the account inspected here and need direct text-specific rechecking before use.
- Egyptian evidence here is strongest for literate institutions and selected excavated contexts. Knowledge of nonelite regional experience is thin in this entry; continuous chronicle evidence for household adoption is scarce. A blank interval must stay blank.
- Colonial-era excavation, selective museum acquisition, elite funerary preservation, and modern display categories can distort the reference sample. A prestigious collection attribution is not an excavation-wide frequency estimate or a license to speak for present-day Egyptians.
- Modern national, racial, or universal-myth packages must not be projected backward. Afroasiatic is a language-family designation, not an ethnic essence; royal theology is not a whole population's mind; “ancient costume” is not one timeless uniform.
- Recheck object dating and contested reconstructions when a design depends on a narrow date. Keep all F material separate from historical evidence, and route any eventual world-bible choice to lore-shokunin rather than recording it as canon here.
