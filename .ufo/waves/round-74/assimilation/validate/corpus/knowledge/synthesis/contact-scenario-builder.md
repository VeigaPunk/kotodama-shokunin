## 1. Operating distinction: one contact history, several clocks

- **Reference** below means an attributed scholarly distinction; **design rule** means this entry's operational scaffolding; **exercise** means invented inputs and outputs, not an attested people or a world-bible proposal.
- Contact is an interaction among situated speakers, households, traders, craftspeople, ritual specialists, and authorities—not two sealed “cultures” pouring together.
- Design rule: give each contact event separate adoption dates for word, object, production skill, ritual practice, doctrinal interpretation, and naming institution. Blank means unresolved, not simultaneous.
- Coupling requires a carrier and a mechanism: a weaver teaches a technique; a broker repeats a unit; a sponsor introduces a name; a priest translates an epithet. Shared dates alone establish no causal arrow.
- Reverse arrows matter: a marriage alliance can secure a warehouse lease; a shrine's feast can create demand for imported cloth; a naming restriction can alter school enrollment and language transmission.
- Never infer the complete package: imported cloth ≠ immigrant wearer; foreign personal name ≠ foreign ancestry; mixed parentage ≠ bilingual childhood; shared sanctuary ≠ identical gods.

## 2. Named frameworks and their safe uses

| Framework | Use in the builder | Calibration / forbidden inference |
|---|---|---|
| Thomason–Kaufman: maintenance/borrowing versus shift-induced interference (1988, chs. 3–5) | First specify who continues speaking what, and who learns whose language | Contact intensity is a heuristic, not an era clock; a trade port need not produce a pidgin, creole, or mixed language |
| Haspelmath: lexical borrowing distinctions (2009) | Separate imported forms, translated expressions, and semantic borrowing; distinguish conventional loans from individual bilingual usage | A foreign-looking token alone establishes neither community adoption nor a date of arrival |
| Dietler: selective consumption and colonial entanglement (2010) | Track how recipients revalue goods and how demand produces dependencies | His case is Mediterranean France; do not universalize it, erase coercion, or equate acquisition with “becoming Greek” |
| Stewart–Shaw: syncretism/anti-syncretism (1994) | Track both combinations and institutions claiming to defend purity | “Pure” and “mixed” are contested classifications, not neutral measures of authenticity |
| This entry: event-and-carrier ledger | Join the four tracks without forcing their outcomes to match | Designer tooling, not a documented universal model of cultural change |

## 3. Contact dossier: fill before inventing words or costume

| Input | Required decision | What it constrains |
|---|---|---|
| Route and season | Travel time, sailing window, storage, overland supply, interruption risk | Which goods, specialists, and texts can actually recur |
| Contact population | Seasonal visitors, resident households, officials, refugees, enslaved workers, apprentices | Access and agency; distinguish voluntary, compelled, and prohibited movement |
| Repertoires | First languages, acquired languages, domains, literacy, interpreter access | Borrowing routes, pronunciation variants, who can challenge a translation |
| Jurisdictions | Warehouse, marriage, inheritance, religious, and document authorities | Whether one person's different names or vows are legally recognized |
| Household organization | Residence after marriage, caregivers, adoption, sponsorship, descent/property rules | Which name pools, languages, heirlooms, and rites children encounter |
| Production system | Finished imports, imported yarn, local weaving, repair, dye access | Why a garment's name, fiber, cut, and motif may have different origins |
| Sacred authorities | Who recognizes a deity, accepts offerings, licenses a feast, controls burial | Limits of ritual cooperation and religious equivalence |
| Status and resistance | Unequal rights, fees, sanctions, elite tastes, exclusion, reform movements | Who benefits from contact and who can refuse its outward signs |
| Evidence envelope | Text genres, likely surviving objects, excluded speakers, missing intervals | What an in-world investigator could know, rather than what the designer knows |

Design rule: model internal differences at least between quay, household, workshop, sanctuary, and registry; do not assign the entire port one bilingualism percentage or one religion.

## 4. Contact configurations: alternatives, not evolutionary stages

| Configuration | Language/material expectation to investigate | Myth and name questions |
|---|---|---|
| Seasonal exchange | Broker terminology and imported finished goods may circulate without resident donor households | Are visitor offerings tolerated? Are names merely transliterated on cargo lists? |
| Resident merchant enclave | Schooling, apprenticeships, and local production may create domain-specific bilingualism | Separate courts or shrines? Does a house name convey property access rather than ancestry? |
| Household incorporation | Caregiver networks may spread intimate vocabulary and names, unevenly | Which relatives name children? Which rite or burial affiliation remains non-negotiable? |
| Mission or state settlement | Learned language, script, official titles, and prescribed dress may spread through institutions | Who requires conversion names, and can people retain household alternatives? |
| Conquest or coerced labor regime | Restricted mobility and pressure toward language shift require explicit treatment | Are names imposed, erased, or reclaimed? Who cannot safely refuse religious identification? |
| Embargo or diaspora dispersal | Goods can stop while inherited vocabulary, expertise, and names persist | Which ritual objects are substituted? Who can still recognize overseas kin or titles? |

These are design possibilities, not predictions of individual behavior. Intermarriage is one transmission route, not a prerequisite for borrowing or a synonym for assimilation.

## 5. Build the loan strata, not a foreign-word garnish

1. Identify the immediate source language and intermediary; distinguish a word's ultimate etymology from the language through which the port received it.
2. Assign each item a domain, carrier, first possible adoption interval, recipient social network, meaning, spoken form, and written form.
3. Specify recipient sound substitutions and syllable repairs **at that interval**; apply them to the whole candidate stratum, not just attractive examples.
4. Apply later recipient changes to inherited items; record subsequent analogy, spelling pronunciation, name conservatism, and reborrowing separately.
5. Compare an early everyday form with a later learned or commercial borrowing only after explaining why speakers would borrow again.
6. Track calques and semantic loans separately: a translated deity-title or a new sense of an old cloth-word leaves no imported sound sequence.
7. Keep translation choices, code-switches, marginal specialist loans, and widely conventional vocabulary in different ledger states.
8. Never date a word simply from an object's earliest find: object arrival, word adoption, and earliest surviving written attestation are different events.

| Minimum lexical row | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| ID; source/intermediary; recipient; interval; carrier; form; sense; register; adaptation; later changes; attestation; uncertainty | Makes a claimed contact stratum reproducible and permits conflicting datings |

**Calibration:** a loan can preserve evidence of an older pronunciation, but backdating requires a defensible sound correspondence and exclusion of alternative transmission routes. A preserved spelling alone is not a preserved sound; borrowing hierarchies are tendencies, not bans.

## 6. Imported material culture: follow the production chain

- Separate **fiber/material → yarn/component → manufacture → cut/assembly → finish/dye → wearing → repair → deposition**. Different nodes can belong to different networks.
- For each object, record source, transport cost, buyer, maker, teaching relationship, substitute materials, restricted users, local name, and ritual afterlife.
- A local artisan can copy a foreign cut in local fiber; an imported textile can be recut into an indigenous garment. Neither requires total costume replacement.
- Design rule: give trade dress at least a work context, a legal/status context, and a ritual context; the same person need not wear one ethnic “uniform” in all three.
- Imported dyes or metal fittings may be the bottleneck even after weaving or tailoring becomes local; apprenticeship alone does not create absent raw materials.
- Retain failed and refused transfers: an expensive fastener, an unsuitable sailcloth, or a sacred motif that buyers will not wear makes selection visible.
- Material biographies and deposition are not mind-reading tools: a reused votive cloth can demonstrate reuse without proving its final owner's theology. Compare Dietler (2010) and the linked inference-method entry.

## 7. Myth syncretism: specify which operation occurred

| Operation | Record this evidence/design decision | Do not silently upgrade it into |
|---|---|---|
| Translation | One community renders an epithet in its own vocabulary | Agreement that the two named beings are identical |
| Juxtaposition | Two powers receive offerings in one venue or ceremony | A single pantheon or common creation story |
| Functional equation | A named speaker says “their protector is our protector” | Universal acceptance by both communities |
| Narrative incorporation | A received figure becomes a guest, ancestor, rival, spouse, or local manifestation in a specified telling | A timeless, uncontested genealogy |
| Institutional fusion | An authority joins offices, revenues, calendars, or cult titles | Identical household practices or personal beliefs |
| Anti-syncretic reform | Reformers prohibit a combination and explain their claim to purity | Proof that the supposedly pure past ever existed unchanged |

- Separate deity-name, epithet, image, ritual action, narrative, and office; mark who equates what, for which audience, from which era.
- Symbols need contextual readings: one knot may index freight certification at the quay and a vow at a shrine. Shared geometry does not establish diffusion; a carrier and chronology remain necessary.
- Stewart–Shaw's caution applies in both directions: do not label another tradition “contaminated,” and do not romanticize every combination as harmonious tolerance.

## 8. Intermarriage and names: institutions, not syllable blending

Use the linked naming-systems entry for slot inventories; the following is a **designer decision schema**, not a claim that all intercultural families follow one pattern.

| Decision | Possible consequences to model | Independent check |
|---|---|---|
| Who confers each name? | Caregiver gives everyday name; ancestor's namesake supplies another; sponsor registers a house affiliation | Identify the conferring person and occasion, not just “both cultures” |
| What transfers through marriage? | Residence, property eligibility, ritual duties, or nothing in the name itself | Do not assume a spouse takes a surname or that a house name is hereditary |
| Which authority records it? | Paired aliases, transliterations, translated names, or a single compulsory legal form | A bilingual cross-reference must establish identity; resemblance alone is insufficient |
| Who raises and teaches the child? | Different languages/names in home, school, workshop, and overseas correspondence | Mixed parentage neither guarantees nor prevents multilingual competence |
| Which affiliation can be refused? | Children retain one system, choose later, face exclusion, or use names strategically | Preserve unequal rights, consent, adoption, and coercion rather than treating every outcome as free choice |
| What changes across generations? | Name pools spread beyond intermarrying families; an occupational byname may later be fixed by law | Do not infer intermarriage backward from later name distributions |

A “mixed” name can be one source name plus recipient morphology, two context-specific names, or no overt mixture at all. Do not manufacture a universal half-and-half naming rule.

| 100–139: charter and specialist register | Sound change has ceased as an active rule; specialist loans introduce marginal /f v/ and new /ki/ sequences | Tariff categories distinguish a reimported premium textile; local production continues | Charter funds one festival, not one theology; a knot-loop badge gets a dated civic use | Registry cross-references names but does not impose hereditary surnames |

No causal claim here is “contact naturally progresses to fusion.” The charter, household, sound change, and embargo are separately stipulated events with different consequences.

## 11. Worked lexical/material ledger and household chain

| ID | Source and arrival | Recipient derivation | Coupled consequence / limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | S /kifa/ “patterned cloth,” adopted during 0–39 | N substitutes /p/ for /f/: /kipa/; later /k/ → /s/ before /i/: /sipa/ | By 70–99 /sipa/ means festival sash, including locally woven examples; its word-history no longer specifies fiber provenance |
| L2 | S /kifa/ borrowed again in 100–139 | Specialist N /kifa/ retains marginal /f/ and post-change /ki/ | Means the charter's premium import category, not every sash; /sipa/ and /kifa/ are doublets, not random spelling variants |
| L3 | S /vanu/ “coil-length,” adopted during 0–39 | N /wanu/, using existing /w/ | A borrowed unit-name can survive a later locally fixed standard; record the standard's value separately rather than inventing an etymology that fixes it |
| L4 | Wake Witness epithet translated during 40–69 | N-language expression glossed “witness of the outward path”; no conlang spelling supplied | A calque/translation decision, not evidence of a borrowed deity-name or proof of identical theology |

- Repairs are staged: early /f/ → /p/, /v/ → /w/; later /k/ → /s/ before /i/; only after that change ceases does a new specialist register license marginal foreign sounds. These are exercise rules, not claims about real phonological inevitability.
- Costume chain: imported cotton cloth → repair → instruction using imported yarn → local weaving → sash ritualization → linen substitution after interruption. Record each transition separately from L1–L2's word dates.
- Symbol chain: freight knot and return-loop remain distinct in earlier contexts; the charter's combined civic badge is a new convention. Two priests can wear it while denying deity equivalence.

| Test | Expected result for this exercise | Failure it detects |

| Remove the year-55 household but retain seasonal shipping | L1/L3 and imported cloth can still exist; this particular G1–G2 chain cannot | Treating every borrowing as evidence of intermarriage |

## 13. Bounded historical anchor and provenance cautions

- **Berenike is a port reference, not a civilization reconstructed here.** Sidebotham (2011) combines excavated artifacts, botanical/faunal remains, and texts for a Red Sea emporium linking the Mediterranean and Asia; use that diversity of evidence to structure questions, not as proof of this exercise's household or cult sequence.
- The publisher contents distinguish Ptolemaic activities, Roman inhabitants/trade, and the late port. This supports keeping political periods and occupational histories separate; it does not license one timeless “Roman port costume.”
- This entry's knowledge of individual Berenike households, local cult identifications, textile catalogs, and intermarriage is thin: Sidebotham (2011) is used at chapter level only (publisher contents checked 2026-10-17: ch. 6 “Inhabitants of Berenike in Roman Times”; chs. 11–13 commercial networks, trade, and the late port). A continuous household-level chronicle has not been established here: household-level detail, exact garment forms, named mixed-parentage cases, and proposed deity equations remain **(to-verify)** against dated publications and primary evidence.
- Do not fill chronicle gaps with seamless narratives. Cargo texts, official records, devotional inscriptions, finds, and later chronicles answer different questions and overrepresent different people; domestic speech and people excluded from record-making can remain poorly visible.
- **Colonial-source bias:** Dietler's material concerns colonial encounters; both ancient accounts and modern categories can privilege colonizers. Preserve local recipients' choices and constraints, and name coerced labor rather than presenting all exchange as free cosmopolitanism.
- **Contested scholarship:** “syncretism” can impose bounded religions that participants did not recognize; anti-syncretic purity claims are themselves historical actions. Use attributed positions and specific practices, not a designer's purity scale (Stewart & Shaw 1994).
- **Rejected shortcuts:** “pots = peoples,” universal symbol dictionaries, and “mixed parentage creates a half-and-half language” are not valid inference rules. Similar motifs or names alone cannot establish a diffusion path.
- Verification scope here is bibliographic records, publisher descriptions/contents, and the cited framework distinctions—not a full-text or catalog-level audit. A quotation, precise historical rate, family pedigree, or site-specific costume claim needs its own page/object/text reference; otherwise mark it **(to-verify)**.

## 14. What a worldbuilder can take; handoff without canon

- Take the **event-and-carrier ledger**: one ID links lexical, production, ritual, symbolic, and household consequences while preserving different adoption intervals and explicit non-adoptions.
- Take the **doublet method**: fix transmission and sound-change chronology before generating names or cloth terms; reserve a separate explanation for reborrowed prestige forms.
- Take the **split garment biography**: fiber, cut, maker, name, wearer, legal status, and sacred use need not share an origin.
- Take **paired name records plus a recognition rule**, not an ethnic naming blender; include households that retain only one system and people whose names were imposed.
- Deliver to lore-shokunin: proposed era table; actor/repertoire map; lexical rows; object chains; attributed cult equations and refusals; household/alias links; alternatives; open verification list; and a clear **reference / exercise / proposed-option** status on every packet.
- Leave deity identity, official names, actual marriages, legal adoption, metaphysical truth, dates, and accepted costume meanings undecided until lore-shokunin's canon process accepts them. This file supplies method and reference knowledge only.
