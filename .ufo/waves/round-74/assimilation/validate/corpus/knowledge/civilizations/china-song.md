## Scope and calibration

- **Reference unit:** a changing imperial society, not a single language community, aesthetic, religion, or costume package. Court, county school, commercial workshop, monastery, farming household, and maritime port require different evidence.
- **Method:** keep four layers separate: attested institution or object → historical interpretation → modern English gloss → optional fictional mechanism. A mechanism proposed here is not a claim about historical motives.
- **Strongest anchors here:** the examination system's institutional distinctions; Zhu Xi's conceptual vocabulary; the coexistence of religious models; the physical properties of one catalogued Ru vessel.
- **Thinner coverage:** ordinary spoken exchanges, non-elite women's personal naming, regional garment construction, and workshop taste speech. Elite prose, prescriptions, surviving luxury objects, and later compilations cannot fill these gaps automatically.

## Era model — stage the coupled systems, not just a dynasty label

| Stage | Institutional/material anchors | Coupling consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Northern Song, 960–1127 | Court at Kaifeng; expanding civil recruitment, schools, print, and commercial activity | Make classical education, urban consumption, and civil office interact; do not begin with Zhu Xi's mature synthesis |
| Eleventh-century reform disputes | Wang Anshi's reforms and opponents contest governance, education, and recruitment | A shared classical vocabulary can authorize conflicting policies; moral agreement does not imply political agreement |
| Late Northern Song court | Huizong's reign, 1100–1126; court patronage and the Ru-ware horizon | Separate imperial procurement and connoisseurship from what an ordinary household can acquire |
| Rupture of 1127 | Jurchen conquest of Kaifeng; displacement and southern reconstruction | Preserve older titles and textual authorities while relocating court, patrons, workshops, and memories |
| Southern Song, 1127–1279 | Court at Lin'an; southern commercial and scholarly networks; Zhu Xi, 1130–1200 | Stage academies, competing teachings, local cults, and commerce together, not a universally imposed philosophical orthodoxy |

- Chronology anchor: Kuhn (2009); recruitment detail: Chaffee (1995); intellectual development: Bol (2008).
- Song coexisted with Liao, Western Xia, Jin, and other polities at different dates. Its boundaries are not those of modern China; neither the north nor frontier populations disappear when the court moves south.
- Avoid the teleology “Tang aristocracy → Song perfect meritocracy → inevitable modernity.” These are disputed transformations with continuities, exclusions, and regional variation, not a universal civilization ladder.

## Language family, writing, and register ecology

- **Family:** Chinese varieties belong to Sinitic, conventionally within Sino-Tibetan. Song territories contained regional speech diversity; a single modern national pronunciation is not recoverable by reading their characters aloud today (Norman 1988).
- **Writing:** Chinese script is morphosyllabic, not a collection of language-independent pictures. Literary Sinitic could circulate across speech communities without making their spoken forms identical.
- **Retrieval convention:** Chinese characters and modern Mandarin pinyin below identify terms; they are not reconstructed Song pronunciations. Traditional character forms here are editorial choices, not facsimiles of a Song edition.
- **Sound anchor:** the *Guangyun* rhyme dictionary was completed in 1008; rhyme-book categories are evidence of a learned phonological tradition, not a recording of every speaker's accent (Norman 1988).
- **Design operation:** maintain separate spoken forms, literary readings, and written headwords. Decide which professions learn each, and which genres tolerate colloquial syntax.

| Communicative setting | Register pressures | What not to flatten |
|---|---|---|
| Classical commentary or academy discussion | Citation, exegesis, moral cultivation, teacher lineage | Not every learned speaker belongs to Zhu Xi's school |
| Policy memorial or examination response | Precedent, ordered argument, deference, remonstrance | Dissent can use the same classics as the policy it attacks |
| Family instruction or ritual prescription | Kinship position, ancestral continuity, household duties | Prescribed behavior is not a census of actual behavior |
| Bookshop and workshop | Edition identity, cost, legibility, carving, correction, reputation | Business competence and examination success are different skills |
| Poetry, song lyric, storytelling | Genre-specific meter, rhyme, performance, allusion | Literary innovation is not simply “vernacular replacing classical” |

## Neo-Confucian lexicon — concepts that join world and conduct

- **Named framework:** Bol's historical treatment of neo-Confucianism resists projecting a finished orthodoxy backward; Thompson's account supplies distinctions within Zhu Xi's synthesis. “Neo-Confucianism” is a modern umbrella, not one timeless native organization.
- These are working glosses, not quotations or one-to-one translations. Many terms long predate Song; the historical move lies in their organization and use.

| Headword | Working range | Coupling lever / translation guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| 理 *lǐ* | Pattern, coherence, principle | Link ethical understanding and the intelligibility of things; not simply statutory law or a separate supernatural substance |
| 氣 *qì* | Psychophysical or vital stuff/process | Bodies, dispositions, and material change share a vocabulary; do not silently translate it as measurable magical energy |
| 性 *xìng* | Nature, including human moral nature | Makes education and cultivation claims about what people can become; school-specific arguments matter |
| 心 *xīn* | Heart-mind | Affects, attention, judgment, and thought are not partitioned by an English heart/brain opposition |
| 仁 *rén* | Humaneness, benevolence | Treat a virtue as enacted through relationships, not a decorative abstract virtue-name |
| 禮 *lǐ* | Ritual propriety, rites | Different character and concept from 理; pronunciation alone erases a useful distinction |
| 道 *dào* | Way | A contested claim to right order and practice, not an automatic synonym for a Daoist religion |
| 道學 *dàoxué* | Learning of the Way | A historical scholarly identification; not interchangeable with everyone who studied classics |
| 格物 / 致知 *géwù / zhìzhī* | Investigating things / extending knowledge | Couple inquiry to cultivation; neither an exact equivalent of laboratory science nor merely collecting facts |
| 敬 *jìng* | Reverent attentiveness, seriousness | Gives posture, disciplined attention, reading, and ritual a shared ethical register |
| 天理 / 人欲 *tiānlǐ / rényù* | Heavenly pattern / human desires | Use within the argument of a particular thinker; “all embodied needs are evil” is an overtranslation |

- **Do not literalize the lexicon:** borrowing *qi* does not establish magic; borrowing *li* does not prove every artisan consciously explained firing through Zhu Xi.
- **Orthodoxy staging:** Zhu Xi's authority developed amid disputes and political attacks. The later Yuan examination settlement should not be installed as the rule of early Song; an exact decree/date used in a scene needs separate verification.
- **Fictional application:** let rival schools disagree over the same headword's implications for taxation, mourning, schooling, or craft training. Keep the school's argument, institutional patron, and opponent's label distinct.

## Examination meritocracy — rank is a process, not a character trait

- **Named framework:** Chaffee's “attempt at meritocracy” keeps institutional aspirations and unequal access in the same model. Recruitment was not exclusively by examination; privilege and other routes also mattered.
- A useful broad sequence is qualifying/local competition → central departmental examination → palace examination/ranking. Rules and school-based alternatives changed; do not substitute an invariant Ming–Qing ladder.

| Term or distinction | Song-sensitive use | Register/design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 科舉 *kējǔ* | Examination recruitment system | A route to status and office, not a religion or proof of personal virtue |
| 進士 *jìnshì* | Prestigious examination category/qualification | Qualification, appointment, actual office, and official rank need separate fields |
| 舉人 *jǔrén* | Recommended/qualified candidate in Song contexts | Do not automatically import the later permanent provincial-degree sense |
| 士 / 士大夫 *shì / shìdàfū* | Learned status and the scholar-official/elite world, context-dependent | “Scholar,” “degree-holder,” and “serving official” are not interchangeable |
| 策 *cè* | Policy-question/response context | Gives institutional reasons to master a formal argumentative voice |
| 糊名 / 謄錄 *húmíng / ténglù* | Concealing identities / recopying scripts | Impartiality is engineered through documents and labor, not merely proclaimed |
| 蔭 *yìn* | Privileged protection/entry associated with officeholding kin | Meritocratic rhetoric can coexist with inherited advantage |
| 書院 *shūyuàn* | Academy; roles and affiliations vary | Intellectual prestige need not be identical to the state's examining authority |

- **Access model:** male eligibility, educational expense, household support, regional opportunity, and connections shape the candidate pool; anonymity at marking does not equal equal opportunity before the examination.
- **Failure model:** repeated preparation and failure help create a learned population larger than the officeholding population. Do not give every literate man a government post.
- **Coupled design:** model training funds → textual access → examination recognition → appointment → dress entitlement. Break any arrow when evidence or a fictional institution requires it.
- **Speech test:** a candidate, a successful graduate without appointment, a clerk, and a serving official should not have identical titles or authority to command.

## Printing and commerce — calibrate “print-capitalism”

- **Comparative frame, not period name:** Anderson (1991) links print-capitalism to imagined national communities in a different historical argument. Song commercial printing does not by itself establish capitalist society, mass literacy, nationalism, or a European-style vernacular transition.
- **Song anchor:** Chia (2002) follows Jianyang publishing across the eleventh–seventeenth centuries. Use its Song evidence selectively; later Ming products, business arrangements, and readership cannot all be backdated.
- **Technology:** woodblock printing remained central; Bi Sheng's eleventh-century movable-type experiment did not instantly replace it. Manuscript copying, manuscript circulation, and printed books continued together.
- **Production chain:** obtain/collate text → prepare calligraphic copy → carve blocks → print → assemble → distribute → correct or recut. Cheap reproduction still depends on skilled labor, capital, paper, storage, and demand.

| Working vocabulary | Useful distinction | Coupling to build |
|---|---|---|
| 版 *bǎn*, 刻 *kè*, 印 *yìn* | Block/edition; carve; print or impress, according to context | Separate ownership of blocks, authorship, carving skill, and sale |
| 卷 *juàn* | Textual division or fascicle | A work's textual divisions need not equal its number of physical bound volumes |
| 書坊 *shūfāng* | Commercial book establishment | Pedagogy, local business networks, and textual prestige can share an institution |
| Government / private-family / commercial imprint | Analytical publishing categories; not interchangeable patrons | The same classic can acquire different authority through sponsor and edition |
| Commentary, anthology, examination aid | Different editorial products, not transparent copies of an author | The organization of knowledge can become a saleable service |
| Colophon, preface, correction notice | Paratextual functions; specific wording depends on the artifact | Credit, donation, authority, and quality can be displayed around the text |

- The table is a research lexicon, not a set of attested shopkeeper utterances. Exact Song trade compounds and local dialogue spellings remain `(to-verify)` until tied to an imprint or dated passage.
- **Commercial/ritual coupling:** a printed religious text may be sponsored for merit or devotion, sold commercially, or both; a sale does not cancel a devotional motive (Ebrey and Gregory 1993).
- **Fictional test:** let a printed examination aid circulate more widely than the expensive schooling needed to exploit it. Knowledge access and credential access should be independently adjustable.

## Mythic and pantheon core — plural authorities, not one divine civil service

- **Named framework:** Hymes (2002) distinguishes bureaucratic and personal models of divinity, which may contend or combine. Heavenly officials are important, but they are not a master key to every cult.
- “Confucian,” “Buddhist,” and “Daoist” identify traditions and institutions, not necessarily mutually exclusive memberships for every household (Ebrey and Gregory 1993).

| Religious layer | Reference core | Worldbuilding coupling |
|---|---|---|
| Heaven and ancestral order | *Tian* 天; ancestral rites and household/lineage obligations | Link legitimacy, kinship, sacrifice, mourning, and the language of proper conduct; Heaven is not automatically a personified creator |
| Buddhist institutions | Buddhas, bodhisattvas such as Guanyin, monasteries, merit, rebirth, salvation | Separate a patron's devotional vocabulary from a scholar's polemic about Buddhism |
| Daoist institutions | Divine ranks, ritual specialists, scriptures, cosmic administration and transcendents | Authority may depend on ritual transmission and registers, not civil examination credentials |
| Local protective cults | Particular mountains, temples, deities, miracle traditions, and patrons | A god may be approached as a personal protector rather than a distant official |
| Ghosts and dangerous dead | Misfortune, contested obligations, exorcism, accounts of strange events | A miracle tale reports a meaningful claim, not independently verified supernatural events |

- Do not give every deity a universally agreed portfolio. Record locality, worshipper, ritual specialist, title-granting authority, and source date before mapping a pantheon.
- Precise regional cult distributions and the local balance of Buddhist, Daoist, and other ritual practices are outside this entry's verified resolution; specific assignments remain `(to-verify)`.

## Symbolic system — correspondences have institutions and contexts

| Symbolic resource | Historical anchor / qualification | Transferable operation |
|---|---|---|
| Yin–yang and five phases, 五行 *wǔxíng* | Correlative cosmology; “phases” is safer than five immutable chemical elements | Connect season, direction, process, and classification without assigning a universal fantasy spell to each |
| 太極 *tàijí* | Cosmological vocabulary developed in Song philosophical discussion | A cosmological diagram needs an author, interpretation, and transmission history; not every familiar modern diagram is Song evidence |
| Written lineage, names, and seals | Textual identity can authorize kinship, ownership, or office | Make reading and authentication skills socially consequential |
| Antiquarian forms and ancient models | Song scholarly and court interest in antiquity (Kuhn 2009) | Old vessel forms can legitimate new ritual or collecting practices; revival is not unchanged survival |
| 青 *qīng* and jade comparisons | Color and material comparison cross-cut modern blue/green categories | Keep hue, translucency, surface, and ethical analogy as separate semantic dimensions |

- A correspondence table is not proof that every person recognized it, nor that rank-color regulations, ceramic color, and cosmological color had identical meanings.

## Costume and material signature — distinguish wearer, occasion, and artifact

| Surface | Song reference base | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Officials' dress | Robes, belts, and headwear including *futou* 幞頭; office and occasion matter | Do not paste later Ming–Qing animal rank squares or a Qing queue onto Song figures |
| Layered garments | Jackets/overgarments, robes, skirts, and trousers vary by wearer and setting | *Beizi* 褙子 is a useful research headword, not one universal reconstruction; dated excavated Southern Song examples (Huang Sheng tomb, Fuzhou, 1243) document particular straight-collared, front-opening, side-slit cuts (Fujian Provincial Museum 1982) |
| Textile economy | Silk production and women's textile labor are major social/material anchors | Fiber, weave, embroidery, dye, and finish are separate status variables; no universal silk wardrobe |
| Gender and the body | Footbinding spread during the period, unevenly; Ebrey (1993) rejects a simple story of uniformly declining female agency | Do not depict all women as bound-footed, confined, nameless, or economically inactive |
| Learned consumption | Books, calligraphy materials, tea equipment, and collected objects | Refinement depends on labor, money, access, and judgment; it is not a trait of every “Chinese” character |
| Court versus market | Luxury procurement and commercial consumption coexist | Modest-looking objects can be expensive; bright or elaborate textiles do not contradict pale ceramic taste |

- Garment labels here guide research, not pattern cutting. For a reconstruction, require a dated excavated garment, securely identified image, or a precise clothing regulation; visual evidence and prescriptive text answer different questions.
- Do not derive every garment directly from neo-Confucian modesty. Climate, textile technology, work, fashion, rank, ritual, and regional inheritance also constrain dress.

## Ru ware — a controlled taste lexicon, not “Song minimalism”

- **Object anchor:** Cleveland Museum of Art, *Brush Washer*, accession 1957.40; dated late 1000s–1127; attributed to Qingliangsi, Baofeng, Henan. The catalogue describes an ash-grey body, soft grey-blue glaze with fine crackle, soft luster, subtle variation, and small “sesame-seed” spur marks.
- **Setting:** Ru court ware belongs to a short late-Northern-Song production horizon. This does not mean all ceramics from the region, or all Song ceramics, were reserved for the court.
- The museum's “simplicity and refined elegance” is modern curatorial description. It is useful evidence about present interpretation, not a transcribed Song connoisseur's speech.

| Taste axis | Controlled descriptive vocabulary | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Hue | Grey-blue; blue-green comparisons where the object supports them; *qing* as a retrieval concept | An exact modern color-chip boundary or one invariant Ru color |
| Surface | Soft luster, fine crackle, subtle variation | A universal doctrine that cracks or firing imperfections were morally preferred |
| Body / glaze | Ash-grey ceramic body versus colored glaze | “Jade” as literal raw material, or an authenticated glaze recipe |
| Manufacture | Small spur marks from firing supports | Decoration or a mystical set of dots deliberately placed for the viewer |
| Form | Restrained profile; balance between vessel shape and glaze | The absence of design labor, cost, or status display |
| Provenance | Court association; kiln attribution; later collection history | Continuous ownership, exact original use, or a known original owner's words |

- **Lexical discipline:** *tiānqīng* 天青 (“sky blue”) for Ru is first datable in later records — the earliest located mention is Zhou Mi's *Wulin Jiushi* on a 1179 Southern Song court event (“天青汝窯金瓶”), with Ming–Qing ceramic texts continuing the label (Zhang 2023). Do not present it here as verified Song workshop terminology.
- The “sky after rain” instruction story is a late-Ming anecdote tied to Chai ware and the Later Zhou ruler Chai Rong, first recorded in Xie Zhaozhe's Wanli-era *Wuzazu* (五雜俎); attributing it to Huizong is even less credible (Zhang 2023). Do not quote it as Huizong's order or infer a glaze specification from it.
- Do not import Japanese *wabi-sabi*, auction-price rhetoric, a fixed surviving-object count, or a modern “five great kilns” shopping list as a complete Song aesthetic system.
- **Coupling exercise, not historical fact:** make a fictional connoisseur judge hue, glaze feel, profile, and provenance while a potter judges firing risk and a merchant judges reproducibility. Their vocabularies should overlap without becoming identical.

## Naming practices — identity, address, and transmission

| Slot | Reference practice | Design constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Surname and personal name, 姓 / 名 | Surname normally precedes the personal name in Chinese order | Do not treat a European-style given-name-first display as indigenous order |
| Courtesy name, 字 *zì* | Especially relevant to educated adult men's social identification | Do not assign one automatically to every person regardless of social evidence |
| Literary sobriquet, 號 *hào* | An additional self/social identification associated with learned life | Keep legal identity, literary persona, and actual address form distinct |
| Office, locality, teacher designation | A person can be identified through role or association; “Master Zhu” is not another hereditary surname | One person needs an alias graph, not several accidentally separate biographies |
| Imperial names | Personal, reign-era, temple, and posthumous designations perform different functions | A later historian's label is not necessarily what a subject would say to a living emperor |
| Women's recorded identities | Sources may identify women relationally or by natal surname (Ebrey 1993) | Archival omission of a personal name is not evidence that the person had none |

- Name avoidance can affect writing, copying, and printed transmission; exact banned character, reign, and avoidance strategy require dated evidence `(to-verify)`, not a blanket claim that every occurrence was erased.
- Generation characters are a possible lineage device, not a compulsory template for every Song household. Verify a particular family's genealogy before using them as historical data.

## What a worldbuilder can take

1. Build a status ledger that separates learned reputation, examination qualification, appointment, office, wealth, and ritual authority; then derive titles and dress permissions from the appropriate field.
2. Design a learned lexicon whose terms operate across ethics and cosmology, but specify who contests their meanings and who pays for instruction.
3. Couple print markets to schooling and religious sponsorship without assuming that increased textual circulation creates either national identity or equal access.
4. Give bureaucratic gods, personal protectors, and ancestral obligations different relationship mechanics; do not resolve their coexistence into one universal pantheon chart.
5. Stage relocation and regime change through surviving book blocks, altered names, displaced patrons, and remembered materials; label such combinations as fictional proposals.
6. Give refined taste an acquisition pathway and a vocabulary of discrimination. A pale vessel can encode expensive access without making an entire society austere.
7. Hand lore-shokunin alternatives with date range, social setting, supporting source, uncertain edges, and dependent naming/dress rules. Only that sibling chooses and records fictional canon.

## Provenance cautions and stop rules

- **Verification boundary:** bibliographic identities and the linked web passages were checked; no claim is made that every cited monograph was inspected page by page. No invented page references or purported primary-source quotations are supplied.
- **Prescriptive bias:** examination regulations, moral treatises, ritual manuals, and court records privilege literate elites and intended order. Archaeology, contracts, epitaphs, and local histories can change the picture; absence from a chronicle is not evidence of social absence.
- **Transmission bias:** later editions, reconstructed compilations, museum collecting, imperial connoisseurship, and modern auction narratives select different pasts. Date the text witness as well as the event it describes.
- **Calibration:** “perfect meritocracy,” “a single Chinese religious bureaucracy,” “Song minimalism,” and inevitable progress toward capitalism are overgeneralizations, not established consensus. A neo-Confucian explanation of all dress is likewise inadequate.
- **Source politics:** colonial-era and Western collecting categories such as “celadon” can remain useful labels but are not native Song self-descriptions. Nationalist and commercial heritage narratives also need scrutiny; neither Western nor Chinese provenance guarantees neutrality.
- **Thin evidence stays thin:** exact vernacular dialogue, local cult prevalence, original vessel use, named garment construction, and period taste adjectives require passage- or object-level checks. Preserve `(to-verify)` markers until those checks exist; do not turn an attractive analogy into testimony.
