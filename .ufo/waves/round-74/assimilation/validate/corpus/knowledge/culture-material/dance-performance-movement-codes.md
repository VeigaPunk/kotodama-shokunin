## Movement vocabulary as a lexicon

Movement vocabularies behave like lexicons: they have units, combinatory rules,
and school-dependent divergence. `Nāṭyaśāstra` defines the compositional unit
hierarchy — karaṇas combine into mātṛkās and aṅgahāras, so a movement
"compound" is built from named atomic units (S871).
The gesture manual tradition extends this to expressive parts: hand gestures,
glances, head/brow/neck movements form an inventory a performer can cite
(S872).
The same pattern is visible in ballet: foot positions are standard across
methods, but arm positions diverge by school — so a "standard" movement code
is school-relative (S868).
A worldbuilder deciding a dance "grammar" should fix, in order: the atomic
units, the combinatorics, and which school's variant the builder proposes to lore.

## Notation systems: writing the moving body

Two living notation systems give a world a way to record movement outside live
transmission. Kinetography Laban/Labanotation is governed by the ICKL, whose
practitioners practice the system originated by Rudolf Laban (S870).
Benesh Movement Notation (BMN) is described by the Royal Academy of Dance as a
system for documenting human movement, used in documentation and
reconstruction of dance pieces (S869).
For a fictional culture, the existence of a notation system changes how dance
is transmitted: it makes reconstruction possible, turns choreography into a
text, and creates a scribal class.

## Dance–song–voice coupling

Several traditions codify performance as a triad that must stay coupled.
Flamenco is the baile (dance), cante (song), toque (guitar playing) triad,
with gender-differentiated movement technique and transmission through
dynasties and clubs (S865).
Tango is defined by UNESCO as the music, dance and poetry of tango together —
an embodied identity that both embodies and encourages diversity and cultural
dialogue (S861).
When a world has a performing art, the coupling rule matters: which of the
arts can be separated, and what breaks when one is removed.

## Festival and procession registers

Performance can be quantified as a procession register. The Carnival of
Oruro's dancers walk four kilometres of processional route and repeat the
journey for a full twenty hours without interruption (S864). Such figures give
a scale anchor: infrastructure (route, duration, numbers), costume endurance,
and the logistics of mass movement.
Masked dance-drama compounds movement with music, theatre and social critique:
Talchum's ensemble of six to ten musicians accompanies masked performers who
humorously explore social issues through dramatic combinations of songs,
dances, movements and dialogue (S866).

## Ritual movement codes

Ritual movement can be codified to the level of mechanics. The Mevlevi Sema
ceremony's inscription records that the whirlers begin to rotate on their left
feet in short twists, using the right foot to drive their bodies around the
left foot (S862): an exact kinematic rule with a training regime and venue
register.
Chhau's movement vocabulary includes mock combat techniques, stylized gaits
of birds and animals, and movements modelled on the chores of village
housewives (S863) — evidence that a movement lexicon can index a culture's
daily labor and martial practice.
A worldbuilder designing a ritual dance can thus specify it as a kinematic rule
set rather than a vague "ceremony".

## Role and stage codification

Theatrical movement is often codified by role class. Kabuki distinguishes aragoto (rough style) and wagoto (soft style) role types, alongside onnagata and the stylized pose (mie) (S867). Role classes give a world a shortcut: movement registers attach to character types, not to individual performers.

## Worldbuilding application

Build an invented culture's movement code in this order:
1. name the atomic movement units and their combinatorics (S871);
2. add the expressive gesture inventory (S872);
3. decide whether the culture writes movement down, and in which notation (S869);
4. if notation is chosen, bind it to the notation authority (S870);
5. fix the coupling rules between dance, music and voice (S865);
6. decide whether the contact/identity register is a triad or a pair (S861);
7. choose whether festival performance is a procession register with scale figures (S864);
8. define the masked/drama compound form (S866);
9. state the ritual movement mechanics (S862);
10. index the movement lexicon to daily labor and martial practice (S863);
11. attach role classes to movement styles (S867).
The refusal rule stays: these are codes for worlds, never claims about what
any real tradition "really means".
