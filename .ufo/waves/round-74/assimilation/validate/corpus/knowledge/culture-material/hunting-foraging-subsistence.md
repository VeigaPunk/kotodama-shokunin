## 1. The register property: subsistence is an argument about landscape and season

**Master claim.** A hunting/foraging culture is not "people who lack farming" — it is a production system that reads a landscape as a schedule. Where the agrarian register argues about the *field* (`agrarian-field-system-lexicon.md:3`), this register argues about the **patch**: which resources are worth working, when they are worth working, whose they are, and what the group does with the surplus. Every subsistence design makes four load-bearing choices, in order:

1. **What is harvested** — the resource list and its risk profile (reliable small returns vs spectacular risky ones).
2. **Where and when** — the seasonal round: territory, timing, and who may take what where.
3. **By whom, with what** — the division of labour and the toolkit it implies.
4. **What happens to the surplus** — immediate distribution, storage, or conversion into status and trade.

**The four-condition test** decides whether an activity belongs to this register:

1. **Wild source** — the organism or material is not planted, herded or owned as a standing crop (edges: managed burning, sown patches, semi-tended groves are horticultural boundary cases, not fails — decide by the *dominant* procurement mode, cf. `algonquian.md:141-142` mixing gardens into the round).
2. **Located, not produced** — skill goes into finding, intercepting and taking, not into altering the growth cycle.
3. **Processed on the production side** — the kill/harvest is transformed (dressed, dried, rendered, cached) before it becomes cuisine (`cuisine-food-lexicon.md:3` receives it at the meal).
4. **Distributed by rule** — shares, taboos and obligations exist; a lone meal eaten alone is an episode, not a system.

Failures are diagnostic: a **garden plot** fails 1–2 (agrarian); a **recipe or feast** fails 3 (cuisine); a **war-bow** fails 2 — its target is not food (warrior); a **totem or sacrifice** passes the animal but not production (myth-symbol); a **harvest festival** fails 1 (calendar); a **famine food** is a cuisine-category (`cuisine-food-lexicon.md:56`), this entry owns the *scarcity logic* that creates it.

## 3. The production chain

The register's backbone is a five-link chain. Design each link; a culture that skips one names its stress point.

| Link | What it contains | Failure mode if skipped |
|---|---|---|
| **Locate** | tracking and sign-reading, territory knowledge, phenological cues, fish runs and migrations, scapulimancy/divination (`divination-omen-systems.md` owns the omen grammar — cite it) | a hunt that always finds its quarry reads as magic; a locate failure is a story engine |
| **Acquire** | stalk/ambush, drive (beaters, fire, cliffs, corrals), trap and snare, net (land and water), angling and weir, harpoon and projectile, gathering and digging, honey-raiding | "they hunt" with no mode is an unfurnished sentence; mode determines toolkit, labour and risk |
| **Process** | butchery and dressing, flensing, gutting, fibre/bark stripping, winnowing, leaching (acorn), detoxification, rendering | meat that teleports to the pot; processing is where craft, gender and taboo actually live |
| **Preserve / store** | drying, smoking (`fire-fuel-light.md` owns the fire), freezing/ice pits, caching, fermenting, rendering fat, storage pits and raised platforms | surplus with no storage technology is a contradiction — it either rots or becomes a delayed-return system (§6) |
| **Distribute** | kill-owner vs sharers, first-share rules, demand-sharing, feast redistribution, meat-for-crops exchange with farming neighbours | a hunting culture with no share rules produces instant hierarchy paradoxes (§6) |

Each link generates its own lexicon — `algonquian.md:148` shows the column set (resource, condition, tool, action, product, access relationship, skill, failure mode); generalize it, don't copy it.

## 4. Toolkit and materials economics

A mobile toolkit is designed under a different constraint set than a workshop's (`craft-artisan-lexicon.md` owns the workshop):

- **Weight and carry** — every gram walks; prefer multipurpose tools (the knife that skins, whittles and eats) over specialist sets, and count what is cached vs carried (`inuit.md:155-159` shows material choice and the operational chain).
- **Maintainability on the trail** — hafting, re-sharpening, re-stringing; the repair skill is part of the toolkit, and `vessel-container-lexicon.md` owns the containers it feeds.
- **Passive vs active gear** — traps, snares, weirs and nets work while the owner sleeps (delayed-return logic); stalking gear spends hunter-hours. A culture's gear mix is its labour budget made visible.
- **Materials ecology** — bone/antler/ivory where wood is scarce (`inuit.md:155`), sinew and gut where textiles are absent, bark and basketry where pots are absent or too heavy; `fire-fuel-light.md:42` — the fuel map is the ecology map, and toolkit is its mobile edge.
- **Risk-shaped variability** — settlement strategy answers the "security problems presented by the environments" foragers live in (Binford, S040 abstract); the narrower claim that toolkit diversity tracks environmental risk is Collard et al.'s temperature finding `(to-verify)` — unopened primary, see Leads.

## 5. Seasonal round and mobility

**The two mobilities (Binford 1980, S040).** Residential *mapping-on* moves the whole camp to the resource; logistical *collecting* sends task groups out from a fixed base and brings the resource home. Pure foragers map on; pure collectors store. Most real systems mix — and the mix is a design dial, not a taxonomy (S044, Kelly: "reviews the anthropological literature for variation among living foragers in terms of diet, mobility, sharing, land tenure, technology, exchange").

| Driver | Effect on the round |
|---|---|
| Patchy, perishable resources (big game, berries) | residential mapping-on; small fluid camps |
| Clumped, storable resources (salmon runs, acorns, rice) | logistical collecting, storage sites, sedentism without farming |
| Predictable seasonality (tropics, regular runs) | loose rounds, broad diet |
| Harsh seasonality (boreal, arctic, desert) | tight rounds, storage critical, winter risk (`algonquian.md:143` the winter-camp row; `inuit.md` Arctic end-state) |
| Rich aquatic edge (coasts, rivers) | delayed-return complexity — the Northwest-Coast pattern: sedentary, ranked, *still foraging* (S044's variability review; the specific rich-coast cases `(to-verify)`) |

**Calibration of dependence.** The Ethnographic Atlas codes gathering dependence in ten bins (EA001, S042): of 1,290 coded societies, 722 sit at 0–5% gathering dependence, 9 above 65%, and none at 86–100%. Even "gatherers" mix — a culture that lives on gathered food alone is outside the coded record; give it a hunting, fishing or exchange share.

**Design rule.** Write the round as a four-row table (season × move × resource × stored surplus) before writing any culture description — `algonquian.md:140-143` is the corpus's model row-set; regional limits at `:147` apply to every row you generalize.

## 6. Sharing, storage and distribution

**Immediate- vs delayed-return (Woodburn 1982, S035).** Immediate-return systems consume within days and distribute on demand — equality is enforced by *not* storing. Delayed-return systems store, and storage invents the problems rank solves: guarding, allocating, inheriting. The opened record (S035) secures only the article's identity ("Egalitarian Societies", *Man* 17(3), 1982); the attribution of the two-category split to it and every detail of its wording are `(to-verify)` pending full text — restate the distinction as a design dial, don't quote Woodburn.

- **Demand-sharing** — design rule: meat is owed, not gifted; asking is the enforcement mechanism and refusal is the scandal (the ethnographic demand-sharing literature, e.g. Peterson, is unopened this round `(to-verify)`). If the hunter eats last, the culture is levelling; if he eats first, ask who guards the stores.
- **Kill-ownership vs consumption** — design rule: the owner of the arrow, the tracker, the dog's owner and the distributor can be four different people; distribution rules are where "egalitarian" gets concrete (specific ethnographic arrow-ownership cases `(to-verify)`).
- **Meat as currency** — meat-for-crops exchange with farming neighbours is a design possibility `(to-verify)`; Kelly's publisher abstract (S044) covers variation in exchange among foragers, but does not specify this exchange partner or medium. The specific Mbuti–farmer exchange case `(to-verify)` — Hart & Hart, unopened; the forager edge of that trade is this entry's, the market register stays at `trade-economy-lexicon.md:3`.
- **Storage as the hinge of rank** — a storable surplus is the single design decision that most determines whether the culture can have chiefs; connect the chain's preserve-link (§3) directly to the rank system.

## 7. Gendered division of labour — evidence and contestation

State this with the live debate, not a slogan on either side:

- **The old paradigm** — males hunt, females gather (the *Man the Hunter* framing, Lee & DeVore 1968, cited in S031 ref. 1). As a universal, this is the entry's baseline calibration target.
- **The corrective** — Anderson et al. 2023 (S031): of 63 foraging societies with explicit hunting documentation, 50 (79%) show women hunting; among the 41 cases scored for intent, the paper reports 36 (87%; 87.8% by exact division) intentional; where hunting is the main subsistence activity, women's participation is universal in the sample. Haas et al. 2020 (S041): a 9,000-year-old female burial with a hunting toolkit at Wilamaya Patjxa, plus "10 other females in statistical parity with early male hunter burials" in the Late-Pleistocene/Early-Holocene Americas sample.
- **The contestation** — Venkataraman et al. 2024 (S032) answers in its title: "Female foragers sometimes hunt, yet gendered divisions of labor are real" (body `(to-verify)`). Anderson et al.'s 2024 correction (S033) records that "concerns were raised about the reliability and replicability of the results", adds page-level locators, and defends its broad "forager" definition and society selection while granting "some of the concerns raised reflect ongoing, open questions in the field". Current safe statement: *women hunt in many foraging societies; a rigid universal male-hunt/female-gather rule is falsified; how far the average division leans is contested* — write the culture's actual rule, then check it against §13.
- **Design consequence** — decide hunt composition deliberately: who hunts what size game, who traps, who gathers, and what the childcare/dogs/partner system looks like (S031's Agta and Aka cases show toolkit and partner variation, not absence).

## 8. Taboo, ritual and the ownership boundary

The kill has a speech-life and a spirit-life; both are owned elsewhere — this entry hands them a real ecology.

- **The forest register** — dangerous game breeds circumlocution (`politeness-taboo-euphemism.md:75,:101`; `sociolinguistics-registers.md:91`; `taboo-strata-lifecycle.md:72`). `animal-symbolism.md:114`: "a forest register in which direct names are lethal and apology-speech to the kill is grammatical" — the ritual owner's licence to *require* a hunt register, not to write its lexicon here.
- **Prey apologetics** — "An animal taboo implies a rite; write both or neither" (`symbolism-synthesis-method.md:69`); the circumpolar bear ceremonial is the model case (`animal-symbolism.md:53`, Hallowell 1926).
- **Territory rules** — sacred groves can ban the hunt itself (`plant-symbolism.md:65`, Osun-Osogbo); access and ownership of patches, runs and fishing stations are part of the production system (who may take what where, §1 choice 2) — the *sacred* part stays in myth-symbol.
- **The relational hunt** — `inuit.md:109` documents hunting framed "through relations and appropriate conduct, not only technical mastery"; a purely mechanical hunt is a calibration failure on the ritual side.

## 12. NON-CANON worked micro-example

**NON-CANON.** The culture, river, species and numbers below are invented to demonstrate the method; nothing here is a claim about a real people.

The **Velk** occupy a cold forest river valley; the river runs a silver fish ("drent") up each thaw. Resource grid: drent runs (thaw), berries + small game (growing season), mast-nut groves (autumn), winter deer yards. Round: riverside summer camp → autumn grove camps → winter village of pit-houses (collecting, not mapping-on). Chain: weirs at named rapids (patch ownership by descent group — §1 choice 2) → smoke-drying at camp racks (the fire is `fire-fuel-light.md`'s) → winter pits. Labour: both sexes weir and gather; the winter deer drive is men's by rule, small-game snares are anyone's — a deliberate §7 choice inside the contested range. Distribution: first fish to the weir-keeper's hearth, then ranked shares by who worked the trap; demand-sharing is suspended while winter stores are being filled in autumn — once winter arrives, refusing a request to open them is the local scandal, an immediate-return ethic layered on a delayed-return base. Taboo boundary: the drent's true name is spoken only at the weir (forest register handed to `taboo-strata-lifecycle.md`); the grove's nut-trees are sacred-owned (handed to `plant-symbolism.md:65` pattern). Stress test — a bad run year: stored nuts vs shared fish exposes whether the share rules bend (they do: descent-group stores open to the valley, creating the first debt ledger → the culture's rank hinge, §6 last bullet).

**Living-practice flag** (per registry practice): hunting/foraging is a live subsistence and identity system for real peoples today — `algonquian.md:145` warns against repackaging a living round as untouched past, and `inuit.md:136-137` marks the ritual register examples as source-specific. Write fictional systems *like* these mechanics, never *as* these peoples.

**Precision traps.** "Hunter-gatherer" is an etic umbrella covering immediate-return bands, ranked sedentary fishing peoples, and part-time foragers at agrarian edges (S033's correction: even "forager" is contested as an inclusion criterion); "subsistence" is not "poverty" (S045's frame); "primitive" is a calibration defect wherever it appears.
