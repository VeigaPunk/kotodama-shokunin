## 1. Manufacture and materials

Perfumery's raw materials and processes: extraction chemistry, yield economics, and where the workshop sat.
- The "most famous of all scents was kyphi," recorded in "three versions on temple walls" at Edfu and Philae and in the Ebers papyrus. (S779)
- "large quantities of rose-water were regularly distilled" in Persia and Iraq from the ninth century, before any Avicenna story; true attar reaches Jahangir's court in 1611-12. (S782)
- The first named alcohol-based perfume is "believed to have been produced around 1370 and derived its base from this herb" - Hungary water on a rosemary base, once serpentine coolers gave Europe high-proof alcohol. (S783)
- Copal is "offered to gods" in Mesoamerican agricultural ritual, "its aromatic smoke" serving as "divine food" that "allowed the link between deities and humans." (S785)
- "the alabastron was a shape introduced into Corinthian pottery from the east" and used as "a flask for perfume or perfumed oils," later displaced by the aryballos. (S798)

## 2. Trade routes and markets

Where aromatics moved and who sold them.
- "The Incense Route ran along the western edge of Arabia's central desert," moving frankincense and myrrh by caravan from South Arabia northward. (S780)
- In later antiquity "Phoenicia eclipsed Egypt in the manufacture" of unguents while Egypt kept the repute of the best cyprus - Pliny's map of the perfume market. (S797)
- Umayyad planners "designed" markets "to function as a set of defined zones," and streets took the name of the trade plied there - attarin rows are attested shop-streets. (S796)
- In early Qing Beijing, "incense clocks burned all night in Beijing's tall drum tower, measuring out the time" for the night watch - scent as municipal time signal. (S795)
- "elite scent culture had already begun to be transformed by the importation of foreign aromatics" before Buddhism arrived - earlier practice stayed local and religious. (S786)

## 3. Containers and wearable scent

The objects that carried smell on the body or through the city.
- In Greek practice, "olive oil served as the base for perfumes" at every level; vessel names - alabastron, aryballos, lekythos - mark the carrying hardware. (S781)
- Glove recipes survive "for scenting gloves" from the 17th century "using ingredients such as musk, ambergris, floral extracts and aromatic spices." (S790)
- A pomander hung at neck or waist carried "strong-scented substances" believed to "disinfect the air and to ward off plague and other diseases." (S791)
- Parisian glovers seized the perfumer's metier: "les gantiers-parfumeurs fabriquent les gants parfumes" whose vogue peaked "a la cour de Louis XIV." (S794)

## 4. Social codes and status

Smell as classification: of goods, of people, of piety.
- Hindu vrata puja offers "sandalwood paste, incense, pastille, camphor, lamps of burning oil or ghi, and fragranced flowers to the deity" - fragrance staged as a system. (S787)
- "More than 3000 kg of rose flowers produce 1 kg of rose oil" - Kazanlak's industrial water distillation prices scent in tonnes of petals. (S789)
- Men "lose their strong-smelling odor" through initiation while "women, on the other hand, by their very sexuality are strong smelling" - odor as a sex/life-stage code. (S788)
- "For hundreds of years, perfumers have categorised the quality of ambergris according to its colour" - whale product graded like gems, now replaced by synthetics. (S793)

## 5. Heritage, games, and reconstruction

Scent as inscribed heritage, played game, and misread equipment.
- UNESCO "inscribed in 2018" the "Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity" element covering Grasse plant growing, processing and perfume composition as living craft. (S784)
- In these scent games, "over several rounds, a host selects varieties of incense for their guests to savor and identify." (S799)
- In Aztec and Maya courts fragrant flowers staged paradise, "como lo registraron observadores espanoles" for "los palacios aztecas" - scent as royal scenography. (S792)
- Herculaneum workshop III.10 held "an oil press for the production of perfume and not a textile press" - the equipment itself was misread. (S800)
