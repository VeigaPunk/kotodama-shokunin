## 1. Frame: what an eschatology is, and the four scopes

An eschatology is a culture's account of **how things end**. It is not the same object as an age-doctrine, and the partition currently has the age-doctrine and not the ending. Three standing distinctions, because conflating them is the commonest defect in generated material:

- **Age-doctrine vs eschatology.** An age-doctrine says *how many* worlds or ages there are and *how time moves* (`myth-symbol/cosmology-cosmography.md:77-90`). An eschatology says *what the last one does*: who ends it, on what verdict, with what survival clause. A culture can hold four ages and no ending doctrine at all (a count with no terminator), or a single age and a scheduled ending. The two are independent axes and are chosen independently.
- **Eschatology vs apocalypse vs millenarianism.** Eschatology is doctrine (what will happen). Apocalypse is a *genre* (a revealed text that discloses it — Daniel, Revelation, the *Zand ī Wahman Yasn*, the k'atun prophecies). Millenarianism is a *social movement* organized around the expectation. The partition's genre file (`myth-symbol/myth-folktale-genres.md`) sorts the record; this entry supplies the doctrine the record carries.
- **The four scopes.** "Ending" names four different objects, and the design work is to keep them apart:

| scope | what ends | attested shape | in-lane anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **person** | one life | judgment, weighing, crossing, rebirth, ancestorhood | §8; the *rite* is `myth-symbol/ritual-liminality.md`, the *direction* is `myth-symbol/direction-symbolism.md` |
| **age** | a world-cycle or dynasty-term | cyclic dissolution and renewal | `myth-symbol/cosmology-cosmography.md:77-90`; `culture-material/era-periodization-models.md:88-90` |
| **dispensation** | the teaching, law, or covenant — not the world | dharma-decline, the broken covenant, the withdrawn mandate | `corpus/knowledge/civilizations/japan-medieval-sengoku.md:88` (mappō); `corpus/knowledge/civilizations/china-shang-zhou.md:82-83` (tiānmìng) |
| **world** | everything, once or repeatedly | terminal judgment, universal conflagration, flood | §3 table; motif block A1000–A1099 per `corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/flood-traditions.md:17` |

**Craft maxim: an ending is a verdict with a schedule.** The verdict is what the ending says about the present (who is right, who is doomed, what must change); the schedule is when. A generated eschatology that names only a date and no verdict is a calendar entry, not a doctrine — and a verdict with no institution (§7) never reaches the culture at all.

## 2. The ending grammar — six axes

Every attested end-complex fixes these six, whether or not its tellers ever state them. They are the design levers, and each choice forecloses others.

| axis | question | attested values | design consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **agent** | who ends it? | a deity (Yahweh, Ohrmazd's victory, Śiva's dance); the dead themselves (Norse, the bound Loki unbound); a mechanism (entropy, the yuga arithmetic, the rollover of a count); no agent (accident, flood, fire) | an agent implies a *policy* and therefore a grievance and an appeal procedure; a mechanism implies no appeal, only reckoning |
| **verdict** | why does it end? | judgment (Abrahamic, Zoroastrian renovation); exhaustion/decay (yugas, ages of iron); crime (flood traditions); completion (a count fulfilled, a term closed); none (pure catastrophe) | the verdict is the culture's moral statement; it must be readable from the creation type (§9) |
| **scope** | what ends? | person / age / dispensation / world (§1) | mixing scopes silently is the commonest defect; mappō is not an apocalypse |
| **survival** | what survives? | nothing; a remnant (Noah, Líf and Lífþrasir, the Zoroastrian preserved seed, the Daoist seed people); everything, transformed (renovation, new heaven and new earth); the cycle itself | **the load-bearing axis** — it decides whether the culture's ethics is preservation, merit, or indifference (§5) |
| **finality** | is the end terminal? | terminal (linear judgment, once); cyclic (return to a like world); renovation (return to a *better* world); spiral (return to a worse one) | finality decides whether the present is *decisive* (terminal) or *rehearsed* (cyclic) — the single biggest generator of institutions |
| **schedule** | when? | dated (epoch arithmetic, a named year, a calendar rollover); undated-but-certain (a thief in the night); sealed (the day unknown and uncalculable); past (an age already ended, a Five Suns reading) | a dated end needs an epoch-owner and an arithmetic; a sealed end needs a *prohibition* to enforce (§6) |

**Two rules the axes produce.** (1) *Finality and schedule must agree*: a terminal ending that is also cyclic is a contradiction unless the cycle is declared to be the terminal mechanism (a count that rolls until it does not). (2) *Survival must be legible*: if the culture cannot say why the remnant was selected, the survival clause is decoration and the ethics it implies will contradict the rest of the bible.

**What the table shows.** The same six axes generate wildly different institutions from identical-looking material: two cyclic systems (Norse, Purāṇic) differ on whether the next age is *better* (renovation) or merely *like* (cycle), and that difference decides whether the culture's ethics is one of preservation or of patient repetition. Two terminal systems (Abrahamic, Zoroastrian) differ on survival — a remnant judged, versus everyone raised — and that difference decides whether the culture's funerary apparatus is about *sorting* or about *holding*.

## 4. Cyclic vs linear — the real variable

The familiar pairing ("Eastern cycles, Western lines") is too coarse to design with, and it hides the axis that actually does the work.

- The operational variable is **whether the present is decisive**. In a terminal system the present is the only run and every act is load-bearing; in a cyclic system the present is one turn among many and the load is on *procedure* (doing the age right) rather than on outcome. A culture can hold a cyclic cosmology and a decisive ethic at once — the Aztec present sun is fated yet every sacrifice matters because it *delays* the ending — and that combination is a generator, not a contradiction.
- **Renovation is the third option and the most institutionally fertile.** A renovation (Zoroastrian frašgird, Norse green earth) makes the ending *good news*: it licenses reform, not resignation. A pure cycle licenses repetition; a terminal judgment licenses preparation. Renovation licenses **repair**, which is why it produces saviours, renovators, and reform movements rather than penitents alone.
- **Direction of the cycle matters and is a design choice.** Purāṇic cycles ascend after the trough (a new golden age), Norse renewal is a fresh green earth after the fire, but a *descending* spiral that never returns is a fourth type the schemes often imply and rarely state. State it, or the in-world historians will disagree about it for free (which is itself usable — `corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/cosmology-cosmography.md:90`).
- **The count can be end-shaped without being cyclic.** The Maya Long Count is a pure count that rolls over: no verdict, no survival clause, no doctrine — which is exactly why the 2012 rollover was a Western *insertion* of an apocalypse into a system that had none (§10). A count is a calendar; an eschatology is a doctrine. If a generated culture has the count and the culture wants an ending, the ending must be supplied from somewhere else, and the entry must say where.

## 5. The remnant — the load-bearing mechanism

Every survival clause needs a selection rule, and the rule is where the culture states its ethics in operational form. Attested selection mechanisms:

| mechanism | who survives | what it implies in-world | exemplars |
|---|---|---|---|
| **merit/verdict** | the judged righteous | an adjudicable ethics; a ledger exists | Abrahamic judgment; Egyptian weighing (the *rite* is `myth-symbol/ritual-liminality.md`) |
| **preservation by office** | the one who kept the rule, or was warned | institutions can save; the warning channel is real | Noah; Utnapishtim; Manu and the fish |
| **seed-keeping** | the worthy are stored away until the next age | the remnant is *managed*, not merely spared — a custodian institution exists | Zoroastrian preserved seed and the saoshyant born of it; the Daoist zhǒngmín 種民 (to-verify passage) |
| **accident/position** | whoever happened to be in the right place | no ethics at all — endings are weather | flood survivors in many local traditions; the Norse pair in the wood |
| **transformation** | the survivors become something else | the next world has a different people, not a continued one | Mesoamerican prior-world survivors turned to animals or fish; Lot's wife turned to salt as the *anti*-remnant |
| **none** | nothing | pure finality; the culture's ethic is entirely about the present, not about continuity | terminal-annihilation readings; the heat-death style ending |

**Two design rules.** (1) *The remnant must be countable.* A tradition that says "the good survive" without a selection procedure has a slogan; a tradition with a procedure (the roll, the warning, the seed, the boat) has an institution. (2) *Name the anti-remnant.* Every strong survival clause has a foil — the drowned generation, the frozen sinners, the turned-to-stone wife, the people of the prior sun. The foil is what makes the clause legible, and it is the cheapest single addition to a thin end-doctrine.

## 6. The dating problem — schedule, epoch, seal, and recovery

Schedules are the axis with the most immediate institutional consequences, because a dated ending is a political instrument and an undated one is a discipline problem.

- **Dated endings require an epoch-owner.** Who may compute the end is who may compute the calendar, and in this lane that is already an established power (`corpus/knowledge/culture-material/era-periodization-models.md:88` — the priesthood owns the epoch; `corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/cosmology-cosmography.md:90` — the arithmetic is a power instrument). A dated end-doctrine without a named calculator is a doctrine with a missing institution.
- **Undated-but-certain endings need a seal.** The Abrahamic solution is a *prohibition*: the day is unknown and uncalculable (Mark 13:32), which converts the expectation into permanent readiness and delegitimizes every calculator. The seal is an institution — it produces a policing function (against date-setters) and a devotional posture (readiness), and it is the single most transferable mechanism in this entry.
- **The failure mode is a missed date, and the recovery must be pre-written.** The disconfirmation literature (Festinger, Riecken & Schachter 1956) documents the classic sequence: a dated prophecy fails, and the group does not simply dissolve — it spiritualizes the date, re-dates, or redefines the remnant. The literature's strong reading (that dissonance *must* intensify belief) is **contested and refined** (§10); what transfers as craft is the *inventory of recovery moves*, not the law:

| recovery move | what it says after the date passes | in-world trace |
|---|---|---|
| **spiritualize** | the end happened *inwardly* | a new exegetical school; the date becomes an allegory |
| **re-date** | the arithmetic was right, the epoch was wrong | a revised calendar; a schism over the epoch |
| **narrow the remnant** | only the true remnant was taken, invisibly | a purity movement; a purge |
| **widen the scope** | the end is *underway*, not complete | a reform program with an open-ended horizon |
| **blame the calculators** | the date was never legitimate | the seal (§6) is reinforced; the calculators are punished |
| **dissolve** | the expectation ends | the movement's records become a genre (the apocalypse as literature) |

- **Past-ending cultures are a fourth schedule type and are under-used.** A culture that holds "the world already ended once and we are in the after" (a post-diluvian reading, a post-Ragnarök reading, a Five Suns reading) has no future schedule at all — its eschatology is *historical*, and its institutions are archaeological and priestly rather than expectant. That is a cheap and legible way to make a culture's end-doctrine distinct.

## 7. Present-tense institutions — the seven things an ending builds

An eschatology that builds nothing is decoration. The attested institution set, as a checklist:

| # | institution | what it does | exemplar |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **calendar hook** | dates the observance of the expectation | the New Fire bundle; the penitential season; the years-remaining almanac |
| 2 | **omen archive / watch** | reads the present for signs of the schedule | the omen bureau and its compendia (`myth-symbol/divination-omen-systems.md`); the eclipse as preview (`myth-symbol/celestial-symbolism-calendars.md:129`) |
| 3 | **the seal and its police** | forbids calculation, or licenses it | Mark 13:32 and the anti-date-setting function; conversely the epoch-priesthood |
| 4 | **the remnant custodian** | keeps the seed, the boat, the rule, the archive | Zoroastrian seed-keeping; the ark; the monastic preservation of the dharma |
| 5 | **the reform program** | converts the verdict into present-tense repair | renovation doctrines; covenant renewal; the purity movement |
| 6 | **the disposal apparatus for persons** | sorts the dead by the person-scope verdict (§8) | judgment, weighing, the bridge, purgatory and intercession |
| 7 | **the revelation genre** | carries the doctrine as a text with a telling-rule | apocalypse as genre; the k'atun prophecy; the sealed book |

**Rule: pick two or three, not seven.** A culture with all seven is a genre pastiche. The institution set should follow from the grammar (§2): terminal-and-sealed endings build 3 and 5; cyclic-and-dated endings build 1 and 2; renovation endings build 4 and 5; post-ended cultures build 2 and 7 and nothing expectant at all.

## 8. Individual eschatology — the verdict apparatus

The person-scope ending is a distinct design object, and the partition currently holds its *rite* (`myth-symbol/ritual-liminality.md`) and its *direction* (`myth-symbol/direction-symbolism.md`) but not its *verdict apparatus*. Four questions, in order:

1. **Is the person's fate adjudicated or automatic?** Adjudication (judgment, weighing, a trial) implies a court, evidence, advocates, and therefore an intercession industry. Automation (rebirth by the mechanism of karma; ancestorhood by funerary correctness) implies a procedural ethics — the rite is the verdict.
2. **Is the fate terminal or revisable?** Revisability (purgatory, intercession, transfer of merit, memorial rites) creates an institution with ongoing customers; terminality creates an institution with none, and shifts all effort to the living moment.
3. **Where is the person's fate kept?** The tomb, the ancestral tablet, the underworld register, the wheel — each is a location that can be visited, audited, or bribed, and each therefore generates a different story engine.
4. **What is the *anti*-fate?** The unquiet dead, the forgotten, the erased-name, the second death. The anti-fate is what makes the apparatus feel real, exactly as the anti-remnant does at world scope (§5).

**Coupling rule.** The person-scope verdict must be consistent with the world-scope verdict: a culture that sorts persons by deed but ends the world without a verdict (a pure mechanism) has a contradiction the auditors will find. State the relationship, or make the inconsistency itself a known in-world theological quarrel (which is realistic and free — `myth-symbol/comparative-frameworks.md` §1 etic/emic rule).

## 9. Derivation and coupling — what this entry does not restate

This entry takes that mapping as an input and supplies the six-axis grammar (§2) that turns the mapped ending into a system. The derivation is one-directional: a creation type constrains an ending's *shape*, not its schedule or its institution set.
- **The age schemes are stated elsewhere and are not restated here.** `corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/cosmology-cosmography.md:77-90` (Family D) and `corpus/knowledge/culture-material/era-periodization-models.md:88-90` carry the arithmetic. ## 10. Calibration — mainstream, contested, discredited, to-verify

**Mainstream (usable as background).** That the attested end-complexes differ on every axis of §2, and that the schemes above exist in the cited texts; that the Zoroastrian material contains a fixed-term cosmos with a final renovation and a resurrection of the dead; that the Norse corpus contains a destruction followed by a renewed earth; that Purāṇic chronology contains exact yuga arithmetic with a traditional epoch; that the Buddhist decline-of-dharma prophecy is a distinct doctrine of dispensation-end and not a world-end; that the Abrahamic end is terminal, undated and sealed in its own texts; that Mesoamerican sources carry serial prior-world destructions; that the tiānmìng doctrine is this-worldly and conditional.

**Contested (state the dispute, do not resolve it).**
- **Zoroastrian priority in apocalyptic.** That Iranian dualism supplied the template for Jewish and Christian eschatology is a live scholarly position and a live dispute (Cohn 1993 surveys the case; the counter-position is that the Jewish material is independently motivated). Do not generate "apocalyptic came from Persia" as fact.
- **The disconfirmation law.** Festinger et al. (1956) is the founding case study; the strong reading (failed prophecy necessarily intensifies belief through dissonance reduction) has been refined and disputed in the decades since. Use the *recovery-move inventory* (§6), not the law.
- **Norse cyclicality.** The in-lane caution stands: "neither endless repetition nor a uniform historical belief in cyclical time should be asserted without argument" (`corpus/knowledge/civilizations/germanic-norse.md:116`). The borrowing question (Christian influence on Völuspá's renewal) is likewise open.
- **Whether "apocalyptic" is a genre or a period's mood** — a definitional dispute in the field, and one that matters here only as a warning against using the word as a synonym for eschatology.

**Discredited or constructed (do not use as tradition).**
- **The 2012 "Maya apocalypse."** A modern construction: the Long Count rollover was a calendrical event and the sources attach no doom to it (`corpus/knowledge/civilizations/maya-classic.md:198`; Aveni 2009; the fabrication note at `corpus/knowledge/culture-material/era-periodization-models.md:90`). The k'atun prophecies in the Books of Chilam Balam are a real colonial-era compilation with a copy-date caveat (`corpus/knowledge/civilizations/mesoamerica-primary-sources.md:76`) and are not evidence for a Maya world-end doctrine.
- **Universal-deluge and its diffusionist readings** — already calibrated in `corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/flood-traditions.md`; not reopened here.
- **Eliade's eternal-return as ethnography** — design language only, per `corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/cosmology-cosmography.md:142`; a ritual does not re-enact the cosmos because a theory says repetition is archaic.
- **"Millenarian movements are primitive."** Millenarianism is a recurring modern-era form too, and the condescending frame (cargo-cult style) is not a usable premise; the movements are evidence about expectation under stress, at any era type.

**Open `(to-verify)` items in this entry** (each marked inline where used): the Zand ī Wahman Yasn bibliographic details; the Rabbinic birth-pangs citations; the Lingbao zhǒngmín passage; the A-chapter catastrophe sub-IDs beyond the four the lane already carries. Each is a citation-identity question, not a claim that the material does not exist.

## 11. Application protocol — building a culture's ending

1. **Choose the scope first** (§1): person, age, dispensation, world. Write it down; do not let one stand in for another later.
2. **Derive the shape from the creation type** (`corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/creation-myths-taxonomy.md:110`) and record the derivation in the handoff sheet (its `:134` metadata slot has the field: "eschatology constraint").
3. **Fix the six axes** (§2) in order — agent, verdict, scope, survival, finality, schedule. Agent before verdict (a mechanism cannot have a grievance); survival before finality (the remnant clause constrains what "final" can mean).
4. **Write the survival clause with a countable selection rule and a named anti-remnant** (§5).
5. **Set the schedule and name its institution** (§6): dated → name the epoch-owner and the arithmetic; undated → write the seal and the prohibition; past → write what the culture does with the aftermath.
6. **Pre-write one recovery move** (§6) for the missed date, or state that the culture's schedule is sealed and the recovery move is doctrinal rather than calendrical.
7. **Pick two or three institutions** (§7) and give each one a visible trace in the calendar, the archive, the law, or the dress.
8. **Build the person-scope verdict apparatus** (§8) and check it against the world-scope verdict.
9. **Derive the lexicon** (names of the last day, the epoch, the remnant, the verdict) and the revelation genre's telling-rule.
10. **Run the consistency gate** (`corpus/knowledge/verification/myth-consistency-gate.md`) and hand off the axis sheet, the derivation, the institution set, and every to-verify item.

**Failure modes, ranked by frequency.** (a) A date with no verdict. (b) A cyclic cosmology with a decisive-ethic institution that contradicts it (or the reverse) left unstated. (c) A survival clause with no selection rule. (d) A dispensation-end written as a world-end (mappō as apocalypse). (e) Seven institutions at once. (f) A person-scope apparatus inconsistent with the world-scope verdict. (g) A modern apocalypse (2012-shaped) projected onto a tradition that had a count. (h) A to-verify item laundered into assertion.

## 13. Canon boundary and handoff

- Nothing here decides what is true in any world. Which ending a fictional culture holds, which verdict it announces, who is in the remnant, and when the end comes are **systems this lane generates**; whether they are canon is lore-shokunin's decision (`PROVINCE.md`; `AGENTS.md`).
- **Handoff shape.** Ship the end-system as: the **scope declaration**, the **six-axis sheet**, the **creation-type derivation**, the **survival clause with its selection rule and anti-remnant**, the **schedule with its institution (epoch-owner or seal)**, the **two-or-three institution set with traces**, the **person-scope verdict apparatus**, and the **to-verify list**. Never ship a date without a verdict, a remnant without a rule, or a derived ending without the derivation.
- **Contested scope → report, never absorb.** If a receiving lane expects an eschatology to license a canon ending — which is not this lane's to grant — or expects a revelation text to certify a world's future, that is reported to the integrator, never absorbed: an end-doctrine is a design object and the gate's PASS is not canon (`corpus/knowledge/verification/myth-consistency-gate.md`). The decision is not this lane's to make unilaterally.
