## 1. What the direction register is — frame, slots, valence

- The direction register is the culture's mapping from a **small closed set of orientations** — four, five, six, eight, or ten slots depending on the partition (§4) — onto value-domains (sacred, auspicious, impure, ancestral, hostile, dead), **plus the two motion facts that colors and animals do not have**: which way you *turn* around a sacred object (§6) and which way the *dead and the prayers face* (§7–§8).
- A direction symbol is specified by three objects, and a handoff that gives fewer is incomplete:
  1. **Frame** — how the culture locates a direction at all (cardinal abstraction, landmark, river, body; §2). This is the register's step zero: the same color assignment is ungrammatical in a landmark frame.
  2. **Slots** — the partition and its real enumeration source (directions + center = 5; directions + above/below = 6; §4). The partition must recur in the culture's counting life (mirrors symbolism-synthesis-method.md §3 step 2).
  3. **Valence** — the primary domain per slot, and the axis the valence runs on (pure/impure, auspicious/inauspicious, living/dead, inside/outside).
- Standing rule (inherited from symbolism-synthesis-method.md §1 and comparative-frameworks.md §1): the grammar is **emic** for the fictional culture; this procedure is **etic** designer tooling, never ascribed. In-world, a direction code is expressed as ritual regulation, omen lore, house rules, and processional custom — never as a compass rose with a legend.
- The register's own consistency rules are the parent method's R1–R8 (one primary domain; closure over the partition; licensed transformations; grounding quota; no unmarked synonymy; named inversion axes; productivity test; emic/etic tags). This file adds two register-specific rules:
  - **D1 — Frame before color.** Declare the frame of reference before assigning any color, animal, or number to a direction (the §4.5 rule, promoted here to a numbered register rule).
  - **D2 — Valence is assigned, not intrinsic.** No direction is auspicious, pure, or dead-bearing by nature; every valence needs an institution that enforces it (a rite, a law, a taboo, a calendrical rule). A valence with no institution is decoration.

## 2. Step zero: the frame of reference

- Levinson's three-way typology (Levinson 2003, carried via the sibling method file) is the designer's test. Ask the culture's speakers to describe where something is without pointing:
  - **Intrinsic** — "at the front/back/left/right of" an object with its own orientation (a person, a house, a boat).
  - **Relative** — "left/right of" from the speaker's viewpoint, projected onto the scene.
  - **Absolute** — fixed bearings regardless of anyone's facing: the cardinal type. Guugu Yimithirr (Cape York, Australia) runs everyday small-scale space on absolute cardinals — a speaker must maintain a running compass fix to say "move your left foot," and gestures are anchored to the world, not the body (Haviland 1993, 1998; Levinson 1997). For such a culture, "north" is not a compass abstraction borrowed from navigation; it is a grammatical primitive.
- **Landmark frames are not deficient cardinals — they are a different system.** Real exemplars:
  - **Bali**: kaja (mountainward) / kelod (seaward) / kangin (sunrise-ward) / kauh (sunset-ward) (Wassmann & Dasen 1998; Atmadja 2018). Because kaja tracks the mountain, compass north in one village is not compass north in the next — Putra et al. (2020) document kaja re-orienting village by village while kangin/kauh stay sun-fixed. A worldbuilder who writes "the temple faces north" for a kaja-frame culture has written a contradiction.
  - **Tzeltal** (Maya, Chiapas): uphill/downhill as the load-bearing axis — carried in the sibling method file's §4.5.
  - **Hawaiʻi**: mauka/makai (mountainward/seaward) — carried in the same place.
  - **Egypt**: the canonical orientation was south-facing (the Nile's upstream "Upper Egypt" lies south; the east is the left — jbt "east" = "left" — carried via cosmology-cosmography.md §9). A culture that orients on a river gets direction words that double as upstream/downstream and as life-direction.
- Decision table (frame → consequences):

| Frame chosen | What must be true of the world | What breaks if ignored |
|---|---|---|
| Absolute (cardinal) | open terrain, visible sky, or a teaching institution that drills orientation | indoor scenes, windowless rooms, caves — the language has no way to say "over there" |
| Landmark (mountain/sea/river) | a dominant, stable landmark or drainage | after migration or conquest, every "direction" word silently re-points; the old words fossilize as history |
| Relative (body) | speakers co-present or narrating from a viewpoint | written directions, letters, maps, instructions to absent readers |
| Mixed (everyday landmark + ritual cardinal) | a professional class (priests, navigators) keeping the abstract frame | the two frames drift apart — a plot engine, and a documented real pattern |

- **The compass trap**: an abstract north requires deciding *which* north — true or magnetic — and their difference (declination) is latitude- and era-dependent. For a navigator culture this is not pedantry: it is the difference between a calendar alignment and a working compass, and a worldbuilder can make the distinction a discovery event (the moment the priests' north and the pilots' north stop agreeing). Keep the distinction visible in handoffs.

## 3. Coining the direction words — the etymology bank

- Real direction terms come from four sources, and the source chosen pre-commits the culture's symbolics (Brown 1983; the lexeme examples below are carried via cosmology-cosmography.md §9 unless noted):

| Source | Real exemplars | What it commits the culture to |
|---|---|---|
| Sunrise/sunset | Latin oriens/occidens "rising/setting"; Arabic sharq/gharb "rise/set" | a sun-watching, horizon-literate orientation; east = birth/beginning, west = ending (then possibly death, §7) |
| Winds | Greek/Mediterranean wind-names behind compass points; Brown (1983) on wind-derived terms | a seasonal-wind world; direction words carry weather and danger |
| River flow | Egyptian upstream/downstream ("Upper/Lower Egypt" = south/north along the Nile); Egyptian jbt "east" = "left" facing south | a linear world; "up" and "down" are moral as well as geographic |
| The body | Chinese běi 北 "north" ← the "back" (two figures back-to-back; 背 "back" derives from it); Scottish Gaelic deiseal "sunwise" ← the right-hand/sun side | the body axis (§5) leaks into the cardinal frame; left/right and cardinality co-define each other |

- Design levers:
  - Coin direction words from the culture's **actual orientation technology** (sun, wind, river, tide, mountain) — then let the ritual register keep an archaic layer from a superseded frame (old river words surviving in liturgy after the migration). Two frames in one lexicon is a history the bible can date.
  - Derive the capital's name from the frame's center claim — "navel" capitals (Delphic omphalos; Quechua qusqu/Cusco — carried via cosmology-cosmography.md §9) — and hand the derivation table to `corpus/knowledge/linguistics/naming-systems.md`.
  - If the conlang has a spatial-deixis system (demonstratives, "hither/thither," verb directionals), decide whether direction marking is **obligatory** (Guugu Yimithirr-style absolute grammar) or **optional** — this single choice reshapes every sentence the culture says about a journey (couples `corpus/knowledge/linguistics/semantics-pragmatics.md`).
  - Keep the taboo layer visible: a direction word that cannot be said aloud at night, a euphemism for the direction of the dead (§7), a substitute for a hostile neighbor's cardinal (couples `corpus/knowledge/linguistics/politeness-taboo-euphemism.md`).

## 4. The closed partition — the 4+1 quincunx and its rivals

- The commonest closed partition is **four directions plus center** (the quincunx), because it is checkable: one matrix must cohere across color, animal, number, season, and institution at once (symbolism-synthesis-method.md §4.5). Real systems to imitate structurally:

| System | Slots | Direction code | Source carrier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chinese wuxing | 5 (E/S/W/N + center) | east qing azure + dragon; south chi vermilion + bird; west bai white + tiger; north hei black + tortoise-snake; center huang yellow | Needham 1956; Zhouli "Kaogong ji" — via symbolism-synthesis-method.md §1 and cosmology-cosmography.md §9 |
| Yucatec Maya quincunx | 5 | red = east; white = north; black = west; yellow = south; yax (green-blue) = center | Sharer & Traxler 2006 — via the two sibling files |
| Mexica cosmogram | 5 | four world-trees at the corners around a center; direction almanac on Codex Fejérváry-Mayer p. 1 | Boone 2000; Codex Fejérváry-Mayer — via the siblings |
| Zuni | 6 (4 + above + below) | north yellow; west blue; south red; east white; above all-coloured; below black | Cushing 1883, "Zuni Fetiches" (fetish text retrieved 2026-10-17) |
| Navajo | 4 | four directions with colors per Witherspoon 1977 — carried via cosmology-cosmography.md (exact pairings to-verify) | sibling carrier |
| Chinese bagua + center | 9 | eight trigram directions + center; the "Yugong" nine provinces as administrative echo | Needham 1959; Shujing "Yugong" — via the siblings |
| Indic ten directions | 10 | four cardinal + four intermediate + zenith + nadir (dig/pal) (to-verify: standard reference not re-retrieved this session) | this entry |

- **Design rules for the partition:**
  - **Pick n from a real enumeration**, not from symmetry: directions + center (5), directions + above/below (6), trigrams (8), provinces or gates (the culture's own administrative count). The partition must recur in the culture's counting life (parent method §3 step 2).
  - **Give the center a job.** A quincunx center is where sovereignty, the capital, the world-axis, or the "unmarked" vantage sits — but the axis-mundi apparatus itself lives in `corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/cosmology-cosmography.md`; here the center is one *slot* in the register, with a color, an occupant, and an exception list (what may not be placed at the center).
  - **Mark UNALIGNED cells with reasons** (parent method §3 step 4). Real systems lump: seven planets do not fit five slots; a sixth animal has no direction. Lumps are where in-world controversy lives — leave them.
  - **Anti-porting rule, register-wide** (from §4.5): direction-color and direction-animal pairs are conventional. Maya east = red and Chinese east = qing (green-blue); Zuni north = yellow and Lakota sets vary by nation. Two fictional cultures never share a direction-color pair without a documented diffusion link (couples `corpus/knowledge/linguistics/language-contact.md`).
  - **Source-variance discipline**: Cushing's Zuni set is one witness, not "the Zuni system"; when two ethnographers disagree, keep both rows and date them (this is the same discipline cosmology-cosmography.md §10 applies to the 13/9 stacks).

## 5. Left and right — the body axis

- **The classic study**: Hertz (1909) argued that the right/left polarity (right = sacred, strong, clean; left = profane, weak, unclean) is a social fact of "primitive classification." Its status today: the *question* is foundational and the *answers* are local — the evolutionary framing (a "primitive mentality" stage) is retired, and the polarity is not universal (Needham's introduction to Right & Left 1973; the same critique trajectory as totemism in animal-symbolism.md §1).
- **Documented counterexamples — the designer's license to invert:**
  - **Meru (Kenya)**: in the Mugwe ritual complex, the *left* hand is the ritually strong one — Needham (1960) titled the paper for exactly this inversion (Africa 30(1): 20–33). One counterexample is enough to forbid "right = good" as a default.
  - **China**: the honored side alternates by era and context — chariot, court, ritual each privilege differently, and the precedence flips across dynasties (Granet, "Right and Left in China", trans. in Needham 1973, pp. 43–58). A court that switched sides is a datable event in-world.
  - **Roman augury**: the valence flips with the observer's facing. Roman practice read signs on the left as favorable, where Greeks read the right as favorable — "Ita nobis sinistra videntur, Graiis et barbaris dextra meliora" (Cicero, De Divinatione 2.82). Same bird, opposite omen, because the augur stood the other way. This is the register's cleanest proof that valence is vantage-dependent, not natural.
- **Design rules:**
  - **Never inherit "right = good."** Decide the culture's polarity, then **give it institutions**: which hand takes oaths, eats, gives and receives, salutes; which side of the road the honored guest walks; which side of the body is the "closed" side of a robe (couples `corpus/knowledge/culture-material/adornment-body-codes.md`).
  - **Record the exceptions** — a left-hand cult, a left-handed warrior corps, a mourning reversal — and name the axis each inversion runs on (parent method R6). An unstated inversion reads as a contradiction.
  - **Let the augural flip exist in-world**: a fictional diviner class whose omens depend on their facing is a legal-institutional fact (who stands where), not a curiosity — it hands the bible a dispute (rival colleges, rival vantages).

## 6. Rotation — the register's motion slot

- Direction is not only *where* but *which way you turn*. Real rotation codes:

| Practice | Direction | Notes / source |
|---|---|---|
| Scottish Gaelic sunwise (deiseal) | clockwise | the "prosperous course"; the reverse (widdershins / tuathal) for cursing and anti-rites — web-retrieved accounts 2026-10-17; Carmichael 1900 passage-level to-verify |
| Hindu pradakṣiṇa | clockwise | the deity is kept on the walker's right; pra + dakṣiṇa "right (also south)"; the inauspicious reverse circuit exists as its named opposite — web-retrieved 2026-10-17 (scholarly edition not re-retrieved) |
| Tibetan kora | clockwise for Buddhists; counterclockwise for Bönpo | same sacred mountain, two directions, two communities — Religions 17(5): 552 (2026), web-retrieved 2026-10-17 |
| Islamic tawaf | counterclockwise | seven circuits around the Ka'ba — pilgrimage guides web-retrieved 2026-10-17 (scholarly source not retrieved) |

- **Design rule — rotation is a faction flag.** Two communities circumambulating the same shrine in opposite directions is one of the most legible schism markers a world can show, and it is cheap: a direction, a counting formula (seven circuits; three rounds; 108 steps), a starting point (the black stone, the east gate), and a sanction for the wrong way. Give the rotation a name in the ritual register; the name is the institution (couples `corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/ritual-liminality.md`).
- **Two traps:** (1) "Sunwise" is defined by the sun's apparent motion — in a southern-hemisphere, tidally-locked, or binary-star world the metaphor's physics change, and the word must be re-coined from the actual sky (couples `corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/celestial-symbolism-calendars.md`). (2) "Clockwise" presupposes clocks — never let an iron-age culture's ritual be described as clockwise in-world; that is an etic note.

## 7. The direction of the dead

- The strongest single direction-valence in the real archive is **death's bearing**:
  - **Egypt**: the realm of the dead lies in the west (jmntt); the dead are "Westerners," and Osiris is "Foremost of Westerners" (Khenti-Amentiu) — web-retrieved Egyptological sources 2026-10-17 (Wb page-level to-verify).
  - **Greece**: the house of Hades lies beyond the river Oceanus at the earth's edge — Circe's directions send Odysseus across Oceanus to the groves of Persephone and the sunless Cimmerian shore (Odyssey 10.508–515; 11.1–22).
  - **Ireland**: the otherworld and the land of the dead lie across the sea to the west (Tír na nÓg); Tech Duinn, the house of Donn, is where the dead gather (web-retrieved sources incl. Oxford Reference s.v. "Tech Duinn", 2026-10-17; page-level primary pending).
- **Do not universalize "west = death."** The pattern is common because sunset and the sea make it cheap, not because it is structural; cultures with river frames, mountain frames, or subterranean frames put the dead elsewhere, and some place them *above* (sky-world ancestors). Treat "west = dead" as one attractor among several, and choose the frame's cheapest bearing for the culture at hand — then check it against the frame from §2 (a landmark culture's dead live "seaward," not "west").
- **Design rules:**
  - The direction of the dead is the register's most **checkable** claim: grave orientation, procession routes, and door positions in tombs survive archaeologically. Decide it early, because it silently constrains funerary costume, calendar (seasons of the dead), and architecture.
  - Couple it: funerary grammar and liminality in `corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/ritual-liminality.md`; the afterlife *geography* (layers, maps, journeys) in `corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/cosmology-cosmography.md`; corpse treatment and grave goods in `corpus/knowledge/culture-material/material-culture-inference-method.md`.
  - **The inverse direction is a plot engine**: a culture that buries its dead facing away from the ancestral direction is announcing a rupture (heresy, conquest, reform). Date it.

## 8. Orientation of prayer and institutions

- **Prayer direction** is the register's institutional core:
  - **Islam**: the qibla — the direction of the Ka'ba (Qur'an 2:144); determining it far from Mecca became a trigonometric problem (al-Bīrūnī, Taḥdīd nihāyat al-amākin — carried via cosmology-cosmography.md §8–§9).
  - **Judaism**: a nested ladder — in the diaspora face the Land of Israel; in the Land face Jerusalem; in Jerusalem face the Temple (Babylonian Talmud, Berakhot 30a, Sefaria text retrieved 2026-10-17). This is the cleanest real template for a scalable direction rule: **the bearing is defined relative to your distance from the center**, and each tier has its own wall (the mizrah wall in diaspora synagogues).
  - **Christianity**: eastward orientation of prayer and buildings (ad orientem) — standard liturgical-historical literature (Jungmann 1951 not re-retrieved this session; treat as the lane's standing reference).
- **Institutional axiality**:
  - **China**: the south-facing rule — ruler and gate face south, and the capital's central axis is a measured, maintained cosmographic claim (Zhouli "Kaogong ji"; Beijing's axis measured off true north by He et al. 2023 — both carried via cosmology-cosmography.md §9).
  - **Bali**: temples and the sacred quarter orient kaja (mountainward) — the frame from §2 is the temple's bearing (Atmadja 2018, web-retrieved 2026-10-17).
  - **Etruria/Rome**: the augur's *templum* — the sky-sector a diviner claims and orients — makes orientation itself part of the divinatory institution (disciplina etrusca; `corpus/knowledge/civilizations/etruria.md`).
- **Design rule — sacred orientation is the most dateable direction fact.** Buildings survive and their bearings are measurable; when a culture reorients its prayer, the change is an event with a cause (a qibla change, a reform, a conquest). Write the reorientation as an event, not a constant, and give it an institution to enforce it (a college of sighters, a decree, a fine).

## 9. Build order and the register audit

Steps (extends parent method §3 for this register):

0. **Fix the substrate**: terrain, dominant landmark or drainage, sky visibility, navigation technology (couples `corpus/knowledge/culture-material/architecture-settlement-lexicon.md`).
1. **Declare the frame** (D1): absolute / landmark / relative / mixed — and which professional class, if any, keeps the abstract frame.
2. **Choose the partition n** from a real enumeration (4+1, 4+2, 8+1, 10) and name the center's occupant and exception list.
3. **Coin the direction words** from the frame's sources (§3) — sun, wind, river, body — and seed the archaic layer from a superseded frame.
4. **Assign valences with institutions** (D2): one primary domain per direction, each with the rite, law, or taboo that enforces it.
5. **Align the other registers** (color, animal, number, season) over the same slots; mark UNALIGNED cells; keep source-variance rows where real cultures disagree.
6. **Set the motion facts**: rotation direction(s) and their inversions (§6); the direction of the dead (§7); prayer and institutional bearings (§8).
7. **Version it**: date-stamp the frame, the valences, and any reorientation; record what the previous era's words mean now (couples `corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/symbolism-synthesis-method.md` §6 change rules).

Audit checklist (register slice of parent §7):
- [ ] Frame declared before any color/animal assignment (D1).
- [ ] Every valence has a named institution (D2).
- [ ] Partition n traces to a real enumeration; center slot has a job.
- [ ] Every direction-color pair either unique to this culture or carries a documented diffusion link.
- [ ] Rotation rule has: direction, count, start point, sanction.
- [ ] Death bearing matches the frame (seaward for a landmark frame, not "west").
- [ ] Prayer/institutional bearings date-stamped; reorientations written as events.
- [ ] All flagged items listed in the handoff packet, not silently dropped.

Worked micro-sketch (non-canon, disposable — canon belongs to lore-shokunin): a highland herding culture with a sacred massif to the southeast. Frame: landmark (massif-ward = "up-honored"; seaward = "down-far"). Partition: 5 — four slopes plus camp. Valences: massif-ward = ancestral/clean; seaward = foreign/dead (the dead are carried seaward, not west); the two cross-slopes = the two moieties; camp = the unmarked center. Rotation: flocks are driven sunwise around the camp at the spring rite; the wrong way is the shepherd's curse-form. Coinage: direction words from slope + wind names (following cosmology-cosmography.md §9's wind-name lever). Note what the sketch demonstrates: the death bearing follows the frame, not the compass.

## 10. Calibration shelf

- **Hertz's polarity**: keep the question (how cultures use the body axis), drop the evolutionism; not universal (Meru left-strong ritual; China's era-alternating precedence; the augural vantage flip). Needham's introduction to Right & Left (1973) is the standing caution.
- **"Four directions" is not universal**: attested partitions run 4, 5, 6, 8, 10; landmark frames may have no cardinal terms at all (Balinese kaja/kelod; Tzeltal uphill/downhill). Never write "all cultures have four directions."
- **Direction-color pairs are conventional** (anti-porting, §4.5): Maya east = red vs Chinese east = qing; Zuni north = yellow; Lakota sets vary by nation and arrive partly through Black Elk's mediated text (DeMallie 1984 — via cosmology-cosmography.md §10). No shared pair without documented diffusion.
- **Pan-Indian "medicine wheel" direction colors**: vary by nation; do not pan-Indianize (sibling §10).
- **Ceremonial-center urbanism** (Wheatley 1971): generator, not settled history; single-cause models are heavily qualified in current urban archaeology (sibling §10).
- **Hamlet's Mill / panbabylonism**: dead as method; do not resurrect even as in-world "ancient wisdom" without labeling it as the culture's own esoteric claim (sibling §10).
- **Ley lines / geomancy-as-physics**: feng shui is a real historical practice with real schools (couples `corpus/knowledge/culture-material/architecture-settlement-lexicon.md`); its modern "earth-energy" rationalizations are not science and must not be written as in-world physics.
- **North is a choice**: magnetic vs true north differ by declination; for navigator cultures make the distinction explicit (§2).
- **Source-variance**: Cushing's Zuni set is one witness; when witnesses disagree, keep both and date them (§4).
- **"Right = good" universality**: false (counterexamples §5).
- **Sunwise / clockwise**: hemisphere- and clock-dependent metaphors; re-coin per world (§6).
