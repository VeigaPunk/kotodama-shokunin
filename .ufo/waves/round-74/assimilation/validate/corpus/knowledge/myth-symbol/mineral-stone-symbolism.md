## 1. What this entry owns, and the register slot it fills

The register family the partition builds its symbolic grammars from is closed at **color, animal, number, celestial body, direction** (`symbolism-synthesis-method.md:23`), and `plant-symbolism.md:3` added the flora sixth. This entry adds the **mineral** seventh — stone, gem and worked metal — and it does so on the partition's own authority rather than on taste. `symbolism-synthesis-method.md:29` tells the worldbuilder to imitate four real closed codes, and names them: **bestiary + lapidary + numerology + heraldic code**. Three of the four are owned in-partition; the lapidary is not, and the only in-partition occurrence of the word is that sentence.

| Component of the package | Owner (read, not restated) | What this entry adds |
|---|---|---|
| Bestiary | `animal-symbolism.md` (`:13,:84-91`) | the *material* of the emblem — the charge's stone |
| Numerology | `number-symbolism.md` | the *count* of stones in a regalia list (assignment stays theirs) |
| Heraldic code | `animal-symbolism.md` §7 (`:84-91`) | stones as charges; the heraldic-lag era device extended to stone |
| **Lapidary** | **none — this entry** | the whole register: properties, code form, value ladder, axis class |

**Routed out, not covered.** What a colour *means* is `color-symbolism.md`; the colour *word* is `color-terminology.md`; a stone's count in a list is `number-symbolism.md`; the ritual that deposits it is `ritual-liminality.md`; the workshop that cuts it is `craft-artisan-lexicon.md`; the law that restricts it is `sumptuary-law-rank-display.md`. This entry owns the material itself as a sign-bearing object and the system of such objects.

**The master claim, stated once: a stone-sign is not a stone — it is a claim about scarcity, origin and control.** Every material assignment a culture makes is downstream of three facts the material itself supplies: how hard it is to get, where it comes from, and what happens to it when it fails. A worldbuilder who assigns "jade = wisdom" without answering those three has borrowed a look, not built a register. This is the mineral analogue of the flora register's own maxim that symbolic plants are chosen by subsistence, not taste (`plant-symbolism.md:15`).

## 2. The five jobs a lithic register does

| # | job | what the culture asks of the material | attested exemplar (in-corpus) | design question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| J1 | **axis** | hold the center; be the world's peg | the pillar row: Egyptian djed, Ame-no-mihashira, Delphic omphalos, Sufi *qutb* (`cosmology-cosmography.md:37`) | does the stone have a *built expression* and a *ritual that uses it*? (`:42`) |
| J2 | **boundary** | mark where one jurisdiction or cosmos ends | the *sulcus primigenius* and *pomerium* (`architecture-settlement-lexicon.md:95`); the black stone a circumambulation starts from (`direction-symbolism.md:114`) | which word does the boundary claim? (`architecture-settlement-lexicon.md:138`) |
| J3 | **prestige** | make rank readable | the Qing hat-button finials graded gem-to-metal, 1st→9th (`sumptuary-law-rank-display.md:53`) | is the sign *assayable*? (`:2,:85,:106`) |
| J4 | **instrument** | do work: cut, weigh, measure, keep time | the Campus Martius obelisk as a sundial (`timekeeping-instruments.md:24`); the steel-grain vocabulary (`craft-artisan-lexicon.md:44`) | what breaks, and what a failed run costs? (`material-culture-inference-method.md:68`) |
| J5 | **heirloom** | carry a lineage; be inalienable | Fu Hao's tomb jades, some Neolithic (`china-shang-zhou.md:106`); pounamu as *taonga* (`maori.md:13-14`) | was the object made for this owner or inherited by them? (`:106`) |

- **Reading the table.** J1–J5 are not exclusive; a stone usually runs on one or two, and the *pair* is what a world bible needs. The Delphic omphalos is J1+J2; the Qing finial is J3 with a J4 origin (a button was a fastener before it was a rank); pounamu is J5+J3.
- **The falsification rule.** A material assigned no job is scenery. If a world bible names a precious stone and cannot say which of J1–J5 it does, the stone is decoration and every rule attached to it (who may wear it, what it wards, what it costs) is unlicensed.
- **Why the jobs are the entry's spine rather than a stone list.** A list of stones is a museum; the jobs are the design surface. The same mineral can be J1 in one culture and J3 in another — the job, not the mineral, is the assignment.

## 3. The six designable properties — where every stone-sign comes from

| # | property | what it is | what it generates | in-corpus anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **scarcity** | how hard the material is to obtain, and who controls the source | prestige, monopoly law, the price ladder | dye-scarcity economics, `color-symbolism.md:2,:114` |
| M2 | **hardness / workability** | what can be done to it, and at what skill cost | the craft ladder, the tool vocabulary, the failure mode | `craft-artisan-lexicon.md:44`; `material-culture-inference-method.md:68` |
| M3 | **colour and lustre** | what it looks like under what light | the whole colour-register coupling | `color-symbolism.md`; `color-terminology.md` |
| M4 | **provenance** | where it comes from: a named quarry, a river, the sky | trade routes, pilgrimage, origin-myths, authentication | `writing-systems.md:52`; `maori.md:13-14` |
| M5 | **inherence / inalienability** | whether the object can leave its owner | heirloom rules, identity, repatriation claims | `china-shang-zhou.md:106`; Weiner 1992 via `material-culture-inference-method.md:9` |
| M6 | **durability / failure mode** | how it ages, and how it breaks | what survives to be found; the "found stone" plot | `material-culture-inference-method.md:69` |

- **The derivation rule (DR-MS-01).** A stone-sign must be derived from at least one of M1–M6, and the world bible must name which. "Emerald = hope" derived from nothing is a borrowed look; "emerald = hope because the only source is a single hostile valley and hope is what the valley's miners sell" is a register entry.
- **The two-property rule.** The strongest assignments use **two** properties at once, and the pair is the design decision. Pounamu is M1+M5 (a single-source river stone that cannot be alienated); the *sarcophagus* stone of Assos is M6+M4 (a stone whose failure mode — it consumes the dead — becomes its name, `funerary-material-culture.md:22`); the Maya jade is M3+M1 (colour and centrality, with the abstract's own limit that not every object carries all the associations, `maya-classic.md:127`).
- **The anti-borrowing rule.** A culture cannot own every stone on the map. Assign the **three to six** materials its geography and economy actually supply, and let the rest be imports — imports are themselves a sign (they mark a trade route, an alliance, or a dependency).

## 4. The lapidary code — the form, and its modern analogues

A **lapidary** is a code book of stones, and the medieval Latin form is the model `symbolism-synthesis-method.md:29` points at. Its entry shape is stable and it is what makes the code *manufacturable, teachable and checkable*:

| field | medieval lapidary entry | what it does | modern analogue |
|---|---|---|---|
| stone | the name, with its synonyms | fixes the referent | the trade name and its synonyms (`craft-artisan-lexicon.md:47`) |
| species / property | colour, hardness, where found | grounds the sign in M1–M4 | the gem lab's 4Cs: carat, cut, colour, clarity (`:47`) |
| virtue | the power it carries | asserts what the sign *does* | the value grade and its market |
| application | where it is used, by whom | assigns the job (J1–J5) | the sumptuary rank slot (`sumptuary-law-rank-display.md:53`) |
| moral / gloss | the preaching turn | makes it teachable | the advertising tagline, the appraisal note |

- **The two rules that keep a lapidary code from becoming a museum.** (1) **Every entry must carry a property field grounded in M1–M6** — a virtue with no material cause is decoration. (2) **Every entry must name who may use it** — an application field that is empty is a missing institution (the house pattern of `sociolinguistics-registers.md` §9).
- **The quality-vocabulary precedent is already in-corpus and it is a *language*.** The Japanese blade vocabulary calls the bloom *tamahagane* 玉鋼 "ball/jewel-steel", divides the billet into *kawagane* 皮鉄 "skin-metal" and *shingane* 心鉄 "heart-metal", and judges the temper line by *nie* 沸 "boiling" and *nioi* 匂 "scent/fragrance" (`craft-artisan-lexicon.md:44`) — metallurgy **speaking anatomy and the senses**. This is the entry's best evidence that a material register is a grammar, not a list: the words themselves are built from other registers.
- **The invented grade ladder is a designable era instrument.** GIA's 4Cs were "systematized mid-20th c." and, in the corpus's own words, "a modern invented grading language that conquered the world's trade in one century; your guild can do this too" (`craft-artisan-lexicon.md:47`). A fictional culture's gem ladder should carry its **invention date** — a ladder presented as immemorial when it was codified in era 3 is an anachronism the worldbuilder's own audit can catch.
- **The four-factor quality formula is a non-Western precedent.** The Kaogongji's four-factor quality formula and the *qiao/zhuo* aesthetic inversion (`craft-artisan-lexicon.md:3`) show that a quality grammar need not be a numeric scale; it can be a **checklist of conditions**. Design the grammar, then decide whether it is a ladder or a checklist — the choice is a cultural signature.

## 5. Stone and metal as a value language

- **A grade ladder is an administrative code.** The single best in-corpus example is the Qing hat-button finial, graded by rank from ruby, coral, sapphire, lapis, crystal and shell down to gold and silver for ranks 1→9 (`sumptuary-law-rank-display.md:53`). It works because the sign is **assayable** — anyone can see the button and know the rank, and a forger must possess the actual stone.
- **The assayability test governs the whole register.** `sumptuary-law-rank-display.md:2,:85` states the rule: a dress law "only survives where the sign is assayable", and the assay clause "names a measurable property". Applied to stone: a *visible* grade (a particular gem) beats a *testimonial* one (a stone said to be a particular gem), and a *measurable* one (weight, cut, a stamped mark) beats both. Rule `:106` — "measurable beats visible beats testimonial" — is this entry's enforcement criterion.
- **The value ladder has a floor as well as a ceiling.** Below the gems sits the ordinary stone: the millstone, the whetstone, the weight. `vessel-container-lexicon.md:5` owns the measure→money→number ladder; this entry owns the material side of the same fact — a stone weight is a *metrological* object and a *prestige* object in the same body, and the two roles can be in tension (a standard weight that is also beautiful invites fraud).
- **Instruments and marks make the ladder enforceable.** The graded object needs a **mark** — a guild stamp, an assay, a hallmark — and `craft-artisan-lexicon.md:87` gives the precedent: the Dürer monogram case (Venice, early 16th c.) as "arguably the first documented trademark infringement proceeding". A fictional culture with a gem ladder but no court of marks has a ladder nobody can police.
- **The economy of scarcity is the enforcement's fuel.** A dye code works because the dyestuff is scarce and its source is monopolised (`color-symbolism.md:114`); a stone code works the same way. Design the *source* first — the single valley, the guarded mine, the river only one clan may work — and the prestige follows. A stone code with no controlled source is a code with no teeth.

## 6. Stone as axis, boundary and witness

- **The axis-stone class is already in the partition as a cosmography row and is unowned as a register.** `cosmology-cosmography.md:37` prints the **pillar** row of the axis-solution table — the Egyptian *djed* ("stability"), the Ame-no-mihashira pillar, the Delphic *omphalos*, the Sufi *qutb* ("pole") — and `:35` prints the tree row as its flora counterpart. This entry owns the *material* dimension of that row: what the pillar is made of, where the stone came from, and what happens when it is moved or replaced.
- **The built-expression test applies, and it is not optional.** `cosmology-cosmography.md:42` (J. Z. Smith): "axis-mundi claims are cheap — a comparativist finds a center wherever he looks. Require (a) built expression (a pillar, a street, a seat) and (b) a ritual that uses it." A standing stone with no ritual is a rock; the register entry is the stone **plus** the rite, and the rite belongs to `ritual-liminality.md`.
- **A stone can be a place-name and a charter at once.** `cosmology-cosmography.md:132` records the Delphic *omphalos* and the Quechua *qusqu* ("navel"/"support-pillar") as the etymological pattern by which a capital is named for its axis — the naming derivation goes to `naming-systems.md:40,:70`, and this entry supplies the *material* that makes the name credible.
- **The boundary stone is a legal object.** `architecture-settlement-lexicon.md:95` records the Roman founding rite: the *sulcus primigenius* (the plough furrow) and the *pomerium* (the law-line) — "the city begins as a furrow and a law-line". A boundary stone is where J2 lives, and `:101` gives the design lever (instrument + founding rite + claim word). The rule `:138` — "fix the boundary word first" — is this entry's naming instruction.
- **Stone is a witness, because it stays.** The flora register's first property is that a plant is *stationary* and therefore ownable (`plant-symbolism.md:15`); the mineral's analogue is that a stone is *stationary and enduring*, which makes it the natural carrier of oaths, boundaries and witness marks. A stone witness is credible for exactly the reason a spoken witness is not: it cannot leave, and it cannot forget. The binding force of an oath is `binding-speech-complex.md`'s surface — this entry supplies only the *object* the oath is sworn on.
- **Rotation makes a stone a faction flag.** `direction-symbolism.md:114` records that two communities circumambulating the same shrine in opposite directions is "one of the most legible schism markers a world can show", and names the black stone as a starting point. A stone at the center of a rotation is doing direction-register work; the direction file owns the motion, this entry owns the stone.

## 7. Stone as instrument — the quarry-to-workshop chain and the material ceiling

- **The extraction chain is the register's origin story.** `writing-systems.md:52` records that the Proto-Sinaitic inscriptions were carved at Serabit el-Khadim, a **turquoise-mining site**, by "Canaanite semilingual miners" applying acrophony to Egyptian signs — the alphabet's birthplace is a **quarry road**. That is the register's strongest single in-corpus fact: a mineral route can be the most consequential site in a culture's history, and a worldbuilder who places a mine should know it may become a writing's cradle.
- **Hardness sets the ceiling on every other material system.** `material-culture-inference-method.md:68` frames the design question — workability, apprenticeship length, craft secrecy, "what breaks, what a failed run costs". A hard-stone economy needs abrasives, and abrasives need their own source; a culture that can cut jade but not drill it will build a different art. The chain runs source → extraction → rough working → finishing → mark, and `craft-artisan-lexicon.md:3` owns the workshop end of it.
- **A monument can be a mechanism.** `timekeeping-instruments.md:24` records the Campus Martius obelisk as a sundial (Pliny *NH* 36.71–72; the "Horologium Augusti" reconstruction is modern scholarship and the item is `(to-verify)` there). J4 in its purest form: the stone is not decorated, it *works*. This is the case that keeps a lithic register from collapsing into ornament.
- **Failure modes are cultural facts.** Bradley's deposition grammar, carried at `material-culture-inference-method.md:56` — "how things end — hoard, votive, grave, refuse — is a cultural grammar" — is the rule for what happens to a retired or broken stone. A cracked temple pillar, a decommissioned standard weight, a defaced boundary stone: each is a designed end, and the design should be written, not improvised.
- **Metal is the register's plastic end.** Metal is a mineral that can be melted and recast, which makes it the register's only *reversible* material — an heirloom can be melted into a new object, and that act is a statement. Lechtman (1984), registered at `material-culture-inference-method.md:7`, is the in-lane source for metal-as-value-system; the workshop recipes are `craft-artisan-lexicon.md`'s.

## 8. The inorganic boundary — what a stone register cannot do

A register earns its file by what it *cannot* say, and the mineral's limits are precise:

| the flora/fauna registers can… | the lithic register cannot… | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| carry life-cycle meaning (seed → flower → fruit) | carry a *life cycle* | a stone's meaning is static or inherited, never grown |
| regrow after cutting (`plant-symbolism.md:15`) | regrow | a broken stone is broken; its end must be *designed* (§7) |
| be a *person's* kin (totem, guardian) | be a person | a stone can be an ancestor's *object*, never an ancestor |
| reproduce | reproduce | a quarry depletes; the era stamp is the exhausted seam |
| be eaten or worn as a living thing | be eaten | the mineral enters the body only as *medicine* or as *salt* (`cuisine-food-lexicon.md`) |

- **The consequence for design.** A stone's symbolic load is carried by **provenance and control** rather than by growth — which is why M1 and M4 do more work in this register than any other property. The flora register's engine is seasonality; the lithic register's engine is **origin**.
- **The "found stone" plot is a stone-register plot.** Because a stone can be *found* rather than made (a meteorite, a river pebble, an old object in a new grave — `china-shang-zhou.md:106`), the register generates a story shape the living registers cannot: the object whose *age* is a claim and whose *origin* is a mystery. That is the mineral register's contribution to narrative, and it belongs to `myth-folktale-genres.md` at the plot level.
- **The anti-animism rule.** A stone that "wants" something is a *different* register's claim (the animist/other-than-human one, `animal-symbolism.md:53`'s spirit-helper slot and the spirit-class territory). This entry models stone as a sign-bearing object; if a culture holds that a stone is a *person*, that is a spirit-class or animism assignment and must be named as such, not smuggled in as a material property.

## 9. Cross-register coupling and era drift

| coupling | what crosses | where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| stone ↔ **colour** | the stone's colour is the colour register's material instance | `color-symbolism.md`; `color-terminology.md` |
| stone ↔ **direction** | the boundary stone, the rotation's starting stone, the corner | `direction-symbolism.md:114,:125` |
| stone ↔ **number** | how many stones, which rank, which zodiac slot | `number-symbolism.md`; `ritual-garments.md:59` |
| stone ↔ **celestial** | the meteorite, the planetary gem correspondence | `celestial-symbolism-calendars.md` |
| stone ↔ **cosmology** | the axis pillar, the temple's foundation deposit | `cosmology-cosmography.md:35,:37,:42` |
| stone ↔ **craft** | the workshop, the grade vocabulary, the mark | `craft-artisan-lexicon.md:3,:44,:47,:87` |
| stone ↔ **law** | the sumptuary ladder and its assay | `sumptuary-law-rank-display.md:2,:53,:85` |
| stone ↔ **writing** | the inscribed stone, the quarry-road script | `writing-systems.md:44,:52` |

- **Era drift, six mechanisms.** (1) **Depletion**: the seam runs out and the stone's meaning inverts from prestige to nostalgia. (2) **Discovery**: a new source collapses the price and democratises the sign — the ladder then needs a new top rung. (3) **Grading invention**: a ladder codified in one era, then read as immemorial (`craft-artisan-lexicon.md:47`). (4) **Theft and translation**: a relic's or palladium's removal is a political event (`siam-thai.md:126`'s Emerald Buddha). (5) **Suspension**: prestige is *lowered* at the approach of the sacred — the ihram case, `ritual-garments.md:28`. (6) **Recycling**: metal melted into a new object, carrying the old name into a new body.
- **The heirloom rule as an era instrument.** `china-shang-zhou.md:106` — "an old object in a later burial does not imply manufacture during the burial's dynasty". Design lever: an heirloom's *age* is legible in a grave and its *acquisition date* is not, so a world's stone objects give the archaeologist-in-world a date for the **object** and only a *terminus ante quem* for the burial — the gap between the two is where dynastic-claim forgeries live.
- **Heraldic lag, extended to stone.** `animal-symbolism.md:84-91` establishes that symbols lag ecology — medieval Europe emblazoned a lion it had never met. The mineral analogue: a culture keeps a stone in its regalia long after its source is lost, so the *stone* dates the *institution*, not the trade.

## 10. Build protocol — the lithic register in eight steps

1. **Fix the geography's minerals.** Which stones and ores does the culture's territory actually supply? (Three to six. The rest are imports, and imports are a sign.)
2. **Assign the sources.** For each: is it a single controlled source, a contested one, or a diffuse one? (M1 + M4 — this step decides the register's economics.)
3. **Run each material through the property matrix (§3).** Name the one or two properties that generate its sign. No assignment without a named property.
4. **Assign the jobs (§2).** Which of J1–J5 does each material do? A material with no job is scenery — either give it a job or drop it.
5. **Write the lapidary entries (§4).** Stone, property, virtue, application, moral. The application field is where the institution lives; leave it empty and the code has no teeth.
6. **Build the value ladder and test its assay (§5).** Measurable beats visible beats testimonial. If nothing is assayable, the ladder exists only at trial.
7. **Write the failure rules (§7).** What happens when each material breaks, is retired, is captured, or is melted? Bradley's deposition grammar is the checklist.
8. **Set the era stamps (§9).** Depletion, discovery, grading invention, theft, suspension, recycling — each gets a date slot, not a date.

## 12. Calibration bank — contested, discredited, constructed

|---|---|---|
| "Precious stones are universal talismans" | **discredited** as a universal | the lapidary virtue-list is a *specific* medieval European form (Albertus Magnus onward); Kunz (1913) compiled it as belief, not fact. Use it as the model for a *form*, never as a claim about what a stone does |
| "Jade = the sacred stone" / "gold = the sun metal" | **contested / overgeneralised** | jade is sacred in Maya and Chinese contexts for *different* reasons (colour+centrality vs colour+workability), and gold's solar reading is one resolution of many. `maya-classic.md:127` already carries the limit: not every object expresses all the associations |
| "Ancient gem-lore is continuous" | **constructed** | the medieval lapidary is not a survival of a primeval tradition; it is a text tradition with datable editions. Design a lapidary as a *composed* code with an author and a date, like `craft-artisan-lexicon.md:47`'s invented ladder |
| "Stone survives, so stone objects were the important ones" | **methodological error** | `material-culture-inference-method.md:69`: textiles dominate daily identity and vanish from most soils.
