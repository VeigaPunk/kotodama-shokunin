VERDICT P03 SUPPORTED

VERDICT P04 NOT-SUPPORTED
evidence: live 200, 195448 B; the quote is present, but the page is a gold Charles II touch-piece coin for scrofula ("It stems from the belief that the monarch was a channel for divine power."). It never mentions hazelnuts, so the hazelnut-amulet teaches and entry are not carried.
VERDICT P05 SUPPORTED
evidence: live 200, 17563 B (Wayback 200, 17599 B same text); Encyclopedia of Shinto body: "Shinsatsu are a type of thaumaturgic talisman (also known as gofu) distributed at shrines and symbolic of a spiritual being or its supranormal power." The next sentences say "enshrined in kamidana, or affixed above gates, on doors and pillars, or on ceilings".
VERDICT P06 SUPPORTED
evidence: live 200, 58165 B; Smithsonian body text, after "scribes in what is now Iraq produced thousands of these bowls between the fifth and eighth centuries C.E.": "Customized to fit individual clients' needs, the talismans were used to fight off bad luck, financial struggles and even severe migraines."
VERDICT P07 SUPPORTED
evidence: live 200, 319854 B PDF (Tuerk, narrative amulets); body paragraph followed by British Museum and Walters intaglio examples: "In addition to holy icons and unorthodox amulets, there survive icons in the amuletic form of engraved stones to be worn on the body."
VERDICT P08 NOT-SUPPORTED
evidence: live 200, 200533 B; the quote is present, but the page is an eighteenth-century hazelnut left at the London Foundling Hospital in 1752. Nothing on it mentions fourteenth-century Norway, infants' families in Norway, or the nut transmitting wisdom, so the added date, place and claim are unsupported.
VERDICT P09 SUPPORTED

VERDICT P10 SUPPORTED
evidence: live 200, 33124 B; Discover Islamic Art "Hand of Fatima" Description: "Jewish silversmiths in Morocco became known for casting and engraving pieces such as this one, used as a talismanic doorknob or jewellery pendant."
