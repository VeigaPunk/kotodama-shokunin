## Draft sentences

An incantation bowl is a household amulet whose potency comes from a combination of elements, beginning with an incantation written on common domestic earthenware (S614).
Levene lists the elements that make it potent: an earthenware vessel, an inscribed incantation, an image of a bound demon, and burial upside down (S614).
As an amuletic object the bowl aims at childbirth difficulty, illness, poverty and afflictions sent by supernatural and human foes (S614).
Bowls sold as talismans were customized to each client's troubles, answering bad luck, money problems and even migraines (S615).
A buyer buried the talisman beneath the house floor to activate its protection, and marketplace magicians sold such bowls to clients of every religion (S615).
Seized from a Jerusalem home and auction house, with some pieces possibly forged, the talismans now circulate as looted antiquities (S615).

What each row's page itself carries, beyond its quote (opened this session; these are the sentences the entry may lean on):

- S614 page: "Aramaic incantation bowls, also known as magic bowls, are types of amulets that consists of an incantation written on common domestic earthenware."; "These elements consist of its being an earthenware bowl that was inscribed with an incantation, adorned with an image, and buried upside-down."; "The incantations in the bowls are often accompanied by graphic images that most commonly appear to be depictions of bound demons."; "Bowls could be buried in one or all of the comers of the house, under the threshold or any of the rooms or entrances of the domestic complex."
