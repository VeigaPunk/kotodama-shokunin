## Calibration item (popular vs. source truth)
Popular claim "there is a coat of arms for my surname / family crest": the report's row -1 shows crest is a helm-top device, not a family-wide emblem; arms are personal and classed by bearer. Row -1's "Crests at first... restricted to a few persons of eminence" shows that elevation, not surname-wholesale.
Popular "only nobles could bear arms": row -1 explicitly classes "Arms of Community... as cities, colleges" and "Borne, with the personal arms, to denote official rank" — i.e. non-noble institutions and officeholders bear arms.
Popular "crest is the whole coat": row -1 "Crest. A figure or device originally worn upon a helm, and now generally represented above a Shield of arms" is verbatim.
Popular "there is a coat of arms for my surname / family crest" is directly refuted by row -2: crests are borne per family representation, "quite irrespective of what the surname in use may have been", and the same crest is regularly granted to people of many different surnames.
Popular "the crest is the whole coat of arms" is further refuted by row -2: the shield and the crest were separate inheritances, and the necessary connection between them is a modern assumption, not early proof.
