draft sentences:
- For worldbuilders, this note gives the canonical monument: Geoffrey Plantagenêt's funerary plaque (d. 1151) in champlevé enamel, worn on shield and conical helmet, a blue field with golden lions and a vair-lined mantle (NEW-ScoutHeraldryOrigins-2, lemans).
- Its dating is a model of hedging: the tomb was erected soon after Geoffrey's death, before 1155, the plaque is dated on stylistic grounds to ca 1150–55, and yet it is called the oldest colour representation of a coat of arms (NEW-ScoutHeraldryOrigins-2, lemans).
- Outsiders often over-read: Geoffrey's only surviving seal (1149) shows no armory, and the story that Henry I gave him a lion shield at knighting in 1127 comes from a chronicle compiled around 1170–75, so it is not sure he ever bore those arms (NEW-ScoutHeraldryOrigins-2, lemans).
- The note shows how honest description stays vague: the number of lions is disputed (Wagner counts six, Pastoureau and Neubecker eight, Pastoureau suggesting a semy), so a builder should not freeze exact counts from this piece (NEW-ScoutHeraldryOrigins-2, lemans).
- Colour is the scarce medieval resource: until stained-glass, manuscripts and mural painting in the mid-13th century, enamels are essentially the only source of colour depictions of armory (NEW-ScoutHeraldryOrigins-2, lemans).
