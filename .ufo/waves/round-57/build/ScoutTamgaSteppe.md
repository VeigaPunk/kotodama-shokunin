draft sentences:
- The base meaning for the entry is ownership, not writing: tamgas are signs of group identity used by medieval Turkic nomads inhabiting Southern Siberia, Mongolia, and Kazakhstan — an abstract sign owned by a group, never an alphabet to be "read". (NEW-ScoutTamgaSteppe-2, abstract)
- A bearer culture puts the mark on almost anything it owns: tamgas appear on memorial and cultic structures (steles, funerary enclosures, balbal steles, tombstones, temple buildings), in petroglyph accumulations, and on portable items — coins of Transoxiana, cultic and household items, prestigious products such as expensive dishware and seals, and items of military and horse equipment — a ready checklist of legal surfaces for a world's marks. (NEW-ScoutTamgaSteppe-2, Introduction)
- Whole rock faces can serve as mark registers: separate collections of tamga signs form an independent type of monument, traditionally called tamgalytas in the Turkic-speaking part of Central Asia — a valley stone where visiting groups advertise their presence. (NEW-ScoutTamgaSteppe-2, Introduction)
- Marks inherit by branch-growth: a base sign gains varieties composed by adding diacritical elements, and two sign groups have many derivatives marking certain divisions of the Karluk federation — one house-sign, many stroke-added branch-marks, the steppe answer to cadency. (NEW-ScoutTamgaSteppe-2, Groups of tamgas)
- Not every mark belongs to a clan: certain sign groups (combinations of many varieties) were emblems of major tribal unions, others were supratribal markers of social status, privileged clans, and alliances, and certain emblems were dynastic signs — dynastic tamgas of the Yaglakar, a khagan clan of the Uyghurs, are known from Syrnakh-Gozy and Kurai I — so one graphic system carries group, rank and dynasty at once, and a popular account that reads every tamga as "the clan's coat of arms" over-reads it. (NEW-ScoutTamgaSteppe-2, abstract)
- On land, the mark works as a boundary device: tamgas on immovable items have the greatest informational value for the study of land use, settlement and movement, and they mark long-term habitation locations and areas of various nomad groups over a particular period of time — carved territory claims a stranger can read. (NEW-ScoutTamgaSteppe-2, Introduction)
