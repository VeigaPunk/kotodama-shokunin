### NEW-ScoutLawOfArms-1
citation: Parliament of Scotland, 1672, Lyon King of Arms Act 1672 (1672 c. 47), "Act concerning the priviledges of the Office of Lyon King at Armes", official revised text, The National Archives / legislation.gov.uk, id `aosp/1672/47`.
url: https://web.archive.org/web/20230725183454/https://www.legislation.gov.uk/aosp/1672/47/1995-08-01?view=plain
route: Wayback capture 20230725183454 (live `https://www.legislation.gov.uk/aosp/1672/47` returns HTTP 202 challenge page to plain GET; the capture is legislation.gov.uk's own plain-text rendering of the Act).
kind: record
tier: A — the statute's official revised text on the national legislation site; primary for what the law of arms in Scotland requires, forbids and registers.
scope: what counts as bearing arms (arms and "Signes armoriall"); compulsory registration/matriculation with the Lyon; who may grant arms; cadency differences as an office allocation; the public register as the rule of arms; statutory penalties for usurpation.
quotes:
- `LOCATED https://web.archive.org/web/20230725183454/https://www.legislation.gov.uk/aosp/1672/47/1995-08-01?view=plain`: "the usurpation of Armes by any of his Maiesties leidges without the Authority of the Lyon King of Armes is expresly discharged"
- `LOCATED https://web.archive.org/web/20230725183454/https://www.legislation.gov.uk/aosp/1672/47/1995-08-01?view=plain`: "the true and unrepeallable rule of all Armes and Bearings in Scotland to remain with the Lyons office as a publict Register of the Kingdome"
- `LOCATED https://web.archive.org/web/20230725183454/https://www.legislation.gov.uk/aosp/1672/47/1995-08-01?view=plain`: "may matriculat the same in his Bookes and Registers and may give Armes to vertuous and well deserving Persones"
- `LOCATED https://web.archive.org/web/20230725183454/https://www.legislation.gov.uk/aosp/1672/47/1995-08-01?view=plain`: "shall pay One Hundred pounds money toties quoties to the Lyon"
teaches: The act makes bearing arms in Scotland a registered right: users must matriculate with the Lyon within a year, usurpation draws a fine, and the register rules all arms.
draft sentences:
- A 1672 Scots statute treated bearing arms as a licensed inheritance, reciting that usurpation of arms without the Lyon King of Arms' authority is "expresly discharged" and ordering every user of "Armes or Signes armoriall" to send in, within a year of proclamation, what arms they used, whose family they descended from, and of which brother of the family, backed by testificats from persons of honour. (NEW-ScoutLawOfArms-1, https://web.archive.org/web/20230725183454/https://www.legislation.gov.uk/aosp/1672/47/1995-08-01?view=plain)
- Registration is the same office that grants: the Lyon is to distinguish arms "with congruent differences", matriculate them in his books and registers, and give arms to virtuous and well-deserving persons, so in this system cadet differences and new grants are allocations made by the heraldic office, not choices made by bearers. (NEW-ScoutLawOfArms-1, https://web.archive.org/web/20230725183454/https://www.legislation.gov.uk/aosp/1672/47/1995-08-01?view=plain)
- The register is declared "the true and unrepeallable rule of all Armes and Bearings in Scotland", kept at the Lyon's office as a public register of the kingdom and transmitted to his successors, with extracts of arms expressing the blazoning issued under the Lyon's hand and seal of office — one canonical ledger, and the paper it issues is itself sealed. (NEW-ScoutLawOfArms-1, https://web.archive.org/web/20230725183454/https://www.legislation.gov.uk/aosp/1672/47/1995-08-01?view=plain)
- Enforcement is penal and reaches objects: unregistered or unauthorised use after year and day costs one hundred pounds "toties quoties" to the Lyon, and all moveable goods and geir on which the unauthorised arms are engraven or otherwise represented escheat to the crown — the mark's carrier (a cup, a door, a garment) is forfeit with the claim it makes. (NEW-ScoutLawOfArms-1, https://web.archive.org/web/20230725183454/https://www.legislation.gov.uk/aosp/1672/47/1995-08-01?view=plain)
- For a worldbuilder, the act is a worked example of inherited identity marks as administration: a fixed window to declare, descent tested at registration, differences assigned "congruent" to the family's tree, a register that outranks private claims, and fines that recur each time the usurpation is repeated. (NEW-ScoutLawOfArms-1, https://web.archive.org/web/20230725183454/https://www.legislation.gov.uk/aosp/1672/47/1995-08-01?view=plain)

### NEW-ScoutLawOfArms-2
citation: Manchester Corporation v Manchester Palace of Varieties Ltd [1955] P 133, High Court of Chivalry, judgment of 21 January 1955, The Duke of Norfolk Earl Marshal, Lord Goddard D.C.L. sitting as assessor and surrogate; contemporary law-report scan (21 pages), Internet Archive item `6556644-Manchester-Corp-v-Manchester-Palace-of-Varieties` (contributor Kevin Underhill, from documentcloud 6556644, OCR ABBYY FineReader 11).

route: direct, HTTP 200 (plain GET of the item's OCR text; the same URL served every quote below).
kind: record
tier: A — the full text of the modern Court of Chivalry's leading judgment; primary for what arms are in law, who hears usurpation, and where display stops being harmless decoration.
scope: arms as a dignity rather than common-law property; the Court of Chivalry's live jurisdiction over usurpation of armorial bearings; the seal-versus-decoration line; how corporate arms pass (grant by the kings of arms, "on seals, shields, banners or otherwise according to the laws of arms").

- `LOCATED https://archive.org/download/6556644-Manchester-Corp-v-Manchester-Palace-of-Varieties/6556644-Manchester-Corp-v-Manchester-Palace-of-Varieties_djvu.txt`: "the corporation of a great city could properly object to their arms being used on any seal but their own"
- `LOCATED https://archive.org/download/6556644-Manchester-Corp-v-Manchester-Palace-of-Varieties/6556644-Manchester-Corp-v-Manchester-Palace-of-Varieties_djvu.txt`: "a deed sealed with an armorial device was thereby authenticated as the act and deed of the person entitled to the arms"
teaches: The 1955 judgment held the Court of Chivalry's arms jurisdiction alive: bearing arms is a dignity, not property, and a city could object to its arms on another's seal.
draft sentences:
- In the Court of Chivalry's 1955 sitting, the headnote states that its jurisdiction in matters relating to armorial bearings, evidenced by the court's records and the lawyers' communis opinio, "is still extant", and that it extends to complaints about usurpation of armorial bearings — usurpation of a mark is a justiciable wrong, not a courtesy dispute. (NEW-ScoutLawOfArms-2, https://archive.org/download/6556644-Manchester-Corp-v-Manchester-Palace-of-Varieties/6556644-Manchester-Corp-v-Manchester-Palace-of-Varieties_djvu.txt)
- The court frames what a coat of arms is: a right to bear arms is "a dignity and not property within the true sense of the term", and is not cognizable by the common law — so a specialised heraldic court, not the ordinary property courts, decides who may bear or use a mark. (NEW-ScoutLawOfArms-2, https://archive.org/download/6556644-Manchester-Corp-v-Manchester-Palace-of-Varieties/6556644-Manchester-Corp-v-Manchester-Palace-of-Varieties_djvu.txt)
- Where the mark sits decides the case: displaying the city's arms as theatre decoration alone was doubted as a ground for intervention, but using them on a company's common seal was a legitimate complaint, because a corporation "could properly object to their arms being used on any seal but their own". (NEW-ScoutLawOfArms-2, https://archive.org/download/6556644-Manchester-Corp-v-Manchester-Palace-of-Varieties/6556644-Manchester-Corp-v-Manchester-Palace-of-Varieties_djvu.txt)
- The reason turns on what a seal does: "a deed sealed with an armorial device was thereby authenticated as the act and deed of the person entitled to the arms", and a variety theatre sealing itself with Manchester's arms looked like an attempt to identify itself with the corporation — seals perform identity, decoration merely quotes it. (NEW-ScoutLawOfArms-2, https://archive.org/download/6556644-Manchester-Corp-v-Manchester-Palace-of-Varieties/6556644-Manchester-Corp-v-Manchester-Palace-of-Varieties_djvu.txt)
- The report shows how arms pass between holders by grant: Manchester's arms, crest and supporters were "granted and assigned" in 1842 by Garter, Clarenceux and Norroy King of Arms "to be borne and used for ever thereafter ... on seals, shields, banners or otherwise according to the laws of arms" — the grant itself names the office that gives the mark and the carriers it may be put on. (NEW-ScoutLawOfArms-2, https://archive.org/download/6556644-Manchester-Corp-v-Manchester-Palace-of-Varieties/6556644-Manchester-Corp-v-Manchester-Palace-of-Varieties_djvu.txt)
