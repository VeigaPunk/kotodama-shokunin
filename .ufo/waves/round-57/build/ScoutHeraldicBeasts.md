teaches: Fox-Davies treats beast postures and monster anatomy as technical vocabulary, while showing that heraldic classifications can vary historically.
draft sentences:
- Use formal blazon attitude terms: Fox-Davies describes rampant as erect in profile, passant as walking, and sejant as seated. (NEW-ScoutHeraldicBeasts-1, ch. XI, djvu.txt:9122-9123; 9295-9298; 9462-9463)
- Use guardant for a head turned toward the spectator; the text shows it with both rampant and passant lions. (NEW-ScoutHeraldicBeasts-1, ch. XI, djvu.txt:9132-9133; 9300-9301)
- Treat the lion/leopard distinction as historical terminology: Fox-Davies calls it a later addition invented by French heralds and says it became obsolete in British armory. (NEW-ScoutHeraldicBeasts-1, ch. XI, djvu.txt:8935-8937; 8943-8944)
- Give a wyvern two legs and a curling tail; distinguish a cockatrice by substituting a cock's head for the wyvern's dragon head. (NEW-ScoutHeraldicBeasts-1, ch. XIII, djvu.txt:11666-11672; 11703-11709)
- Fox-Davies groups mythical and legendary creatures as monsters and notes that most have a comparable natural-history counterpart; avoid treating every heraldic name as a literal zoological species. (NEW-ScoutHeraldicBeasts-1, ch. XIII, djvu.txt:11239-11243)
- A demi-lion rampant is presented as a common crest, while the treatise warns that over-elaboration confuses the clear advertisement of identity. (NEW-ScoutHeraldicBeasts-1, ch. XI, djvu.txt:9734-9751)
