## 4. Three design decisions no existing entry answers (answers question 4)

Each one is a gap that I checked against the owners above.

1. **Transmission: how a mark changes as it passes.** It can pass by birth order (cadency), by marriage and union (marshalling: impalement, quartering, escutcheon of pretence), by grant to retainers, or by derivation of variants (kamon variants, tamga modification).
   - Corpus state: 0 hits for cadency, marshalling or impalement anywhere. `warrior-material-culture.md:53` stops at "hereditary, court-registrable", and `:58` notes only that lords grant mons and houses share them.
   - What the worldbuilder decides: whether a mark is one person's at a time (one head bears the undifferenced arms) or the whole lineage's, and what visual operation records a younger son, a marriage, an heiress or a merger of houses. These are the operations a lore record of "which house bears what" needs.
2. **The bearer unit: who the mark identifies.** The candidates are an individual, a lineage, a clan of unrelated families sharing one device (Polish herb, shared kamon), an office (Mamluk office devices, rank badges, the galero's tassel count), or a following (livery, Mamluk composite emblems).
   - Corpus state: pieces exist in separate files (`mamluk.md:133`, `costume-semiotics.md:43`, `warrior-material-culture.md:58`). No file gives the cross-system bearer table. Polish clan arms (0 hits) and the heraldic galero (0 hits) are absent.
   - What the worldbuilder decides: the bearer unit fixes what a usurpation, inheritance dispute or promotion looks like on the page.
3. **The authority regime: who grants, registers and polices marks, and with what remedy.** The range runs from a sovereign heraldic court with prosecution (the Lyon Court) and a granting college (the College of Arms), through assumable arms registered only by custom, to no heraldic authority (Japan, per `warrior-material-culture.md:54`), with sumptuary enforcement as the separate route for rank badges (`sumptuary-law-rank-display.md:22`, `:53`).
   - Corpus state: `warrior-material-culture.md:66` names "heralds' courts" in one clause; "law of arms", "College of Arms" and "Lord Lyon" have 0 hits.
   - What the worldbuilder decides: whether counterfeit arms is a crime, a scandal or nothing, and so which in-world documents exist (grants, visitations, rolls, registers).

A runner-up gap is merchant and house marks (Hausmarke), which have 0 hits. They sit next to `craft-artisan-lexicon.md:76-82` and `writing-media-surface-economy.md:114`, so they are a small addition, not a fourth decision.
