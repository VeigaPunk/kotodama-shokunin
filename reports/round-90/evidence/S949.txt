<p id="mwIg">A <b id="mwIw">votive offering</b> or <b id="mwJA">votive deposit</b> is one or more objects displayed or deposited, without the intention of recovery or use, in a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred" title="Sacred" class="mw-redirect" id="mwJQ">sacred</a> place for <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious" title="Religious" class="mw-redirect" id="mwJg">religious</a> purposes. Such items are a feature of modern and ancient societies and are generally made to gain favor with <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernatural" title="Supernatural" id="mwJw">supernatural</a> forces.</p>

<p id="mwKA">While some offerings were apparently made in anticipation of the achievement of a particular wish, in Western cultures from which documentary evidence survives it was more typical to wait until the wish had been fulfilled before making the offering,<link rel="mw:PageProp/Category" href="./Category:All_articles_with_unsourced_statements" about="#mwt2" typeof="mw:Transclusion" id="mwKQ" data-mw='{"parts":[{"template":{"target":{"wt":"citation needed","href":"./Template:Citation_needed"},"params":{"date":{"wt":"January 2012"}},"i":0}}]}'/><link rel="mw:PageProp/Category" href="./Category:Articles_with_unsourced_statements_from_January_2012" about="#mwt2"/><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;" about="#mwt2" id="mwKg"><span typeof="mw:Entity">[</span><i><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2012)">citation needed</span></a></i><span typeof="mw:Entity">]</span></sup> for which the more specific term <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex-voto" title="Ex-voto" id="mwKw">ex-voto</a> may be used. Other offerings were very likely regarded just as gifts to the deity, not linked to any particular need.</p>

<p id="mwLA">In Buddhism, votive offering such as construction of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stupa" title="Stupa" id="mwLQ">stupas</a> was a prevalent practice in Ancient India, an example of which can be observed in the ruins of the ancient <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikramshila_University" title="Vikramshila University" class="mw-redirect" id="mwLg">Vikramshila University</a><sup about="#mwt5" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-1" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-1"}}'><a href="#cite_note-1" id="mwLw"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwMA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwMQ">[</span>1<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwMg">]</span></span></a></sup> and other contemporary structures. Votive offerings have been described in historical Roman era and Greek sources, although similar acts continue into the present day—for example, in traditional <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_culture" title="Catholic culture" class="mw-redirect" id="mwMw">Catholic culture</a> and, arguably, in the modern-day <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coins_as_votive_offering" title="Coins as votive offering" id="mwNA">practice of tossing coins</a> into a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wishing_well" title="Wishing well" id="mwNQ">wishing well</a> or fountain. The modern construction practice of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topping_out" title="Topping out" id="mwNg">topping out</a> can be considered an example of a votive practice with ancient roots.</p>

<p id="mwNw">In <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology" title="Archaeology" id="mwOA">archaeology</a>, votive deposits differ from <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoard" title="Hoard" id="mwOQ">hoards</a>; although they may contain similar items, votive deposits were not intended to be recovered.</p>

<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" id="mwOg" data-mw='{"autoGenerated":true}'/></section><section data-mw-section-id="1" id="mwOw" aria-labelledby="Ancient_offerings"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Ancient_offerings">Ancient offerings</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Votive_offering&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Ancient offerings"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwPA">In <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe" title="Europe" id="mwPQ">Europe</a>, votive deposits are known from as early as the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic" title="Neolithic" id="mwPg">Neolithic</a>, with polished <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axe" title="Axe" id="mwPw">axe</a> <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoard" title="Hoard" id="mwQA">hoards</a>, reaching a peak in the late <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age" title="Bronze Age" id="mwQQ">Bronze Age</a>. High status <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artifact_(archaeology)" title="Artifact (archaeology)" id="mwQg">artifacts</a> such as armor and weaponry (mostly <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shield" title="Shield" id="mwQw">shields</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sword" title="Sword" id="mwRA">swords</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spear" title="Spear" id="mwRQ">spears</a> and arrows), fertility and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult" title="Cult" id="mwRg">cult</a> symbols, coins, various treasures and animal statuettes (often dogs, oxen and in later periods horses) were common offerings in antiquity.</p>

<p id="mwRw">The votive offerings were sacrificed and buried or more commonly cast into bodies of water or peat <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bog" title="Bog" id="mwSA">bogs</a>, whence they could not possibly have been recovered. In certain cases entire ships have been sacrificed, as in the Danish bog <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nydam_Mose" title="Nydam Mose" id="mwSQ">Nydam Mose</a>. Often all the objects in a ritual hoard are broken, possibly 'killing' the objects to put them even further beyond utilitarian use before deposition. The purposeful discarding of valuable items such as swords and spearheads is thought to have had ritual overtones. The items have since been discovered in rivers, lakes and present or former <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland" title="Wetland" id="mwSg">wetlands</a> by construction workers, peat diggers, metal-detectorists, members of the public and archaeologists.</p>

<p id="mwSw">A saying by <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogenes_of_Sinope" title="Diogenes of Sinope" class="mw-redirect" id="mwTA">Diogenes of Sinope</a> as quoted by <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogenes_Laërtius" title="Diogenes Laërtius" class="mw-redirect" id="mwTQ">Diogenes Laërtius</a>, indicates the high level of votive offering in Ancient Greece:<sup about="#mwt7" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-2" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-2"}}'><a href="#cite_note-2" id="mwTg"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwTw"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwUA">[</span>2<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwUQ">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

'There would have been far more, if those who were not saved had set up offerings.'</p></blockquote>

<p id="mwVA">The <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasuries_at_Olympia" title="Treasuries at Olympia" id="mwVQ">Treasuries at Olympia</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delphi#Treasuries" title="Delphi" id="mwVg">Delphi</a> (including the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athenian_Treasury" title="Athenian Treasury" id="mwVw">Athenian Treasury</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siphnian_Treasury" title="Siphnian Treasury" id="mwWA">Siphnian Treasury</a>) were buildings by the various Greek city-states to hold their own votive offerings in money and precious metal. The sites also contained large quantities of votive sculptures, although these were clearly intended to glorify each city in view of its rivals as well as to give thanks to the gods. Votive offerings were also used as atonement for sins committed against a god or goddess. The offerings were in certain cases created by a separate person due to the gifter having an injury or other circumstances, which was allowed.<sup about="#mwt12" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-3" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-3"}}'><a href="#cite_note-3" id="mwWQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwWg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwWw">[</span>3<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwXA">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mwXQ">Some Greek offerings, such as bronze tripods at <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delphi" title="Delphi" id="mwXg">Delphi</a>, were apparently displayed for a period and then buried in groups. At <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympia,_Greece" title="Olympia, Greece" id="mwXw">Olympia</a> many small figurines, mostly of animals, were thrown onto the huge pile of ashes from <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_sacrifice" title="Animal sacrifice" id="mwYA">animal sacrifices</a> at the altar outside the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Zeus,_Olympia" title="Temple of Zeus, Olympia" id="mwYQ">Temple of Zeus</a>. Much knowledge of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_art" title="Ancient Greek art" id="mwYg">ancient Greek art</a> in base metal comes from these and other excavated deposits of offerings. Arms and armour, especially helmets, were also given after a victory.</p>

<p id="mwYw">In <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica" title="Mesoamerica" id="mwZA">Mesoamerica</a>, votive deposits have been recovered from the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olmec" title="Olmec" class="mw-redirect" id="mwZQ">Olmec</a> site of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Manati" title="El Manati" class="mw-redirect" id="mwZg">El Manati</a> (dated to 1600–1200 BC) and the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_civilization" title="Maya civilization" id="mwZw">Maya</a> <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Cenote" title="Sacred Cenote" id="mwaA">Sacred Cenote</a> at <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chichen_Itza" title="Chichen Itza" id="mwaQ">Chichen Itza</a> (850–1550 AD).</p>

<p id="mwag">Archaeologists have recovered some votive offerings in ancient Sparta from the 5th century BC. These votive offerings give evidence to the presence of literacy in Spartan culture. Placing greater emphasis on inscriptions which seem to have been made by the individual making the offering, archaeologists can interpret that, of the early dedicators, there were very few in number and that most, if not all, were from the upper classes. One piece of pottery was found that may have had measurement signs on it. This would indicate an everyday literacy among the Spartans if this is true. Unfortunately, scholars have not recovered any other piece of pottery with a similar inscription to support that single find.</p>

<p id="mwaw">The 13 Ancient <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Votive_Stones_of_Pesaro" title="Votive Stones of Pesaro" class="mw-redirect" id="mwbA">Votive Stones of Pesaro</a> were unearthed in 1737 on a local <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesaro" title="Pesaro" id="mwbQ">Pesaro</a> farm in the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pesaro_e_Urbino" title="Province of Pesaro e Urbino" class="mw-redirect" id="mwbg">Province of Pesaro e Urbino</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Italy" title="Roman Italy" id="mwbw">Italy</a>, and date to pre-Etruscan times. They are inscribed with the names of various <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_mythology" title="Roman mythology" id="mwcA">Roman gods</a> such as <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo" title="Apollo" id="mwcQ">APOLLO</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mater_Matuta" title="Mater Matuta" id="mwcg">MAT[ER]-MATVTA</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salus" title="Salus" id="mwcw">SALVS</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fides_(deity)" title="Fides (deity)" id="mwdA">FIDE</a>, and IVNONII (<a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juno_(mythology)" title="Juno (mythology)" id="mwdQ">Juno</a>).<sup about="#mwt15" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-4" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-4"}}'><a href="#cite_note-4" id="mwdg"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwdw"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mweA">[</span>4<span class="cite-bracket" id="mweQ">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<section data-mw-section-id="2" id="mweg" aria-labelledby="Curse_tablets"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Curse_tablets">Curse tablets</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Votive_offering&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Curse tablets"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwew">A <b id="mwfA">curse tablet</b> or <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defixio_(curse_tablet)" title="Defixio (curse tablet)" class="mw-redirect" id="mwfQ">defixio</a> is a small sheet of tin or lead on which a message wishing misfortune upon someone else was inscribed. Usually found rolled up and deliberately deposited, there are five main reasons for dedicating a curse tablet: Litigation, Competition, Trade, Erotic Ambition, or Theft.<sup about="#mwt18" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-5" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-5"}}'><a href="#cite_note-5" id="mwfg"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwfw"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwgA">[</span>5<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwgQ">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mwgg">Of those in Britain the vast majority relate to theft. The two largest concentrations are from the sacred springs at <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath,_Somerset" title="Bath, Somerset" id="mwgw">Aquae Sulis</a>, where 130 examples are recorded, and at Uley, where over 140 examples are visible.
The use of the curse-tablet in seeking restoration of stolen property is strong evidence of invoking divine power through a non-traditional religious ceremony, often involving some form of water-deposition. The usual form of divine invocation was through prayer, sacrifice and altar dedication<sup about="#mwt21" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-6" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-6"}}'><a href="#cite_note-6" id="mwhA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwhQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwhg">[</span>6<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwhw">]</span></span></a></sup> so access to this information provides useful insights into Roman provincial culture.</p>

</section><section data-mw-section-id="3" id="mwiA" aria-labelledby="Threats"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Threats">Threats</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Votive_offering&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Threats"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwiQ">Many unrecovered ancient votive offerings are threatened in today's world, especially those submerged in wetlands or other bodies of water. Wetlands and other aquatic sites often protect and preserve materials for thousands of years, because of their natural occurring <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypoxia_(environmental)" title="Hypoxia (environmental)" id="mwig">anaerobic</a> environments. However, many seabeds have been disturbed, rivers and streams have been stretched out or re-routed in the landscape, and many wetlands have been fully or partially drained or landfilled for various reasons in the last 100–200 years. Therefore, many remaining objects are in danger of oxidation and eventual rapid deterioration. The leading causes of the disturbances are <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dredging" title="Dredging" id="mwiw">dredging</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottom_trawling" title="Bottom trawling" id="mwjA">bottom trawling</a> fishing boats, agricultural activities, peat cutting, groundwater extraction by water wells and establishments of larger infrastructural facilities like expressways, water treatment plants, and in some instances, large-scale nature re-establishment projects.<sup about="#mwt23" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-7" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-7"}}'><a href="#cite_note-7" id="mwjQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwjg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwjw">[</span>7<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwkA">]</span></span></a></sup><sup about="#mwt26" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-8" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-8"}}'><a href="#cite_note-8" id="mwkQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwkg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwkw">[</span>8<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwlA">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

</section></section><section data-mw-section-id="4" id="mwlQ" aria-labelledby="Judaism"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Judaism">Judaism</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Votive_offering&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Judaism"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwlg">The <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torah" title="Torah" id="mwlw">Torah</a> makes provision for "free-will offerings" which may be made by any individual. These are different from votive offerings which are linked to a vow. cf Leviticus 22.23 where the Hebrew root letters for a freewill offering are נדב (nadab), but for a votive offering are נדר (nadar). In this verse a clear differentiation is made between the two. See Strongs numbers H5068 where the Hitpael is to volunteer, or make a free will offering and H5087 where the Qal is to vow a vow.<link rel="mw:PageProp/Category" href="./Category:All_articles_that_may_contain_original_research" about="#mwt27" typeof="mw:Transclusion" id="mwmA" data-mw='{"parts":[{"template":{"target":{"wt":"OR","href":"./Template:OR"},"params":{"date":{"wt":"January 2026"}},"i":0}}]}'/><link rel="mw:PageProp/Category" href="./Category:Articles_that_may_contain_original_research_from_January_2026" about="#mwt27"/><sup class="noprint Inline-Template " style="white-space:nowrap;" about="#mwt27" id="mwmQ"><span typeof="mw:Entity">[</span><i><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:No_original_research" title="Wikipedia:No original research"><span title="The material near this tag possibly contains original research. (January 2026)">original research?</span></a></i><span typeof="mw:Entity">]</span></sup></p>

<p id="mwmg">When <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon" title="Solomon" id="mwmw">Solomon</a> built the first temple he provided a number of furnishings above and beyond what had been commanded to <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses" title="Moses" id="mwnA">Moses</a> on <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Sinai" title="Mount Sinai" id="mwnQ">Mount Sinai</a> (see <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Solomon" title="Temple of Solomon" class="mw-redirect" id="mwng">Temple of Solomon</a>).</p>

<p id="mwnw"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_Torah" title="Oral Torah" id="mwoA">Oral tradition</a> in <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbinic_Judaism" title="Rabbinic Judaism" id="mwoQ">Rabbinic Judaism</a> also speaks of a huge golden grape vine <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_artifact" title="Cultural artifact" id="mwog">artifact</a> outside of the holy site of the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_in_Jerusalem" title="Temple in Jerusalem" id="mwow">Temple in Jerusalem</a> before its destruction by the Romans.<sup about="#mwt28" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-9" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-9"}}'><a href="#cite_note-9" id="mwpA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwpQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwpg">[</span>9<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwpw">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mwqw">The tradition of votive offerings has been carried into Christianity in both the East and the West. The particular type of the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Votive_crown" title="Votive crown" id="mwrA">votive crown</a>, originally Byzantine, was also adopted in the West.</p>

<p id="mwww">According to <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Tradition" title="Sacred Tradition" class="mw-redirect" id="mwxA">Sacred Tradition</a>, after <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_the_Great" title="Constantine the Great" id="mwxQ">Constantine the Great</a>'s conversion and subsequent victory at the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Milvian_Bridge" title="Battle of the Milvian Bridge" id="mwxg">Battle of the Milvian Bridge</a>, he donated one of the crosses he carried in battle to the Church. This cross is reputed to be preserved on <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Athos" title="Mount Athos" id="mwxw">Mount Athos</a>.</p>

<p id="mwyA">One of the most famous <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church" id="mwyQ">Orthodox</a> votive offerings is that by <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint" title="Saint" id="mwyg">Saint</a> <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_of_Damascus" title="John of Damascus" id="mwyw">John of Damascus</a>. According to <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography" id="mwzA">tradition</a>, while he was serving as <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vizier" title="Vizier" id="mwzQ">Vizier</a> to <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliph" title="Caliph" class="mw-redirect" id="mwzg">Caliph</a> <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Walid_I" title="Al-Walid I" id="mwzw">Al-Walid I</a>, he was falsely accused of treachery and his hand was cut off. Upon praying in front of an <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icon" title="Icon" id="mw0A">icon</a> of the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theotokos" title="Theotokos" id="mw0Q">Theotokos</a> his hand was <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle" id="mw0g">miraculously</a> restored. In thanksgiving, he had a silver replica of his hand fashioned and attached it to the icon (<i id="mw0w">see image at right</i>). This icon, now called "<a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojeručica" title="Trojeručica" id="mw1A">Trojeručica</a>" (The Three-handed) is preserved at <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilandar" title="Hilandar" id="mw1Q">Hilandar</a> Monastery on <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Athos" title="Mount Athos" id="mw1g">Mount Athos</a>.</p>

<p id="mw1w">Orthodox Christians continue to make votive offerings to this day, often in the form of <i id="mw2A"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tama_(votive)" title="Tama (votive)" id="mw2Q">tamata</a></i>, metal plaques symbolizing the subject of their <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayer" title="Prayer" id="mw2g">prayers</a>. Other offerings include <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candle" title="Candle" id="mw2w">candles</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosphora" title="Prosphora" id="mw3A">prosphora</a>, wine, oil, or <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incense" title="Incense" id="mw3Q">incense</a>. In addition, many will leave something of personal value, such as jewelry, a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pectoral_cross" title="Pectoral cross" id="mw3g">pectoral cross</a> or <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_decoration" title="Military decoration" class="mw-redirect" id="mw3w">military decoration</a> as a sign of devotion.</p>

<section data-mw-section-id="8" id="mw4Q" aria-labelledby="Roman_Catholicism"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading4"><h4 id="Roman_Catholicism">Roman Catholicism</h4><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Votive_offering&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Roman Catholicism"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb" id="mw4g"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Colina_de_las_Cruces,_Lituania,_2012-08-09,_DD_12.JPG" class="mw-file-description" id="mw4w"><img resource="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Colina_de_las_Cruces,_Lituania,_2012-08-09,_DD_12.JPG" src="//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/Colina_de_las_Cruces%2C_Lituania%2C_2012-08-09%2C_DD_12.JPG/500px-Colina_de_las_Cruces%2C_Lituania%2C_2012-08-09%2C_DD_12.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&amp;utm_campaign=parser&amp;utm_content=thumbnail" decoding="async" loading="lazy" srcset="//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/Colina_de_las_Cruces%2C_Lituania%2C_2012-08-09%2C_DD_12.JPG/960px-Colina_de_las_Cruces%2C_Lituania%2C_2012-08-09%2C_DD_12.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&amp;utm_campaign=parser&amp;utm_content=thumbnail 2x" data-file-width="5386" data-file-height="2389" data-file-type="bitmap" height="177" width="400" class="mw-file-element mw-file-upright" style="--mw-file-upright: 1.6" id="mw5A"/></a><figcaption id="mw5Q"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_of_Crosses" title="Hill of Crosses" id="mw5g">Hill of Crosses</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania" title="Lithuania" id="mw5w">Lithuania</a></figcaption></figure>
<p id="mw6A">In the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" title="Roman Catholic Church" class="mw-redirect" id="mw6Q">Roman Catholic Church</a>, offerings were made either to fulfill a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vow" title="Vow" id="mw6g">vow</a> made to God for deliverance, or a thing left to a Church in gratitude for some favor that was granted. Today, votives can be lit <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Votive_candle" title="Votive candle" id="mw6w">votive candles</a>, offered flowers, statues, vestments and monetary donations. Traditional special forms of votive offering <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_voto" title="Ex voto" class="mw-redirect" id="mw7A">ex votos</a> include small silver models of the afflicted part of the body, inscribed stone tablets, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_art" title="Folk art" id="mw7Q">folk art</a> <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retablo" title="Retablo" id="mw7g">paintings of an incident of danger</a> such as the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Votive_paintings_of_Mexico" title="Votive paintings of Mexico" id="mw7w">votive paintings of Mexico</a> and model ships donated by sailors who have survived a dangerous voyage. In the Spanish-speaking world a <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milagro_(votive)" title="Milagro (votive)" id="mw8A">milagro</a> is a small metal offering, equivalent to the Orthodox <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamata" title="Tamata" class="mw-redirect" id="mw8Q">tamata</a>.</p>

<p id="mw8g">Many Catholic churches still have areas where such offerings are displayed. <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre-Dame-des-Victoires,_Paris" title="Notre-Dame-des-Victoires, Paris" id="mw8w">Notre-Dame-des-Victoires, Paris</a>, displays over 10,000, with a military specialization and including many military decorations given by their recipients. The <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Votive_Church,_Vienna" title="Votive Church, Vienna" class="mw-redirect" id="mw9A">Votive Church, Vienna</a> is a late example of many churches which are themselves votive offerings, in this case built to give thanks for a narrow escape from assassination by Emperor <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_I_of_Austria" title="Franz Joseph I of Austria" class="mw-redirect" id="mw9Q">Franz Joseph I of Austria</a> in 1853.</p>

<p id="mw9g">Medieval examples include:</p>

<ul id="mw9w"><li id="mw-A">Several <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Votive_crown" title="Votive crown" id="mw-Q">votive crowns</a>, such as those in the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_of_Guarrazar" title="Treasure of Guarrazar" id="mw-g">Treasure of Guarrazar</a></li>
<li id="mw-w">Probably the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Crown_of_Lombardy" title="Iron Crown of Lombardy" class="mw-redirect" id="mw_A">Iron Crown of Lombardy</a></li>
<li id="mw_Q"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_III_of_England" title="Henry III of England" id="mw_g">Henry III of England</a> had a golden statue of his queen made and placed on the shrine of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_the_Confessor" title="Edward the Confessor" id="mw_w">St. Edward</a> at Westminster</li>
<li id="mwAQA">A falcon in wax at the shrine of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Wulstan" title="Saint Wulstan" class="mw-redirect" id="mwAQE">Saint Wulstan</a> by <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_I_of_England" title="Edward I of England" class="mw-redirect" id="mwAQI">Edward I</a></li>
<li id="mwAQM">A diamond and a ruby, adorning the tomb of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Becket" title="Thomas Becket" id="mwAQQ">St. Thomas Becket</a> at <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury_Cathedral" title="Canterbury Cathedral" id="mwAQU">Canterbury</a></li>
<li id="mwAQY">Numerous crutches, left in the grotto at <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lourdes" title="Lourdes" id="mwAQc">Lourdes</a></li>
<li id="mwAQg">The song "O Wilhelme, pastor bone" composed by <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Taverner" title="John Taverner" id="mwAQk">John Taverner</a> is a Votive Antiphon dedicated to <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Wolsey" title="Cardinal Wolsey" class="mw-redirect" id="mwAQo">Cardinal Wolsey</a></li></ul>

<p id="mwAQs">Especially in the Latin world, there is a tradition of votive paintings, typically depicting a dangerous incident which the offeror survived. The <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Votive_paintings_of_Mexico" title="Votive paintings of Mexico" id="mwAQw">votive paintings of Mexico</a> are paralleled in other countries. In Italy, where more than 15,000 ex-voto paintings are thought to survive from before 1600, these began to appear in the 1490s, probably modelled on the small <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predella" title="Predella" id="mwAQ0">predella</a> panels below <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altarpiece" title="Altarpiece" id="mwAQ4">altarpieces</a>.<sup about="#mwt35" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-10" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-10"}}'><a href="#cite_note-10" id="mwAQ8"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwARA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwARE">[</span>10<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwARI">]</span></span></a></sup> These are a form of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_art" title="Folk art" id="mwARM">folk art</a>, typically painted on tin plates salvaged from packaging. Other examples may be large and grand paintings, such as <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titian" title="Titian" id="mwARQ">Titian</a>'s <i id="mwARU"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacopo_Pesaro_being_presented_by_Pope_Alexander_VI_to_Saint_Peter" title="Jacopo Pesaro being presented by Pope Alexander VI to Saint Peter" id="mwARY">Jacopo Pesaro being presented by Pope Alexander VI to Saint Peter</a></i>, given in thanks for a naval victory.</p>

<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb" id="mwASA"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Stupika_and_artifacts_Bali_8th_century.jpg" class="mw-file-description" id="mwASE"><img resource="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Stupika_and_artifacts_Bali_8th_century.jpg" src="//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Stupika_and_artifacts_Bali_8th_century.jpg/250px-Stupika_and_artifacts_Bali_8th_century.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&amp;utm_campaign=parser&amp;utm_content=thumbnail" decoding="async" loading="lazy" srcset="//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Stupika_and_artifacts_Bali_8th_century.jpg/500px-Stupika_and_artifacts_Bali_8th_century.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&amp;utm_campaign=parser&amp;utm_content=thumbnail 2x" data-file-width="4190" data-file-height="2699" data-file-type="bitmap" height="161" width="250" class="mw-file-element" id="mwASI"/></a><figcaption id="mwASM"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stupika" title="Stupika" id="mwASQ">Stupika</a> which contains Buddhist votive tablets, 8th-century <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bali_Kingdom" title="Bali Kingdom" id="mwASU">Bali</a></figcaption></figure>
<p id="mwASY">In <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism" title="Buddhism" id="mwASc">Buddhism</a>, votive offerings usually take the form of a small clay or <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terracotta" title="Terracotta" id="mwASg">terracotta</a> tablet bearing Buddhist images, usually <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddharupa" title="Buddharupa" class="mw-redirect" id="mwASk">Buddharupa</a> and contain text. These tablets are left in sacred Buddhist sites by devotees as an offering during their pilgrimage. An example are Buddhist Tibetan votive tablets made for a pilgrimage to <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhgaya" title="Bodhgaya" class="mw-redirect" id="mwASo">Bodhgaya</a>. Votive tablets served both as meritorious offerings and as souvenirs. Most were made of clay, while a more rare and expensive tablet was made from metal.<sup about="#mwt41" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-12" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-12"}}'><a href="#cite_note-12" id="mwASs"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwASw"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwAS0">[</span>12<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwAS4">]</span></span></a></sup> Historically, votive tablets can be found in Asian Buddhist lands, from Japan,<sup about="#mwt44" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-13" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-13"}}'><a href="#cite_note-13" id="mwAS8"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwATA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwATE">[</span>13<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwATI">]</span></span></a></sup> India, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, Cambodia, China, Indonesia and Malaysia. In Thailand, votive tablets are known as <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_Buddha_amulet" title="Thai Buddha amulet" id="mwATM">Thai Buddha amulets</a>, a kind of Thai Buddhist blessed item used to raise temple funds by producing the amulets.</p>

<p id="mwATw"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayagapata" title="Ayagapata" id="mwAT0">Ayagapata</a> is a type of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Votive" title="Votive" class="mw-redirect" id="mwAT4">votive</a> slab associated with worship in <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism" title="Jainism" id="mwAT8">Jainism</a>. Numerous such stone tablets were discovered during excavations at ancient Jain sites like <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankali_Tila" title="Kankali Tila" id="mwAUA">Kankali Tila</a> near <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathura" title="Mathura" id="mwAUE">Mathura</a> in India. Some of them date back to the 1st century. These slabs are decorated with objects and designs central to Jain worship such as the <i id="mwAUI"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stupa" title="Stupa" id="mwAUM">stupa</a></i>, <i id="mwAUQ"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmacakra" title="Dharmacakra" class="mw-redirect" id="mwAUU">dharmacakra</a></i> and <i id="mwAUY"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triratna" title="Triratna" class="mw-redirect" id="mwAUc">triratna</a></i>.<sup about="#mwt47" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-Brit_14-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":"Brit"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-Brit-14"}}'><a href="#cite_note-Brit-14" id="mwAUg"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwAUk"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwAUo">[</span>14<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwAUs">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mwAUw">A large number of <i id="mwAU0">ayagapata</i> (tablet of homage) votive tablets for offerings and the worship of <i id="mwAU4">tirthankara</i> were found at <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathura" title="Mathura" id="mwAU8">Mathura</a>.<sup about="#mwt49" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-15" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-15"}}'><a href="#cite_note-15" id="mwAVA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwAVE"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwAVI">[</span>15<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwAVM">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mwAVQ">These stone tablets bear a resemblance to the earlier <i id="mwAVU">Shilapatas</i>, stone tablets that were placed under trees to worship <i id="mwAVY"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaksha" title="Yaksha" id="mwAVc">Yakshas</a></i>. However, this was done by indigenous folk communities before <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism" title="Jainism" id="mwAVg">Jainism</a> originated, suggesting that both have commonalities in rituals.<sup about="#mwt50" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-Brit_14-1" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":"Brit"}}'><a href="#cite_note-Brit-14" id="mwAVk"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwAVo"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwAVs">[</span>14<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwAVw">]</span></span></a></sup> A scholar on Jain art wrote about an <i id="mwAV0">Ayagapata</i> discovered around Kankali Tila: "The technical name of such a tablet was <i id="mwAV4">Ayagapata</i> meaning homage panel."<sup about="#mwt53" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-16" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-16"}}'><a href="#cite_note-16" id="mwAV8"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwAWA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwAWE">[</span>16<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwAWI">]</span></span></a></sup></p>
