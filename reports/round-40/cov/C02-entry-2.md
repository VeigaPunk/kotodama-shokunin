## 2. Founder effects and migration trajectories

- **The registered core:** an initial population can disproportionately shape the feature pool later learners encounter; founding date alone cannot predict dominance (`migration-founder-effects.md:13,:25` — Mufwene 1996). The mechanics reading this entry adds: the founding mix fixes the **pool of availability** — a feature absent from it cannot be retained, only re-borrowed, re-introduced, or innovated. A retention claim on a map therefore requires the retained feature to be a member of that community's founder pool (or of a later contact input).
- **Selection vs innovation:** a homeland form absent from today's standard may have been widespread at departure; compare the colony against its source dialects **at departure**, not against a later metropolitan standard (`migration-founder-effects.md:49-50`). The pool is a departure-era sample, not the modern homeland.
- **Founder ≠ earliest, ruling, or ancestral:** a linguistic founder population need not coincide with a land claim, a dynasty, or a genetic cluster (`migration-founder-effects.md:14`). On a map the founder mix is a **speech-network fact** — who actually transmitted daily speech (:63) — never a charter fact.
- **Transitivity over trajectories:** second arrivals meet an already-narrowed pool; sustained new immigration re-broadens or redistributes it; sustained homeland contact can re-supply features the first mix lacked (:44). Transmission needs a story — who plays, learns, and works together (:43). "First arrivals always win" is a registered invalid shortcut (:44); so is "small population means fast sound change" (:41).
- **The determinism debate, at registered granularity:** Trudgill (2004) argues new-dialect formation is deterministic given the input mixture and universal acquisition biases — the strong reading behind "the inevitability of colonial Englishes". The corpus's own caution governs: a strong theoretical position, not a universal law (`migration-founder-effects.md:32`). Working rule: use determinism as a **prior** for what an unmotivated map will look like, never as a generator of one.
- **What §2 forbids on a map:** retention without pool membership (F-DC-02's pool leg); founder dominance without a transmission story; founder mix read off charter, dynasty, or ancestry.

## 3. Koineization as a process model

- **Definition frame, at registered granularity:** contact among mutually intelligible varieties → mixing, leveling, and the possible emergence of a shared variety (Siegel, via `urban-rural-register-ecology.md:26`). A koine is formation through variety contact — distinct from a lingua franca (use across groups, possibly a pre-existing language, `urban-rural-register-ecology.md:34`) and from pidgins/creoles (`language-contact.md:89-90`; `dialect-faction-map-workflow.md:82`). Those boundaries are owned; they are restated here as one clause each, not developed.
- **The five processes, with the map prediction each licenses:**

| Process | What it does | What a consistent map shows | Limit (registered) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mixing | input varieties pool variants | the koine's inventory is a subset of the settler mixes | demography alone does not determine outcomes (`dialect-faction-map-workflow.md:86`) |
| Leveling | minority variants die | not all minority features vanish — salience sustains some (:88) | not uniform eradication |
| Simplification | specific regularization | feature-specific (a merger, a paradigm loss), declared as such | never universal grammatical loss (:89) |
| Reallocation | survivors split the work | two input variants coexist with **different distributions** — stylistic, register, semantic (:90) | coexistence is not failed koineization |
| Focusing | shared norms stabilize | partial, feature-specific, contested (:91) | stability is never total |

- **The deadline reconciliation rule (this entry's house method):** `sociolinguistics-registers.md:64` states a new variety "emerges in 1–2 generations"; `dialect-faction-map-workflow.md:94` records that Milton Keynes showed "substantial but incomplete focusing in the child generation" and forbids exporting a fixed one/two-generation deadline, and :93 that the sequence is "not a mandatory ordered life cycle". Reconciliation: the 1–2 generations figure is a compressed reading of the MK observation, and the two files are consistent once it is read as an **attested case**, not a law. Rule for maps: a built koine states its **focusing state** (unfocused / partially focused / focused, per feature); it never states a deadline.
- **Standardization ≠ koineization** (`dialect-faction-map-workflow.md:96`): an official written norm can be imposed with no new spoken variety; a spoken koine can arise with no academy. Map consequence: the chancery standard and the street koine are separate layers with separate formation dates.
- **What §3 forbids:** a koine identical to the largest settler group's dialect with no reallocation trace (F-DC-05); a focusing deadline stated as law (F-DC-04); koine/lingua-franca/pidgin substitution (owned boundaries above).

## 4. Focal, relic, and transition areas — the concept mechanics

- **Definitions (registered):** focal area = an innovation appears early or strongly at a node and along connected routes; relic area = retention of a specified earlier form while another form spreads elsewhere; transition area = alternation or crossed distributions; discontinuous pocket = similar forms in separated settlements (`dialect-faction-map-workflow.md:69-72`). The concepts are feature- and era-relative, not cultural personality types (:75-77) — owned, and the reason whole-area labels are meaningless.
- **The production mechanics this entry owns:** focal areas arise where **participation in innovation networks** is dense and outward-facing — loose uniplex networks adopt standards, urban innovators are network-loose (`sociolinguistics-registers.md:65`, Milroy); relic areas retain where **dense multiplex networks close** over local forms, or where maintenance is deliberate (`dialect-faction-map-workflow.md:70`). The generalization: every area is simultaneously focal for some features and relic for others, because the same diffusion process places areas differently per feature and per network — an isolated valley can retain one ending and innovate another (`dialect-faction-map-workflow.md:76`).
- **Status-establishment rules as concepts:** focal status is established only by a **dated diffusion hypothesis along a named network**; relic status only by **retention against a dated comparator** (`dialect-faction-map-workflow.md:69-70`, checklist :182). This entry adds the concept rule those procedures presuppose: "focal" and "relic" name a relation between **a feature, a date, and a network** — never a place's essence; and a relic claim joins §2: the retained feature must be a member of the area's founder pool or later contact input.
- **What §4 forbids on a map:** a whole-area label ("the ancient dialect", "the pure speech" — the discredited decay reading, `sociolinguistics-registers.md:148`); a focal city with no diffusion path; a relic feature absent from the area's pool; every feature's focal city being the same node (real diffusion produces cross-cutting isoglosses, `dialect-faction-map-workflow.md:55`).

## 5. Anti-language formation — conditions, renewal, second life

- **The registered mechanism set (survey, not restated):** relexicalization, overlexicalization, metaphor/deformation, resocialization, turnover (`linguistic-anthropology.md:117-123`); the parent grammar is preserved (`register-lexicon-ceilings.md:182`); the category's status is contested (`register-lexicon-ceilings.md:238-240`; `dialect-faction-map-workflow.md:122`). §5.3's decision table (`linguistic-anthropology.md:129-137`) stays the which-type chooser; this entry owns the formation conditions and lifecycle the survey compresses.
- **Formation conditions (the measured gap):** an anti-language presupposes an **anti-society** — a defined excluded group whose shared concerns are high-stakes (`linguistic-anthropology.md:117` names the construct in passing; no entry owns it). The operative diagnostic is **overlexicalization zoning**: lexeme density clusters exactly on the anti-society's preoccupations — many terms for what the group lives by, none for what it does not (`linguistic-anthropology.md:120` gives the cant example). On a map: the zones are checkable **before** the society is named — a "cant" spread thin across all semantic fields contradicts the formation mechanic (F-DC-03), and the zones themselves propose what the stakes were.
- **Renewal dynamics:** turnover is registered (`linguistic-anthropology.md:123` — argot leaks and decays as it becomes fashionable; constant renewal). The formation reading: renewal pressure is **endogenous** — leakage to the parent society drives replacement, so an argot with a stable lexicon across generations is inconsistent unless it is sealed, and sealing needs the registered threat model (`dialect-faction-map-workflow.md:123` — who must not understand, how they learn, what happens after disclosure).
- **Second life (the measured absence — 0 files):** elements of an anti-language can migrate into parent-society style — the fashionable endpoint of the registered leakage dynamic. **(to-verify)** the specific analysis of anti-language "provinces" surviving as the parent society's high style is attributed to Halliday (1976) at concept level only: no in-corpus entry records a full-text reading of *American Anthropologist* 78(3), so the attribution and its cases are unverified; the design trajectory stands on the corpus's own leakage (:123) and resocialization (:122) dynamics and is usable as a lifecycle state regardless. Map rule: a second-life claim never reads backwards as proof the anti-society persisted (DR-DC-07).
- **Lifetime:** an anti-language dies with its institution, and fossil argots are archaeology surface (`sociolinguistics-registers.md:84`) — owned; §5 adds only that the death is a lifecycle state §6's row 7 tests.
