## 2. The sacred specialization of diglossia

`sociolinguistics-registers.md` §3 supplies the general machinery (H learned in school, used for elevated domains; Fishman's widening: H and L need not be the same language). The sacred case is a specialization with its own parameter settings, and the settings are where the design work lives:

| Parameter | General diglossia | The sacred setting | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Domain set | H spreads across sermon, news, protocol | H concentrates in ritual events; non-ritual H use is a concession, not a default | every non-ritual H use is legible as a political act (a claim, a reform, a usurpation — §8) |
| Acquisition | school | school **plus embodied drill**; usually no native speakers by construction | competence stratifies by access to teaching, not by talent (§3) |
| Codification | grammar + literature | a closed canonical corpus plus a correction institution | a variant is not a typo; it is a recension claim (Meyendorff's case, §8) |
| Authorization | carried by the institution's language generally | utterance validity is separable from the speaker's fluency | the empty office becomes possible (§3, row 6) |
| H/L relation | usually one language, two varieties | a free design choice (Fishman) | a foreign or dead H imports interpreters as a power class (`sociolinguistics-registers.md:74`, Waquet) |
| Stability | stable while institutions hold | the most stable H allocation on offer — H has no native speakers to innovate | `language-shift-obsolescence-revival.md:110`: H "is nobody's mother tongue and therefore has no transmission to lose" |

The load-bearing move is the last row plus row four: sacred diglossia is stable *not because speakers preserve it* but because the two ordinary engines of change — native acquisition and daily instrumental use — are absent by design. What can still move it is institutional: a reform, a translation authorization, a recension dispute (§8). Write the institutions first; the register's history is their history.

## 3. The six competences

"Knows the sacred language" is the single most expensive simplification in this area — the synthesis counterpart already warns against the single flag (`religion-language-co-evolution.md:76`). This entry builds the warning into a model. Six competences, separately acquirable, separately losable:

| # | Competence | What it is | Acquisition mode | Typical holders |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hearing recognition | knows the code when heard; can tell H from L, revered from ordinary | exposure | the whole congregation |
| 2 | Recitation accuracy | reproduces canonical strings with the recitation norm (§6) | embodied drill, teacher to body (Larios) | cantors, novices, the trained |
| 3 | Liturgical literacy | reads the canonical text in the canonical script | schooling | scribes, lectors |
| 4 | Comprehension | parses and glosses; can say what the string asserts | taught exegesis | scholars, the school-trained |
| 5 | Composition | productive grammar: can generate new, well-formed H | long apprenticeship inside a living composing tradition | rare specialists |
| 6 | Authorization | the office's license that makes the utterance count | the institution, not a skill | officeholders |

Two rows do the myth-breaking. **Row 2 without row 4 is the normal adult state** in many arrangements: efficacy attaches to the wording, not the paraphrase (Tambiah; Staal — `linguistic-anthropology.md:99`), so recitation neither needs nor produces comprehension, and a fluent reciter may require a translator for new prose (the synthesis counterpart's §3 table row, `religion-language-co-evolution.md:37`, is this row in its own vocabulary). **Row 6 with no row at all** is the empty office: an authorization that survives the death of every linguistic competence behind it. It is undramatic when it works and is the plot when it does not — a scripturally valid utterance from an officeholder who cannot parse a word of it.

Decay order (§8 gives the ladder): 5 dies first (a closed canon removes the compositional need), then 4, then 3, then 2, then 1. Row 6 is not on the decay curve — it decays only with the office.

## 4. Liturgical fossilization: mechanics per layer

`historical-linguistics.md:237` owns the fossil's *use* ("frozen registers are time capsules... generates in-world philology, schisms over recitation, plot misreadings") and `:247` its stratum row; the synthesis counterpart owns the *checklist* of layers (`religion-language-co-evolution.md:47-56`). This entry states the **mechanics** — for each layer, what freezes, by what mechanism, and what keeps moving:

| Layer | What freezes | Mechanism (why this layer resists drift) | What keeps moving |
|---|---|---|---|
| Text | word sequence, hymn order, inherited morphology | closed canon + correction institutions; composition dead (§3 row 5) so no innovation *enters* | commentary, selection, editorial correction |
| Sound | the recitation norm (§6) | motor drill, teacher to body (Larios); performance invariance (Rappaport, via `ritual-liminality.md:112`) | the regional accent underneath the norm |
| Meaning | the canonical gloss | efficacy attaches to the wording, not the paraphrase (Tambiah; Staal) — paraphrase is a different act, so ordinary semantic drift never touches the recited string | everyday senses outside the rite |
| Performance | who speaks which passage, in what sequence | rubrics (the synthesis counterpart's performance layer, `religion-language-co-evolution.md:55`) | tempo, amplification, audience roles |

Three selection pressures, not entropy, do the freezing: **(a) no native acquisition** — children do not innovate in a language nobody learns at home; **(b) a closed canon** — the compositional channel that would carry innovation is dead by design; **(c) an invariance doctrine** — the form's unalterability is the authority's warrant (Rappaport via `ritual-liminality.md:112`). A liturgy lacking all three drifts like any other variety. And the counterweight, already recorded in the synthesis counterpart and load-bearing here: **"textual stability requires work"** (`religion-language-co-evolution.md:61`) — teachers, copying, correction, rehearsal, funding. "Unchanged since origin" is a claim about maintenance institutions, never about entropy. Distinguish inheritance from learned borrowing while at it: a form reintroduced from the sacred corpus need not show the sound changes its everyday cognates underwent (`:60`; the diachronic reading is `historical-linguistics.md`'s work, not this entry's).

## 5. Event-typed language rules

The synthesis counterpart names the events and their policy questions (`religion-language-co-evolution.md:64-73`); `:14` fixes the rule — "specify the event, not merely the religion". This entry turns that rule into a design instrument: **each ritual event type selects the competences (§3) its efficacy claim requires, and names its violation class**. A violation is only diagnosable relative to the required competence — that is why one "knows the sacred language" flag always fails.

| Event type | Efficacy claim | Required competence | Violation class |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canonical recitation | the wording itself constitutes the act | 2 (recitation) | mis-recitation — a translation is not a repair for it |
| Sermon / instruction | comprehension carries the content | 4 + fluency in L | vernacular intrusion; doctrine drift |
| Private petition | intent suffices | none in H | disfavored free words, if the institution disfavors them |
| Communal hymn / response | participation | memorized refrain (partial 2) | tune/text split; melody carrying words nobody parses |
| Naming / initiation | the name-act binds | 6 + the formula | unauthorized name-act — routed to `naming-systems.md` and the round's E1, not treated here |
| Authorized translation | mediation under authority | 4 + 6 | unauthorized gloss — the fidelity surface is E3's (`translation-protocol-fidelity.md`), coupled not covered |

Design procedure: table every ritual event the culture has against the six competences; each row names the required competence and the violation class; **empty cells are missing institutions** (the house pattern of `sociolinguistics-registers.md` §9, applied here). The table is also the audit grid for scenes: a character's competence vector (which of the six they hold) determines, per event, whether they can officiate, follow, or only be present.

## 6. Recitation norms and chant

- **A recitation norm is a phonological target enforced in performance.** It is the sound layer of §4, and it is a designable object: which contrasts it preserves, which realizations it requires, which deviations are errors and which are regional. Because performance enforces it, it can preserve contrasts the L vernacular has lost — vowel quantity, accent, sandhi. The real anchor case: Vedic accent, sequence and recitation technique as "a cultivated sign of continuity" (`religion-language-co-evolution.md:100`, per Jamison & Brereton 2014), with UNESCO's inscription describing the preservation techniques (usable for the techniques, not for chronology — its error flagged at `:106`).
- **Redundancy can be engineered into the performance itself.** The vikṛti recitations (pada, krama, jaṭā, ghana) re-derive each string from its permutations, making the canon self-checking without writing (`orality-formulaic-language.md:47,134`; Staal 1961; Howard 1986). Design lever: a temple can out-remember an archive; documents burn, recitations are distributed across trained bodies.
- **Chant extends the norm with melody**; it is not a separate competence so much as row 2 under musical constraint. Do not build "chant = gibberish": Staal's syntactic-meaninglessness point is about specific mantra strings and is one account (§10), not a license for incomprehensible ritual everywhere.
- **Ship the norm, not just the words.** The recitation norm is the layer in-world philology will later read (the dating work is `historical-linguistics.md:237`'s surface) and the first thing a reform touches (§8). A handoff that names the H's lexicon but not its recitation norm has shipped half a register.

## 7. Closure devices, and what closure is not

The excluding work is done by ordinary linguistic means, not by secrecy: **archaism** ("archaism equals authority", `linguistic-anthropology.md:97`), **formula density** and **parallelism** (`orality-formulaic-language.md` §5's inventory), a **specialized lexicon**, and the **metalinguistic gate** — parsing requires the taught gloss, so the untaught hear words, not sentences (the synthesis counterpart's esoteric-interpretation row, `religion-language-co-evolution.md:40`: stealing the book does not confer competence). Three boundaries keep this honest:

1. **Difficulty is not secrecy.** The counterpart's caution stands: "mere difficulty is not proof of deliberate secrecy" (`:84`), and closure requires maintenance — leakage is the normal state (`:85`). A sacred register can be fully public and fully closed at once: officiants explain in ordinary speech while reserving procedural knowledge (`:87`).
2. **Liturgical force is not attestation force.** `register-strata-attestation.md` owns the register whose marked speech binds *with a record consequence*; the liturgical register's acts bind cosmologically, without a record leg. Keep them apart or oaths and rites blur into one machine.
3. **Divine-name avoidance is routed out.** It is the name-taboo system (`politeness-taboo-euphemism.md:104`; `naming-systems.md:3`), owned by that entry pair and this round's E1; §5's naming row cites it and stops.

## 8. The register's own decay and revival ladder

## 9. Worked micro-example (NON-CANON)
