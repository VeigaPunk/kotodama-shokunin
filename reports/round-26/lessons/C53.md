## 7. Lessons entry — what this round's C work teaches

1. **"Stale" is two different facts, and the disposition differs.** A reading
   that was *true and then superseded* earns a new row; a reading that was
   *false when written* is a defect. A single staleness label forces a reader to
   "correct" history that was honest. The discriminator is the arrival time of
   the contradicted bytes relative to the reader's own stamp — a fact obtainable
   from `git log`, external to both artifacts.
2. **A checker's green is exactly the columns it reads.** The round-25 index
   passed its own tool at `findings: 0` while carrying two pointers that
   contradicted bytes already on disk. The tool verified rows, pins, formats and
   owners; it never resolved a pointer. The round-25 C35 lesson said this in the
   abstract; round 26 supplies the concrete instance.
3. **A point-in-time finding copied forward becomes a false present-tense
   claim.** The round-12 lesson correctly said "no `validation.json`" of its
   22:57Z listing; the index, written a day later, restated it as a live fact.
   The fix is not to distrust the lesson but to re-measure before re-asserting —
   the same discipline the lane applies to pins, applied to prose.
4. **A frame difference is not drift.** 49 data rows vs 51 pipe-prefixed lines is
   the table's own header and separator. Filing that as arithmetic drift would
   manufacture a defect from a counting convention; naming the frame is the
   correct disposition.
5. **Filing discipline: a filed index is not edited to fix a reading it
   contains.** The round-25 index is committed (`fc13cd2`); its rev-2 block
   says a later edit supersedes it. The corrected state therefore lands as this
   new file, with the old cells quoted and the new readings dated. The reader
   gets both; the history stays intact.
