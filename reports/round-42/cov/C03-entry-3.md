## 2. The six properties that make a place holy

A site is holy by an *observable* property, and the property determines the institution. Name the property before naming the site (DR-ST-01).

| # | property | what it is | attested exemplars | institution it forces |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | theophany / hierophany | a god appeared, spoke, or was seen there | Dodona's oak oracle (`plant-symbolism.md:64`); Delphi (already carried as a *divination* institution, `divination-omen-systems.md:121`); Sinai, Horeb | an oracle seat with a licensed reader; a founding narrative as charter (`myth-folktale-genres.md:66`) |
| P2 | relic | a body-part, an object, or a *contact* trace is kept there | the *scrinium* → shrine etymology (`architecture-settlement-lexicon.md:78`); the *cappella* from St Martin's cloak (`:79`) | a custody office; a reliquary object; theft and translation as political events |
| P3 | tomb | the founder, saint, hero, or royal dead is buried there | the dome-over-tomb row (`architecture-settlement-lexicon.md:43`); tomb cult (`civilizations/greece-classical.md:65`) | a funerary-cult calendar; the tomb as a descent-group anchor (`funerary-material-culture.md`) |
| P4 | natural feature | the place is the feature: spring, cave, mountain, grove, confluence, meteorite | Osun-Osogbo, ~75 ha and 40 shrines along a river (`plant-symbolism.md:65`); river bathing at confluences (`bathing-and-body-hygiene.md:51`); the Teotihuacan cave reading (`cosmology-cosmography.md:116`, flagged influential-not-consensus) | a seasonal access window; a purity rule keyed to the feature |
| P5 | boundary / pillar mark | the place is where one jurisdiction or cosmos *ends* | the *pomerium* and the *sulcus primigenius* (`architecture-settlement-lexicon.md:95`); the *haram* root spanning sanctuary and prohibition (`:85`) | a law-line; a threshold rite (`:125`); a claim word (`:101`) |
| P6 | miracle / case archive | the place has a *record* of answered petitions | the Asclepieia *iamata* votive inscriptions (`divination-omen-systems.md:126`) | a kept archive; the votive object; the byproduct institution (`:135`) |

- **Reading the table.** P1–P6 are not exclusive: a site usually runs on two or three, and the *pair* is what a world bible needs. Osun-Osogbo is P4+P1; the Bodhi tree is P1+P4 with an explicit lineage claim — "a *historical, locatable* tree, whose successors are vegetatively propagated" (`plant-symbolism.md:45`), which is the cleanest in-corpus model for a site whose *sanctity is a descent claim*.
- **The falsification rule.** A site with none of P1–P6 is scenery. If a world bible names a holy place and cannot say which property makes it holy, the place is decoration, and every rule attached to it (access, festival, custody) is unlicensed.
- **Portability warning.** P2 and P6 are the properties most often invented without machinery: a relic needs a custody office and a theft history, and a miracle archive needs a scribe. A world with relics but no reliquary-keepers and no archive has borrowed a *look*.

## 3. The site typology — eight site kinds a worldbuilder can assign

| # | site kind | defining property | the design question it settles | real anchors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | natural hierophany | P1/P4 | is the god *in* the feature, or does the feature merely mark where the god was? | Dodona (`plant-symbolism.md:64`); Balinese mountainward temples (`direction-symbolism.md:137`) |
| 2 | built shrine housing a feature | P2/P4 | the building *contains* the holy thing — the shrine-as-container etymology | *scrinium* (`architecture-settlement-lexicon.md:78`); *garbhagriha* "womb-house" (`:83`) |
| 3 | relic site | P2 | who holds it, and what happens when it is moved? | *cappella* (`:79`); the translation of relics as a political act |
| 4 | tomb site | P3 | is the tomb a destination, a descent-group anchor, or both? | dome-over-tomb (`:43`); Greek tomb cult (`civilizations/greece-classical.md:65`) |
| 5 | oracle seat | P1 + a licensed reader | does the site answer questions, and who is allowed to ask? | Delphi (`divination-omen-systems.md:121`); Dodona (`plant-symbolism.md:64`) |
| 6 | healing / incubation shrine | P6 | does the site keep records, and who reads them? | Asclepieia and the *iamata* (`divination-omen-systems.md:126`) |
| 7 | boundary / pillar site | P5 | what does the site *separate*, and who may cross? | *pomerium* (`architecture-settlement-lexicon.md:95`); *haram* (`:85`) |
| 8 | palladium site (portable holy place) | P2/P5 | what happens when the holy object is *captured and carried away*? | the Emerald Buddha as a dynastic palladium (`civilizations/siam-thai.md:126`) |

- **Site kind 8 is the hard case and the most useful one.** A palladium breaks the entry's own place-logic: the sanctity travels with the object, so the *site* is wherever the object currently is. Design it deliberately, and decide what the abandoned site becomes — a ruin, a rival claimant, or a new kind-1 hierophany. `civilizations/siam-thai.md:113,126` carries the real case; this entry supplies the decision.
- **The diagram question.** Once the sites are named, they form a map, and the map's *geometry* is a claim about the world: radial (Cusco's 41 ceque lines from the Coricancha through 328 huacas, `cosmology-cosmography.md:68`), quartered, linear-corridor, or archipelago. Reuse `cosmology-cosmography.md:75`'s geometry menu rather than inventing a fifth. **Anti-porting rule (inherited):** a geometry is not portable without the institution that services it — Cusco's lines work because "huacas [are] serviced on a calendrical round" (`:68`) and because the roads extend the diagram to the provinces (`:115`).

## 4. The route as rite — the entry's real object

A pilgrimage is a rite of passage whose three phases are *places*, and the place-grammar is the rite's visible form.

| phase (van Gennep, carried at `ritual-liminality.md:5,23`) | spatial form | what the worldbuilder must decide | real anchors |
|---|---|---|---|
| separation | the departure: the home threshold, the farewell, the shedding of ordinary dress | who may leave, who must stay, and what the traveller ceases to be | ihram exchanged for street clothes (`ritual-garments.md:27`); the threshold as smallest liminality (`architecture-settlement-lexicon.md:125`) |
| liminality | the road: stages, stations, hostels, tolls, the company of strangers | the *itinerary*: fixed or free? and who protects the road | *tambos* at day intervals and *qullqas* for storage (`transport-mobility-lexicon.md:35`); the caravan as communitas (`ritual-liminality.md:110`) |
| incorporation | arrival, the act at the site, and the return with a changed status | what the pilgrim *brings home*, and who may not re-enter | the votive object and the badge (§5); re-entry policing (`ritual-liminality.md:118`) |

- **The three route types.** (a) **Fixed itinerary** — a canonical sequence of stations; the pilgrim's freedom is in the travelling, not the route. (b) **Votive ramble** — the destination is fixed and the route is the pilgrim's own; the institution is then the *destination's* reception machinery. (c) **Circuit / circumambulation** — the movement *is* the rite and there is no destination change: seven circuits of the Ka'ba (`direction-symbolism.md:112`), *pradakṣiṇa*, *kora* clockwise vs Bön counterclockwise (`:10`). Name the type before designing the stages (DR-ST-03).
- **Route maintenance is the institution, not the road.** The partition's transport file states the rule flatly — "a route is only real if someone maintains it" (`transport-mobility-lexicon.md:28`) — and its decision row DR-TR-02 requires a maintenance clause (labor obligation, calendar rite, or tax) for every named route and bridge (`:117`). **Every pilgrim road this entry hands off must carry one.** The best in-corpus model is the bridge that is rewoven annually by four communities (`:52`) — a route whose upkeep *is* a festival.
- **The road as cosmogram.** The Inca network radiates from Cusco to the four *suyus*, "so the network is a cosmogram with a logistics layer" (`transport-mobility-lexicon.md:35`). A pilgrimage network is where a world's cosmology becomes walkable — which is exactly `cosmology-cosmography.md:27`'s definition, operationalized.
- **Direction is not decoration.** The circumambulation direction and the direction the pilgrim faces at the site are register claims (`direction-symbolism.md:112,:132`), and `:125` notes the register's most *checkable* consequences — grave orientation, procession routes and tomb-door positions survive archaeologically. A route's directionality should be stated because it constrains architecture and costume downstream.

## 5. The votive economy — the object, the receipt, the token

- **Three objects, three institutions.** (a) The **votive deposit** — the object left at the site; it needs a deposition rule, which is `material-culture-inference-method.md:56`'s deposition grammar applied to a shrine instead of a grave. (b) The **inscribed receipt** — the dedication text; it needs a scribe and a script stage, which is why the coupling to `writing-systems.md:44,:52,:102` matters. (c) The **token** — the cheap, mass-produced mark that the pilgrim takes *home*; it needs a manufacturing economy, which is why `sumptuary-law-rank-display.md:2`'s assayability test applies: a token survives as an institution only if it is hard enough to counterfeit to be worth policing.
- **The archive is the byproduct.** The Asclepieia kept votive inscriptions and case records, and `divination-omen-systems.md:135` names the rule generally: every omen institution produces a second, unintended product — "medicine from incubation cases". Apply it here: a healing shrine produces a medical archive; a victory shrine produces a battle record; a boundary shrine produces a jurisdiction map. **The archive is often the more useful institution for a world bible than the shrine.**
- **Badges are archaeology.** The in-corpus precedent for a cheap pilgrim token being *evidence* is the vessel file's use of Petrie's sequence-dating (`vessel-container-lexicon.md:120`) and the transport file's use of the Q'eswachaca collapse (`transport-mobility-lexicon.md:52`) — a class of small objects, dated in layers, that proves a practice existed at scale. A pilgrim token is that class.
- **The economy around the site.** Hostelry, tolls, market, protection of the road: `civilizations/arabia-pre-islamic.md:120` carries a whole section titled "Meccan pilgrimage economy: lexicon without an assumed trade empire", and `:122` is the caution that must be inherited with it — Crone's challenge to projecting earlier commerce onto Muhammad's Mecca. **Rule: a sanctuary's wealth is evidence of a *later* economy, not of its ancient sanctity.** `civilizations/mali-songhai.md:71` is the same warning at the scale of a single royal journey ("an economic-shock case *with limits*").

## 6. The shrine institution — custody, access, and who pays

| custody model | who holds the site | what follows | real anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| lineage / descent group | a family or clan, often the founder's | hereditary office, an archaic register, resistance to reform | the tomb as descent-group anchor (`funerary-material-culture.md`) |
| monastic order / clergy | a resident community under a rule | a daily liturgy, a scriptorium, a hostel | the *iamata* archive (`divination-omen-systems.md:126`) |
| guild / professional college | a trade body with an interest | fees, a training path, a portable licence | the divination guild rows (`divination-omen-systems.md:121`) |
| state bureau | a government office | a calendar it controls, a road it maintains, a claim it enforces | the road managers named among Inca officials (`transport-mobility-lexicon.md:28`) |
| lay trust / wardens | a civic body | contested custody, audit, and the scandal | `civilizations/vietnam.md:108`'s caution about later heritage rhetoric |
| no custodian | the site is open | erosion, souvenir-taking, and eventually a *new* custodian claiming it | — (a design option, not a gap) |

- **Access law is the entry's sharpest deliverable.** Three variables settle it: *who may enter* (all, co-religionists, a caste, a sex, an office), *when* (always, seasonally, on a computed date), and *what must be true of the visitor* (purity state, dress, escort, payment). `architecture-settlement-lexicon.md:85` supplies the vocabulary proof that one root can span sanctuary, prohibition and the household's protected half; `:138` supplies the build rule — fix the boundary word first, then build its rite.
- **The restricted-knowledge boundary is not optional.** `verification/myth-consistency-gate.md:34` states it as a gate rule: "Do not expose restricted ritual knowledge to fill a table. Mark an access boundary and choose a less extractive comparison." This entry therefore designs *access rules* for fictional sites and never enumerates a living tradition's restricted material. Every real-world case routes through `verification/real-world-inspiration-boundaries.md:2,:6` before use.
- **The festival coupling.** A pilgrimage is a movable feast in a calendar (`calendar-festival-systems.md:107`), and `bathing-and-body-hygiene.md:74` already lists pilgrimage among the schedule's life-crisis triggers. Decide the festival slot and the site's access window together; a site open year-round has no pilgrimage, only tourism.

## 7. Sacred geography as a diagram — and the era drift of a holy map

- **The map is a claim.** `cosmology-cosmography.md:133` states the rule for toponyms — "a fictional map is a set of etymological arguments" — and it holds for sites: a site name is a charter, and the derivation table goes to `naming-systems.md:40,:70`. `naming-systems.md:70`'s critical-toponymy point applies with full force: renaming a holy site is an act of power, and `:97` shows a place-name spelling shifted by a royal name taboo.
- **Six drift mechanisms a holy map runs on** (adapting `symbolism-synthesis-method.md:104`'s change rules to place): *revelation* (a new hierophany outranks the old), *translation* (a relic or palladium moves, and sanctity moves with it), *conquest* (the site changes hands and the access rule changes, not the geography), *reform* (a new regime demotes the old sites), *neglect* (a route stops being maintained and the site becomes a legend — the DR-TR-02 failure mode), *invention* (a modern heritage regime manufactures a site, which is `civilizations/vietnam.md:108`'s caution).
- **Date-stamp every site.** A world bible that names a holy place without an era of establishment and an era of current use has left the map undated, and the era files own the periodization the dates must key to.
- **The diaspora / lost-site case.** If the site is unreachable (destroyed, occupied, submerged), the pilgrimage survives as *text* or as a substitute direction — which is where the route's documents (`writing-systems.md:44,:52`) and the qibla-type single-line geometry (`cosmology-cosmography.md:72,:75`) do the work the road can no longer do.

## 9. Build order — applying this to a fictional culture

1. **Fix the property** (§2): which of P1–P6 makes each named place holy. Write it as a sentence the culture's own scholars could say.
2. **Assign the site kind** (§3) and the **diagram geometry** (§7), reusing `cosmology-cosmography.md:75`'s menu.
3. **Name the site** and hand the derivation to `naming-systems.md` (§7); decide whether the name is a charter claim, a taboo, or a description.
4. **Pick the route type** (§4: fixed itinerary, votive ramble, or circuit) and lay the stages.
5. **Write the maintenance clause** for the route (DR-TR-02, `transport-mobility-lexicon.md:117`) — labor, calendar rite, or tax. No route ships without one.
