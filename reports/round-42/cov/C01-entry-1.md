## 1. Frame: why stone is a register, and what it is not

A **register** in this lane's method is one of the small closed inventories a culture's symbolic grammar maps onto a shared partition (`corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/symbolism-synthesis-method.md:23`). Colour, animal, number, celestial body and direction are the five; flora was added by `plant-symbolism.md`. Stone is the sixth slot, and the method file already names it — "the medieval Latin package: bestiary + **lapidary** + numerology + heraldic code" (`:29`) — which is why its absence is a **gap rather than a design choice**.

The question a register entry must answer first is: **what property of this material class makes it symbolizable at all?** For flora the answer is *stationarity* — "a plant cannot flee, so it can be owned, inherited, fenced, taxed, and used as a boundary marker in a way an animal cannot" (`corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/plant-symbolism.md:15`). For stone the same property is not merely present but **maximal**, and it is joined by four more that no other register has together:

| property | what it means | why it matters for a register |
|---|---|---|
| **stationary** | a mineral does not move, grow visibly, or flee | it can be **owned and titled** more absolutely than anything else — a boundary stone is a legal instrument |
| **unrenewable** | a quarry or a lode does not replenish within a human lifetime | supply is a **fixed stock**, so a scarcity ladder is possible (a herd can be bred back; a lode cannot) |
| **divisible without loss of kind** | a gem splits into two gems | value is **fractionable and countable** — the only register whose units can be both a treasury and a treasury's change |
| **unforgeable in kind, forgeable in appearance** | you cannot grow a diamond, but you can fake one | generates the **assay institution** and the fraud plot (§7) |
| **workedness** | the stone's meaning depends on *who shaped it and how* | a register with a **labour term** built in — raw, dressed, carved, inscribed, set |

**Craft maxim (DR-MS-01): a stone's symbol is generated by a property or a provenance, never by a mood.** The two mistakes this entry exists to prevent are (a) treating a stone's meaning as intrinsic ("emeralds are hope") and (b) treating it as arbitrary. Real lapidary systems are neither: they reason from a **material fact** (hardness, colour, fracture, where it came from, what it was shaped into) to a meaning. Build the fact first and the meaning follows; that is exactly the discipline `color-symbolism.md:11` states for colour codes ("A color never carries a meaning by itself; a **code** does").

Four boundaries, because conflating them is the commonest defect in generated material:

- **Stone is not colour.** Colour is a *valence* system; stone is a *carrier* system. A red stone is not "a red" — it is a stone that is red, and the interesting question is whether the culture reads the *stone* (its hardness, its origin) or the *red*. The two registers couple and must not merge; the colour entry owns meaning, this one owns material.
- **Stone is not metal-as-currency.** The precious metals are a *lithic* class (§3 class 1) but they carry an additional property no gem has — **meltability into a fungible standard**. That property belongs to `trade-economy-lexicon.md`; this entry owns the metal as a *symbol* and as a *material*, and never re-covers minting, seigniorage or exchange rates.
- **Stone is not architecture.** The building stone is a *cost, a technique and a statement*; the building *vocabulary* is `architecture-settlement-lexicon.md`. This entry supplies the material side of the statement.
- **Stone is not an archaeological period.** The 24 `lithic` hits in the corpus are all "Neolithic"/"lithic assemblage" senses — a clean false-positive class recorded in the `- gap filled:` sweep. The *period* vocabulary is not this entry's.

## 2. The five sign-properties

Every lapidary meaning a designer writes should be derivable from one or more of five sign-properties. This is the register's generating table (DR-MS-02), and it is the direct analogue of the colour register's carrier rules.

| # | sign-property | the question | attested load |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | **colour** | what does the hue say? | the choshen's twelve stones differentiated by hue, one per tribe (`corpus/knowledge/culture-material/ritual-garments.md:59`); the whole colour register couples here |
| **P2** | **hardness and durability** | what does it resist? | diamond/emery as the *cutting* substance, jade as the *unbreakable* substance — the metaphor grammar of hardness (a vow "hard as stone"), and the reason a hard stone is a ruler's stone |
| **P3** | **fracture and form** | how does it break, and what shape does it take? | conchoidal fracture (obsidian, flint) as the *blade* class; cleavage as a *divination* surface (crystal-gazing); the natural crystal as a *given* shape needing no cutter |
| **P4** | **provenance** | where did it come from? | the *charter* mechanism — a stone from the sacred mountain, a meteoric iron "fallen from the sky", a jade traded 2,000 km; the Emerald Buddha as a **dynastic palladium** whose value is largely its history (`corpus/knowledge/civilizations/siam-thai.md:126`) |
| **P5** | **workedness** | who shaped it, and how? | the *labour* term: raw boulder vs dressed block vs carved image vs inscribed stele vs set gem; the heirloom rule (`china-shang-zhou.md:106`) is a P5 fact — an old object in a new burial |

**Rule (DR-MS-03): fix P4 before P1.** Provenance is the load-bearing property in real lapidary systems and the one designers skip. A stone's *origin story* is what makes it a charter object — a palladium, a relic, a boundary witness — while its colour only makes it a decoration. If a designer can say only what colour a stone is, the stone has no institution attached to it.

## 3. The eight material classes

A culture's lithic register is filled from a small number of material classes, and each class carries a different symbolic load *because its material properties differ*. Eight classes cover the attested range (DR-MS-04).

| # | class | members | load-bearing property | attested symbolic load | design lesson |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **the precious / native metals** | gold, silver, electrum, copper, tin, meteoric iron | native (found pure) vs smelted; **meltable to a fungible standard** | gold as the incorruptible/royal metal; electrum's natural gold-silver alloy as a *pre-metallurgy* treasure; meteoric iron as "sky-metal" | the metal class has a **double life** (symbol here, currency in `trade-economy-lexicon.md`); design the symbol, hand off the currency |
| **2** | **the gemstones** | diamond, corundum (ruby/sapphire), beryl (emerald/aquamarine), topaz, garnet, spinel | **hardness + hue + clarity**; cuttable to a light-returning form | the choshen's tribal set (`ritual-garments.md:59`); the Emerald Buddha as palladium (`siam-thai.md:126`); crown-jewel sets as regalia | the gem class is the **rank** class: it is where sumptuary law bites (`sumptuary-law-rank-display.md`) |
| **3** | **the ornamental hardstones** | jade (nephrite/jadeite), turquoise, lapis lazuli, obsidian, carnelian, amazonite | **toughness** (jade's interlocking fibre resists fracture), **opacity**, **carvability** | Taube's Maya jade ↔ maize/centrality/rulership/breath (`maya-classic.md:10`,:127); pounamu as inalienable *taonga* (`maori.md:13`,:14); turquoise in Mesoamerican and Persian prestige | the hardstone class is the **carved** class — its value is the *object*, not the carat, which is why it is the best class for an **heirloom** institution |
| **4** | **the worked industrial stone** | flint, chert, basalt, granite, marble, alabaster, sandstone, slate | **abundance + workability + localness** | flint as the blade-stone of the first toolkits; marble as the *statue and temple* stone; alabaster as the *translucent lamp* stone; slate as the *writing* stone | the industrial class is the **common** class: it is what makes a "stone culture" legible without any luxury at all |
| **5** | **the salt and evaporite family** | rock salt, sea salt, natron, alum, gypsum | **edibility + preservative power + dissolution** | salt as covenant and hospitality (the shared salt); natron as the Egyptian embalming agent; alum as the mordant that makes a dye fast | the salt class is the **covenant** class — the only lithic class a person *eats*, which is why it carries oath and hospitality |
| **6** | **the pigment minerals** | ochre (hematite), cinnabar, malachite, azurite, orpiment, Egyptian blue | **colour that is a stone** — a mineral crushed to make a colour | ochre as the oldest deliberate pigment (burial and body use); cinnabar as the red of both life and mercury-poison; lapis lazuli crushed as the ground of ultramarine | the pigment class is the **bridge** to the colour register: it is where "the dye came from a stone" is a fact |
| **7** | **the meteoric and exotic** | meteoritic iron, tektite, fulgurite, amber, jet, pearl, coral | **non-local origin** or **organic-but-stone-like** | meteoric iron as sky-metal and the smith-god's material; amber as the northern sun-stone traded into the Mediterranean; jet as the mourning stone; pearl as the water-born jewel | the exotic class is the **charter** class: its value is the *route*, so it makes trade history into myth |
| **8** | **the carved sacred object** | the *betyl*/*baetyl* (anointed stone), the *lingam*, the boundary stele, the inscribed tablet, the foundation stone | **a stone that is an object of cult or law** | the anointed standing stone; the inscribed law stele; the foundation deposit; the reliquary casket | class 8 is **not a mineral but a function**: it is where the register becomes an *institution*, and it is the class a designer should reach for when the world needs a *place* rather than a *treasure* |

**Rule (DR-MS-05): pick three to five classes, and at least one must be common.** A register filled only from classes 2, 3 and 7 is a treasure catalogue with no world under it. The industrial class (4) and the salt class (5) are what make the lithic register *lived-in* — a quarry, a salt road, a millstone. The corpus's own partition method is the model: 3–5 load-bearing slots chosen from a real enumeration (`corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/symbolism-synthesis-method.md:48`).

## 4. The four meaning mechanisms

A material class becomes a symbol by one of four mechanisms. Name which one a given stone uses, or the stone is decoration (DR-MS-06).

| # | mechanism | form | attested exemplar | what it generates in-world |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M1** | **material property → metaphor** | the stone's physical behaviour becomes an abstract quality | hardness → steadfastness; incorruptibility (gold) → purity/royalty; translucency (alabaster) → the lamp and the soul; the jade ↔ **breath** reading (Taube, `maya-classic.md:10`) | a **metaphor grammar** — the culture's vocabulary of durable qualities; couples `semantics-pragmatics.md` |
| **M2** | **provenance → charter** | where it came from authorizes who holds it | the Emerald Buddha as dynastic palladium (`siam-thai.md:126`); meteoric iron as sky-metal; a stone from the sacred mountain set in the throne | a **title-deed**: the object's origin legitimates the holder — the register's interface to `sacred-kingship-mythic-charter.md` |
| **M3** | **scarcity → rank** | how hard it is to get sets who may have it | crown jewels; the gem set on the choshen; pounamu's kaitiaki restriction (`maori.md:13`) | a **sumptuary economy** (§6) — this mechanism is the register's political surface |
| **M4** | **container-name fossil** | a material property becomes the *name of a thing* | Pliny's *sarcophagus* stone of Assos, which "consumes the bodies interred in it" and therefore **gives its name to the coffin** (`corpus/knowledge/culture-material/funerary-material-culture.md:22`; the same mechanism for vessels in `vessel-container-lexicon.md`) | a **lexical fossil** — the register leaves its mark in the language; couples `naming-systems.md` and `semantic-fields-lexicon-building.md` |

**The fossil rule (DR-MS-07).** M4 is the mechanism that makes a lithic register *visible in the text* rather than only in the lore: when a stone's property names an object, every use of the object's word is a memory of the stone. Real cases: *sarcophagus*, *obsidian* (< Obsius), *magnetite*/*magnes*, *amethyst* (read as "not-drunk"), *jet* (< Gagates, a river), *adamant*/*diamond*, *chalcedony*, *lapis*/*lazuli* by route. Build one such fossil deliberately and the register will read as a language rather than a table.

## 5. The scarcity ladder

Scarcity is what converts a geology into a prestige economy, and the conversion has a **shape**: not a smooth price curve but a ladder of *institutions* that switch on at each rung. This is the lithic analogue of the dye-scarcity economics that `color-symbolism.md` owns (and which the corpus names as "the model for a stone-scarcity economics" — `reports/round-42/scout/K02-myth-gaps.md` R1).

| rung | scarcity condition | institution that switches on | attested shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | local and abundant | the **quarry/mill**: a trade, a measure, a local name | millstone, building stone, salt pans |
| **2** | local but effortful | the **mine**: a labour regime, often coerced | the silver mines as a punishment and a state enterprise |
| **3** | non-local, one route | the **trade route and its toll**: the stone's name is a place-name | amber's northern route; lapis from Badakhshan; the Silk Road's jade |
| **4** | non-local and rare | the **gift and the dowry**: the stone travels as an *object*, not a commodity | the gem as a diplomatic gift; the heirloom gem |
| **5** | unique | the **palladium**: the object *is* the polity's legitimacy | the Emerald Buddha (`siam-thai.md:126`); crown regalia as found title-deed (`sacred-kingship-mythic-charter.md`) |
| **6** | not obtainable at all | the **myth**: the stone exists only in the story, and its absence is the plot | the stone only the god can lift; the gem at the bottom of the sea |

**Rule (DR-MS-08): put at least one stone at rung 5 and one at rung 6.** Rung 5 gives the world a *palladium* — an object whose loss is a political event, which is a plot engine. Rung 6 gives it a *quest* — a stone that cannot be had, which is the register's myth surface. A register with only rungs 1–4 is an economy, not a symbol system.

## 6. Rank: the sumptuary interface

The rank mechanism (M3) is enforced, and enforcement is where this register meets the law. The corpus already owns the enforcement side (`corpus/knowledge/culture-material/sumptuary-law-rank-display.md`); what this entry supplies is the **grading** the law grades against.

Four grading systems recur, and a designer should pick one (DR-MS-09):

| grading | unit | who grades | attested shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **by material** | the stone itself | the trade + the assay office | "only the royal house may wear the red corundum" |
| **by mass** | weight | the treasury | a weight threshold in the law ("no gem above N carats") |
| **by provenance** | origin | the court, the temple | "only stone from the sacred quarry may be set in a crown" |
| **by workedness** | who shaped it | the guild | "uncut stones may be worn by any free person; cut stones only by nobles" — the lapidary as gatekeeper |

**The gatekeeper rule (DR-MS-10).** Grade by *workedness* and the **lapidary becomes a political office**, because they decide what counts as a cut stone. That is the single most powerful rank device in this register, and it is why §7's trade ladder is not a footnote.

## 7. The lapidary as an institution

The lapidary is a trade, and the corpus already owns the **shape** of a trade: a ladder, a mark, and an anti-fraud machine (`corpus/knowledge/culture-material/craft-artisan-lexicon.md`). This entry supplies the lapidary's *instance* of each (DR-MS-11).

| element | the lapidary's version | the design content |
|---|---|---|
| **the ladder** | apprentice → journeyman → master cutter; the *polisher* as a separate and lower rung; the *setter* as a third | the trade's internal hierarchy is the register's **labour term** (P5); a stone's meaning can attach to a *stage* of work |
| **the mark** | the cutter's mark, the assay mark, the guild's hallmark | the mark is what makes a stone **attributable** — and attribution is what makes a fake detectable (couples `craft-artisan-lexicon.md`'s signature typology) |
| **the test** | the streak test, the hardness test (scratch), the specific-gravity test, the water test (clarity), the **heat test** | a **rung in the ladder of proof**: the assay office is the register's court, and the fraud plot is generated by the gap between what a stone *looks* like and what it *is* |
| **the forgery** | the doublet (a real stone face on a cheap backing), the dyed stone, the glass imitation, the **substituted heirloom** | this is the register's most reliable **scene engine**: a stone that is not what it is claimed to be is a crisis of authority, not just of money |
| **the restorer** | the re-cutter, the re-setter, the polisher of an old gem | generates the **authenticity problem**: is a re-cut heirloom still the heirloom? (couples the heirloom rule, §8) |

**The institutional rule (DR-MS-12).** Give the lapidary at least one **non-economic power**: the right to certify, the right to refuse a commission, the right to seal a stone. A trade with only a price has no scenes; a trade with a **veto** is a political actor. The corpus's own model is the craft guild's entry control (`craft-artisan-lexicon.md`) — apply it to stone.

## 8. The heirloom rule and the heirloom-dating trap

Two rules, and the second is the one that catches auditors.

**The heirloom rule (DR-MS-13).** Because a stone is stationary, unrenewable and unalterable in *kind* (though not in form), it is the material class best suited to carry **inheritance**. An heirloom stone is a stone whose *chain of custody* is the meaning. Attested: pounamu as inalienable *taonga* with kaitiaki guardianship (`corpus/knowledge/civilizations/maori.md:13`,:14) — a stone that is *not alienable*, which is a stronger institution than inheritance; the Fu Hao tomb jades (`china-shang-zhou.md:106`); the Emerald Buddha as a palladium that moves with the dynasty (`siam-thai.md:126`).

**The heirloom-dating trap (DR-MS-14).** `corpus/knowledge/civilizations/china-shang-zhou.md:106` states the rule and this entry generalizes it: **an old object in a later burial does not imply manufacture during the burial's dynasty**. Stones outlive their owners and move between them, so a stone's *deposition date* is not its *manufacture date* and neither is its *use date*. For a worldbuilder this is not merely an inference caveat — it is a **plot generator**: a stone found in a modern grave whose carving is three centuries older is exactly the kind of object that starts a story, and a culture that has learned the rule can *date a forgery by it*. The corpus's inference ladder (`material-culture-inference-method.md`) is the general form; this is its lithic instance.

## 9. Build order

1. **Fix the material substrate first** (the register method's step 0, `symbolism-synthesis-method.md:47`): the culture's geology, its access to stone, and its technical level. Everything below derives from this.
2. **Choose 3–5 material classes** (§3), including at least one **common** class (industrial or salt) — otherwise the register is a treasure catalogue.
3. **Pick the sign-properties** (§2) each chosen class will carry — and **fix provenance (P4) before colour (P1)**, because provenance is what attaches an institution.
4. **Assign each stone a mechanism** (§4): M1 metaphor, M2 charter, M3 rank, or M4 fossil. A stone with no named mechanism is decoration.
5. **Build the scarcity ladder** (§5) with at least one stone at rung 5 (palladium) and one at rung 6 (mythic/unobtainable).
6. **Choose the rank grading** (§6) and install the enforcement interface — hand the *law* to `sumptuary-law-rank-display.md`, keep the *grades* here.
7. **Build the lapidary** (§7): the ladder, the mark, the test, the forgery, and **one non-economic power**.
8. **Install the heirloom institution** (§8) and state the culture's knowledge of the heirloom-dating trap — a culture that knows it has a forensic tradition, which is a scene.
9. **Write one M4 fossil** (§4): a stone whose property names an object in the language. Hand the lexicon to the language files (`semantics-pragmatics.md`, `semantic-fields-lexicon-building.md`) — do not invent it here.
10. **Align with the partition** (§2 of the method): place the stone slots against the culture's existing colour, direction and number partitions, mark any **UNALIGNED** cell with a reason rather than forcing smoothness (`symbolism-synthesis-method.md:51`), and run the audit.
11. **Hand off** (§11) — reference and method only.

## 11. Calibration bank

**The lapidary genre — usable as genre, discredited as physics.** Pliny's book 37 and the medieval lapidaries (Marbod of Rennes, Philippe de Thaon) are the **primary sources of the genre this lane imitates** (`symbolism-synthesis-method.md:29`), and their *material* and *provenance* reports are the usable layer. Their **reputed-powers** layer — the stone that cures, protects, or reveals — is **discredited as a physical claim** and is retained here as an attested **belief** a fictional culture may hold and act on. The disposition is the corpus's own for Frazer: keep the folk model, refuse the method (`corpus/knowledge/linguistics/politeness-taboo-euphemism.md:73`).

**Sympathetic magic — refuse as explanation, keep as emic model.** The reasoning that a stone's colour or origin *causes* its effect (like-cures-like, the part for the whole) is discredited as an explanation of why lapidary systems exist, and survives as the **folk model** the practitioners themselves use. In-world it should mostly *work*, because in-world the belief is what characters act on — but the entry must not present it as a mechanism.

**"Crystal energy", chakra–mineral correspondences, gem therapy — a modern invented tradition.** These are **contemporary** constructions, not ancient doctrine: legitimate as a *fictional* culture's practice and illegitimate as a description of any past one. This is the register's analogue of the corpus's other invented-tradition flags (rune-casting, the 2012 construction, floriography's Victorian codification).

**Attributed readings that must stay attributed.** Taube's Maya jade ↔ maize/centrality/rulership/breath associations are an **iconographic interpretation**, with the in-lane caution already written: "Not every jade object expresses all associations simultaneously" (`corpus/knowledge/civilizations/maya-classic.md:127`). The choshen's twelve stones as a zodiac set is **attributed to Philo and Josephus** (`ritual-garments.md:59`), not asserted. The Emerald Buddha as palladium is Reynolds's analysis with an explicit anti-universal caveat (`siam-thai.md:126`).

## 12. NON-CANON worked micro-example (illustrative only — decides nothing)

*A fictional culture, the Vasq, is invented here only to show the register producing a system. Nothing below is a claim about any real culture.*

- **Substrate (§9 step 1).** The Vasq live on a granite plateau with one river cutting a limestone gorge, no native metals, and a single high pass to a coastal people who have amber. Their technical level: no smelting, cold-worked native copper from the coast.
- **Classes (§3): four.** Class 4 industrial (granite and limestone — the **common** class, required by DR-MS-05); class 5 salt (the plateau has brine springs — the **covenant** class); class 7 exotic (coastal **amber** — the **charter** class); class 3 hardstone (a local tough greenstone used for axe-heads — the **carved/heirloom** class). - **Sign-properties (§2).** Granite carries **P2 hardness** (the metaphor of the unbreakable); limestone carries **P5 workedness** (the *dressed* stone — their architecture is cut, not built up, and cutting is the labour that marks status); salt carries **P4 provenance** (the brine spring is the covenant site, and the *shared salt* is the oath); amber carries **P4 provenance + P3 form** (a fossil resin from a far coast, and its *natural* shape needs no cutter — the reverse of the limestone rule).
- **Mechanisms (§4).** Granite → **M1** (hardness → steadfastness; a granite oath is unbreakable and cannot be dissolved, only buried). Limestone → **M5-via-workedness**: **M4 fossil** — the Vasq word for *wall* is the word for *cut limestone*, and their word for *law* is the same word, because law is published on cut limestone stele. Salt → **M2 charter** (the brine spring authorizes whoever holds it; the spring's keepers hold the oaths). Amber → **M3 rank** (the single imported luxury, therefore the rank marker; and, because it comes by one pass, the pass-toll is the rank's enforcement).
- **Scarcity ladder (§5).** Rung 1: granite and limestone (local, abundant — the quarry trade). Rung 3: amber (non-local, one route — the pass toll). **Rung 5 (palladium, required by DR-MS-08):** the **Brine Stone** — a greenstone axe-head of the local class 3 hardstone, said to be the first stone ever cut on the plateau, held by the spring's keepers; whoever holds it may administer oaths. **Rung 6 (mythic, required):** the *Copper Man* — a native-copper figure the coastal people are said to have and will not sell; it exists only in the story, and the Vasq's entire diplomatic vocabulary is built on its absence.
- **Rank grading (§6): by workedness.** Any free Vasq may carry *uncut* amber; only a *cut* amber (worked by the lapidary) marks noble status — so the **lapidary holds the veto** (DR-MS-10), because they alone decide what counts as cut.
- **The lapidary (§7).** Ladder: apprentice → cutter → master; the *polisher* is a lower rung and the *setter* (who mounts amber in copper) a third. Mark: the master's chisel-signature on the amber's back, invisible when worn — so the mark is a **verification device** a buyer checks. Test: the **float test** for amber plus the *scratch test* against granite. Forgery: **coastal glass** sold as amber, and the crisis plot — a noble's cut amber proves to be coastal glass, which is a *rank* fraud, not a money fraud. Non-economic power (DR-MS-12): the master lapidary may **refuse a commission**, and a refusal is publicly read as a judgment on the petitioner's status.
- **Heirloom (§8).** The Brine Stone is **inalienable** — it cannot be sold, only held; and because the Vasq know the heirloom-dating trap (DR-MS-14), they have a forensic tradition: a stone found in an old wall whose chisel-signature is a modern master's is *proved* a forgery by the signature alone. That forensic tradition is a scene engine and a political weapon.
- **The fossil (§4 M4).** *Law* = *cut limestone*. Every time a Vasq says "law," they say "stone."

## 1. Frame: what a purity code is, and the four things it is not

A purity code is a culture's answer to one question: **what is out of place, and what must be done about it?** The classic formulation is Mary Douglas's — dirt is "matter out of place" (`corpus/knowledge/linguistics/politeness-taboo-euphemism.md:73` states the phrase in-lane) — and the craft value of the formulation is that it makes pollution *relational* rather than substantive. Nothing is dirty in itself; a thing is dirty relative to a classification. Change the classification and the dirt moves. That is the single most useful property for a worldbuilder, because it means a purity code is generated by the culture's **category system**, not by its chemistry.

Four boundaries, because conflating them is the commonest defect in generated material:

- **Purity is not hygiene.** They are different systems and must not be merged (`corpus/knowledge/culture-material/bathing-and-body-hygiene.md:50`). Ritual purity is about *status* — who or what is fit for a sacred act. Hygiene is about *contamination* — what makes a body sick. A purity bath may also clean; a hygienic bath may acquire ritual force. **The leaks between the two are the design work** (`:52`), and the design rule is to state separately what the culture considers unclean, what it considers unsanitary, and which institution owns each (`:53`).
- **Purity is not taboo-as-lexicon.** A taboo is a *word or act under prohibition*; a purity code is the *classification that generates the prohibitions*. The two meet at the vocabulary (`politeness-taboo-euphemism.md:73-80`) and at the taboo domain inventory (`:75`), and they part there: this entry owns the code, that entry owns the words.
- **Purity is not sacrifice.** The rite discharges the impurity; it does not define it. The piacular type — "destroy the blame-bearer to cleanse the group" (`corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/ritual-liminality.md:57`) — is one *rung* of §5's ladder, and the entry that owns it owns the ritual machinery. What this entry adds is the impurity the rite is aimed at, and the rule that decides **who counts as the blame-bearer**.
- **Purity is not law.** A purity code becomes law when an institution enforces it (§7); until then it is a classification with consequences. Keeping the two apart is what lets a designer stage the interesting case — a code the courts have stopped enforcing but the household still obeys.

**The craft maxim: a purity code is a classification plus a consequence.** A generated code that names what is unclean but attaches no consequence is an aesthetic; a code with consequences but no classification is arbitrary. Build the classification first, derive the consequences from it, and the code will read as lived-in.

## 2. The pollution grammar — seven axes

Every purity code a designer builds fixes seven independent things. Name them explicitly and the code stops being a mood and becomes a system (DR-PP-01).

| # | axis | the question it answers | attested range |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | **source** | what is unclean? | §3's eight classes — corpse, blood, birth/sex, food, anomaly, stranger, the sacred, the ruler |
| **A2** | **transmission** | how does the pollution move? | contact (touch, ingest, proximity, gaze); inheritance (birth, kin); occupancy (a place or a time is polluted); **none** (an intrinsic status that does not spread) |
| **A3** | **scope** | what can be polluted? | person, object, place, time, speech — and the combinations (a polluted *day* in a clean house) |
| **A4** | **gradient** | how bad is it? | a scale, not a switch — the corpus's own model for a graded law is the Islamic five-rulings ladder (`corpus/knowledge/culture-material/cuisine-food-lexicon.md:83`, *al-aḥkām al-khamsa*) |
| **A5** | **reversibility** | can it be undone? | removable (with a technology, §5); **automatic** (time alone clears it — Hertz's interval, §5 rung 1); terminal (outcaste, exile, exposure) |
| **A6** | **adjudicator** | who decides whether it applies? | the person; an elder; a priest; a court; an ordeal; a physician; the state |
| **A7** | **asymmetry** | does purity transmit as readily as pollution? | usually **no** — pollution is contagious and purity is not, which is why cleansing is harder than soiling. The exception is §8's red-heifer paradox, where the purifying agent pollutes the purifier |

Two design consequences follow immediately, and they are the reason the axes are separate:

- **A2 and A7 together set the code's social temperature.** A code with contact transmission and strong asymmetry produces an anxious society of avoidances and boundary offices. A code with *no* transmission (intrinsic, non-spreading status — the outcaste whose touch pollutes nothing because the status is fixed, or the sacred person whose charge is not contagious) produces a stratified society with stable castes and no panic. Both are attested shapes; they are different worlds.
- **A5 is the plot engine.** A reversible pollution generates a *procedure* (a rite, a wait, a payment) and therefore scenes. A terminal pollution generates a *crisis* and therefore exile, concealment, or violence. An automatic pollution generates a *calendar* (§7). Choose A5 and you have chosen the culture's dominant narrative about uncleanness.

## 3. The eight source classes

The corpus's real cultures pollute from a small recurring set of sources. Eight classes cover the attested range; each carries its attested exemplars and the design lesson (DR-PP-02).

| # | source class | what it is | attested exemplars | design lesson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S1** | **the corpse and death** | the body of the dead as the paradigm pollutant | Hebrew Bible: the corpse is the strongest impurity (*ṭumah*), and the red heifer's water is its remedy (Num 19); Zoroastrian: the corpse demoness *Druj Nasu* and exposure on the *dakhma* so as not to defile earth (`corpus/knowledge/culture-material/funerary-material-culture.md:24`; `corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/death-afterlife-eschatology.md:49`) | death-pollution is the **cross-culturally commonest** class and the safest default; its duration is the mourning schedule (`funerary-material-culture.md:55`,:127), so S1 and the calendar couple hard |
| **S2** | **blood and body fluids** | life-substance that must not cross the body boundary | Lev 15 (genital flux); Lev 17:11 (blood as the life, forbidden to eat); the menstrual taboo family | blood-pollution usually attaches to a **boundary**, not to the fluid: what matters is *crossing*, which makes S2 a category rule, not a chemistry |
| **S3** | **birth and sex** | the reproductive events as liminal states | Lev 12 (post-partum impurity); the cross-cultural couvade and post-partum seclusion family | birth-pollution is **temporary by construction** — it marks a transition, so it pairs with `ritual-liminality.md`'s three-phase grammar |
| **S4** | **food and carrion** | what may be eaten, and what has touched the mouth of another | Lev 11 / Deut 14; Islamic *ḥalāl/ḥarām*; the Hindu *jootha* — tasted food carries the eater, so sharing it is intimacy or pollution by caste (`corpus/knowledge/culture-material/cuisine-food-lexicon.md:94`,:139) | the *jootha* mechanism is the most transferable single device here: pollution by **prior contact with a person**, not by the substance |
| **S5** | **category-straddlers (the anomaly)** | the thing that crosses a classification boundary carries the pollution load | the pig failing the Leviticus hoof/cud conjunction (`corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/animal-symbolism.md:69`); the bat in insult vocabulary; the barnacle goose eaten in Lent as "fish" (`symbolism-synthesis-method.md:68`) | the anomaly rule is the corpus's most-developed purity device (`symbolism-synthesis-method.md:40`,:68; `plant-symbolism.md:20`) — **this entry owns its mechanism, the register entries own its instances** |
| **S6** | **strangers and outsiders** | the person outside the classification | the general stranger-pollution family; the caste outsider; the *pharmakos* — the scapegoat exile driven out to carry the group's pollution (`corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/ritual-liminality.md:57`) | S6 is where purity codes **become political**; the design move is to name the office that decides who is outside, and to give the outsider a *route in* or refuse it explicitly |
| **S7** | **the sacred itself** | the holy as a dangerous charge, not a safe one | Otto's *mysterium tremendum*; the Hawaiian *kapu* radiating from the ruler's person (`corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/sacred-kingship-mythic-charter.md:84`); the Hebrew *qodesh* whose contact consecrates and therefore restricts | §8 — the single most under-used class, because designers default to "sacred = pure" when the attested logic is often "sacred = charged, therefore dangerous" |
| **S8** | **the ruler** | the sovereign as a permanent, managed pollution source | the *kapu* system and its 1819 abolition (`sacred-kingship-mythic-charter.md:35`,:84); the taboo-set cost accounting at `:127-141` | S8 is S7 with an administration: the ruler's charge is **budgeted**, and each taboo names the office that pays for it (`:140`) |

**Rule (DR-PP-03): pick three to five sources, not eight.** A code with all eight is a concordance, not a culture. The corpus's own partition method is the model — 3–5 load-bearing axes, chosen from a real enumeration (`corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/symbolism-synthesis-method.md:48`). Two of the chosen sources should be **boundary** sources (S2 or S5, which fire at a category edge) and one should be a **status** source (S6, S7 or S8, which attaches to a person rather than an event), or the code will have no politics.

## 4. The polarity base — purity needs an axis to run on

Pollution is not free-floating; it is one pole of a polarity the culture already holds. The partition's method fixes the base explicitly: "choose 3–5 load-bearing polarity axes (hot/cold, wet/dry, **pure/impure**, raw/cooked, high/low)" (`corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/symbolism-synthesis-method.md:48`). Two rules for using it:

- **Attach the purity axis to a material or spatial base, never to a bare abstraction.** The attested purity axes are carried by something: fire and water (Zoroastrian), blood and boundary (Leviticus), the sea and the tide (Polynesian *kapu*), the hearth (Vedic), the threshold and the shrine (Shinto *harae*). A purity axis with no carrier is a mood; with a carrier it generates vocabulary, architecture and rite.
- **Let the axis be violated visibly.** The corpus's rule for a closed symbolic grammar is that it should impose well-formedness on its own rendering — "a compositional rule that makes malformed combinations visibly wrong — a blasphemy you can see" (`corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/symbolism-synthesis-method.md:64`). For a purity code the analog is a **visible sign of pollution**: a garment, a mark, a seat, a distance. If a polluted person is indistinguishable from a clean one, the code has no scene surface and therefore no existence on the page.

The **graded precinct** is the spatial form of this axis (§6), and the **body axis** is its somatic form: the right/left polarity once read as clean/unclean (Hertz 1909, `corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/direction-symbolism.md:93`) is a *local* answer, not a universal one — the entry that owns it records the Meru inversion and the Chinese era-alternation, which is exactly the designer's license to invert the somatic map.

## 5. The purification ladder — ten rungs

Purification is not one act. The attested technologies form a ladder from cheapest and most automatic to most destructive, and the rung a culture prefers is a statement about its theory of pollution (DR-PP-04). Rungs are ordered by **cost to the community**, not by efficacy.

| rung | technology | what it says about pollution | attested exemplars | cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **time** | pollution decays on its own | Hertz's decomposition interval, the mourning duration (`corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/ritual-liminality.md:24`); Lev 12's fixed post-partum days | free to the polity, expensive to the person — a *window*, so it is also a schedule |
| **2** | **water** | pollution is a removable substance | the *mikveh* with its 40 *seʾah* minimum (`corpus/knowledge/culture-material/bathing-and-body-hygiene.md:51`); *wuḍūʾ* and *ghusl* (`:51`); Shinto *harae*; the *lustratio* of fields and flocks | needs infrastructure (§7) — this rung is why purity codes build baths |
| **3** | **fire and smoke** | pollution is destroyed or driven off | the Zoroastrian fire as a purifying agent; fumigation and censing; the burnt offering's smoke | fuel cost; couples `corpus/knowledge/culture-material/fire-fuel-light.md` |
| **4** | **blood and sacrifice** | pollution must be *paid for* with life | the purification offering (*ḥaṭṭāʾt*) in Lev 4–5; the piacular type (`ritual-liminality.md:57`) | animal cost; requires a priesthood to administer |
| **5** | **substitution and transfer** | pollution can be *moved* to a bearer | the Azazel goat sent into the wilderness (Lev 16, `ritual-liminality.md:57`); the *pharmakos* exile; the Roman *suffitio*; the *everriator* who swept the polluted house out | the transfer needs a **willing or powerless bearer** — this rung is where a code becomes cruel, and the design brief should say who is eligible |
| **6** | **speech** | pollution is undone by *saying* | confession, the naming of the sin, absolution; the performative declaration of clean status | cheap, but it requires an **authorized speaker**, which is a power class |
| **7** | **material removal** | the polluted object is scrubbed, scraped, or taken away | scraping a plastered house (Lev 14:41); washing garments; the removal and burial of the polluted object | labour; generates a servicing trade |
| **8** | **destruction** | the polluted thing must stop existing | burning the polluted object; the red heifer burned whole outside the camp (Num 19) | total loss of the object |
| **9** | **expulsion and segregation** | the polluted *person* is removed, not cleaned | the leper outside the camp (Lev 13–14); the *pharmakos*; the outcaste; the quarantine | the person's whole productive life; the code's harshest rung |
| **10** | **the null case — no purification** | the pollution is **permanent** | the hereditary outcaste; the permanently disqualified priest (Lev 21); the exposed corpse's permanent taint | a standing population outside the code — this rung defines the culture's **permanent outsiders** |

**Two rules for the ladder:**

- **A code that only uses rungs 1–3 is a hygiene state in period clothes** (the same failure the corpus names at `bathing-and-body-hygiene.md:53`); a code that only uses rungs 8–10 is a machine for producing outcasts. The interesting codes **mix rungs across source classes** — the corpse gets time and water (rungs 1–2) while the anomaly gets destruction (rung 8) and the stranger gets expulsion (rung 9).
- **Rung 6 is the tell.** A culture with a strong speech rung has an *authorized interpreter of purity* — a priest, a confessor, an inspector — and therefore a class of experts who can sell certainty. That is a scene engine and a political structure at once.

## 6. Space: the graded precinct

A purity code that exists only as a rule is not yet a place. Its spatial form is a **gradient of zones** from most charged to least, with a controlled crossing at each step, and the crossings are the institutions (DR-PP-05).

| zone | rule | attested shape |
|---|---|---|
| **the innermost / most charged** | entry restricted to a qualified few, often on a schedule | the holy of holies (Lev 16); the *cella*; the shrine's inner sanctum |
| **the qualified court** | the qualified many; purity checked at the threshold | the temple court; the *pronaos*; the mosque's *muṣallā* |
| **the common ground** | the general public, still under a purity rule | the outer court; the *temenos* boundary; the precinct's *pomerium* |
| **the settlement** | ordinary life, with the code running quietly | the house with its threshold, hearth and latrine placement |
| **the outside** | where the polluted and the disposing go | the *dakhma* out of the settlement (`funerary-material-culture.md:62`); the leper's camp; the rubbish ground; the execution site |

Three craft rules:

- **Every threshold needs a gatekeeper and a test.** A graded precinct with no inspection at the boundary is decoration. The gatekeeper may be a priest, a guard, a porter, or a householder — but it must be *someone*, and their test must be a thing that can fail.
- **The disposal site must be placed, not implied.** Where the polluted object goes when it leaves the precinct is the code's most visible decision, and real systems place it deliberately out of sight (`funerary-material-culture.md:62`: "a place that must be out of the settlement"). Name the distance, the route, and who may walk it.
- **Time is a zone too.** A polluted *day* in a clean house is the same gradient run on the calendar: the Atonement day, the periodic purification, the mourning anniversary. This is where the purity code couples to `corpus/knowledge/culture-material/calendar-festival-systems.md` — and its absorbed-anomaly rule (`:43`, the intercalary unit that gains a status of its own) is the model for a *polluted* time-unit that becomes an institution.

## 7. Purity policing — the seven institution slots

A purity code is enforced by institutions, and the institutions recur. Seven slots cover the attested range; a code with fewer than three is a private scruple, not a public system (DR-PP-06).

| slot | function | attested shape | what it generates in-world |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the inspector** | diagnoses whether the pollution applies, and at what grade | the Levitical priest diagnosing scale-disease (Lev 13); the *muḥtasib*; the public-health officer | a **professional class** and a fee; the inspector is the code's expert and its most corruptible office |
| **the court / ordeal** | adjudicates a *disputed* pollution | the ordeal; the oath; the oracular verdict (couples `corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/divination-omen-systems.md`) | procedure, precedent, and the possibility of a *wrong* verdict — a scene engine |
| **the quarantine** | segregates the polluted for a duration | the leper outside the camp; the post-partum seclusion; the plague-house | a building, a roster, and an economy of provisioning the segregated |
| **the certification** | marks a thing or person as clean, for a price | the kosher and halal certification bodies (`corpus/knowledge/culture-material/cuisine-food-lexicon.md:116`, and the identity-drift row at `:84`); the *hechsher*; the seal | a **market** in certainty — the corpus's own prompt: "who sells certainty?" (`:88`) |
| **the calendar** | schedules the recurring purifications | the Day of Atonement (Lev 16); the *ōharae*; the annual *lustratio* | a dated observance, a closed season, and a seasonal economy |
| **the registry** | records who is polluted, for how long, and when they are released | the priest's register; the parish book; the quarantine list | a durable record and a **dated** pollution history — the code's memory |
| **the boundary office** | decides who may marry, enter, eat, or work with whom | the marriage-eligibility court; the caste council; the guild's exclusion | the code's political surface: this is where purity becomes **rank** |

**The rank rule (DR-PP-07).** Purity becomes *rank* when the boundary office is hereditary. Dumont's reading of caste as a purity-ranked hierarchy is the standard statement of the mechanism (and is heavily contested as a description of any real society); used as a design lever it is the single most powerful purity device available, because it converts a classification into a **genealogy**, and a genealogy is a plot. If the boundary office is *not* hereditary, the code produces classes that can be left — which is a different and equally usable world.

## 8. The sacred as a pollution source, and the ruler's budget

The most under-used class is S7: the sacred is not the opposite of the polluted, it is often **the same kind of thing** — a charge that must be managed. Otto's *mysterium tremendum* names the double aspect (attractive and dangerous); Robertson Smith's early thesis made taboo and holiness one contagious force differing in sign; and the corpus already carries the shape at the ruler: "the king's person is taboo — what happens to things and people that touch him" (`corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/sacred-kingship-mythic-charter.md:84`), instantiated as Hawaiian *kapu*.

**The red-heifer paradox (DR-PP-08).** Numbers 19 gives the cleanest attested statement of the danger-of-holiness: the water of purification is made from the ashes of the red heifer, and yet "the priest who burns her shall wash his clothes and be unclean until evening" (Num 19:7–8) — **the agent of purification is itself polluting**. This is the exception to A7's asymmetry rule, and it is design gold: it lets a designer build a code in which the *purifiers* are a permanently charged class, set apart from the ordinary clean, needing their own purification, and therefore structurally indispensable and structurally untouchable. A priesthood like that is a political institution.

**The ruler as S8 (DR-PP-09).** The ruler is S7 with an administration, and the corpus already supplies the accounting frame: the taboo set is designed *with its workarounds and its budget*, and "each taboo buys legitimacy and spends administrative capacity. A polity with six taboo classes needs six offices" (`corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/sacred-kingship-mythic-charter.md:140`). This entry supplies what that frame is *made of*: the ruler's charge is a **source class** (S8) whose transmission (A2) and gradient (A4) the designer fixes, and whose cost is the office count. Add the abolition date — the Hawaiian system ended in 1819 under Liholiho (`sacred-kingship-mythic-charter.md:35`) — and you have the further device: **a purity code can be abolished in a datable event**, which is an era marker and a scene.

## 9. Build order

1. **Fix the classification first.** List the culture's category boundaries (what counts as a person, an animal, a food, a kinsperson, a stranger). Pollution is generated at their edges and violations — this is the Douglas/Leach engine (`symbolism-synthesis-method.md:40`,:68), and it must come before any list of unclean things.
2. **Pick the purity axis and its carrier** (§4): 3–5 load-bearing polarity axes including `pure/impure`, each carried by a material or spatial base.
3. **Choose 3–5 source classes** (§3), with at least two boundary sources and one status source.
4. **Fix the seven grammar axes** (§2) for each chosen source — source, transmission, scope, gradient, reversibility, adjudicator, asymmetry. State them in a table; if two sources share all seven, merge them.
5. **Choose the ladder rungs** (§5) per source class, and name who is eligible to be a substitute bearer (rung 5) — the code's cruelty is decided here, so decide it explicitly.
6. **Build the graded precinct** (§6) with a gatekeeper and a test at every threshold, and place the disposal site by name and distance.
7. **Install at least three policing slots** (§7), and state for each whether it is hereditary (rank) or open (class).
8. **Derive the vocabulary.** The code generates its own lexicon: the names of the states, the grades, the rites, the offices, the polluted places. Hand that to the language files (`couples` `corpus/knowledge/linguistics/semantics-pragmatics.md`, `politeness-taboo-euphemism.md`) — do not invent it here.
9. **Date it.** Give the code a revision history: a reform, a tightening, an abolition, an archaism. A purity code with no dated change is a static prop; the corpus's own drift mechanisms (`symbolism-synthesis-method.md` §6) apply.
10. **Run the audit** and hand off (§10).

## 11. Calibration bank

**Rival readings — keep all three alive.** For the same body of material there are three live explanations, and the corpus already runs the set for dietary law (`corpus/knowledge/culture-material/cuisine-food-lexicon.md:86`). **Douglas (1966)**: the code is category logic — the anomaly is abominable, and "holiness" means classificatory wholeness (`corpus/knowledge/myth-symbol/animal-symbolism.md:61`). **Milgrom (1991)**: the code expresses holiness as *separation from death* — carnivores and carrion-eaters are rejected because they eat death. **Harris (1985)**: the code is ecological cost — pigs compete with humans for grain and shade-water, cattle are worth more alive than dead. These are rival **levels**, not a winner to declare; write both (or all three) into the world and let the exegetes argue — "one law, two rival explanations — in-world purity debates write themselves" (`animal-symbolism.md:69`). **Douglas herself revised the reading** in *Leviticus as Literature* (1999), reading the book as a ring composition rather than a rule set; a designer who ports the 1966 engine should know its author later qualified it.

**Discredited frames — refuse on first use.** **Frazer's laws of contagion and similarity** (*The Golden Bough*, 1922) are discredited as ethnographic history, and the corpus registers Frazerian evolutionism as discredited (`corpus/knowledge/verification/calibration-registry.md:8`). They survive as a usable **emic folk model** of how taboo-speakers reason — say the name and it comes; the charge travels by contact — exactly as the corpus already uses them for taboo (`corpus/knowledge/linguistics/politeness-taboo-euphemism.md:73`). In-world the model should mostly *work*, because in-world the belief is what the characters act on. **Lévy-Bruhl's "primitive mentality" / pre-logical participation** (*La mentalité primitive*, 1922) is discredited and is named here only to be refused: pollution is not a pre-scientific error that reason outgrows, it is a classification with consequences, and the cultures that hold one are not deficient in logic. **Frazer's evolutionary stadialism** — magic → religion → science — is refused for the same reason.

**Four folk models this lane refuses.**
1. **"Purity codes are really about hygiene."** False as method, and the corpus already states the separation (`corpus/knowledge/culture-material/bathing-and-body-hygiene.md:50`). The two systems are different; their **leaks** are the design work, not their identity.
2. **"Purity codes are really about sex."** False. Reproduction is one source class among eight (S3), and in most attested systems the corpse (S1) is the stronger pollutant. A code reduced to sexual regulation is a modern anxiety wearing period clothes.
3. **"Purity codes decay into hygiene as a culture modernizes."** A stadial claim in Elias's shape (Elias 1939; in-lane at `corpus/knowledge/culture-material/cuisine-food-lexicon.md:92`). Elias's *history* is mainstream; the *law of refinement* is contested, and a designer should write threshold-drift as a **dated event with a cause**, not as a rung on a ladder.
4. **"Sacred = pure, profane = impure."** False, and it costs the designer §8's best material. Durkheim's sacred/profane is a *different* axis from pure/impure (`corpus/knowledge/verification/calibration-registry.md:10`), and the danger-of-holiness means the sacred can itself be the polluting agent — the red-heifer paradox (Num 19:7–10) is the proof text.

**Contested but usable.** **Dumont's purity-as-caste-rank** (*Homo Hierarchicus*, 1970) is mainstream and heavily contested; used as DR-PP-07's design lever it is powerful, and it is not a description of any real society's system. **Otto's numinous** (1917) is mainstream in the phenomenology of religion and contested as a universal; cited for the vocabulary of the danger-of-holiness, not as evidence. **Robertson Smith's identity of taboo and holiness** (1889) is historically important and superseded; cited as the intuition's source. **Bourdieu's disgust-as-class-marker** (1979; in-lane at `corpus/knowledge/linguistics/sociolinguistics-registers.md:97`) is mainstream and supplies the certification market's demand side.

## 12. NON-CANON worked micro-example (illustrative only — decides nothing)

*A fictional culture, the Sarn, is invented here only to show the grammar producing a code. Nothing below is a claim about any real culture.*

- **Classification first (§9 step 1).** The Sarn divide the living world into **the breathing** (people, herd animals, birds, the sea's fish) and **the still** (stone, salt, worked metal, the dead). Their category edge is the **breather that stops** — a thing that was in one class and is now in the other. That edge is where pollution lives.
- **Axis (§4).** Their polarity axes are *dry/wet*, *fed/unfed*, *pure/impure*, *still/breathing*; the purity axis is carried by **salt** (dry, still, unfed).
- **Sources (§3): three.** S1 the corpse (the breather that stopped); S4 the unfed food — a thing that has been eaten from but not finished (the *jootha* mechanism, borrowed at `cuisine-food-lexicon.md:94`); S6 the *drowned* — the person who died in the wet, whose body was never recovered into the still.
- **Grammar (§2).** S1: contact transmission, person-scope, high gradient, removable in 7 days, adjudicated by the salt-keeper, strong asymmetry. S4: contact transmission, object-scope, low gradient, removed by fire, adjudicated by the householder. S6: **no transmission** — the drowned's kin are not polluted; the drowned person is a *permanent* pollution in the water, which is why the sea is *unfed* and must not be eaten from.
- **Ladder (§5).** S1 → rung 1 (time, 7 days) and rung 2 (salt-water immersion at the salt-keeper's pool); S4 → rung 8 (the unfinished food is burned); S6 → **rung 10, the null case** — nothing cleans the drowned, so the culture's answer is *avoidance*, and the sea's edge is a permanent boundary.
- **Precinct (§6).** Innermost: the salt-keeper's dry pool. Qualified court: the salt-house. Common: the village. Outside: **the ebb-line**, where the drowned are mourned and where nothing is built. Thresholds are tested by salt — a pinch on the tongue for a person, a salt cast for a boat.
- **Policing (§7).** Inspector = the salt-keeper (open office, not hereditary → **class, not rank**); court = the elders' salt-verdict; calendar = the seven-day release; registry = the tide-marks cut into the pool's rim.
- **The tell (§5 rung 6).** The salt-keeper alone may say the release formula, which makes them the culture's only purity expert — and their fee is the village's standing grievance. That grievance is the scene.
