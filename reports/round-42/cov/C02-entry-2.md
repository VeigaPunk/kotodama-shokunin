## 1. What this entry licenses, and the boundary it draws

- A **boundary-map procedure**, not a doctrine: how to decide what counts as pollution in a fictional culture, how it travels, who carries it, and what discharges it — and what the resulting rule set says about the culture's categories.
- The master claim, stated once: **purity is not a substance; it is a boundary map.** A culture is not "clean" or "unclean" in the abstract — it has drawn categories, and pollution is what the culture believes crosses them. This is why `animal-symbolism.md:69` can hold two rival explanations of one law without contradiction: the *rule* is real, the *reason* is argued.
- The partition boundary, in three lines:

## 2. The seven polarity axes — what a purity system can run on

A purity system declares **which axes are load-bearing** and which are merely polite. Real systems run three to five; a culture running all seven is a culture that cannot eat.

| # | axis | pollution is… | attested anchor (in-lane) | design consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | inside / outside | crossing the group's threshold | Hajj *ihram* and the sacred enclosure (`ritual-garments.md:28`) | gates, thresholds, and a boundary office |
| 2 | whole / broken | loss of completeness | Lev 11's hoof-and-cud conjunction; Douglas's classificatory wholeness (`animal-symbolism.md:69`) | the law becomes morphology-checkable |
| 3 | ordered / anomalous | the category-straddler | bat, pig, barnacle goose (`symbolism-synthesis-method.md:68`) | one boundary-straddler per boundary, loaded |
| 4 | living / dead | the corpse, the mourner, the killer | Zoroastrian Druj Nasu vs Egyptian corpse-necessity (`death-afterlife-eschatology.md:49`) | decides burial, exposure, and whether the dead may be touched |
| 5 | own / other | the stranger, the foreigner, the captive | the *pharmakos* exiled for the group (`ritual-liminality.md:57`) | the system's most dangerous export |
| 6 | cooked / raw | the unprocessed, the unmixed | the culinary triangle as generator; *shaʿatnez* wool-linen ban (`cuisine-food-lexicon.md:3`; `textile-technology.md:86`) | the kitchen becomes a purity site |
| 7 | clean / dirty-as-matter | the physically out-of-place | the hygiene side, deliberately *not* this entry's (`bathing-and-body-hygiene.md:50`) | the antisepsis trap (§7) |

- **The load-bearing rule:** choose 3–5 axes, not 7, and declare them in-world. A culture whose citizens cannot name which axis a given rule runs on has rules without a system — which is realistic for a *decayed* purity code and is an era signal, not a design failure.
- **The anti-conflation rule, from in-lane:** `israel-ancient-hebrew.md:150` records "Sacred / common; clean / unclean" as **intersecting** classifications, "not interchangeable labels for good/evil or ethnic worth." Four quadrants, not one ladder. The most common worldbuilding error is collapsing them into a single morality dial.
- **The axis-to-register coupling:** the axes are where this entry meets the register files. `direction-symbolism.md:25` already puts `pure/impure` on the direction register's valence axis; an impure direction is this entry's boundary map written spatially. `color-symbolism.md:11,:134` runs the same coupling for color — and carries the explicit warning not to port "white = purity" (`symbolism-synthesis-method.md:136` audit rule 10).

## 3. The anomaly engine, and its two rival readings

- **The engine** (cited, not restated — `symbolism-synthesis-method.md:68`, `animal-symbolism.md:69`): the thing that crosses a category boundary carries the pollution load. Assign one boundary-straddler per major boundary; load it.
- **Two rival readings, both kept alive in-world** — this is the partition's own established pattern for one law with two explanations (`animal-symbolism.md:69`; `cuisine-food-lexicon.md:86` names three: Douglas's anomaly, Milgrom's holiness-as-separation, Harris's ecology):
  - **Classificatory** (Douglas): the pig fails the hoof/cud conjunction, so it is abominable *because* it is wrong for its kind. Purity rules encode cosmology.
  - **Materialist** (Harris; Milgrom for the holiness variant): the rule is ecologically or doctrinally motivated and the anomaly reading is a retrofit.
- **The craft consequence:** write the rule, then write the *argument*. A world where nobody disputes the reason for its purity law is a world whose purity law is furniture. `animal-symbolism.md:69`'s closing phrase — "one law, two rival explanations — in-world purity debates write themselves" — is the whole design lesson, and this entry does not improve on it, only applies it at system scale.
- **Calibration:** the anomaly account is **mainstream as a method and contested as a universal** — the partition records Douglas's own limit at `symbolism-synthesis-method.md:40` ("her grid/group sociology is a separate tool"). Do not present "anomaly ⇒ taboo" as a law of cultures. It is a generator.

## 4. The four contamination carriers — and why the carrier choice fixes everything downstream

The single most useful design decision in a purity system is **how pollution travels**, because the carrier determines the purification apparatus, the enforcement office, and the scandal shape.

| carrier | pollution moves by | attested anchor (in-lane) | what it forces downstream |
|---|---|---|---|
| **contact** | touch, body fluid, corpse, object | corpse doctrine and the dakhma (`death-afterlife-eschatology.md:49,:99`); *Mikvaot* immersion rules (`bathing-and-body-hygiene.md:51`) | boundary officers, quarantines, disposal rules, an object ledger |

- **The immaterial carrier is the one worldbuilders forget**, and it is the one that couples this entry to the lane's namesake ground. A culture where pollution travels by *word* has no quarantine and no bath — it has a speech code, a name-avoidance register, and an office that polices utterance. `animal-symbolism.md:46` records the mechanism as a "name-taboo animal" row; `politeness-taboo-euphemism.md:21,:73` owns the lexical machinery. **This entry owns the status side of the same carrier.**
- **Carrier multiplicity is realistic.** A real system usually runs two carriers (typically contact + ingestion for the body, proximity + immaterial for the group). Declare which are load-bearing; the rest are etiquette.

Immaterial ⇒ a speech code and a name register. Choose the carrier and the entry you need to read next is determined.

## 5. The agents — who is the pollution

`ritual-liminality.md:57` names the payoff of the piacular rite as "the institution that decides who is the pollution." That institution is the load-bearing one, and it has five attribution regimes:

| regime | the pollution is… | attested anchor (in-lane) | the design risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the polluter** | the one who acted | the killer, the mourner, the transgressor (`ritual-liminality.md:32`) | guilt and pollution collapse into one dial — usually wrong |
| **the anomalous thing** | the category-straddler itself | the pig, the bat, the barnacle goose (`symbolism-synthesis-method.md:68`) | the object gets punished; write animal trials as the real historical extreme (`animal-symbolism.md:75`, Evans 1906) |
| **the stranger** | whoever is outside | the *pharmakos* exiled at Thargelia (`ritual-liminality.md:57`) | the system becomes an ethnic weapon — write the faction that says so |
| **the office** | whoever holds the rank | royal purity and the substitute-king (`sacred-kingship-mythic-charter.md:96,:97,:120`); Vedic status-change purification (`india-vedic.md:126`) | the holder is polluted *by the role*, not by an act |
| **the unattributable** | no one — it is simply present | pollution with no responsible party | **this is what a purity crisis actually looks like** |

- **The unattributable regime is the most useful and the least used.** A system where every pollution has a culprit is a system with a courtroom. A system that also has *ambient* pollution — the season, the place, the aftermath of a battle, the year itself — needs a calendar of discharges rather than a docket of verdicts. The piacular/expiation row at `ritual-liminality.md:57` and the purification-runs row at `:79` (Lupercalia, well attested) are two real shapes of it.
- **Attribution is political.** `java-majapahit.md:146` warns against reducing water infrastructure to "a universal purification cult"; `persia-sasanian.md:76` warns that coupling purification to stewardship vocabulary is a *design choice*, "not an undocumented Sasanian institution." Both warnings are the same rule: name the attribution regime you are writing, and label it as a design choice where the record does not say.

## 6. Purity as a transferable status — the acquisition and loss table

The second master move: **purity is a status a person holds, not a bodily fact.** It can be acquired, lost, lent, and spent. Write it as a four-by-four.

| | acquisition route | loss route |
|---|---|---|
| **ascribed** | birth into a rank; the kin group's standing | kin-link: the immaterial carrier runs through relatives |
| **achieved** | rite completion; ordeal survived | transgression; contact; ingestion |
| **conferred** | office, initiation, the purity profession | deposition; the office is vacated or the incumbent is polluted |
| **ambient** | the place, the day, the season | the same, expired |

- **The rule that makes this useful:** a culture's purity *market* is readable from which cells are open. A culture where purity is only ascribed is a caste system with no exit; one where it is only achieved is a meritocracy of rites; one with a **conferred** cell has a profession and a fee; one with an **ambient** cell has a calendar.
- **The inversion case, in-lane and exact:** `ritual-garments.md:62` — on Yom Kippur the high priest strips gold and blue and serves in four plain linen garments. "Office is real, and it is *suspended* in the Presence." Purity rank is not a monotonic ladder; approach to the sacred can require *lowering* it. `ritual-garments.md:28` gives the general row (liminal dress is uniform, undyed, anonymous). A fictional cult with no such day has no way to express the difference between rank and fitness.
- **The cost rule:** every purity status a character holds is a resource with a maintenance cost. `ritual-liminality.md:32`'s funerary sequence (body washed, mourners cut off, interval, redistribution) is a purity status moving through a schedule with an economy attached.

## 7. The purification apparatus — a discharge ledger matched to carrier

Purification is not a mood; it is a **matched discharge** for a named carrier. Six families, each with its carrier and its cost:

| family | discharges | attested anchor (in-lane) | cost / failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ablution** | contact, ingestion | *mikveh* and the 40 *se'ah* rule; *ghusl*/*wuḍūʾ*; baptism; Kumbh river bathing; *petirtaan*; Vedic bathing (`bathing-and-body-hygiene.md:51`; `ritual-liminality.md:86`; `java-majapahit.md:146`; `india-vedic.md:126`) | needs water, a place, and a rule for what makes water fit |
| **lustration** | proximity | purification runs and lustration (`ritual-liminality.md:79`) | needs a route and a schedule; fails silently |
| **substitution** | attributed pollution | the scapegoat, the *pharmakos*, the substitute-king (`ritual-liminality.md:57`; `sacred-kingship-mythic-charter.md:96`) | needs a victim supply — the system's ugliest dependency |
| **confession / declaration** | immaterial | the word-carrier's own discharge | needs an audience and an authority; the confession is itself a speech act |
| **ordeal** | contested attribution | the trial that decides who is polluted | needs a result the culture will accept — and the result is a political fact |
| **time** | ambient | the interval; mourning duration; the calendar | needs a calendar and someone to enforce expiry |

- **The matching rule:** a discharge that does not match the carrier does not work — and *that is a design feature*, not a bug. A culture whose official purification is ablution while its actual fear is the immaterial carrier has a live doctrinal crisis. `bathing-and-body-hygiene.md:50`'s rule ("they are different systems and must not be merged") is the same point from the other side.
- **The antisepsis trap, stated explicitly:** *status*-pollution and *hygiene*-contamination are different systems, and merging them is the single most common modern-worldbuilding error. But note the historical reverse: real cultures *do* rationalize a status rule as hygiene (and modern public health has inherited status language — `number-symbolism.md:90`'s *quarantena*, forty days, is a sacred-adjacent number escaping into public health law). Write the conflation as a *belief inside the world*, never as the entry's own analysis.

## 8. Enforcement, economy, and the profession

- **The purity profession.** Where a system has a **conferred** status cell (§6) it has specialists: the reader who declares a person impure (`divination-omen-systems.md:100` — "the reader was impure" as a procedural escape), the official who grants a discharge, the inspector of vessels, the slaughter-licenser, the burial contractor. `divination-omen-systems.md:202` requires the system to carry "one procedural escape" or die at its first visible failure — the purity office *is* that escape in many systems, and it is therefore also the system's most corruptible node.
- **The economy.** Purity is priced wherever it can be conferred: fees for discharge, tolls at thresholds, licensed suppliers of clean goods, the resale of second-hand purity. `trade-economy-lexicon.md` owns the measure-and-market machinery; this entry supplies the good. The pattern to write: **who profits from an impurity that cannot be proved either way.**
- **The scandal market.** A purity system with a *contested attribution* cell generates scandal as a product — accusations, counters, and the reputational market that trades in them. `eschatology-world-end-systems.md:105,:122` names the endpoint: a purity movement that converts a verdict into present-tense repair. The escalation ladder is short and worth writing deliberately: **rule → dispute → movement → purge.**
- **The failure modes**, named so a designer can choose one: (a) the unfalsifiable system (every outcome confirms the rule — the calibration critique of the anomaly account), (b) the exhausted system (so many load-bearing axes that ordinary life is impossible), (c) the captured system (the office sells the discharge), (d) the collapsed system (the culture stops believing and keeps the forms — an era signal, and the most interesting one).

## 9. Era drift — how a purity system changes

Six mechanisms, each leaving a different fossil:

| mechanism | what changes | fossil it leaves |
|---|---|---|
| **rationalization** | the stated reason is replaced (anomaly → hygiene) | the rule survives its explanation; the old reason becomes folklore |
| **relaxation** | axes drop out of load-bearing | rules nobody can explain; a "decayed code" |
| **intensification** | an axis is promoted | a purity movement (`eschatology-world-end-systems.md:122`) |
| **transfer** | a rule moves carrier (contact → immaterial) | a quarantine that becomes a speech code |
| **capture** | the profession takes the system | fee schedules in the record; the scandal market |
| **revival** | a lapsed system is reconstructed | invented tradition; the `symbolism-synthesis-method.md` era-versioning discipline |

- **Coupling to the lexical drift:** `politeness-taboo-euphemism.md:73` and the two taboo-lifecycle entries (`linguistics/taboo-strata-lifecycle.md`, `synthesis/taboo-euphemism-lifecycle.md`) own the *word* drift; the mechanisms above are the same six shapes running on *statuses*. A culture's purity reform and its euphemism treadmill are usually the same event reported twice — which is a plot device, and a dating tool for in-world documents.
- **The dating rule:** when a purity rule's stated reason does not match its carrier, the rule is older than the reason. `politeness-taboo-euphemism.md:73`'s "matter out of place" is the diagnosis; the fossil is the evidence.

## 11. Application protocol — building a culture's purity system

1. **Fix the material substrate first** (`symbolism-synthesis-method.md:47`, step 0): water availability, disposal practice, food system, and whether the culture has a calendar. A purity system with no water and no calendar runs on the immaterial carrier and on time, and looks completely different.
2. **Declare 3–5 load-bearing axes** from §2, in-world, by name. Write the two the culture *argues about*.
3. **Choose 1–2 carriers** from §4. Then write the apparatus the carrier forces — and only that apparatus.
4. **Assign one boundary-straddler per major boundary** (§3), and load it. One per boundary; more is noise.
5. **Name the attribution regime** (§5) — including whether the culture has the *unattributable* cell. If it does, build a calendar of discharges; if it does not, build a docket.
6. **Write the acquisition/loss table** (§6) and mark which cells are open. The open cells are the culture's purity market and its politics.
7. **Match a discharge to each carrier** (§7). Then write the mismatch the culture officially denies.
8. **Name the profession and the fee** (§8), and the failure mode the culture is currently in (§8).
9. **Date the code** (§9): pick a drift mechanism and leave its fossil in the record.
10. **Audit before handoff**: no unqualified universal claim (`symbolism-synthesis-method.md:136` rule 10); no ported real-world code (`color-symbolism.md:11`); the status/hygiene boundary intact (`bathing-and-body-hygiene.md:50`); the clean/unclean and good/evil quadrants un-collapsed (`israel-ancient-hebrew.md:150`); every "unclean" reported as source terminology (`inuit.md:140`); canon left to lore-shokunin.

## 12. NON-CANON worked micro-example

A river-delta culture. **Substrate:** annual flood, abundant water, rice and fish, a lunar calendar, cremation. **Axes (3, load-bearing):** inside/outside, living/dead, cooked/raw. **Carriers (2):** contact (the corpse, the flood mud) and immaterial (a spoken curse, and the name of the drowned). **Boundary-straddler:** the eel — it is a fish that walks the mud between the river and the field, so it is the loaded animal and its season is closed. **Attribution:** the polluter, plus an ambient cell (the flood month itself). **Status table:** purity is achieved by the funeral rite and conferred by the tide-priest; ambient in the flood month; lost by kin-link, so a family's standing moves with its dead. **Discharges:** ablution at the confluence (contact) and a name-release recitation (immaterial); time discharges the ambient. **Profession:** the tide-priest sells the name-release, and the scandal is that the fee is higher in the flood month — which is exactly when the ambient pollution is already high. **Drift:** rationalization, in progress — the eel taboo is now defended as a fishery rule, and the older generation still calls it a boundary. **Audit:** no universal claims; the "eel" is the culture's own boundary-straddler, not a ported pig; the status/hygiene boundary holds; canon deferred to lore-shokunin.

- **What the example demonstrates, in one line each:** the carrier choice generated the whole apparatus; the ambient cell generated the calendar; the kin-link loss generated the family-politics plot; and the drift fossil is a dating tool for in-world documents.
