Description:
This object represents the "Hand of Fatima" also known as 
Khamsa
 (the Arabic word for "Five"), "Hand of Venus" or "Aphrodite". Jewish silversmiths in Morocco became known for casting and engraving pieces such as this one, used as a talismanic doorknob or jewellery pendant.



Carthaginian excavations traced the open hand to ancient Egypt, whose variations appear across all faiths and time as a representation of God and believed to grant protection against ill will. In Islam, Fatima, the beloved daughter of Prophet Muhammed (PBUH) defends anyone regardless of faith against evil.



The hand appears symmetrical with the five fingers indistinguishable, embodying the secret codes of the number five in Islam. The fingers allude to the five pillars of Islam, five daily prayers and five keys to the unseen. While silver casts additional amuletic power, the phoenix and salamanders symbolize life, rebirth and fertility.
