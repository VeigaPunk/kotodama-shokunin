Ca06 assembles; Ca09 checks inventory coverage; Ca07/Ca08 independently sample; Ca13 audits mismatch causes; Ca16 checks final consistency (`targets.json:19-34`). Do not let an O queue disposition overwrite a Ca measurement.
- **O owns defect lifecycle:** one canonical queue row per defect, with its source references, fixed-with-evidence or carried disposition, and one accountable owner. O01 drafts; O02 verifies claimed fixes as a non-author; O03 checks carried ownership/reason/next-touch; O05/O06 check census equality and fixed-evidence pointers; O09 routes carried items to round 38+ (`targets.json:36-47`).
- Draft Ca06 and O01 independently from their inventories, then reconcile by stable source path + finding ID. Store cross-references (for example `recount_ref`) rather than cloning a finding into multiple queue rows. IDs such as `RC-4` are local to a source file: distinguish `recount-ledger.json#/findings/RC-4` from a command row such as `#/rows/RC-33-4` (`reports/round-36/calib/recount-ledger.json:65-76,192-215`).
- “Owner” means the person/role accountable for the next action, not the seat that discovered or re-ran a command. Where the source says `to-verify`, keep attribution unknown; do not infer the writer from the incident owner. The r36 ledger explicitly assigns the RC-1 and RC-2 actions to the integrator, leaves RC-1 cause `to-verify`, and assigns RC-4 to the integrator (`recount-ledger.json:211-221`).
