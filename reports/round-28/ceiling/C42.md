## 2. The general lesson, and why it is a lesson and not a restatement

The entry's one-sentence claim:

> **A register's derivation operation constrains which semantic fields it can
> natively spell.**

That is general. The partition previously taught how to *build* a register's
lexicon (`sociolinguistics-registers.md` §4, `semantic-fields-lexicon-building.md`
§3–§4, `linguistic-anthropology.md` §5) and how to state a *law* over it
(`register-strata-attestation.md`). None of them said that the **operation** the
register is built by is a function with a **domain**, and that the domain — not
the word list — is what bounds coverage. The entry's §2 is the load-bearing
distinction: an **operation ceiling** (the operation cannot produce a legal form
at all) is not **blocking** (Aronoff 1976: a listed form pre-empts a rule-derived
one) and not a **lexical accident** (Bauer 1983: nobody has coined it yet). The
three have incompatible remedies, and misdiagnosing the first as the third is what
produces "just coin more words" — or worse, a gate widened until the illegal forms
pass.
