## 1. The predicate, derived

The D4 syllable template is `(C)V(V)(C)`
(`examples/culture-d4/components/02-phonology.yml:82`), with `onset_clusters:
false` (`:92`) and a single-consonant coda drawn from eight consonants
(`:119`). The inventory has fourteen consonants (`:61-75`). Therefore:

- A syllable may **begin** with any of the 14 consonants (or no consonant,
  word-initially inside a root).
- A syllable may **end** with one of only 8 (`n s k l r t d m`).
- The six remaining consonants — `b g h j p w` — may only be onsets.

`reverse` is character reversal (`tools/gates/argot-derivation-gate.py:239`), so
the base's **first** character becomes the form's **last**. A form whose last
character is not a licensed coda has an illegal final syllable. Hence:

> a base may be reversed **iff** its initial consonant is one of the eight codas.

Stated as the instrument does:

```
reverse is legal  <=>  the base-initial consonant is a licensed coda
```

## 3. Why this is the design-relevant finding

The mechanism converts a **description** into a **decision rule**.

- **It says where to look.** Before choosing an operation, check the **coda set**
  against the fields that must be sayable — not the word list, which will change.
- **It says what cannot be fixed.** Because the constraint is arithmetic over the
  template, **no amount of coinage closes it**. A designer who reads the ceiling
  as a lexical gap will add words and re-measure the same ratio. This is the
  single most important consequence, and it is why the round-26 refusal to add an
  "escape hatch for abstractions" is recorded as a decision rather than an
  oversight (`reports/round-26/register/review.md` §3).
- **It predicts the direction of the ceiling.** A reversal's ceiling is about
  **initial** consonants. A lengthening operation's ceiling would be about which
  segment may be lengthened; the D4 `lengthen` is the *stressed* root-initial
  vowel (`examples/culture-d4/components/04-lexicon.yml:112-116`, declared as the
  `LENGTHEN` pattern/output pair in the packet's `morphology.derivation_rules`),
  so its domain is
  broader and it produced no illegal form in this tree (measured: 33 of 33
  fire-axis roots lengthen legally — not this entry's claim, and not used by it).
  A truncation's ceiling would be about minimum length. The predicate form
  transfers; the consonant list does not.

## 4. Limits, stated so the predicate is not over-read

- **Template-specific.** The biconditional holds for `(C)V(V)(C)` with a single
  licensed coda and no onset clusters. A language with onset clusters, long codas,
  or word-final vowel restrictions has a different predicate. §13.5 and the new
  entry's §10 both say so.
- **Reader-dependent.** The predicate is stated in terms of "legal", which is
  whatever the legality reader decides. §4.2 of the new entry is the worked
  demonstration that the reader moved the count. The mechanism is stable across
  the two readers (it is a statement about consonant positions, not about
  syllabification), but the *counts* are not.
- **One tree.** Measured against packet `543d68a8…` and gate `b5406bcd…` at HEAD
  `69cecbc`. Both move; the instrument prints both digests on every run.
- **Not a claim about any real language.** The mechanism is arithmetic over a
  NON-CANON drill's template.
