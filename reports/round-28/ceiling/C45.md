## 1. The record, and why seven rows

The lane's entry shape asks for a decision record — rules the entry **adopts**,
each with evidence, a consequence if violated, and a recheck trigger (the shape
`verification-independence-anti-circularity.md` §8 uses and which both round-27
and round-28 entry instruments recognise: `entry-audit.py` `DECISION_RE`;
the round-28 runner's `DECISION_RECORD_RE`). Seven rows, because the entry states
seven separable rules:

| ID | rule | evidence class |
|---|---|---|
| `RLC-1` | a register built by a mechanical operation must have its **domain measured** before its law is written | **measured** (`19/8`; `4 of 5` abstractions refusing) |
| `RLC-2` | the ceiling is stated as a **predicate over the input**, not a word list | **measured** (`27/27` biconditional) |
| `RLC-3` | operation ceiling ≠ blocking (Aronoff 1976) ≠ lexical accident (Bauer 1983) | external + method |
| `RLC-4` | a legality count is only as good as the **reader**; a moved reader moves the count | **measured** (filed 18/9 vs delivered 19/8, delta `L-45`) |
| `RLC-5` | the three dispositions are **exhaustive**, and each forbids one claim | method + the D4 disposition |
| `RLC-6` | if the ceiling is accepted, its **price is declared with the law** | the D4 `LL-1`/`LL-2` declaration |
| `RLC-7` | a register's **readability** ≠ its **coverage** | method + Labov/Irvine-Gal by path |

**Three of the seven rest on this round's measurement** (`RLC-1`, `RLC-2`,
`RLC-4`), which is the ratio the entry claims and the reason it is not a
restatement of the round-27 design.

**Two labels I checked hardest, because they are the ones a reader would take on
trust:**

1. **The verlan analysis is labelled as contested in its *account*, not in its
   *data*.** The attested orders are data; `LOOP`+`MIRROR` is Gotowski's
   explanation of them, and the entry names the competing accounts (Plénat 1995;
   Weinberger & Lefkowitz 1992). A reader who needs only "the domain is
   constrained" can use the data; a reader who needs the mechanism must know it is
   one analysis.
2. **Aronoff is used for the diagnosis, not for a claim about the ceiling.**
   Blocking is lexical; the ceiling is phonological. §2 of the entry keeps them
   apart, and `RLC-3` is the row that makes the separation a rule. Conflating them
   would produce the wrong remedy ("coin the word and the gap closes"), which is
   exactly what the mechanism in §13.3 rules out.
