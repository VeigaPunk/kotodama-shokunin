## 2. The split between measured and adopted

Five of the ten rows (`RSA-1`, `RSA-4`, `RSA-6`, `RSA-9`, `RSA-10`) rest on a
**command run this round**, and each names its counters. The other five rest on
method or on an external framework, and the entry says so in the evidence column
rather than dressing a design opinion as a measurement.

This split is the point. A decision record whose every row cites a measurement
is either a very thin record or a dishonest one; a record whose rows are all
opinion cannot be rechecked. The entry mixes them **and labels which is which**,
so a reader knows exactly which rows a later round can falsify with a command.

## 3. The two rows that only exist because of the mid-round landing

`RSA-9` and `RSA-10` were not in the entry's first draft. They were added after
the D seat landed the law and the entry was re-measured, and they generalise the
two defects that re-measurement exposed:

- **`RSA-9` — address, registration and fields.** The five `RGL` rows exist in
  the packet, in a **new top-level key** `register_law:`, while the auditor that
  measures this exact gap read `registers:`. So a packet containing the law
  returned `HOLD` (`law_rows=0 speech_act_rows=0`). **That half was closed later
  in the round** — the auditor now reads the block and every row gained a
  `force`, so `law_rows=5 speech_act_rows=5` and `L6` is gone. What survives is
  the *registration* half, and it is sharper: `x59`'s `L9-HOLD` reports that the
  assembler declares `register_law` in **neither** `PACKET_KEYS` nor
  `RECORD_FIELDS`, so stripping an `id` from a `register_law` row assembles exit
  0 and lands `id: None`, while the same edit to a `lexicon` row refuses exit 2.
  The general rule: *landing the rows is not landing the law*; name the key,
  **register the block with the assembler**, and populate the fields your own
  schema declares. The `speaker` field is still empty at the last stamp.
- **`RSA-10` — pins move with the surface.** The packet moved
  (`dd25077b…` → `c5441e3d…`) and its pins did not, so
  `typed-pipeline-gate.py` reported `FAIL L1-profile` naming all three stale
  digests. **Discharged later in the round:** the O seat re-pinned
  `tools/gates/typed-pipelines.json` profile `d4` to `86079a67…`, and the same
  command now returns `summary: 20/20 legs passed`, exit 0. The general rule
  stands and is now *demonstrated end to end*: a landing that touches
  `examples/culture-d4/` owes a re-pin, and a stale pin is a verification
  failure, not cosmetic drift. This is the same class the round-26 landing
  recorded in `reports/round-26/register/repin.json`
  (`cascade_reported_not_repaired`).

Both rows are recorded with the **observed** evidence at each stamp and neither
is repaired by this seat: the component, the packet and the pins belong to the D
and O claims. `RSA-10`'s failure was repaired by its owner, not by this entry;
the entry records the repair and does not claim credit for it.
