## 1. Why an external leg was required at all

The claim text reads "cited external scholarship for the speech-act framework".
The partition already carries Austin and Searle as one-line mentions
(`semantics-pragmatics.md:88`), so an entry that only repeated them would be
filler. The external leg does two jobs the mentions cannot:

1. **It supplies the vocabulary the law is written in** — felicity conditions,
   misfires vs abuses, the five classes, constitutive rules, deontic powers.
2. **It supplies the calibration** — which of those frameworks is mainstream,
   which is contested in its details, and which framing is botched. Without the
   second job an entry hands a designer a generator with no failure warning.

## 3. The three calibration claims the entry actually makes

Calibration is only useful if it changes a decision. Three of §9's rows do:

- **"Some words are inherently performative" — botched.** Austin abandoned the
  performative/constative dichotomy in favour of illocutionary force. Consequence
  for a designer: no word is magic by nature; the institution is what confers
  force. (An in-world word that *is* magic by sound is a legitimate different
  system — but it must not be labelled speech-act theory.)
- **"Binding speech is primitive and modern law is rational" — discredited in
  this form.** Documentary legal language is *more* formulaic than most ritual
  speech. Consequence: a designer building a "rational" chancery register should
  make it *more* frozen, not less.
- **Searle's constitutive rules — contested in the details.** Consequence: the
  entry uses the X-counts-as-Y form as a **generator** and does not rest any
  lane rule on it. The register-law rows are built from Austin's conditions and
  the delivered tree's institutions, not from Searle's ontology.

## 5. Limits

- The entry is **not** a survey of speech-act theory; it uses four results and
  discards the rest of the field (conventional implicature, presupposition,
  illocutionary logic) as out of scope.
- No claim here is about any real community's practice; each real case is a
  cited design exemplar.
- The Searle contestation is reported from an encyclopedia's summary of Guala
  and Hindriks, not from their papers; a round needing the argument itself must
  fetch it.
