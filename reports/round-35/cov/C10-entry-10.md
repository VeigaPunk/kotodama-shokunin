**One further boundary is a red line, not a division of labour.** Several of the strongest exemplars here come from living traditions (Yoruba *àṣẹ*, Ifa; Nuer cursing; Moroccan *baraka*). `../verification/myth-consistency-gate.md:20` states the house rule — "Do not expose restricted ritual knowledge to fill a table. Mark an access boundary and choose a less extractive comparison" — and `../verification/real-world-inspiration-boundaries.md:91` gives the red lines. This entry therefore cites living-practice material only through published scholarship, at the level of the reported institution, and designs no fictional system as a copy of a real one's ritual detail.

## 2. Core framework — one machinery, four channels, one ledger

**The craft maxim: a word holds only where an executor exists.** Every binding word in a real myth system resolves to a party who *does something* when the word is broken — a god who punishes, an institution that seizes, a mechanism that fires, or a court that tries. Where no executor exists, the word is not binding; it is etiquette. The commonest failure in invented systems is a culture of mighty oaths whose oaths cost nothing, because the designer wrote the formula and forgot the executioner. The design question is never "how solemn is this oath?" but **"who finds out, and what do they do?"**

**The binding triangle.** Every instance of binding speech has three positions, and a designer who fixes all three has a working system:

1. **Speaker** — who may utter it (one person, a class, anyone; and in which register — the register law is the neighbour entry's object).
2. **Word** — what was said, and whether it is *fixed* (a memorized formula that no one's will can alter) or *free* (improvised). Fixed words bind by form; free words bind by institution. That distinction is stated and developed at `../linguistics/register-strata-attestation.md:74-89`; this entry uses it and does not restate it.
3. **Executor** — the party or mechanism that acts on breach. This is the position that generates the culture.

**The four channels, and the direction each binds.** The four channels are not a list; they are one machinery with four directions:

| Channel | Direction of binding | What is bound | Minimal form |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Oath** | self-binding, with a witness | the speaker's future conduct | "I will do X; if not, may Y befall me" |
| **Curse** | other-binding, without consent | another party's body, property, fertility, or fortune | "may Y befall you, because of Z" |
| **Blessing** | other-binding, without consent, toward good | another party's fortune, and often their descendants | "may Y be yours, because of Z" |
| **Name-power** | object-binding through reference | the named party's identity, and thus everything held in their name | "the name is spoken, written, withheld, or replaced" |

- **The four channels share one engine.** All four require (a) a *formula or formula-class*, (b) an *executor*, and (c) an *uptake* — someone who hears and is bound by having heard. A culture that has one channel almost always has all four, because the machinery is the same; what varies is the *licensing* (who may utter which channel) and the *executor* (who or what acts).
- **Curse and blessing are one channel with two valences.** They are the same speech act with the polarity flipped, and real systems know it: Deuteronomy 27–28 is one liturgy with two columns, the people answering *Amen* to each; Norse *níð* and *blessan* are opposed formulas in the same metre; the Latin *benedictio* and *maledictio* are morphologically twin. Design them as a pair, because a culture's blessing rules are where its curse rules show their seams: a system where blessings are irrevocable but curses are cheap is internally inconsistent unless the culture *says* why.
- **Name-power is the channel that makes the other three possible.** An oath sworn by a god's name binds the god as executor; a curse delivered "in the name of" X routes the sanction through X; a blessing carried by a lineage's name travels through the name. Name-power is therefore the *substrate* channel — and this is why `../linguistics/naming-systems.md` §6's machinery (what may be said, by whom) sits underneath everything here.

**The one rule that keeps this entry safe.** No claim below says what any real community believes *now*, or that any real practice is a survival of anything. The exemplars are cited as *attested institutions in texts and scholarship*, and the design material is invented. `../myth-symbol/myth-ritual-theories.md:30-36` records what happened to the school that read ritual and myth as survivals of a primitive stage (Frazer → Cambridge → collapse); this entry does not repeat that error, and §12 flags it.

## 3. Master channel table

The working table a designer fills. Every column is a decision; the last two columns are where invented systems usually fail.

| Channel | Genre | Speaker licensed | Uptake required | Executor | Sanction shape | Release | Failure mode | Visibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Oath** | sworn assertion or promise, with witness and self-imprecation | office-holders, soldiers, officials, witnesses, anyone in a court | the witness (human or divine) and the audience | god, community, or institution | forfeit, exile, death, sacral outlawry, loss of office, the self-curse taking effect | expiation, substitute victim, annulment by the authority that licensed it, expiry with the term | the **unwitnessed oath** — no one can check it, so it binds nothing and the culture knows it | high: oaths are public, formulaic, and often recorded |
| **Curse** | imprecation against a party, with cause stated or suppressed | the wronged, the dying, the parent, the professional, the licensed practitioner | none needed — curses are usually unilateral | god(s), an automatic mechanism, or the dead | illness, childlessness, blight, sleeplessness, death, dynastic failure | rare and expensive: reversal rite, counter-curse, transfer, appeasement | the **unaimed curse** — spillover onto the household or the utterer | low to medium: often hidden, deposited, or anonymous |
| **Blessing** | benediction over a party, with or without stated cause | the senior (parent, priest, ruler, saint-figure), the departing, the dying | the recipient's presence; sometimes a spoken formula in return | god(s) or an inherited quality | fertility, prosperity, longevity, succession, protection | generally **none** — this is the channel's defining property | the **recalled blessing** — a blessing that can be revoked is a promise, and the culture's stories must know it | medium: blessings are public at life-crises, private otherwise |
| **Name-power** | naming, withholding, erasing, renaming, or refusing a name | depends on the operation: parents, rulers, priests, scribes, initiates | the named party, and the community that uses the name | the name itself (automatic), or the institution that enforces the taboo | loss of standing, loss of protection, exposure to the other channels, or the taboo's penalty | renaming, restoration, the taboo's expiry (a reign, a lifetime, a mourning period) | the **over-tabooed name** — a name no one may say is a name no one can use in law or trade | high in writing cultures (missing characters are datable), low in oral ones |

- **Read the table as a dependency graph, not a menu.** Executor availability drives everything: a culture with no institution that can seize property cannot have a forfeiture oath; a culture with no god who cares about speech has to invent one or route its oaths through the dead, the ancestors, the city, or the archive. Choose the executor first and the formula will write itself; choose the formula first and you will get a system with beautiful words and no teeth.
- **Visibility is a plot dial.** A channel with high visibility produces public drama (the trial, the assembly, the witnessed vow); a channel with low visibility produces the hidden-crime plot (the curse buried under the threshold, the name struck from the register). A culture can be characterized by which channels it makes visible and which it hides — and a story's tension comes from the mismatch (a public oath culture with a hidden curse practice).

## 4. The oath

**Formula anatomy.** The attested oath is a four-part machine, and a worldbuilder can vary each part independently:

| Part | Function | Real forms | Design question |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Invocation** | names the witness(es) | Zeus, Earth, Sun, and the Erinyes (*Iliad* 19); Styx (*Theogony* 775–806); "by the gods", "by my father's head", "by the ring" | which powers can be called to witness — and what happens when a culture has no such power (§4.4) |
| **Assertion or promise** | states what is sworn | the terms of the truce (*Iliad* 3); the recruit's undertaking (Polybius 6) | is the content a statement, a future act, or both? |
| **Self-imprecation** | turns the sanction on the speaker | "may God do so to me and more also" (1 Sam 3:17; Ruth 1:17); the *devotio* formula (Livy 8.9); the *kishōmon*'s named penalties (`../culture-material/trade-economy-lexicon.md:123`) | is the curse automatic, or does the witness choose to enforce it? |
| **Uptake** | makes the oath an event | the assembly hears; the people answer *Amen* (Deut 27); the ring is reddened (`../culture-material/adornment-body-codes.md:17`) | who must hear it for it to hold — a god, a crowd, a record? |

- **The self-imprecation is the oath's signature and its best design lever.** An oath that ends "may I be struck if I lie" makes the speaker the *target* of their own curse, which means the oath channel is the curse channel pointed inward. This is why a culture's oath formulas and its curse formulas share vocabulary — and why a designer who has written one has mostly written the other.
- **The unconditional self-curse is the archaic mode.** The *devotio* (Livy 8.9) is the limiting case: the commander speaks a formula consigning himself and the enemy's army to the underworld powers, then rides into the enemy line to die. Real systems keep such formulas as *reported speech in historians*, which is exactly how a worldbuilder should keep them — an archaic formula quoted in a later text, with the culture's own lawyers explaining why nobody does that any more.

**4.1 The four enforcement models.** The oath's teeth come from one of four places, and most real systems run two or three at once:

| Model | Executor | Attested exemplar | What it costs the culture |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Divine enforcement** | a god who hears and punishes | the oath by Styx — the false-swearer loses breath for a year and is cut from the gods' council for nine (*Theogony* 775–806); Varuṇa's noose on the perjurer (*Rigveda* 7.86, 7.89) | requires a god who cares about speech, and a priesthood to say so; sanction is invisible and slow, so the culture needs stories about it landing |
| **Automatic enforcement** | the word itself, as a mechanism | the *geis*: the binding imposed on a hero, whose breach is fatal regardless of who wished it (Rees & Rees 1961); the curse-tablet deposited with a spirit | no institution needed; but the world's physics must make words causally potent, and then every idle word is a weapon — a culture must invent ways *not* to bind (silence rules, formula reservations) |
| **Communal/institutional enforcement** | the community, the kindred, the guild, the court | the Roman *sacramentum* — the army's oath, with the community's full weight behind it (Polybius 6); compurgation, where the oath-helper's own oath is at stake (Pollock & Maitland 1895); the guild's freedom oath (`../culture-material/craft-artisan-lexicon.md:65`) | the sanction is legible (forfeit, exile, loss of office) and the institution must have real power; produces the courtroom and the assembly scene |
| **Ordeal enforcement** | a procedure that decides, with the god's verdict presumed | the medieval judicial ordeal, with rules, officiants, and a losing party (Bartlett 1986) | the culture must trust the procedure; when it stops trusting it, the whole channel converts to communal enforcement — a datable institutional shift |

- **The design rule: name the model, then let the culture argue about it.** Real systems are contested internally — the *Iliad*'s oaths are sworn with the gods as witnesses while the poem's own plot shows gods ignoring them; Varuṇa's enforcement is a doctrine the Vedic poets assert and worry about. A worldbuilder should write the doctrine *and* the doubt, because the doubt is where the plots are.
- **A culture with all four models is not "richer"; it is incoherent** unless the four are assigned to different oath classes (military oaths by ordeal, commercial oaths by institution, private oaths by god). Assign per class, not per culture.

**4.2 Oath institutions to place on the map.** Each of these is a physical or social object a story can use:

- **The oath ring.** The Norse evidence is already in this corpus: oaths sworn on temple rings reddened with sacrificial blood (Landnámabók, Hauksbók version, ch. 268; Eyrbyggja saga ch. 4 — cited at `../culture-material/adornment-body-codes.md:17`). The ring is the *witness made portable*: it can be carried to a distant dispute, which is exactly why it outlives the temple that housed it.
- **The covenant archive.** A covenant that is written is a covenant that can be *consulted*; the archive is the institution that turns a spoken word into a document. The Chinese *méng* (covenant, sworn with blood-smearing) had an archive (to-verify: the *Zuo Zhuan* passage), and the Mesopotamian and Hittite treaty corpora are the same object at state scale. Design consequence: an archive makes oaths **datable** — a culture with covenant archives can date its own history by them.
- **The oath-scroll.** The Japanese *kishōmon* — a written oath invoking named divine punishments on breach, attested medieval practice already integrated at `../culture-material/trade-economy-lexicon.md:123` — is the oath as *document*, and it demonstrates the outsourcing pattern: the institution writes the curse and the gods execute it. The design lever that entry states ("decide who *witnesses* the oath — god, ancestor, king, tally — and your breach-penalty follows", `:127`) is this channel's central choice.
- **The oath-helper and the wager of law.** Compurgation — the defendant's oath supported by a fixed number of oath-helpers whose own oaths are at stake — is the institution that makes the *community* the executor without a trial (Pollock & Maitland 1895, to-verify: page). It scales badly and produces the "twelve good men" scene, which is why it is a superb era-marker: a culture moving from oath-helpers to evidence has changed its epistemology.
- **The oath season and the oath site.** Midwinter is the attested oath-and-gift season in the Germanic and Roman calendars (the corpus's `../culture-material/calendar-festival-systems.md:117`), and computus oaths and seals are recorded as administrative jargon (`:134`). An oath calendar is a cheap way to make the channel visible: one day a year when the culture's words are re-bound.

**4.3 The oath-witness slot in a pantheon.** If a culture's oaths are divinely enforced, its pantheon needs a role this corpus's `pantheon-archetypes.md:36` does not list among its recurrent roles: **the witness**, a power whose function is to hear and punish speech, not to rule a domain. Real instances: Horkos ("Oath"), born of Eris and "who most troubles men" (*Theogony* 226–232); Styx as the gods' own oath; Varuṇa as the guardian of *ṛtá*; Mithra as contract itself. Two design consequences:

- **The witness can be a function rather than a person.** Horkos is barely a personality; the role is a *capacity* the system needs. A culture can staff it with an ancestor, a dead founder, a city, or a river — the corpus's cosmology file owns which powers exist, and this entry only asks that *one* of them be able to hear.
- **A witness who can be deceived is a plot engine.** Every myth system that stakes its oaths on a witness generates stories in which the witness is tricked, absent, or bought — the trickster entry's territory (`./trickster.md`).

**4.4 When the culture has no oath-witness.** The interesting case, and the one invented systems most often miss. Three attested solutions: route the oath through **the dead** (ancestors as witnesses, common wherever the lineage is the legal person); through **the institution** (the archive, the guild, the army — the word binds because the record binds); or through **the body** (the oath sworn on one's own health, children, or eyes, where the self-imprecation is the whole enforcement). A culture with none of the three cannot have binding oaths, and should be written as a culture of *hostages and sureties* instead — which is a different, and equally playable, legal world.

## 5. The curse

**Seven genres.** The curse is not one act; it is a family, and the genres differ in who may utter them, whether cause must be stated, and who executes.

| # | Genre | Who utters | Cause stated? | Executor | Real exemplar |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Parental / dying curse** | a parent, especially at death | usually stated | god(s) or fate | Isaac's blessing and its cursed inverse (Genesis 27–49); the dying man's imprecation across the Indo-European and Semitic record |
| 2 | **The wronged party's curse** | anyone with a grievance | stated | god(s) | the psalmist's imprecations; the legal curse in the *méng* and treaty corpora |
| 3 | **The contractual self-curse** | the swearer, against himself | implied by the formula | witness or mechanism | the *devotio* (Livy 8.9); the *kishōmon* penalty clause (`../culture-material/trade-economy-lexicon.md:123`) |
| 4 | **Execration of enemies and objects** | the state, or a licensed practitioner | named, and *written* | the rite itself | the Egyptian execration texts — named enemies on figurines, ritually broken (Sethe 1926; Redford 1992); the Lu Ban hidden build-curse, hostile writing as architecture (`../culture-material/craft-artisan-lexicon.md:99,121`) |
| 5 | **Deposited curse (tablet, bound object)** | anyone who can pay a specialist | often suppressed | a spirit, a god, or the object | the Greek and Roman *defixiones*/*katadesmoi* (Gager 1992) |
| 6 | **The tomb/place curse** | the founder, the dead, the owner | "whoever does X" — an open target | the dead, or the god of the place | the "whoever violates this grave" formula (Gager 1992) |
| 7 | **Prohibition-curse (*geis* type)** | a person with authority over the subject (a druid, a mother, a fate) | not stated — it *is* the prohibition | automatic | the Irish *geis* and its fatal breach (Rees & Rees 1961) |

- **Genre 7 is the design hinge.** A *geis* is not a punishment for an act; it is a *rule that becomes lethal when broken*, imposed without explanation. Systems that include it get a specific and valuable narrative machine: the hero who must not do a small thing, and the world that arranges the small thing. Systems that include it must also answer the fairness question in-world, because the audience will ask it.
- **The open-target curse (genre 6) is the institution-builder.** "Whoever moves this stone" makes every future person a potential target and requires an executor who can act on a stranger's act — a god, or the dead. It is the cheapest way to give a culture a *sacred place* with teeth.

**5.1 Curse physics — four properties to set.** A designer who sets these four has a curse system; one who sets none has a mood.

1. **Retention** — how long the curse waits. Curses that fire immediately are weapons; curses that wait are *contracts with the future*, and waiting curses are what make dynastic stories possible (the curse on the house, not on the man).
2. **Targeting** — can a curse miss? The attested world is full of spillover: the curse meant for one man that takes his household, the blessing stolen by a substitute. Targeting rules generate the culture's *scapegoat and substitute* machinery — which the corpus already owns at `./ritual-liminality.md` §4, and which this entry cites rather than restates.
3. **Spillover and backfire** — does the curse return on the utterer? The strong version (the curse is a substance that pollutes its carrier) produces a culture of professional cursers who must be ritually clean; the weak version produces a culture where anyone may curse and only the target suffers. Choose one and hold it.
4. **Reversibility** — see §9. The single most important dial: a curse system with no release is a world of one mistake, which is a world with exactly one story.

**5.2 Counter-curse and defense.** Every real curse ecology has defenses, and the defenses are the more interesting half: the *voces magicae* and "barbarous names" whose force is held to lie in the sound rather than the sense (Betz 1986) — a defense that requires knowing the name's *original* form; amulets, apotropaic writing, and threshold deposits (Gager 1992); the counter-curse that returns the imprecation to its sender; and, at the institutional level, **the prohibition**: Qur'an 2:102 and 5:90 place sorcery and divining arrows outside the permitted, which converts the whole channel from a technology into a sin (and the corpus's `./divination-omen-systems.md` §11 calibrates the modern misuse of that material). A culture that criminalizes cursing has not removed the channel; it has made every curse an *accusation*, which is a legal plot.

**5.3 The curse's one hard rule.** A curse must have a *cause grammar*: the culture must be able to say why this curse is legitimate. Real systems supply the grammar from kinship (the parent's right), from property (the violated grave), from justice (the wronged party), or from office (the state's execration). A culture that cannot state the cause grammar has curses that are just violence, and its stories cannot distinguish the curse that *should* land from the one that should not — which is precisely the distinction the corpus's consistency gate asks for at `../verification/myth-consistency-gate.md:20`.

## 6. The blessing

**Irrevocability is the channel's defining property.** The strongest single exemplar in world literature is Genesis 27: Isaac, deceived, blesses Jacob, and when Esau cries out, the text's answer is not that the blessing is void but that it *stands* — the words were spoken, and a blessing once given cannot be recalled. This is the design fact to build from:

- **A blessing is a transfer, not a wish.** It moves something from blesser to blessed — fertility, succession, protection, standing — and it is therefore *zero-sum* in most attested systems. That is why blessings can be *stolen* (Genesis 27), *bought* (the birthright), *deferred*, or *split*, and why the blessing scene is a legal scene with witnesses.
- **The blessing's executor is usually the same as the curse's** — a god who honours what was spoken — which is exactly why the two channels are one. What differs is the *release*: curses have release procedures (§9); blessings characteristically have none. A culture that *can* annul blessings has invented a court of appeals over its own gods, and should be written with that consequence visible.
- **Blessing as an inherited quality (not an act).** The *baraka* complex — blessing as a quality that attaches to persons, tombs, and lineages and can be transmitted by contact or descent (Geertz 1968; Westermarck 1926, both to-verify: page) — is a different design: the blessing is not an event but a *substance in the family*, which makes lineage politics into blessing politics. **Living practice: cite the scholarship, invent the fictional system** (`../verification/real-world-inspiration-boundaries.md:91`).
- **The blessing's uptake.** A blessing over a present recipient is public; a blessing sent to an absent one needs a messenger or a written form. Where the culture writes blessings down, the blessing becomes an *instrument* — a thing that can be carried, read aloud, forged. The corpus's `../culture-material/calendar-festival-systems.md` and `../culture-material/trade-economy-lexicon.md` own the occasions and the instruments; this entry only flags that a written blessing is a different object from a spoken one.
- **The priestly formula.** Numbers 6:22–27 — the blessing with a fixed wording, given by a licensed speaker, with the deity as the party who "puts the name" on the people — is the type-specimen for a *blessing register*: a fixed form, a licensed utterer, and a named effect. It is the blessing's answer to the register law at `../linguistics/register-strata-attestation.md:47-89`, and a worldbuilder who wants blessings that bind should give them exactly these three properties.

**6.1 The asymmetry table (design consequence).** If the culture's blessings are irrevocable and its curses releasable, it will develop a *trade in blessings* and a *procedure for curses* — two institutions, two professions, two kinds of story. If the reverse, it will develop a *terror of cursing* and a *casual blessing habit*. Neither is wrong; the failure is a culture whose two channels have the same rules, because then the pair carries no information.

## 7. Name-power

**The channel's object is the name, and the name is a handle.** Name-power is what makes the other three channels executable: an oath sworn "by X" needs X to be summonable by name; a curse "in the name of Y" routes through Y; a blessing carried by a lineage travels because the lineage's name travels. Four operations, and each is a separate institution:

| Operation | What it does | Real exemplar | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Speak / withhold** | the name is uttered, or kept back | the divine name read as a substitute (the *adonai* reading — the capsule at `../synthesis/taboo-euphemism-lifecycle.md:110`); the tabooed name of the dead | a name no one may say is a name no one can use in law, trade, or oath — the taboo has a *cost*, and the culture must pay it |
| **Write / erase** | the name is fixed in a medium, or struck from it | Chinese *bìhuì* avoidance and the missing-character dating method (`../linguistics/naming-systems.md:94-97`) | writing makes name-power *archaeological*: later scholars date documents by which names are absent |
| **Rename** | a new name replaces an old one | the witnessed renaming of a blade (`../culture-material/warrior-material-culture.md:109`); the novice, the enslaved, the married, the dead who all get new names (`../linguistics/naming-systems.md:106`) | renaming is the culture's *status-change* technology, and it is the one release procedure name-power offers |
| **Refuse the name** | the party will not answer to it | the false name that gives immunity — Odysseus' *Outis* (`../linguistics/naming-systems.md:106`) | if names bind, then a false name is armor, and a culture of binding names is a culture of aliases |

- **The secret-name institution.** Two attested forms, both about *leverage*: the secret name of a city or god, kept so that an enemy cannot use it (the secret name of Rome, and the *evocatio* by which a besieger calls the enemy's tutelary god out of the city — Macrobius, *Saturnalia* 3.9; Pliny, *NH* 28.18, both to-verify: section); and the name extracted by force or trickery — Isis obtaining Ra's secret name in the Egyptian legend (P. Turin 1993, trans. Lichtheim 1976, to-verify: designation). The design consequence is that a secret name must be *usable* by whoever holds it: if knowing the name grants nothing, the secret is decoration.
- **Names as components of the person.** In Egyptian thought the *rn* (name) is one of the constituents of a person alongside the *kꜣ*, *bꜣ*, *šwt*, and *jb* (Allen 2000, to-verify: page) — which makes name-erasure a form of killing, and is the reason the corpus's `../linguistics/naming-systems.md` §6 can treat erasure rites as legal acts. A culture whose metaphysics gives the name its own existence cannot treat a name change as paperwork.
- **The "true name" motif, calibrated.** The folklore machinery is owned by `../linguistics/naming-systems.md:106` (ATU 500, the Rumpelstiltskin type; the three canonical moves — true name = power over, false name = immunity, renaming = rebirth). This entry adds only the *ledger* consequence: a true-name system needs an executor too. If the true name is spoken and nothing happens, the culture has a *belief* about names, not a binding system — and the difference is exactly the difference between §2's maxim and a mood.
- **The substrate channel needs a physical carrier.** A name that is a handle must be *attached to something*: a written register, an object that bears it (the corpus's `../culture-material/adornment-body-codes.md` covers name-bearing objects), a recitation that keeps it alive, or a witness who remembers. Cultures with none of these cannot run a name-power system, because nobody can prove what the name was.

## 8. The enforcement ledger

This is the entry's central instrument. Fill it for every binding word in the culture; a row without an executor is a row to delete or re-route.

| Column | The question | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Word** | what exactly is bound? | the formula, and whether it is fixed or free |
| **Binder / bound** | who is bound by whom? | self (oath), other (curse, blessing), object (name) |
| **Witness** | who is present? | a god, a crowd, a record, an object; if nobody, the word is private and unprovable |
| **Detector** | who finds out about a breach? | the missing column in most invented systems; without a detector the executor never fires |
| **Executor** | who or what acts? | divine, automatic, communal, ordeal (§4.1) |
| **Sanction** | what happens? | legible (forfeit, exile, death) or illegible (blight, dreams, dynastic failure) |
| **Cost of enforcement** | who pays to run the sanction? | a priesthood that must be maintained, a court that must be convened, a mechanism that must be fed — an unfunded executor is a fiction |
| **Release** | how is it undone? | §9's six procedures; "none" is a legitimate and consequential answer |
| **Public record** | where does the culture store it? | the archive, the stele, the song, the witness; this is what makes later stories possible |

**The three ledger checks.** Run these on any proposed binding word:

1. **The executor check.** Name the party who acts on breach, and the mechanism by which they learn of it. If either is missing, the word does not bind — it is etiquette, and the culture's own cynics should say so in the text.
2. **The cost check.** Name who funds the executor. A god who punishes oath-breakers needs a cult; a court needs a treasury; an automatic mechanism needs a world whose physics is stated somewhere else in the corpus. Unfunded sanctions are the commonest invented-system defect.
3. **The story check.** Ask what the culture's *stories* say about a broken word — because a myth system's real rules are the ones its narratives enforce. A culture whose myths punish perjury only when the victim is noble has an aristocratic oath system regardless of what its law code says.

**The false-enforcement trap.** A culture can *believe* an executor exists where none does, and the belief is itself an institution — but the designer must then decide whether the belief is true in the world. Three options, all playable: the executor is real (the myth is physics); the executor is unreal but the belief works through people (the myth is a social mechanism — the corpus's `../myth-symbol/myth-ritual-theories.md:51-56` Malinowski line, calibrated there); or the executor is unreal and the belief is a con that the story exposes. What is not playable is a text that cannot tell which of the three it is running.

## 9. Release — the six procedures

A binding word that cannot be undone is a world with one mistake in it. Every real system therefore has release machinery, and the machinery is one of the most legible things about a culture.

| # | Procedure | How it works | Attested exemplar | What it says about the culture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Expiation** | a graded penalty that discharges the bond | the *kaffāra* for a broken oath — feeding the poor, or fasting (Qur'an 5:89) | the bond is real but *priced*; the culture has a tariff, which means it has administrators |
| 2 | **Substitution** | another party or victim takes the sanction | the substitute-victim logic of sacrifice (`./ritual-liminality.md` §4) | the culture can transfer guilt; expect a market in substitutes, and a class of people who serve as them |
| 3 | **Absolution by the licensing authority** | the institution that let the word be spoken unsays it | annulment of vows (Numbers 30 — the household's windows); the ruler's pardon | power sits with the licenser, not the god; the appeal is political |
| 4 | **Reversal** | the curse is turned back on its sender | counter-curse practice; the "return to sender" formulas of the tablet corpora (Gager 1992) | the culture thinks of binding as *directional* — a physics, not a verdict |
| 5 | **Expiry** | the bond lapses by time, term, or the death of a party | oaths sworn for a term; mourning-period name taboos that expire | the culture has a clock in its law, which is a sophisticated thing to write |
| 6 | **Renaming / re-identification** | the bound party becomes someone else | the renaming operations at `../linguistics/naming-systems.md:106`; the novice who dies and is reborn under a new name | identity is changeable, so a bond can be escaped by ceasing to be the person who was bound — the darkest and best of the six |

- **Design rule: every channel gets one release, or the culture says why not.** Blessings are the standard exception (irrevocable by definition, §6). A culture with no release for curses must have an in-world explanation — a doctrine, a myth, or a physics — because the audience will supply one if the text does not, and usually a worse one.
- **Release is where the era story lives.** A culture that moves from expiation to absolution has moved power from the priesthood to the state; one that moves from ordeal to evidence has moved it from the god to the court. Release procedures are therefore *era markers*, and the corpus's era files (`../culture-material/era-periodization-models.md`) can stage the change.

## 10. Build protocol

1. **Fix the metaphysics first, and only as much as you need.** Decide whether words are causally potent in this world (automatic enforcement possible) or only socially potent (institutional enforcement only). Everything downstream depends on this one bit, and a culture can hold *both* — a potent word for some classes of speech, a merely social word for others — if the boundary is stated.
2. **Choose the executors, one per channel.** Divine, automatic, communal, or ordeal. Assign per channel, and let the culture argue about the assignment (§4.1).
3. **Write the witness slot into the pantheon** (or route the oath through the dead, the institution, or the body — §4.4). Cite `./pantheon-archetypes.md` for the role typology; this entry only requires that a witness exists.
4. **Write one formula per channel, and keep it fixed.** A memorized formula no individual can alter binds by form (`../linguistics/register-strata-attestation.md:80`); an improvised one binds only by institution. Vary the formula's parts (§4) rather than its fixity.
5. **Fill the ledger for each formula** (§8), including the detector and the cost. Delete or re-route any row with no executor.
6. **Assign releases** (§9), one per channel, and write the culture's own doctrine about the exceptions.
7. **Set the visibility dial per channel** (§3): which bindings are public, which are hidden, and which are recorded. The mismatch between the culture's public doctrine and its hidden practice is the plot engine.
8. **Stress-test with four scenes**: a perjurer discovered (does the executor fire?); a curse with an innocent household in the blast radius (does targeting hold?); a blessing claimed by the wrong heir (is it revocable?); a name erased from the register (can the person still act in law?).
9. **Couple the output** to the register law (`../linguistics/register-strata-attestation.md`), the name system (`../linguistics/naming-systems.md` §6), the ritual occasions (`./ritual-liminality.md`), and the trade/legal instruments (`../culture-material/trade-economy-lexicon.md`) — and hand off to lore-shokunin for any decision about what is actually true in the world.

## 11. Worked micro-example (NON-CANON — a design exercise, not lore)

*Labelled NON-CANON per the corpus convention: this is an invented exercise showing the ledger in use. It asserts nothing about any world, and no part of it is a claim about any real culture.*

**The setting.** A river-valley confederacy of five oath-bound towns. Words are potent only where a *witness* is present: the confederacy's doctrine says the river hears. The five executors are therefore: the river (divine, for public oaths), the town assembly (communal, for commercial bonds), the kin-group (communal, for marriage and fosterage), the *bound stone* at each ford (automatic, for boundary oaths — the stone is where the formula was first spoken, and tradition holds that a broken oath sworn on it sickens the oath-taker), and, for accusations between towns, a water-ordeal (ordeal enforcement, with the accused's hands held in the ford).

**The four formulas** (each fixed, memorized, and short): the oath "by the ford and the five"; the curse "let the water take what you took"; the blessing spoken over a newborn by the eldest woman present; and the name-operation — a town's *true name* is spoken only at the ford at midsummer, and otherwise a public name is used.

**The ledger, filled for one row each:**

| Word | Binder/bound | Witness | Detector | Executor | Sanction | Cost | Release | Record |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the ford oath | self | the river + the assembly | a townsman's accusation at the ford | the river (sickness) *and* the assembly (exclusion from the ford market) | illness + commercial death | the assembly maintains the ford; the priesthood maintains the doctrine | expiation: a year's toll paid to the wronged party, and a re-swearing | the oath-roll kept at each ford, cut into a tally stick |
| the water-curse | other | none | the target's kin | the river | blight, stillbirth, drowning | kin-group feud, which is the actual cost | reversal only, by a rite at the ford, and only with the utterer's presence | none (curses are hidden by design) |
| the newborn blessing | other (the child) | the household | — | the river, through the name given | fertility and protection, carried by the child's water-name | the eldest woman's authority, which the town must recognize | none — this is the channel's definition | spoken into the town's name-roll |
| the true name of the ford | object (the town) | the river | the priesthood, which keeps the calendar | the name itself | the town's standing at the ford; if the name is misused, the ford oath against the town lapses | the midsummer rite | renaming at the next midsummer | the true name is deliberately unwritten |

**What the exercise demonstrates.** (a) The executor and the detector are different parties — the assembly detects, the river executes — and the split is where the plots live (an accusation that the assembly believes and the river does not, or the reverse). (b) The curse's real cost is the feud, not the blight, which is what makes it a *political* instrument. (c) The blessing's irreversibility is what makes the name-roll politically important. (d) The secret true name is *usable* (it can void a town's oaths), so it is not decoration. (e) Every row has a cost column filled — the funded-executor check of §8.
