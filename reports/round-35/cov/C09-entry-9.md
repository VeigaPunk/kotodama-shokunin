## 2. The four questions every death-doctrine answers (the design axes)

| # | Question | The options a culture actually picks from | What the answer silently commits the culture to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | **What survives?** | nothing / a shadow that persists but is diminished / a dual-soul pair that splits / a continuous self that travels / a causal stream with no self / the body itself, later | the funerary technology (what must be preserved, burned, exposed, or fed) and the whole ghost question (§3) |
| Q2 | **Where does it go?** | one place for all / places sorted by merit / places sorted by manner of death / a place that is a *journey* rather than a location / nowhere (dissolution) | the destination's institutions — gates, ferrymen, clerks, courts, halls — i.e. a bureaucracy or a landscape (§4) |
| Q3 | **Who decides?** | automatic law (no judge) / a court with named assessors / a ledger kept by clerks / an ordeal-journey / the kin, by ritual recall / the deceased, by choice (the Er model) | where authority sits: a priesthood, a state bureau, a lineage, or nobody — which is a political question (§5) |
| Q4 | **What do the living owe?** | burial form and timing / offerings and feeding / mourning stages and dress / name management (erasure or preservation) / the dead's debts and the living's inheritance | the *calendar* and the *budget*: obligations turn the dead into a recurring cost and a recurring festival (§7) |

- **Design rule D1 — the four answers must be mutually consistent, and the inconsistency is content.** A culture that says "nothing survives" cannot also stage a funeral that feeds a ghost; if it does, the contradiction *is* the institution (a rite kept for the living's sake, with an exegete faction that says so). Write the faction, don't fix the contradiction.
- **Design rule D2 — pick the load-bearing answer and mark the rest as satellite.** Real systems are layered: a public doctrine, a household practice, and a skeptical counter-voice coexist (`china-tang.md:108` documents exactly this layering for Tang local practice vs official taxonomy). Give the culture all three and let them disagree in the open.
- **Design rule D3 — the doctrine's date matters.** A death-doctrine is an artifact with a history: purgatory was defined at Lyon II (1274) and Florence (1439) and reaffirmed at Trent (session 25, 1563) — i.e. the doctrine a fictional church holds has an *adoption date* that can be written into its era record. Date every doctrine you install; undated doctrines read as timeless, which is the one thing real ones never are.

## 3. Q1 — the survival typology, with the exemplar bank

| Type | What survives | Attested exemplars | Funerary consequence | Design trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Diminished shadow** | a weak, thirsty, dusty remnant that needs feeding | Mesopotamian eṭemmu/gidim (the netherworld where the dead eat clay and drink muddy water: Akkadian *Descent of Ištar*; *Gilgamesh* VII/XII — George 1999/2003); the kispu offering rite; Hebrew rĕpāʾîm in Sheol (Genesis 37:35; 42:38 — a place, not a reward); Greek psychē/eidōlon (a strengthless image: *Odyssey* 11) | the offering economy is the point: libations, food, the pouring of water — the dead are *fed* | Do not read the bleakness as atheism; the bleakness is the institution that makes the living's duty continuous |
| **Dual-soul split** | two or more components with different fates | Egyptian ka / ba / akh (Taylor 2001; the body must be preserved for the ba's return — hence mummification as doctrine, not sentiment); Chinese hun 魂 / po 魄 (*Zuo Zhuan*, Duke Zhao 7: Zichan on the violently dead Boyou; the summons rite in *Chuci*, "Zhao Hun") | one component needs the grave, another needs a rite, a third needs a name; the funeral becomes a *multi-channel operation* | A dual-soul system without a scheduled rite for each component is incoherent: the soul that ascends must be given an address to ascend *from* |
| **Rebirth** | a causal continuity that is not the same person | Hindu ātman and the body-change analogy (*Bhagavad Gita* 2.22; *Brhadaranyaka* 4.4; *Chandogya* 5.10 two paths); Buddhist rebirth with **no** permanent self (anattā/anātman — a stream, not a passenger); Jain; Platonic/Orphic/Pythagorean metempsychosis (*Phaedo* 81c–d; *Meno* 81a–d; *Republic* 10.614b–621d Er; Obeyesekere 2002) | if rebirth is *immediate*, the funeral is a send-off; if there is an **interim**, the funeral becomes a *timed operation with an end date* (the 49-day convention; Tibetan bardo) | "The same person comes back" is the doctrine most cultures with rebirth do **not** hold — the Buddhist no-self caveat is the single most important calibration here |
| **Resurrection** | the body and the person, restored at a future event | Zoroastrian (the final renovation, Frašokereti — Bundahišn ch. 30, locator `to-verify`); Jewish (Isaiah 26:19; Daniel 12:2; Ezekiel 37); Christian (Matthew 25:31–46; Revelation 20:11–15); Islamic (Qur'an 39:68; 75) | **burial becomes load-bearing**: cremation is now a doctrine problem, grave orientation is an eschatological claim, and the whole calendar bends toward a date | Resurrection doctrines are **calendar-bound**: they create waiting, and waiting creates date-setting factions (§8) |
| **Ancestor-continuity** | the dead person continues as a *role* inside the lineage — an office, not a ghost | Chinese ancestral tablets and the 49-day/Ten Kings apparatus (Teiser 1994); Roman Manes/Lares with the Parentalia and Lemuria (Ovid, *Fasti* 2 and 5, locators `to-verify`); Korean jesa and mourning grades (`korea-joseon.md:18,36`, Deuchler 1992); African ancestor cults with the ancestor/**ghost** distinction (Fortes 1965); Malagasy famadihana and the Hertzian double funeral (`ritual-liminality.md:24,32`; Bloch 1971) | secondary burial, bone-turning, and the *shelf*; the dead get a physical address the lineage controls | The ancestor is not a feeling: it is a *title with duties*. A culture whose ancestors have no duties has designed a cemetery, not a cult |
| **Dissolution / absorption** | nothing personal survives; the material returns | Upaniṣadic mokṣa; Buddhist nirvāṇa; Stoic return to the logos and ekpyrosis (Long & Sedley 1987); Epicurean "death is nothing to us" (Epicurus, *Letter to Menoeceus*, Diogenes Laertius 10.122–135; Lucretius 3) | the funeral becomes hygiene, law and grief-work — no feeding, no journey | This is the **skeptic faction's** doctrine and it is design gold: a culture can hold a public doctrine of survival and an elite doctrine of dissolution, with the funeral serving the public one |
| **No doctrine** | the question is left open on purpose | real societies with a thin eschatology but a thick funerary rule | obligations without metaphysics | Do not confuse this with primitiveness: an unspecified afterlife with a fully specified burial is a coherent, common arrangement |

- **Cross-cutting: the corpse's status is a separate variable.** Zoroastrian doctrine makes the corpse *polluting* (the Druj Nasu; exposure in a dakhma so as not to defile earth — Vendidad 5–6, chapter-level `to-verify`), while Egyptian doctrine makes the corpse *necessary*. Same question ("what is a body for?"), opposite institutions. Decide the corpse's status explicitly; it decides burial, cremation, exposure, and whether the dead may be touched.

## 4. Q2 — destination geography as an institution, not a map

- **Rule D4 — draw the destination with institutions, never with a picture.** Every destination that matters in real systems is staffed. Ask: who is at the door, who keeps the record, who feeds whom, and who may leave. The five institution slots:

| Slot | Function | Attested exemplars | Craft use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gatekeeper / ferryman** | passage is licensed and can be refused | Charon and the fare (*Frogs*, c. 139–270; the mouth-coin reading is contested archaeologically — Stevens 1991, locator `to-verify`); the Chinese Naihe bridge 奈何橋 (popular Ten Kings material — Teiser 1994); the Zoroastrian Chinvat bridge (Yasna 46.10; Vendidad 19) | a refused passage is a plot engine: the unburied dead who cannot cross (Greek ataphoi, §6) |
| **Record-keeper / clerk** | the destination is *administered* | Thoth recording the weighing (*Book of the Dead* 125; Allen 1974); the Chinese underworld bureaus and the 生死簿 ledger (as used in *Journey to the West* ch. 3, locator `to-verify`); the Book of Life (Revelation 20:12) | a ledger makes the afterlife *auditable* — and a bureaucracy in the afterlife is a direct projection of the state that imagined it |
| **Court / assessors** | destination is decided by a procedure | the Egyptian tribunal with 42 assessors (spell 125); the Chinese Ten Courts (Teiser 1994); the Greek judges Minos, Rhadamanthys, Aiakos (Plato, *Gorgias* 523a–527a) | a court generates appeals, bribes, advocates, and calendar days — a whole second government |
| **Host / destination society** | the dead live *somewhere with rules* | Valhöll and Hel (Snorri, *Gylfaginning* 34; **not every warrior goes to Valhöll** — *Grímnismál* 14 gives Freyja half, `germanic-norse.md:115`); Xibalba's lords and their ballcourt (*Popol Vuh*; `maya-classic.md:115,122`); Mictlan's nine levels and four-year journey (Sahagún, *Florentine Codex* Book 3 Appendix) | the destination's society mirrors the living one's hierarchy — or deliberately inverts it (an inversion is a critique the culture is making) |
| **Feeder / interface** | the living can still transact with the dead | kispu offerings (Mesopotamia); Egyptian offering formulae and letters to the dead (Taylor 2001; specific letter texts `to-verify`); the Ghost Festival with monastic offerings and filial obligation (Teiser 1988, carried at `china-tang.md:112`); Obon with welcome and send-off fires (`ritual-liminality.md:88`) | the interface is where the *economy* is: offerings, paper money, feast days, and the offices that sell them |

- **The sorting principle is the doctrine's most political choice.** Sort by merit (judgment systems), by **manner of death** (the Mexica pattern: warriors to the Sun, the drowned and lightning-struck to Tlalocan, women dying in childbirth to the west, everyone else on the four-year journey to Mictlan — Sahagún Book 3 Appendix; the Greek "special dead" likewise), or not at all (Sheol, Hades, Hel: one destination, no sorting). Manner-of-death sorting tells you what the culture *fears and honors*; merit sorting tells you what it *polices*.
- **The journey is often the destination.** When the doctrine is a road with ordeals (Mictlan's four-year passage with its tests and its river and its dog; Xibalba's trial houses), the design deliverable is the *itinerary*: stages, obstacles, provisions, guides, and the fee — an itinerary is a much better handout than a diagram.
- **Boundary note:** the destination's *shape* (layers, verticality, whether it is below or across water) is `cosmology-cosmography.md`'s (`:45`) and the dead's *direction* is `direction-symbolism.md`'s (`:117,125`). This file owns what happens *inside* those coordinates.

## 5. Q3 — the judgment-machine typology

| Machine | How it decides | Attested exemplar | Calibration note | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Weighing** | the person's record is measured against a standard | Egyptian: the heart against the feather of Maat, with Thoth recording and the devourer waiting (*Book of the Dead* spell 125; Allen 1974; the in-corpus reading at `egypt-ancient.md:89` insists the heart is *not* weighed against a generic decorative feather) | distinct from the Greek **fate**-weighing (kerostasia: Zeus weighs the fates of Achilles and Hector, *Iliad* 22.209–213) — a moral scale and a fate scale are different machines; do not merge them | weighing needs a *standard* the culture must be able to state: what is on the other pan? |
| **Ledger** | clerks read a written record | the Chinese underworld bureaucracy: registers, magistrates, the Ten Courts (Teiser 1994 on the medieval Buddhist making of the system; the 玉曆寶鈔 as the iconographic channel, edition `to-verify`) | the Ten Kings are **not** Buddhist canon — they are a Chinese popular systematization with an apocryphal scripture behind them | a ledger requires literacy-in-the-afterlife and a state that keeps records — the doctrine is a mirror of administration |
| **Interrogation** | the dead is questioned, sometimes in the grave | Islamic grave questioning by Munkar and Nakir (hadith channel, **not** Qur'an — say so); the barzakh as the interim state (Qur'an 23:100) | distinguish Qur'anic from hadith material whenever you import it | interrogation makes the *first night* after burial the crisis point — a scene-setting gift |

| **Kin-recall / no judge** | the living's ritual determines the dead's state | Roman Manes handled by rite (Parentalia, Lemuria; Ovid *Fasti* 2 and 5, locators `to-verify`); the ancestor cult's feeding cycle (Fortes 1965); the Chinese unattended-dead altars 泰厲/公厲/族厲 (*Liji*, "Ji Fa") | this is the largest family in the world's religions and the least dramatized in fiction | with no judge, the *living* are the machine — and their failure is the horror (the ghost nobody feeds, §6) |
| **Self-choice** | the dead chooses, then lives with it | Plato, *Republic* 10.614b–621d (Er: lots, lives, and the choice) | the myth is Plato's philosophical device, not a civic cult | a chosen destiny makes the afterlife a *judgment on character* without any judge — useful for a culture with weak institutions |

- **Design rule D5 — judgment doctrines centralize authority; recall doctrines distribute it.** Installing a court means installing interpreters of the court (a priesthood or a bureau); installing kin-recall means the household keeps the power. Choose the authority you want, then pick the machine.
- **Design rule D6 — a judgment needs a *record* and a *standard*, and the standard is the culture's ethics syllabus.** State the pan, the ledger's columns, or the questions — the content of the standard is the most efficient place to encode what the culture values (and what it merely claims to).

## 6. The bad death and the unquiet ghost — the management institution

- **Every culture with a death-doctrine has a bad-death category**, and the category is where the machinery is visible. The cross-cultural pattern: a death that is untimely, violent, unburied, or unattended produces a dead who is *not yet a proper ancestor* and therefore interacts with the living — and the living respond with a specialized office.
- **Attested exemplar bank:**

| Category | Attested form | What it generates |
|---|---|---|
| Untimely / violent / unburied | Greek aōroi (untimely), biaiothanatoi (violent), ataphoi (unburied) — the restless dead who cannot cross (Johnston 1999); Antigone is an argument about funerary rights, not a family quarrel (`ritual-liminality.md:109`) | funerary law, the burial-duty conflict, and the hero-cult machinery that absorbs the powerful dead (`greece-classical.md:97,98`) |
| The wronged dead | Chinese 冤鬼/怨鬼 (the vengeful ghost) and 厲 li — the unattended dead at three levels, 泰厲/公厲/族厲 (*Liji*, "Ji Fa"); the Ghost Festival feeds the hungry ghosts (Teiser 1988, carried at `china-tang.md:112`); *Zuo Zhuan* Zhao 7 (Zichan explains Boyou's ghost as the product of a violent death and an unappeased hun/po) | placatory rites, an altar for the unattended, an annual feeding festival — i.e. an institution that *exists because* some dead have no family |
| The politically wronged | Japanese goryō 御霊: the vengeful noble spirit pacified by court rite (the 863 goryō-e; Sugawara no Michizane as the classic case — *Nihon Sandai Jitsuroku*, entry-level locator `to-verify`); the in-corpus note that political grievance can acquire ritual consequences (`japan-heian.md:88`) | the state as exorcist: the ritual that converts a dangerous ghost into a god is a *legitimacy machine* |
| The unclean dead | Roman larvae/lemures vs the benign lares (Ovid, *Fasti* 5, Lemuria, locator `to-verify`); Slavic navi/upir; the folkloric revenant whose treatment is a folk-hygiene complex (Barber 1988 — the book's point is that the *pathology* reading of revenant beliefs is the calibration, not the vampire) | apotropaic rite, crossroads burial, the bean-throwing; a design shelf that must stay calibrated or it produces cartoon vampires |
| The mound-dweller | Norse draugr/haugbúi (saga-mediated; the *Grettis saga* episode — text-level locator `to-verify`) | the grave as a *place to be disturbed* — grave-robbing becomes a theological act |

- **Design rule D7 — the bad-death category must name the management office.** If a culture has restless ghosts and no exorcist, altar-keeper, or feeding rite, the ghosts are set dressing. Name the office, its fee, and its calendar day.
- **Design rule D8 — the unquiet dead are a *status*, not a species.** The same person can be a dangerous ghost before the rites and a benign ancestor after them (this is exactly Hertz's double funeral: the soul is a liminal ghost near the living during the first burial, and an incorporated ancestor after the second — `ritual-liminality.md:24`). Make the transition *stageable*: the worldbuilder's horror scene is the interval, and the culture's competence is the rite that closes it.

## 7. Q4 — what the living owe: the counter-flow machinery

- **Burial and corpse doctrine.** Four attested regimes, each with a different logic: inhumation-with-provision (Egypt; grave goods as *supply*), rapid simple burial (Hebrew/Islamic norms: the body is returned quickly), **exposure** (Zoroastrian: the corpse is pollution and must not defile earth — Vendidad 5–6, chapter-level `to-verify`), and **timed, geomantic burial** (Chinese practice: the burial date and site are chosen by specialists). These are not styles; they are doctrines about what the body is. Couple the choice to `material-culture-inference-method.md:56,81` before designing a single grave.
- **Offerings and the ancestor economy.** The recurrent machinery: a *feeding* schedule (kispu; Roman Parentalia in February and the Lemuria in May; the Chinese Ghost Festival in the seventh month; Japanese Obon — `ritual-liminality.md:88`; Korean jesa), a *medium* of transfer (libation, food, paper money, incense, light), and an *administrator* (household, lineage, monastery, state). Design the transfer medium as an economy: it has a price, a counterfeit problem, and a suppression history.
- **Mourning stages and dress.** The grammar is `ritual-liminality.md`'s (separation/liminality/incorporation; the death row of its life-crisis table at `:32`) and the dress is `ritual-garments.md`'s (wǔfú grades, keriah and sackcloth, deuil blanc, Victorian stages, East Asian white — `:3,11`); Roman mourning as a *garment* rather than a hue is the in-corpus caution (`rome.md:110`). This entry's contribution is the rule: **mourning duration is a debt schedule** — each stage ends an obligation (seclusion, abstention from work, abstention from sex, abstention from festival), and the schedule's length is a rank claim (the higher the rank, the longer the obligation).
- **Name management.** Two opposed institutions from one principle (the name is the thing): *erasure* (the Egyptian concern for the persistence of the name against its destruction; Roman *damnatio memoriae*; the Chinese posthumous name and the imperial naming taboo bìhuì 避讳, which removes an emperor's personal-name characters from print and office titles — `naming-systems.md:22,40`; the death-and-name taboo domain at `politeness-taboo-euphemism.md:75`) and *preservation* (the ancestor tablet that must be fed; the memorial inscription that makes the dead public — `germanic-norse.md:64`). Pick one per rank: most cultures erase some dead and preserve others, and *which* is the doctrine.
- **The dead's property.** Inheritance, debts, and the tomb are one system (Goody 1962): a death is a property event, and the funerary complex is often the mechanism by which claims are made. If the culture's funerals do not touch property, the funerals are incomplete.
- **Design rule D9 — write the counter-flow as a *ledger with two columns*:** what the dead receive (food, fire, money, names, days) and what the living receive (legitimacy, inheritance, protection, the right to mourn). A doctrine where the flow is one-way is a doctrine that will not survive contact with an economy.

## 8. Eschatology — the end-of-the-world event grammar

- **Boundary first:** the *ages* of the world (Hesiod's five ages, the yugas, the 12,000-year Zoroastrian scheme, the Aztec Five Suns, Joachim's three statuses) belong to `era-periodization-models.md` and `cosmology-cosmography.md:77`; the flood belongs to `flood-traditions.md`. This section owns only the **event grammar** for the end — how the world ends *for persons*.
- **Five levers:**

| Lever | Options | Attested exemplars | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Agent** | fire / water / ice / battle / judgment / earthquake / entropy | Stoic ekpyrosis (Long & Sedley 1987); Norse Fimbulvetr and Surtr (*Völuspá*); the Mexica successive destructions (each sun ended by a different force — the Sun Stone and the annals tradition, via `cosmology-cosmography.md` §5); the earthquake (Qur'an 99) | the agent is the culture's elemental theology; it should agree with its cosmology's elements, not contradict them |
| **Target** | judgment *on persons* vs cosmic reset | Last Judgment (Matthew 25:31–46; Revelation 20:11–15) vs the cyclic renewal (*Völuspá* 59–66: the earth rises again green, Baldr returns; the Zoroastrian Frašokereti — Bundahišn ch. 30, locator `to-verify`) | judgment ends *history*; a reset ends *an era* — the second leaves a world to live in, the first does not |
| **Shape** | final-and-once vs cyclic | the Abrahamic single end vs the Hindu kalpa-cycle, the Stoic conflagration and rebirth, the Norse destruction-and-renewal | cyclic eschatologies **de-fang** apocalypse: the end is routine, and the culture can therefore be calm about it |
| **Date** | unknowable / computable / already running | the "no man knows the day" rule (Matthew 24:36) vs date-setting factions; the Kali Yuga as a decline already in progress; the 2012 phenomenon as a *modern* reading of a Long Count rollover (`cosmology-cosmography.md:145`, Aveni 2009 — a rollover, not a prophecy) | a computable date creates a faction and a calendar; "already running" creates a culture that legislates for the end |
| **Escape** | none / the righteous are spared / the worthy are transferred / the dead are raised into it | Noah's ark as survival; the Zoroastrian raising of the dead into the renovation; the rapture-style transfer; the 144,000 (Revelation 7) | an escape clause is a *membership machine*: who is on the list, who keeps it, and what it costs |

- **Design rule D10 — an eschatology must have a *witness institution*.** If the end is datable, someone computes it (priests, astronomers, a bureau); if it is not, someone is licensed to say so, and the licensed denial is itself an office. A culture with an apocalypse but no office that argues about it has an apocalypse nobody believes.
- **Design rule D11 — the end is a *law* by the time you finish.** Real eschatologies generate legislation: penitential calendars, property rules for the last days, pilgrimage booms, and prohibitions on predicting the date. Give the world's end a legal footprint or keep it in the temple.

## 9. Coupling and era drift

- **Couple deliberately, by owner:** funerary rite grammar → `ritual-liminality.md`; world shape/layers/ages → `cosmology-cosmography.md` (`:45,77`); direction of the dead and grave orientation → `direction-symbolism.md` (`:117,125`); mourning dress → `ritual-garments.md`; seasons of the dead → `calendar-festival-systems.md`; mourning meals and fasts → `cuisine-food-lexicon.md`; death euphemism and the funeral-industry register → `politeness-taboo-euphemism.md` (`:26,75`); name taboo and posthumous names → `naming-systems.md` (`:22,40`); grave-goods inference → `material-culture-inference-method.md` (`:56,81`); tomb and funerary architecture → `architecture-settlement-lexicon.md`; the dead in tale inventories → `motif-index-usage.md:36` and `myth-folktale-genres.md`; descent narratives → `hero-journey-variants.md`; the dead as a divination channel (necromancy, the word-omen) → `divination-omen-systems.md`; calibration routing → `calibration-registry.md:3,29`; handoff → `worldbible-integration-contract.md:2`.
- **Era-drift mechanisms specific to the doctrine of the dead (six, each dateable in-world):**
  1. **Doctrine reform** — a definition is promulgated and the doctrine's date becomes an era stamp (purgatory: Lyon II 1274, Florence 1439, Trent 25 1563).
  2. **Sectarian split** — a ritual apparatus is kept while its doctrine is rejected (the Reformation's removal of purgatory left the funeral largely intact), or a doctrine is imported with a rite (the 49-day rite and the Ten Kings entering China as a Buddhist apparatus).
  3. **State takeover** — the state legislates the corpse (funeral sumptuary law as early as the Twelve Tables, Cicero, *De Legibus* 2.59–66; cremation and burial regulation in modern states) — the doctrine becomes an administrative object.
  4. **Medicalization and professionalization** — dying moves from household to institution, and the death-vocabulary moves with it (the funeral-industry euphemism register, Mitford 1963, carried at `politeness-taboo-euphemism.md:26`).
  5. **Revival / invention of tradition** — a modern state or movement nationalizes a death rite (the modern Día de Muertos as a national construction, Brandes 2006) and retrofits an ancient pedigree; date the *invention*, not the pedigree.
  6. **Migration and diaspora** — the rite travels and the doctrine does not (or the reverse), producing the classic in-world tension: a funeral the second generation performs without knowing what it is for.
- **Design rule D12 — date the doctrine, not just the rite.** A handoff that gives the funeral's form without the doctrine's adoption date cannot be era-stamped, and an undated doctrine silently claims eternity.

## 10. Build protocol — the death-doctrine in eight steps

1. **Answer Q1–Q4 in one paragraph each**, in the culture's own voice (an in-world catechism line, not designer prose). If a question cannot be answered, the doctrine is not yet a system.
2. **Choose the survival type** from §3 and fix the **corpse's status** (necessary, polluting, neutral, or a resource) — this single decision constrains everything downstream.
3. **Draw the destination as institutions** (§4): gate, record, court, host society, feeder. Name the staff.
4. **Install the decision machine** (§5) and state its *standard*: the pan, the columns, the questions, or the tests.
5. **Write the bad-death category and its management office** (§6): what makes a death bad, what the dead becomes, who handles it, what the rite is, what it costs.
6. **Write the counter-flow ledger** (§7): what the dead receive, what the living receive, on what schedule, at whose expense. Include the property dimension.
7. **Set the eschatology levers** (§8) and give the end a legal footprint and a licensed interpreter.
8. **Audit** — run the four-question consistency check (D1), the office test (§1), the calibration rows (§12), and the coupling map (§9); then hand the doctrine to `worldbible-integration-contract.md`'s pipeline as *options*, never as canon.
