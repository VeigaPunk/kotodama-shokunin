## 3. Specific false-confirmation risks (highest-value per row)

1. **OTT-06**: any retrieved secondary date (e.g. an encyclopedia, a blog, a snippet of Quataert) risks being read as confirming note 45's *reported disagreement*. The claim is what Quataert's note reports (1725/1726/1727 together), not which date is correct. A passage that gives one confident date without showing the disagreement does not support the claim as worded. Mirrors of the Quataert PDF (ResearchGate, course pages) must be checked for provenance; a snippet must never stand for the note.
2. **S09-07**: lexical coincidence with ordinary 'scissors' nouns (Slovak *nožnice*, Russian *ножницы*) risks over-read in both directions: (a) treating the dictionary form as identifying the historical term, or (b) treating the implement reading as proven for the corpus row. Wiktionary is crowd-edited; the SAV portal behind it was not fetched last wave. Absence of the form in Rod/rožanicy contexts is consistency evidence, not attestation.
3. **S09-08**: the single largest laundering risk in the set — a successful archive.org **metadata** fetch must not set `retrieved_passage: true`. Prior wave already recorded exactly this trap. Content at entry lines 97/119/124/175 stays unchecked unless actual pages of Ivanits are retrieved (lending-restricted; 401 on file download).

The entry flags image, attributes, *spelling*, and *etymology* separately; a picture confirms none of the four. ## 4. Source-authority pitfalls (cross-cutting, ordered by expected frequency)

1. **Catalogue/metadata ≠ content**: archive.org item pages, NDL records, publisher product pages, DOI landing pages, Crossref JSON, and library catalogues prove *that a source exists*, never *what it says*. `retrieved_passage` must be false unless directly relevant text was read.
2. **Snippet laundering**: search-result fragments, Google Books result lines, and JSTOR page-1 previews quoted as if the passage were read in context.
3. **Abstract ≠ argument**: publisher abstracts support bibliographic facts, not an author's thesis structure (directly at risk for TIB-A16, meso-13/14).
4. **Secondary restatement**: textbook/blog/news/encyclopedia paraphrases standing in for the named primary (TURK-07, SR-REG-07, KIN-A07, meso-16).
5. **Emic/institutional statements as neutral evidence**: dalailama.com (TIB-A12) — fine as an attested emic position, not as case evidence; do not adjudicate living authority.
6. **Crowd-edited references**: Wiktionary/Wikipedia are locators; underlying dictionary portals and academic sources must be named in `limits` when not fetched directly (S09-07, TURK-06).
7. **OCR witnesses**: djvu.txt transcriptions are one witness of a printed text; record as transcription with locator (KIN-A07, ORALITY-05 if re-consulted).
8. **Unofficial translations**: Japanese Law Translation and mirror statute sites are not 道路交通法施行令 (COL-06).
9. **Version/venue drift**: preprint vs journal-of-record versions carrying different metadata (PHON-A11); misdated citations in OA secondaries (TURK-07).
10. **Verification by absence**: a zero-hit search in one corpus of text is consistency evidence only, never proof of non-existence (TURK-06, S09-07).

## 5. Coupling pitfalls (language / myth / era / dress)

- **Keep verdicts claim-scoped and couplings inspectable.** Each audit's `coupling_implication` should say what the claim, if it moved, would do to language/myth/era/dress reasoning — not restate the bibliography. Three recurring failure shapes expected:
  1. **Date drift → era staging**: OTT-06, meso-13/14/15/16 are all dating questions feeding era/dress or era/language staging. A wrong or "conveniently resolved" date silently re-stages garments, decrees, and documents; the prior rows correctly refused to resolve disagreements. The round's discipline is *preserving the disagreement*, so a `supported` verdict that picks one date is a coupling harm even when a date is real.
  2. **Lexical/implement readings → myth and dress rows**: S09-07 (scissors reading touches a kinship-rite costume-table row), SOG-29 (iconography touches symbol grammar), TURK-06/07 (wolf-ancestry and qut touch legitimacy language and myth). The inference edge (lexical observation → costume inference, per provenance-audit-ledger-format.md §2) must stay labeled as inference, never as a second attestation.
  3. **Register/lexicon counts → generation rules**: SR-REG-04 (speech-level counts feed politeness modeling), KIN-A08 (borrowing counts feed contact vectors), ORALITY-05 (line counts feed formula-density calibration). Inflating a partial or tradition-specific count into a universal number would propagate into tooling, which is why the template forbids manufacturing one universal count.
- One notation, one manuscript, one treatment, one language: none of the twenty rows licenses generalization across manuscripts, paintings, treatments, or languages. Where prior waves drew exactly that boundary (meso-15, meso-13, SR-REG-07), it must survive into the new audits.
